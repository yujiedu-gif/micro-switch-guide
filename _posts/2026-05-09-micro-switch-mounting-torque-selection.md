A defensible micro switch mounting torque selection depends on the documented switch and the mounting interface, not on a universal value. Without applicable instructions that identify the fastener, mounting arrangement, and relevant conditions, there is no supported numeric torque to recommend. Do not substitute an actuator-force figure or an electrical contact rating: those describe different requirements. First establish which documentation governs the switch and the support it is mounted to, then use only a torque limit that applies to that arrangement. Even a documented, correctly applied tightening torque does not by itself prove that the switch operates correctly or is suitable for the circuit and load.

## What micro switch mounting torque selection means

Mounting torque selection is the task of choosing an allowed tightening torque for a particular documented fastener and mounting arrangement. It concerns the joint formed by the mounting fastener, switch body, and supporting interface. It does not describe how much force the actuator needs to move, nor does it describe the electrical conditions that the contacts can handle.

There is no single mounting-torque value that can be applied to every micro switch. The applicable value, if specified, depends on the documented switch and installation conditions. The fastener type and the support or threaded interface also matter because their requirements may impose separate limits. A value stated for one arrangement cannot automatically be carried over to another switch, fastener, or mounting surface.

If the available information does not identify a torque for the actual arrangement, do not estimate one from the switch’s size or appearance, and do not treat an unrelated value as a substitute. The selection boundary is the applicable documentation. Until that documentation is available and matched to the installation, the numeric torque remains unresolved.

This is a documentation-based selection task, not an invitation to infer a value from general descriptions of micro switches. A switch may have a documented mounting limit, while the fastener or the supporting material may have a different documented constraint. The chosen value must remain within the limits that actually apply to the assembled arrangement. If one of those limits or its conditions is unknown, the available evidence does not establish a safe torque value for that installation.

## Find the governing limits for the switch and mounting interface

Start with the switch-specific installation instructions. Check whether they state a mounting-fastener torque and identify the conditions attached to it. A value is useful only if its scope matches the switch and the mounting arrangement under consideration. Preserve stated conditions rather than detaching a number from its instructions.

Next, identify the fastener and the actual support or threaded interface. Use their applicable documented limits where provided. The switch instructions do not automatically establish what a separate supporting interface permits, and an interface limit does not automatically establish the switch’s own mounting limit. Consider both rather than assuming that one document covers every part of the joint.

Before choosing a value, establish whether the documentation describes the actual fastener arrangement and mounting conditions. If the fastener, switch, support, or installation differs from what the instructions cover, the stated torque may not apply. Do not resolve a mismatch by selecting a value from a visually similar component or a different installation.

If instructions are missing, ambiguous, or mismatched, obtain authoritative installation information for the documented switch and interface. Do not fill gaps by guessing, by transferring a torque from another design, or by treating a general reference as switch-specific evidence. Until the applicable limits and conditions are clear, a numeric selection is not supported.

In practical terms, the information needed is: the applicable switch installation instructions, the specified mounting fastener and arrangement, the support or threaded interface, and any documented torque limits and conditions for those parts. This checklist identifies evidence to find; it does not create a torque value or prescribe a fastening procedure. If the documentation does not resolve which requirement governs, that uncertainty must remain explicit.

## Keep mounting torque separate from actuator and electrical requirements

Tightening torque concerns the mounting fastener and the joint it forms. Actuator force concerns the mechanical input and operation of the switch. These are different parameters, with different meanings and governing information. An actuator-force specification does not tell you how tightly to secure a mounting screw, and a mounting-torque limit does not establish the force needed to operate the actuator.

The term “release force” is also not a mounting-torque instruction. In the cited standard definition, release force is the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. The reference to force or torque describes the actuator-related release parameter; it does not set a tightening torque for the mounting fastener. Do not transfer that definition or a value associated with it into a mounting requirement.

Electrical contact ratings address electrical conditions, not the mechanical tightening of the mounting fastener. A contact rating therefore cannot establish a mounting torque, and a mounting-torque value cannot establish electrical suitability for a particular circuit or load. Keep each requirement tied to the documentation that specifies it rather than translating a number from one category into another.

This separation matters when instructions or labels present several parameters together. A number belongs to the parameter, unit, state, and conditions actually documented. Do not infer a mounting value from operating force, release force, or an electrical rating merely because all concern the same switch. If the mounting limit is absent, a value for another parameter does not supply it.

## Apply the documented value and verify the boundary

When the applicable documentation provides a torque, use it only under its stated fastener, mounting, and installation conditions. Confirm that the installed fastener arrangement corresponds to those instructions and that the switch is seated as directed. Follow the relevant installation instructions for checks of seating and mechanical clearance; do not invent a separate procedure or assume that a general check substitutes for the documented requirements.

If the conditions do not match, pause the selection rather than adapting the number by guesswork. A torque documented for a different fastener or support may not apply to the installed arrangement. The correct response to missing or mismatched information is to resolve the documentation boundary, not to claim that a particular value is universally acceptable.

A torque-compliant mounting establishes only that the documented tightening requirement was followed under its stated conditions. It does not, by itself, establish correct actuator movement, contact behavior, wiring, or suitability for an electrical load. Those questions require their own applicable evidence and checks; do not treat mounting torque as a test of the complete switch or equipment.

For wiring work, the cited United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements. This boundary does not create a switch-specific wiring procedure or change how mounting torque is selected.

The decision is therefore limited but clear: select a numeric mounting torque only when applicable documentation covers the switch, fastener, support, and installation conditions. Where that basis is missing or uncertain, do not infer a value. Where it is documented, apply it within scope and treat mounting verification as distinct from proof of mechanical operation or electrical suitability. That is the boundary for micro switch mounting torque selection.

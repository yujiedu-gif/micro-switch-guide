# Comparing Electrical Boxes at Switch Locations

Electrical switch box types are best compared by where and how they will be installed, how they fit the wall or mounting surface, and whether their capacity suits the installation. This guide concerns electrical boxes that house wiring and a wall switch at a building location. It does not cover choosing a micro switch mechanism, identifying switch terminals, or wiring a switch.

A box and a switch are different components. The box provides a location for the wiring and switch; it does not establish the switch’s electrical ratings or suitability for a particular load. Compare box formats by construction, mounting method, available capacity, and installation conditions. Treat each as a selection factor to verify, not as a guarantee that a box fits a particular project.

## What electrical switch box types does this guide compare?

The box types discussed here are enclosures used at building switch locations. They may be installed while framing is accessible or fitted into an existing finished wall, and they may be attached to framing or mounted by another method suited to the location. The relevant comparison is the relationship between the box and its installation context, not the internal design of the switch.

A switch’s electrical ratings are a separate matter. A rating for one current type, load, or test condition does not establish a rating for another. Use only the rating explicitly stated for the applicable current type, load, and test condition, as shown in the applicable specification sheet. Box format alone cannot answer that question.

For box comparisons, consider four dimensions: construction material, mounting method, physical format, and capacity. Also account for the conditions at the installation location. These dimensions help organize the choice, but they do not replace applicable installation requirements or confirm that a particular box is suitable.

## Which box formats suit different mounting situations?

New-work and old-work describe broad installation contexts. A new-work box is considered when the wall or framing is accessible during installation. An old-work box is considered when fitting a box into an existing finished wall. The distinction is about access and installation context; it does not, by itself, establish capacity, compatibility, or approval for a specific location.

Mounting method is another point of comparison. Some boxes attach to framing, while others are installed in an existing wall without the same access to framing. The wall construction, available space, and the method by which the box is secured affect which format can be considered. Do not assume that a box intended for one mounting situation will suit another just because its outer dimensions appear similar.

For a surface-mounted location, compare formats intended for the relevant mounting context with those used within a wall. The surface and the way the box is supported matter. The available claims do not establish particular mounting hardware, substrate requirements, or a universal method for securing any box, so those details must be verified for the specific installation.

Classify the location first: accessible framing, an existing finished wall, or a surface-mounted arrangement. Then check the proposed box’s mounting information and the installation requirements that apply. This process narrows the comparison without turning a general box category into a project-specific approval.

## How do box material and shape affect the comparison?

Metallic and nonmetallic boxes are broad construction categories. Their labels alone do not show that one is universally better or suitable for every switch location. Compare the actual box’s documented characteristics with the installation conditions and applicable requirements. Claims about grounding, bonding, or other code-dependent details require installation-specific evidence; they should not be inferred from material alone.

Shape and gang count are layout considerations. A rectangular format may be considered for a single-device layout, while multi-gang formats provide a wider arrangement for more than one device. The intended layout is only one part of the check. A particular shape or gang count does not establish that the box has sufficient capacity or that it fits the wall and mounting arrangement.

Physical dimensions and appearance can help distinguish formats, but they do not prove suitability. Two boxes that look similar may differ in their documented capacity or installation conditions. Conversely, a box that appears larger is not automatically acceptable for a particular installation. Check the information for the specific box rather than relying on visual comparison.

Keep enclosure protection questions separate from this comparison. An enclosure protection classification has a defined standards scope; it does not follow from a box’s material, shape, or appearance. This guide does not explain how to choose a weatherproof box or interpret an IP rating. Those questions require their own evidence and scope.

## How should you check capacity and installation conditions?

Capacity is installation-specific. Conductor and device occupancy must be checked against applicable requirements; a general box category or external size does not establish that the installation meets them. Relevant variables include the box’s documented dimensions, the number of devices, the conductors present, and the conditions of the installation.

Do not treat capacity as a universal property of a shape or material. The information needed depends on the particular box and the installation. A box’s stated dimensions may be part of the assessment, but dimensions alone do not confirm compliance or settle how conductors and devices are accounted for under applicable requirements.

Detailed capacity calculations and jurisdiction-specific decisions are outside this comparison. Use the dedicated capacity guide and applicable code for those checks. If requirements depend on the jurisdiction or installation details, resolve those before treating a candidate box as appropriate. This guide does not calculate box fill or approve a particular installation.

Keep the switch-device question separate as well. Box capacity and mounting do not identify switch terminals, determine wiring, or establish electrical ratings. For any electrical rating, the applicable specification sheet must state the current type, load, and test condition; a value for one condition does not establish another. The box comparison cannot fill gaps in switch-specific evidence.

## What should you verify before choosing among electrical switch box types?

Use this checklist to organize a comparison:

- **Location and access:** Is the installation at accessible framing, in an existing finished wall, or on a surface?
- **Mounting method:** How is the candidate box intended to be mounted, and does that match the wall or support available?
- **Format:** Does its shape and gang count suit the intended layout?
- **Capacity:** Have the box dimensions, number of devices, conductors, and installation conditions been checked against applicable requirements?
- **Environment:** Have conditions at the location and any relevant requirements been evaluated using evidence for the specific installation?
- **Documentation:** Are the box’s stated characteristics and the applicable requirements available for review?

Treat each item as a verification task, not a presumption. Neither appearance nor a broad category such as new-work, old-work, metallic, or nonmetallic proves compatibility or suitability. Resolve box questions using the specific box information and installation requirements. Keep switch selection, terminal identification, and wiring decisions outside the box-format comparison.

The practical comparison is therefore about mounting context, format, and capacity considerations, followed by checks against the requirements for the actual location. Before settling on electrical switch box types for an installation, verify the specific box information and applicable requirements rather than relying on a visual match or category name.

Micro switch contact welding is a contact-level fault in which contacts remain electrically joined when the documented contact arrangement and actuator state indicate they should separate. Persistent operation in an installed circuit can raise suspicion, but it does not by itself prove that the switch contacts are welded. The actuator, terminal identification, wiring, or other equipment circuitry may also explain the behavior.

A sound diagnosis therefore separates the isolated switch from the surrounding circuit. First establish the documented contact arrangement and reference states, then compare continuity in defined actuator states under safe test conditions. A click or one continuity reading is not enough to certify the switch or the larger assembly.

## What micro switch contact welding means

Contact welding means that contacts inside a switch remain electrically joined when they should separate according to that switch’s documented contact arrangement and reference state. It describes the electrical condition of the contacts, not merely a circuit that stays on. Persistent current or an output that does not change in an installed system is a symptom with several possible causes, not a diagnosis of welded contacts.

A miniature snap-action switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement and is commonly used for position or limit detection. That general function does not establish the arrangement of a particular switch. The terminal identities, intended contact states, and reference condition must come from documentation for the switch being checked. Do not assume a common, normally open, or normally closed configuration when it has not been documented.

This distinction matters because a switch may fail to change state for reasons other than contacts remaining joined. The external mechanism may not move the actuator as intended. Terminals may have been identified incorrectly, or wiring and other equipment circuitry may keep the installed circuit in the same apparent state. Contact welding is supported only when the observed behavior is attributable to the isolated switch contacts and conflicts with the documented expected behavior.

## How micro switch contact welding can happen

Contact welding is a possible contact-level consequence of electrical stress, but the available evidence does not establish a universal cause, threshold, or risk level for all micro switches. Electrical ratings are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. A rating value therefore has meaning only with its specified load and test conditions; it cannot be transferred to a different load or treated as a general limit for every switch.

For example, MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets tie rating values to stated load and test conditions. That scope does not establish that any particular switch has a particular rating, nor does it identify an actual cause of a suspected failure in an installed device. Without the relevant switch documentation and test context, the electrical stress that may have contributed remains unresolved.

The diagnostic implication is narrow: a history or condition consistent with electrical stress can make contact damage a possibility, but it does not prove that the contacts have fused together. Do not infer a welding event just because a circuit remains energized, or infer suitability or failure from an isolated rating value. The load, operating conditions, documented rating, and observed contact behavior all matter, and the evidence here does not supply product-specific values or a welding threshold.

## Symptoms that can suggest welded contacts

A persistent circuit state can raise suspicion if the documented contact arrangement should separate in the relevant actuator state. For example, if the installed function does not change when expected, that observation identifies a symptom to investigate. It does not locate the fault inside the switch. The actuator may not be reaching the relevant position, the terminal arrangement may have been misread, or a wiring or equipment-circuit condition may produce a similar result.

An audible click is also limited evidence. It can indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove continuity, contact condition, correct terminal identification, or suitable operation under the installed load. A click therefore cannot distinguish welded contacts from a problem in the mechanical input path or from a fault elsewhere in the equipment.

Look for a distinction between what is observed at the equipment and what is measured at the isolated switch. If the equipment continues to show the same state, that is an installed-system symptom. Only a properly isolated check, with documented terminals and defined actuator states, can provide evidence about the switch’s own contact behavior. Even then, the result must be compared with the expected state for that particular arrangement. Without that comparison, a reading that appears persistent is not enough to label the contacts welded.

## How to check a micro switch for contact welding

An isolated continuity check addresses the switch contacts, not the safety or condition of the complete installed circuit. Before such a check, the switch must be isolated from the circuit so other paths do not affect the measurement. Work involving electrical hazards must stay within the applicable safe procedure. Under the cited United States workplace rule, exposed live parts generally must be deenergized before work; a qualified person must verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and work on energized parts is limited to qualified persons. If safe isolation or the required work boundary is uncertain, stop and leave the task to qualified personnel.

Use only documented terminal identities and reference states for the specific contact arrangement. With the switch isolated, compare continuity in defined actuator states against the expected behavior for that arrangement. The evidence pack does not specify a particular terminal layout, state sequence, or meter procedure for an unidentified switch, so none should be assumed. If the documentation does not establish which terminals to test or what behavior is expected, the check cannot support a reliable welding diagnosis.

Persistent continuity in a state where the documented contacts should separate is evidence consistent with contacts remaining joined. It supports suspicion of a contact-level fault when the switch is isolated, the terminals are correctly identified, the actuator state is defined, and the expected behavior is documented. It does not, by itself, establish why the condition occurred, whether it is permanent, or whether every function of the switch is defective. A single reading also cannot rule out a measurement problem or establish the behavior in all relevant states.

## What a test result does, and does not, prove

Interpret the result according to what was actually tested. A mismatch between isolated continuity and documented contact behavior supports a switch-level contact problem. If the isolated switch follows its documented behavior, the persistent installed-system symptom may instead involve actuator movement, terminal identification, wiring, or other equipment circuitry. That result narrows the question but does not identify which external cause is responsible.

Likewise, a click without a matching electrical state change does not establish welding. It may show mechanical movement while leaving the electrical contact condition unresolved. Conversely, an unexpected continuity reading is meaningful only if isolation, terminal identity, actuator state, and expected behavior are all established. Do not use an isolated reading to infer that the installed circuit is safe, or to declare the surrounding equipment good or bad.

Stop when the contact arrangement or expected states are undocumented, when safe isolation cannot be established, or when the available test does not distinguish switch behavior from other circuit paths. The defensible conclusion may be that contact welding is consistent with the evidence, not verified, or that the evidence is insufficient to distinguish it from another fault. A diagnosis should not exceed what the documented states and isolated observations show.

In short, micro switch contact welding is a specific failure of contacts to separate as expected, not a synonym for any persistent circuit condition. Establish the documented arrangement, isolate the switch safely, and compare continuity across defined actuator states. Treat persistent installed behavior, a click, or a single reading as clues only unless the test conditions and expected contact behavior support a narrower conclusion.

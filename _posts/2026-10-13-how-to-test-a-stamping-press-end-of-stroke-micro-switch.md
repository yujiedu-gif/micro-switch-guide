How to test a stamping press end of stroke micro switch begins with a strict boundary: test the switch’s isolated contacts, not the press’s control system or stopping function. Some stamping press designs use a mechanically actuated micro switch to sense end of stroke, but the presence, contact arrangement, and effect of that switch depend on the specific equipment design. Do not assume that a physical layout or a click identifies the terminals or proves the press responds correctly.

Proceed only under the press-specific safe-work procedure and with the switch electrically isolated as required. Then identify the switch, its documented terminals, and the actuator state before taking a continuity reading. If you cannot establish safe isolation, the relevant documentation, or your qualification to perform the work, stop and refer it to qualified personnel.

## What an end-of-stroke micro switch test can establish

An isolated continuity check can establish whether the documented contact pair shows the specified continuity behavior at defined actuator states under the test conditions used. It cannot establish that the press’s wiring, control logic, interlocks, or end-of-stroke stopping function operates correctly. That distinction matters because the switch is one component in an equipment circuit, and a test of the component does not test the complete circuit.

Some stamping press designs use a micro switch as a mechanically actuated end-of-stroke input. That possibility is not proof that every press uses one, that the switch occupies a particular position, or that a given contact state causes the press to stop. Those details are design-specific. Follow the press documentation to identify the relevant component and its documented role; do not infer the arrangement from a general description of stamping presses.

Keep four questions separate: does the mechanism move the switch actuator as documented; are the switch terminals identified from the applicable marking or diagram; do the isolated contacts behave as specified at defined actuator states; and is there a fault elsewhere in the press circuit or mechanism? A continuity test addresses only the contact behavior being measured. A click may indicate mechanical movement, but it is not a continuity result and does not validate the control system.

## How to test a stamping press end of stroke micro switch safely

Before approaching the switch, follow the press-specific safe-work procedure and applicable de-energization and qualification requirements. The procedure must establish that the equipment is safely de-energized and address stored energy and access hazards under the applicable requirements. Do not treat an off command, a stopped press, or an isolated meter reading as proof that the equipment is safe to access.

The switch must be electrically isolated as required by the documented procedure before an isolated contact check. Press documentation and the switch’s documentation are needed to establish the correct isolation method and test conditions. Do not improvise an isolation step or proceed on the assumption that disconnecting an apparent lead is sufficient. This article does not supply a press-specific energy-control procedure.

Do not proceed if safe isolation cannot be established, if the switch or relevant terminals cannot be identified from documentation, or if the work is outside your qualifications. Stop and have qualified personnel resolve the uncertainty. Do not bypass the switch, energize the press to observe its response, or use the contact check as authorization to return equipment to service.

## Identify the micro switch, actuator, and documented terminals

Use the press documentation to locate the switch associated with the end-of-stroke input. Observe the documented mechanical relationship between the press mechanism and the switch actuator. The actuator is the part moved by the external mechanism; seeing it move can help distinguish mechanical input from contact measurement. Do not change the switch position, linkage, or adjustment while identifying that relationship.

Identify terminals from the switch marking and applicable connection diagram, checked against the equipment documentation. Do not infer terminal identity from terminal location, wire color, switch shape, or what the press appears to do. A wiring diagram and rating boundary applies to the exact switch and equipment: use the documented connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load, rather than a generic physical-layout inference. The documented rating and circuit function do not make an isolated continuity check a test of the installed circuit.

Use the documented contact arrangement and reference condition to determine which contact pair and actuator states to compare. COM means common, and NO or NC describes contact state only when that arrangement and reference condition are documented for the switch. Do not assign those labels by appearance or assume a state from the press’s end-of-stroke behavior. If terminal identification or the reference state remains uncertain, do not guess; stop and seek qualified help or the required documentation.

## Check isolated contact continuity at defined actuator states

With the switch electrically isolated as required by its documented procedure, use an appropriate meter method to check continuity across the documented contact pair. Record the actuator state for each reading, using the states identified in the switch or press documentation. Compare the observations only with the specified contact behavior under the applicable test conditions. Do not invent a resistance threshold, actuator force, travel limit, voltage, or test limit when none is documented.

Keep the mechanical observation separate from the electrical result. If the documented mechanism moves the actuator, note that movement without adjusting it. Then take the isolated contact reading for the defined state. Where the procedure specifies another actuator state, record and compare that state separately. A click is not evidence that the contacts have continuity, and continuity in one state alone does not establish the expected behavior in another state.

Travel terminology may help describe documented actuator states, but it does not supply a test limit. Under MIL-PRF-8805K sections 6.5.14 and 6.5.16, pretravel runs from free position to operating position, while overtravel runs from operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. Use those terms only when relevant to the documented design and test; do not infer a required travel distance or apply an undocumented force or limit. Similarly, an insulation-resistance evaluation is distinct from a continuity check: under the applicable MIL-PRF-8805K requirement and referenced test method, it evaluates specified electrically isolated points using the applicable test method and conditions. Do not substitute one type of test for the other.

## Interpret results without diagnosing the whole press

An unexpected, inconsistent, or changing reading does not identify a cause by itself. Possible points to review include terminal identification, actuator movement, the switch contacts, the test setup, and measurement conditions. Compare readings with the documented contact behavior and test method before drawing a conclusion. If the documentation, isolation condition, or test setup is uncertain, the reading cannot establish whether the switch has failed.

A reading that matches the specified isolated contact behavior supports only a conclusion about the tested contact pair in the tested actuator state and conditions. It does not prove that wiring is correct, that the press control logic or interlocks work, or that the press will stop at end of stroke. A press fault may also involve the linkage, wiring, control system, or another part of the equipment. Do not treat one component test as a diagnosis of the whole press.

Do not bypass the switch, energize the equipment to confirm behavior, or return the press to service based on an isolated continuity result alone. If a reading is unexpected, the mechanical relationship is unclear, or the cause could be outside the switch, stop and refer the unresolved work to qualified personnel under the applicable procedures. How to test a stamping press end of stroke micro switch is therefore a bounded component check: establish safe isolation, use documented terminals and actuator states, and leave press-level operation to the required equipment procedures and qualified personnel.

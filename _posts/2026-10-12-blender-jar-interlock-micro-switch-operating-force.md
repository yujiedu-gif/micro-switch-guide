## What blender jar interlock micro switch operating force means

Operating force, also called actuating force in the cited standard, is the force or torque applied to the actuating mechanism to operate the contacts. This describes force at the switch actuator, not a universal force at the jar or lid. A switch specification applies under its stated test conditions. Without those conditions and the applicable switch information, a particular numerical value or a comparison with the force a person feels at the lid cannot be established.

Operating force also differs from release force. Release force is the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. It describes the return threshold, not the force required to operate the contacts in the first place.

Neither quantity is the same as the force required to seat a jar or close a lid. Seating involves the appliance’s physical arrangement and may include actions that do not translate directly into force on the switch actuator. A user may feel resistance from the jar, lid, or linkage, while the switch specification describes the actuator input needed to operate the contacts. Those forces are related through the installed mechanism, but the switch’s operating-force definition alone does not give their relationship.

Keep three terms distinct when discussing movement. Pretravel is the distance or angle through which the actuator moves from free position to operating position. Overtravel is the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. These are travel definitions, not force definitions. They help describe where operation occurs and how much movement is available beyond that point, but they do not supply a force value for a complete blender assembly.

Aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch maintenance applies only when the aircraft design uses a micro switch to monitor the landing gear condition in question. Inspection and verification must follow the approved instructions for that aircraft and configuration. Those instructions govern access, safety controls, test conditions, limits, and maintenance disposition. This workflow does not establish switch function, adjustment dimensions, or permission to return an aircraft to service. Do not transfer assumptions from another aircraft installation.

The inspection separates three questions: Does the installation allow the switch to be actuated as specified? Does the switch change state under the prescribed test? Does that state produce the expected indication? Keeping those checks distinct helps direct fault isolation without assuming that an incorrect indication proves the switch itself has failed.

## Scope and safety before aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch maintenance

First confirm from the applicable aircraft maintenance information that the configuration uses a micro switch for the monitored landing gear condition. Do not infer its purpose from appearance, location, or another installation. The switch's precise function and the relationship between its state and cockpit or other indication remain configuration-specific.

Before access or testing, use the approved maintenance instructions for the required isolation, access, gear support, and protection against unintended movement. Follow the procedures and limits applicable to the aircraft and the task. This article does not provide a generic method for supporting landing gear, controlling aircraft movement, or making an electrical test safe. If the approved instructions or necessary configuration information are unavailable, the inspection cannot be defined from a generic procedure.

Use the aircraft's approved maintenance data to establish the required switch state, actuation point, operating limits, test sequence, and acceptance criteria. Do not infer adjustment direction or limits from a switch's physical layout. A generic connection diagram or apparent terminal position is not enough to identify electrical function. Where electrical connections are part of the procedure, use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment rather than relying on physical-layout inference.

## Inspect the micro switch mounting and actuation path

Within the approved access limits, inspect the switch body and mounting for visible damage, looseness, or evidence of wear. Check fasteners and the associated actuator, linkage, or target for damage, contamination, interference, or movement that could affect contact. Include only components identified by the aircraft instructions as part of the monitored installation; do not assume that nearby hardware belongs to the switch's actuation path.

Observe the physical relationship between the actuator and the linkage or target according to the approved inspection procedure. Determine whether contact occurs at the specified point and whether movement follows the specified sequence. Look for binding, inconsistent contact, or signs that the target or mounting shifts. A switch that is secure but contacted incorrectly may not reach its intended state; conversely, apparent alignment alone does not prove that the switch operates correctly.

Treat actuator position, travel, contact point, and adjustment limits as specific to the installation. Use the applicable maintenance data for each value and acceptance decision. Do not apply a universal gap, dimension, travel, or adjustment instruction. If the observed geometry falls outside the stated limits, or the limits are unavailable, do not create a correction by estimation. Record the condition and follow the approved maintenance process.

## Verify the switch state and landing gear indication

Carry out the state and indication check only under the aircraft-approved test conditions and sequence. Use the specified test points, instruments, electrical limits, and acceptance criteria. The required switch state may depend on the particular installation and test condition, so a state observed in isolation cannot establish correct operation unless the procedure defines how to interpret it.

Compare the observed switch state with the state required at the prescribed actuation condition. If the switch does not change state as specified, return attention to the approved checks of actuation geometry, movement, mounting, and switch operation. Do not assume an electrical failure until the procedure's mechanical and electrical checks support that conclusion.

If the switch state changes as prescribed but the landing gear indication is absent or inconsistent, the fault may lie downstream of the switch. Continue only with the connector, wiring, circuit, and indication checks permitted by the maintenance data. Follow the documented circuit information and limits for the exact equipment. Do not substitute a generic terminal map, infer a permissible load, or bridge a circuit to see whether the display changes.

An IP code, if supplied for an enclosure, states defined enclosure-protection characteristics. It does not by itself establish electrical rating, chemical compatibility, corrosion resistance, vibration endurance, condensation performance, or complete application suitability. Evaluate any such application requirements separately; the code is not a substitute for aircraft-specific approval or maintenance instructions.

## Isolate intermittent or incorrect indications

For an intermittent indication, begin by checking whether the specified actuation is repeatable under the approved test. Observe the actuator, mounting, and linkage or target for binding, marginal alignment, or movement only within the access and test limits in the instructions. Record when the indication changes or fails to change, if the prescribed procedure calls for that observation. Do not provoke movement or vibration beyond the approved test conditions.

Next compare switch state with indication. If actuation is inconsistent or the switch state fails to change as specified, continue with the permitted mechanical and switch checks. If state changes correctly but the indication remains absent, incorrect, or intermittent, follow the approved downstream path through connectors, wiring, and the indication circuit. Check only the points and conditions named in the applicable maintenance data. This staged approach helps distinguish an actuation problem from a switch-state problem or a downstream circuit fault without presuming which component is defective.

Avoid bypassing protections, making unapproved adjustments, or using improvised electrical checks. If the fault remains unresolved, the available procedure does not cover the observed condition, or required limits are uncertain, stop and escalate through the applicable maintenance process. Do not treat a temporarily correct indication as proof that an intermittent fault has been resolved.

## Document findings and confirm maintenance disposition

Record the observed installation condition, switch-state results, indication results, and any corrective action as required by the applicable maintenance procedures. Include the maintenance reference and test conditions required for traceability. Report unresolved, intermittent, or out-of-limit findings through the prescribed process rather than recording an assumed cause as confirmed.

After a permitted correction, repeat only the verification specified by the approved instructions. Confirm the required switch state and resulting indication under the stated test conditions, and document the results. A successful check supports the prescribed maintenance disposition; it does not authorize an individual to return the aircraft to service.

The inspection is complete only when findings and disposition have been recorded and handled under applicable maintenance procedures. Leave return-to-service decisions to authorized personnel under applicable requirements. This workflow does not replace aircraft-specific approved maintenance data or expand the authority of the person performing the inspection.

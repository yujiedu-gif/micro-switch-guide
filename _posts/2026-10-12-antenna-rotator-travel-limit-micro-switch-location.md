## Separate the switch, trip point, and travel stop

Three positions are easy to confuse but describe different things:

- **Switch mounting point:** the fixed location where the switch is attached to the rotator’s structure.
- **Trip point:** the position in the moving mechanism where a cam, lever, or other feature actuates the switch.
- **Travel stop:** the boundary that limits movement, physically or electrically.

These points may be near one another, but they need not coincide. A switch may be mounted away from the trip point when a moving linkage transfers motion to its actuator. The trip point may also occur before a hard mechanical stop. Thus, finding the switch does not by itself reveal where travel ends, and finding a travel boundary does not prove that the switch is mounted there.

The travel boundary can be physical or electrical, so avoid treating every apparent endpoint as a hard stop or every switch operation as direct evidence of the rotor’s final mechanical position.

The identifier **md3304x** is a text string to verify, not enough evidence by itself to identify a particular switch or establish its specifications. Without reliable documentation that explicitly connects this exact identifier to a defined item, its identity and characteristics remain unresolved. This guide explains how to examine the marking and its context, distinguish observations from conclusions, and decide which claims still need evidence. It does not identify a product, provide ratings, or establish compatibility or wiring.

## What does md3304x establish by itself?

By itself, **md3304x** establishes only that this character sequence has been supplied or observed. It does not establish that the item is a micro switch, what type of component it is, who made it, or what application it serves. It also does not establish electrical ratings, dimensions, terminal arrangement, certification, or compatibility.

Keep the observation separate from any interpretation. For example, if the text appears on a component, the supported observation is that the component bears that marking. If it appears in a parts list, the supported observation is that the document contains the identifier. Neither observation alone proves that the text maps to a specific component or version. A label can be abbreviated, incomplete, or used differently across records, so interpretation requires supporting documentation.

Do not treat a similar-looking item or a shared portion of a marking as proof of identity. Appearance and partial text may help locate records to investigate, but they do not establish that two items are electrically or mechanically equivalent. Likewise, a search result or an isolated mention is not sufficient unless its source and context reliably connect the exact identifier to a defined item.

The practical boundary is simple: report what was directly observed, then state which conclusions have been verified and which remain unknown. If no authoritative exact-identifier match is available, leave identity and specifications unresolved rather than completing the record by inference.

## How to verify md3304x in its document context

Start by recording the marking exactly as it appears. Preserve capitalization, punctuation, spacing, and neighboring text; do not silently normalize or expand the identifier. Note whether **md3304x** is printed on the component, shown in equipment documentation, or copied from another record. These contexts are not interchangeable, and the source matters when judging what the text refers to.

Next, examine the surrounding information. A nearby diagram, parts list, revision notation, or equipment reference may clarify what the marking labels, but only if the document makes that relationship explicit. Keep a record of the document title, revision or date when available, the relevant page or section, and the exact wording that links the identifier to an item. Avoid treating nearby text as a definitive link when the document does not establish one.

Then look for authoritative documentation that explicitly identifies the exact marking and defines the item or applicable variant. Confirm that the document’s identifier matches the observed text, including any suffixes or other distinguishing characters. Check whether the document applies to the same equipment context and revision. A document that refers only to a similar code, a broader family, or an unspecified variant does not resolve the identity of the exact item.

Finally, compare the documentation with the observed marking and record both matches and discrepancies. If the component label is incomplete, the records conflict, or the documentation does not cover the observed version, state that the match is unresolved. Do not fill gaps by assuming that a familiar-looking format has a standard meaning. This process can produce a useful result even when that result is limited: the identifier was checked, the available evidence was assessed, and specific facts remain unverified.

## Which claims need separate evidence?

Identity and manufacturer claims require an authoritative source that explicitly connects the exact identifier to the item. A resemblance to another component, a partial code match, or an undocumented assumption is not enough. If such evidence is absent, neither the item’s identity nor its manufacturer should be stated as fact.

Electrical ratings require documentation that applies to the exact item and the relevant conditions. Under MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. A rating therefore cannot be inferred from the identifier or transferred from a superficially similar item. Durability claims also depend on their test scope: MIL-PRF-8805K qualification and conformance results apply only with the specified electrical load, operating sequence, cycle count, and environmental or test conditions.

Terminal arrangement likewise needs exact-device evidence. A generic changeover contact may be labeled common, normally closed, and normally open, but the exact device marking or schematic controls. Those generic labels do not establish the terminals on an unidentified item. A wiring diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load must be documented for the exact switch and equipment; physical layout alone is not a reliable basis for identifying terminals. Do not use **md3304x** itself to determine wiring.

Certification and compatibility are separate questions, too. A certification claim needs applicable documentation for the exact item, and a compatibility claim needs evidence tied to both the item and the intended equipment or function. Similar appearance, shared partial markings, or a general category label cannot establish suitability as a replacement. An IP code, if one is documented, states defined enclosure-protection characteristics; it does not by itself establish electrical rating, chemical compatibility, corrosion resistance, vibration endurance, condensation performance, or complete application suitability.

## What to do when the identifier remains unresolved

If available evidence does not make an exact match, treat the identity and specifications as unknown. Keep the observed text, its source, and the unresolved questions together so that later documentation can be checked against the original context. A clear record should distinguish confirmed observations from assumptions and should not present an inference as a product fact.

For decisions that affect equipment or safety, use the equipment documentation and applicable qualified procedures rather than relying on the identifier alone. Applicable deenergization and qualification requirements remain relevant when examining electrical connections or evaluating a switch. This article does not provide wiring instructions, and a text string cannot substitute for the exact switch’s documented connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load.

Do not select a replacement or claim compatibility based solely on **md3304x**, a similar appearance, or a partial marking. Resolve those questions only with exact, applicable evidence. If the evidence remains incomplete, the reliable conclusion is limited: the marking was observed, but the item’s identity, ratings, terminals, certification, and compatibility have not been established.

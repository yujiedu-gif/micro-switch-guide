The **ru micro switch** marking is best treated as a clue to a component’s recognition status, not as a complete statement of what the switch can do or where it may be used. Its meaning for a particular switch must be confirmed against authoritative records and documentation for the exact component. Appearance alone does not establish a rating, approval, or permitted application.

That distinction matters because a marking can be easy to overread. The verification task is specific: identify the component, confirm its status in relevant records, review any stated conditions, and check its electrical ratings separately. Component recognition also does not, by itself, establish approval of finished equipment containing that component.

## What does the ru micro switch marking indicate?

The RU marking, when present on a micro switch, indicates an association with component recognition. That interpretation is a starting point, not proof of the status of an unidentified switch. Confirm the marking and exact component identity against authoritative certification documentation before relying on it.

A visible marking does not independently state the full scope of recognition or the conditions under which it applies. The exact part identity and the authoritative record matter. If the identification is incomplete, unclear, or does not match a record, the component’s status remains unresolved from the marking alone.

The mark also is not a standalone performance specification. It does not, by itself, establish electrical limits, environmental performance, or suitability for a particular circuit or product. Those facts must come from the applicable documentation for the exact switch and its conditions of use.

In practical terms, read RU as a prompt to verify component recognition, not as a substitute for verification. Do not extend that interpretation into claims about protection, switching capacity, or an application unless the relevant documentation supports them.

## What the marking does not tell you

The marking alone does not establish switching capacity, voltage, current, ingress protection, or suitability for a particular application. These are separate questions that require supporting documentation. No specific value or capability can be inferred merely from the presence of RU.

A recognition mark should also not be treated as a complete certification statement. The mark alone does not establish that every requirement applicable to a product has been met, nor does it identify every condition or limitation associated with the component’s recognition. The scope must be checked in authoritative records and related documentation.

Component recognition and approval of finished equipment are distinct. A component’s recognized status does not, on its own, establish that equipment incorporating it is approved. The evaluation of finished equipment can involve the exact component, how it is installed, the circuit, and applicable conditions. Do not transfer a conclusion about the component automatically to the product as a whole.

The same caution applies to electrical ratings. Rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. For a micro switch, use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment rather than inferring connections or ratings from a generic physical layout. The marking does not replace these checks.

## How to verify a marked micro switch

Use a component-specific verification sequence rather than relying on the appearance of the mark:

1. **Record the identification.** Note the exact component identification visible on the switch and in its documentation. Record the marking as it appears. If the identifying information is incomplete or cannot be matched consistently, do not assume which component or recognition record applies.
2. **Check authoritative records.** Compare the marking and exact part identity with authoritative certification records. Confirm that the record corresponds to the component under review, rather than treating a similar-looking mark or general component description as sufficient.
3. **Read the conditions and limitations.** Review the applicable documentation for conditions associated with the recognition. A matching identity is not enough if the intended use or installation does not meet stated conditions. If the documentation does not resolve a relevant condition, treat that point as unknown rather than filling it in by inference.
4. **Check electrical ratings separately.** Consult the applicable specification sheet for the exact switch. Ratings are tied to stated load and test conditions; do not treat them as universal capacities. For connection details, follow the documented connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal function or permissible load from physical layout alone.

For ratings covered by MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, the relevant values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions. That does not make the values universal across switches or conditions. The exact applicable documentation must support the rating being considered.

The resulting check has two separate outcomes: whether the exact component’s recognition status is supported by authoritative documentation, and whether its documented conditions and ratings fit the intended use. The RU marking by itself answers neither question completely.

## When the micro switch is used in finished equipment

A recognized component is not the same thing as approved finished equipment. Evaluation of equipment can depend on the exact component, its installation, the circuit, and applicable conditions. Therefore, a component-level record should not be used as the sole basis for a conclusion about the product containing that component.

For equipment-level conclusions, consult the relevant end-product documentation and evaluation. Check that the component identity and its conditions are addressed in that context; do not assume that recognition of a component establishes approval of the full device. The equipment documentation, rather than the visible marking alone, must support any conclusion about the finished product.

Keep the verification questions separate: does authoritative documentation support recognition of this exact component, and does the relevant equipment evaluation support its use in the finished product? Check the documented electrical ratings and conditions as part of that review. The **ru micro switch** marking is a starting point for identifying a recognition status, not a universal rating, a complete approval, or proof of suitability.

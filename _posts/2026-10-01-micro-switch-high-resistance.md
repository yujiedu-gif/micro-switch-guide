A **micro switch high resistance** reading is an observation, not automatically proof that the switch itself is defective. The first question is where the meter was connected: directly across a defined pair of contacts on an electrically isolated switch, or across a path that still includes wiring, terminals, or other circuit components. Those measurements have different boundaries and cannot be interpreted as the same test.

Contact resistance is a measured switch requirement, but its meaning depends on the specified connection points and test procedure. Without those details, a reading alone cannot establish whether the switch meets a requirement, performs properly with a load, or is responsible for a circuit problem. The sections below separate what an isolated contact measurement can show from what an in-circuit observation leaves unresolved.

## What micro switch high resistance can, and cannot, tell you

A resistance value across an isolated switch contact pair concerns that pair under the measurement conditions. An in-circuit reading, by contrast, may include a path through wiring, terminals, or other circuit components. If those parts remain connected, the measurement is not limited to the switch contacts. A high value observed in that arrangement should not be attributed automatically to the switch.

The distinction matters because the number on a meter is meaningful only in relation to the points measured and the state of the circuit. A reading taken across an installed path may indicate resistance somewhere in that path, but it does not identify which part produced the observation. Nor does it establish that every component in the path was measured in the same way or under the same conditions.

Even a reading taken directly across isolated contacts has a limited scope. It describes the measurement made at the selected contact pair and actuator state. It does not, by itself, establish the condition of other contacts, terminals, connected wiring, or the complete equipment. It also does not demonstrate how the switch will perform with a particular load. A resistance observation and a conclusion about load performance are different kinds of evidence.

Accordingly, treat “high” as a comparison that requires a documented reference. The relevant specification or test procedure must identify the connection points, conditions, and any applicable limit. Without that information, do not infer a universal acceptable or unacceptable resistance threshold. The reading may warrant closer investigation, but it is not a complete diagnosis.

## Conditions that define a micro switch contact-resistance reading

A contact-resistance result is interpretable only when the measurement boundary is clear. Identify the terminal pair using documentation applicable to the switch. Do not assume terminal names, contact arrangements, or state descriptions that have not been documented for that device. The selected pair must correspond to the contact path being evaluated.

Record the actuator state for the measurement. A contact reading is tied to the state in which the contacts were measured; a result without that state is incomplete evidence. If a test procedure specifies how the actuator is positioned, that condition belongs with the result. Do not treat readings taken in different actuator states as directly comparable without support from the applicable procedure.

Also record whether the switch was electrically isolated. An isolated measurement is bounded to the selected switch contacts, while a measurement made with circuit connections in place may include additional paths. The contact-resistance definition in MIL-PRF-8805K identifies contact resistance as a measured switch requirement whose method, connection points, and limit must come from the applicable specification and test procedure. It does not supply a universal value that can be applied to every switch and measurement setup.

For that reason, preserve the test conditions alongside any recorded value: the documented terminal pair, actuator state, isolation condition, measuring method, and applicable requirement. If one of these is unknown, state that limitation rather than filling it by assumption. A bare number without those details may be difficult to interpret, compare, or relate to a requirement.

Do not substitute a different electrical test for a contact-resistance test. Insulation resistance, for example, concerns resistance between specified electrically isolated points under the applicable test method and conditions. It is not the same measurement as resistance through a closed contact path. The name of a test and its connection points matter; a value from one test cannot answer the question posed by another.

## How to distinguish the switch reading from circuit resistance

First determine where the original measurement was made. If it was taken across an installed circuit path, document the points and the connected path as tested. The result may include wiring, terminals, and other circuit components, so it cannot isolate the switch as the source of the resistance. Do not treat that installed-circuit reading as a switch contact-resistance result unless the applicable method defines it that way.

To evaluate the switch contacts separately, use only a test setup and isolation procedure supported by the applicable documentation. The contacts must be electrically isolated for the result to be attributed to that switch contact path, and the terminal pair and actuator state must be documented. Follow the supplied isolation and test-safety instructions. If safe isolation or testing is outside the available instructions or your qualifications, leave that work to qualified personnel rather than improvising a procedure.

Where the approved procedure supports both observations, an isolated contact reading can be compared with the relevant installed-circuit observation. Such a comparison may help distinguish a reading confined to the contact path from one affected by the broader circuit. It does not, by itself, locate every source of resistance. Differences can guide further investigation, but interpretation remains limited by the measurement points, states, and conditions used.

Keep the conclusion proportionate to the evidence. A result that meets a documented contact-resistance requirement under its specified method supports only that defined measurement. It does not prove load performance, suitability for a particular circuit, or the condition of the complete equipment. Similarly, an elevated observation does not alone establish that the switch is defective. The measurement must be interpreted against the appropriate requirement and within its test boundary.

## When an elevated reading remains unresolved

If a reading remains elevated, distinguish what is known from what is still uncertain. An isolated result across documented contacts narrows the observation to that contact path under the tested state and conditions. It does not automatically separate a contact issue from a measurement-method issue or establish why the result occurred. An in-circuit result leaves the boundary wider because terminals, wiring, and other circuit components may contribute.

Do not assign the cause to a terminal or wire solely because the installed reading is high, and do not assign it to the contacts solely because the reading was taken near the switch. The connection points and isolation condition determine what the result includes. Further inspection or qualified diagnosis may be needed to distinguish among possible causes; the evidence here does not establish a repair method or a universal next test.

A click or actuator movement is not a substitute for a defined electrical measurement. It does not establish contact resistance or load performance. Likewise, one favorable isolated reading cannot prove that the full switch assembly or the surrounding equipment is good. Each conclusion must stay within the state, connection points, method, and conditions actually tested.

The practical decision boundary is therefore narrow: a properly defined contact-resistance measurement can be compared with its applicable requirement, while an installed-circuit reading describes a broader path. A high reading may justify further investigation, but neither result alone proves the complete switch or equipment good or bad. For **micro switch high resistance**, identify the measurement boundary first, then interpret the value only against documented conditions and requirements.

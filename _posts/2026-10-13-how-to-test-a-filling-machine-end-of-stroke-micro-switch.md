A machine symptom at the end of a filling stroke does not, by itself, show that the micro switch has failed. To learn how to test a filling machine end of stroke micro switch, separate the mechanical actuation check from an isolated contact check and interpret each result against the documentation for the specific switch and machine. These checks can assess whether the switch changes contact state at defined actuator positions. They cannot alone establish that the machine’s wiring, control logic, or overall operation is correct.

## What testing a filling machine end of stroke micro switch can establish

A bounded test asks whether the documented contact arrangement responds as expected when the actuator is in specified positions. It does not answer every question raised by a machine-level symptom. The stroke-end mechanism may fail to reach the actuator; the switch may not respond as documented; terminals may be misidentified; wiring or the control system may be at issue; or a measurement setup may not be adequate to interpret the result.

Keep those possibilities separate. If the mechanism does not move the actuator through the relevant event, a contact reading cannot establish that the switch has an internal fault. Conversely, a contact response that matches the switch documentation does not show that the machine circuit receives or acts on that response correctly.

A click is not a substitute for a contact check. It may indicate movement, but it does not establish continuity, correct terminal identification, or successful machine operation. Likewise, one continuity reading has meaning only when the switch is isolated, the terminals are documented, and the actuator state is defined. The useful conclusion is therefore narrow: compare an isolated switch’s observed state with its stated expected state under the specified conditions, then keep any broader machine diagnosis separate.

## Make the machine safe and identify the exact switch arrangement

Before an isolated continuity check, make the machine safe and isolate the switch according to the machine’s approved procedure. Do not use this guide as a basis for probing energized equipment. The applicable United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements.

Use the specific switch and equipment documentation to identify the contact arrangement, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load. Do not infer terminal identities from physical layout or assume that a particular device has COM, NO, or NC terminals. Continuity expectations depend on the documented arrangement and reference condition; without those details, a reading cannot be assigned a universal expected state.

Isolation must also be sufficient for the intended measurement. Follow the approved procedure for disconnecting the switch from the circuit before checking it. If the isolation method, terminal identity, or expected state is unclear, stop rather than treating an ambiguous reading as a diagnosis. Work that involves energized parts, hazardous access, or an unresolved electrical condition belongs with qualified personnel.

## Check whether the stroke-end mechanism actuates the micro switch

With the machine in a safe condition, trace the mechanical path from the stroke-end event to the switch actuator. Observe whether the relevant linkage reaches and moves the actuator during the intended event. This check comes before interpreting a contact reading because an actuator that is never moved into the relevant position cannot demonstrate how the switch responds at that position.

If the linkage misses the actuator or moves it incompletely, the observation points to a mechanical-input question. It does not prove that the switch contacts are sound or faulty. Keep the distinction precise: the mechanical check concerns whether the input reaches and moves the actuator; the isolated electrical check concerns the documented contact response at defined actuator states.

Do not adjust the mechanism based on this general test description. The approved machine procedure governs any adjustment, and adjustment is outside this diagnostic scope. If the mechanism’s intended movement or safe observation method is not clear, leave that assessment to personnel following the machine’s approved process.

## How to test a filling machine end of stroke micro switch with continuity

Only after the approved isolation procedure has been followed, and the switch has been disconnected sufficiently to isolate it, check the documented terminals with a suitable continuity method. Keep the actuator in defined positions for each observation. The positions and expected results must come from the specific switch documentation, including any stated reference condition or test conditions. Do not assume that all switches change state in the same way or that a particular terminal pair should be open or closed without that evidence.

Record the actuator position and the observed continuity result together. Compare that result with the documented expected contact state for the same terminals and conditions. If the contact arrangement, terminal markings, reference state, or measurement conditions are unavailable, the result remains inconclusive rather than proving a failure.

A click does not establish electrical continuity, and a continuity result does not establish correct wiring or operation of the filling machine. The test concerns an isolated switch under the stated conditions. It does not test the installed circuit’s control logic, confirm that the stroke-end linkage operates correctly throughout machine operation, or establish machine safety. Do not extend the result beyond the component and conditions actually checked.

## Interpret the result without misdiagnosing the machine

If the isolated switch’s response differs from its documented expectation, the switch or the test setup merits further qualified assessment. The discrepancy alone does not identify every possible cause: terminal identification, actuator position, isolation, measurement method, and test conditions all affect whether the comparison is valid. Do not conclude that replacement or adjustment is required from an unexplained reading.

If the isolated response matches the documentation but the machine symptom remains, that result does not prove the machine is functioning correctly. The remaining question may concern terminal identification, wiring, linkage behavior under operating conditions, or control logic. Resolving those issues requires the approved diagnostic process for the equipment, not a broader claim based on the isolated switch test.

The decision boundary is simple: use the isolated check only to compare documented switch behavior at defined actuator states. Leave energized circuit testing, hazardous access, and unresolved faults to qualified personnel. Neither a click nor a passing isolated test validates the complete machine circuit or establishes that the machine is safe to operate.

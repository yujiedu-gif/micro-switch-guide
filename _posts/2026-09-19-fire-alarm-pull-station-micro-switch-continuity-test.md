A fire alarm pull station micro switch continuity test can show whether the switch’s documented contacts conduct in a defined actuator state, provided the switch is identifiable and electrically isolated. It cannot establish that the pull station, its wiring, the alarm panel, or the complete fire alarm system will operate correctly. The distinction matters because a component-level reading describes only the component under the test conditions; it does not verify the surrounding life-safety system.

The method below applies only when the particular pull station uses an identifiable micro switch and safe access is permitted under authorized procedures. Terminal names, expected contact behavior, and actuator reference states must come from documentation for that specific switch. Do not infer them from the pull station’s appearance, label, or function.

## What a fire alarm pull station micro switch continuity test can establish

A micro switch is an electromechanical component whose contacts can change state in response to mechanical actuator movement. Some pull station designs use one, but the pull station’s function does not establish that a micro switch is present. Confirm the component’s identity from applicable documentation or other authorized identification methods before treating the procedure as a micro switch test.

When the switch is isolated from the circuit, a continuity reading can indicate whether identified contacts conduct in the actuator state being tested. The reading is meaningful only when the terminals, reference state, and expected behavior are known for that particular switch. It is an observation about those contacts under those conditions, not a complete functional verdict.

A result consistent with the documented contact behavior does not prove that the switch is correctly connected in the pull station, that the equipment’s wiring is correct, or that the alarm panel will respond. It also does not establish system readiness. Those questions concern other parts of the equipment and require their own authorized inspection or testing procedures.

## Identify the micro switch and its documented contact states

First establish that the pull station actually contains a micro switch and that the relevant component can be identified safely. Do not assume that a familiar-looking mechanism, a pull-station label, or a contact change elsewhere in the equipment identifies a micro switch. If identification is uncertain, the switch-level continuity result cannot be interpreted reliably.

Use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. A generic physical-layout inference is not a reliable substitute. Do not assign terminal names based on where a terminal sits, how a connector looks, or what is common in other designs.

The same caution applies to contact states. COM means common, while NO and NC describe contact relationships at a specified reference condition when that arrangement is documented. Do not assume those terminals are present, or assume which state corresponds to an unactuated pull station. The switch documentation must define the applicable terminals and reference actuator state before a reading can be compared with an expected result.

If the documentation does not identify the terminals or expected behavior for the relevant state, do not guess. A meter may display a reading, but without a defined contact arrangement and reference state, that reading does not answer whether the switch behaves as specified.

## Prepare an isolated continuity check safely

Before any measurement, follow the authorized procedures that apply to taking the relevant equipment out of service and isolating the switch electrically. Fire alarm equipment has a system-level role, so access, isolation, and testing must remain within permitted procedures. Work beyond those procedures belongs to qualified personnel. Do not treat this article as permission to access or alter a fire alarm installation.

A continuity meter is used for the switch-level check only when the component is electrically isolated. An in-circuit reading can reflect paths through other equipment and does not establish the isolated switch’s contact behavior. Keep the actuator state explicit: record which documented state is being checked, rather than referring vaguely to “normal,” “open,” or “closed” without a defined reference.

The comparison also depends on using the documented terminals and expected behavior for that state. If the switch cannot be isolated as required, if terminal identification is uncertain, or if safe access is not permitted, stop. Do not substitute an in-circuit reading or an assumed terminal map for the specified conditions.

Numerical travel limits are requirements of the applicable specification sheet; they cannot be inferred from the micro switch category. If actuator movement or travel is part of evaluating a suspected problem, consult the applicable documentation rather than assuming how far the actuator should move. A continuity reading alone does not establish that movement is within a specified limit.

## Interpret readings by actuator state, not by the pull station label

Compare the meter observation with the documented contact behavior for the identified terminals and the exact actuator state tested. A reading consistent with that behavior supports a narrow conclusion: the tested contacts conducted, or did not conduct, as indicated under the isolated test conditions. Keep the conclusion at that level.

If the observation differs from the documented expectation, it identifies a discrepancy to investigate, not a cause. Possible factors include terminal identification, actuator movement, contact condition, or the measurement setup. The reading alone does not distinguish among them. Do not declare the micro switch defective, or the pull station faulty, solely from one unexpected observation without resolving the test conditions and applicable documentation.

Likewise, one expected reading does not prove that every relevant contact state is correct. A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove electrical continuity, correct terminal identification, or suitable operation in the equipment. Neither a click nor a single continuity result verifies the switch under its installed conditions or establishes that it is suitable for the circuit.

The actuator state must stay tied to the documented reference. Do not translate an isolated result into a claim that the pull station is “normal,” “ready,” or in a particular alarm state. The relationship between the switch contacts and the equipment’s behavior depends on the actual design and circuit, neither of which can be inferred from the component-level reading alone.

## What the continuity result does not verify

An isolated continuity result does not verify pull-station wiring, the alarm panel’s response, alarm transmission, or readiness of the complete fire alarm system. It does not establish that the switch is connected to the intended circuit or that the surrounding equipment functions as intended. Those conclusions require authorized system-level inspection and testing, not extrapolation from a switch reading.

Keep the decision boundary clear: a documented, isolated check can describe contact behavior for a particular switch in a defined actuator state. It cannot certify the pull station or the system. If the switch identity, terminal documentation, expected state, or safe isolation conditions are unavailable, stop rather than infer a result. Any broader question must be handled through the applicable authorized procedures and by qualified personnel where required.

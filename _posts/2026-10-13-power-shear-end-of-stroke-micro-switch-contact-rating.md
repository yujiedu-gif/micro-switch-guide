A power shear end of stroke micro switch contact rating describes the electrical use limits documented for that switch under stated conditions. It does not, by itself, show that the switch is suitable for every circuit in a power shear or explain how the machine stops. To judge suitability, match the exact switch documentation to the actual circuit, load, and operating conditions.

The end-of-stroke function identifies a role in the equipment, not the electrical duty at the contacts. In a particular design, the contacts may directly control a load or provide a signal to another control element. The machine’s circuit documentation must establish which arrangement applies.

## What does a power shear end of stroke micro switch contact rating tell you?

A contact rating tells you the electrical use limits documented for the micro switch under specified conditions. Those conditions matter: a rating stated for one current type, load, or test arrangement does not establish the same capability under a different one. The rating is therefore evidence about a defined use, not a general statement that the switch can handle any electrical task associated with a power shear.

Keep the switch’s contact rating separate from the shear’s end-of-stroke function. The function describes when the mechanism actuates the switch in the equipment. It does not tell you whether the contacts carry power to a load, pass a control signal, or operate as part of another documented circuit arrangement. Nor does the machine’s application name establish the contact rating needed for that arrangement.

This distinction is important because the same observed machine function does not reveal the contacts’ electrical duty. A switch that changes state at the end of travel might directly interrupt a load in one design, while another design might use its contact change as an input to a separate control element. Without documentation for the particular shear, neither arrangement can be assumed.

Accordingly, a rating label alone does not settle suitability. Read it together with the applicable specification sheet and the equipment’s circuit information. If the required conditions or the switch’s role are not documented, the available evidence is incomplete; that uncertainty is not proof of either suitability or a mismatch.

## Match the contact rating to the actual circuit conditions

Start with the documentation for the exact switch and equipment. Identify the electrical conditions specified for the switch contacts and the conditions in the particular shear circuit. The comparison must preserve the stated current type, load, and test condition. A rating for AC does not establish a DC rating, and a rating for one load type or operating arrangement does not establish another.

Check whether the documentation states the applicable voltage, current, and load type, along with whether the circuit is AC or DC. Then determine what the contacts actually do in the documented arrangement. If the switch contacts directly control a load, compare the documented rating with that contact duty. If the contacts signal another control element, establish that role from the circuit documentation rather than assuming it from the end-of-stroke function. The downstream control arrangement still needs to be understood from its own documentation.

The circuit diagram, terminal markings, and equipment information should agree on the switch’s connection and function. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity or electrical duty from a generic physical layout, the switch’s location, or the fact that the shear reaches an end of stroke.

If a needed condition is absent, stop short of a suitability conclusion. For example, a stated current type without the relevant load conditions may not resolve the application; likewise, knowing that the switch actuates at stroke end does not establish whether its contacts carry the load or signal another control element. Check the applicable specification sheet and equipment circuit documentation. If those sources do not identify the relevant conditions, treat the rating’s applicability as unresolved rather than extending it by assumption.

## What end-of-stroke behavior does not tell you about the contacts

The point at which the actuator moves does not identify the contact arrangement or reference state. End-of-stroke describes an actuation event in the machine; it does not, on its own, tell you which terminal is common, whether a contact is normally open or normally closed, or what the contact state is under a specified reference condition. Establish those details from the exact switch documentation and the relevant test conditions.

Similarly, a contact change alone does not establish what the power shear does next. The resulting machine behavior depends on the documented circuit and control arrangement. The end-of-stroke switch could be involved in a stopping action, but the application label alone does not establish stopping logic, reversal, or any other behavior. Do not infer the machine’s response without its circuit documentation.

Keep three questions distinct: where the actuator is moved, what contact state is documented at a defined reference condition, and what the equipment circuit does in response. Evidence for one does not answer the other two. A position in the stroke is not a terminal designation, and a terminal designation is not a complete description of the machine’s control behavior.

This boundary also applies when a switch is examined outside the machine. A continuity result is limited to the switch’s isolation condition, tested actuator state, and measurement conditions. It may help establish a contact state for that test, but it does not establish the installed wiring, the circuit’s operation, or the suitability of the complete shear arrangement.

## Verify documentation and define the decision boundary

For a documented interpretation, compare the exact switch specification with the equipment’s circuit information. Confirm the stated current type, load, and test conditions for the rating; identify the switch’s documented terminal connections and circuit role; and compare those facts with the specified operating arrangement. Keep each conclusion within the conditions the documents actually cover.

If checking continuity is appropriate and can be done under an applicable safe procedure, record the isolation condition, actuator state, and expected contact behavior from the switch documentation. An isolated reading describes only the tested switch in that state. It does not prove that the installed circuit is wired correctly, that the machine’s control behavior is as intended, or that the contact rating matches the actual load.

A decision is supported only when the relevant rating conditions and the circuit’s contact duty are documented and can be compared. If the switch’s role, load, current type, or other required condition is unknown, the appropriate conclusion is that suitability has not been established. Missing evidence is not evidence of a mismatch, but it also cannot support a positive suitability judgment.

Do not inspect or test energized equipment unless an applicable safe procedure expressly permits it. Follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements, and leave work beyond that boundary to qualified personnel. The power shear end of stroke micro switch contact rating is meaningful only within its documented electrical conditions and the particular circuit arrangement; the end-of-stroke label alone cannot supply either one.

A fire alarm pull station micro switch contact rating describes the electrical conditions under which that switch’s contacts are specified to operate. It does not, by itself, establish that the complete pull station is approved for a particular system or that the system’s signaling circuit meets its requirements. The rating must be read with its stated voltage, current, and load conditions, then compared with the documented circuit conditions for the specific equipment.

That distinction matters because a pull station’s contact rating and the signaling input’s requirements answer different questions. A rating can be applied only when its conditions match the circuit being evaluated. If the rating or circuit details are missing or unclear, suitability remains unverified; it should not be inferred from a generic switch description or the device’s appearance.

## What the fire alarm pull station micro switch contact rating means

A contact rating is a limit specified for a contact under stated electrical and load conditions. Its meaning depends on the conditions attached to it. A voltage and current value should therefore not be detached from the documentation that defines how the contact may be used. When separate ratings are given for different load types or electrical conditions, each applies only within its stated scope.

The rating describes the switch contacts, not every property of the pull station. It does not by itself establish the station’s approval, the signaling circuit’s supervision behavior, or the system-level requirements. Those are separate matters that must be determined from documentation for the actual device and system.

Pull station construction and contact use vary by design. Do not assume that every pull station contains a micro switch, or that switches used in different stations share one contact rating. A generic micro switch rating or a familiar-looking component is not evidence of the rating for a particular installed device.

## Read voltage, current, and load conditions together

Treat a voltage-current pair as a specified condition, not as a universal permission to switch any load at those values. First identify the voltage and current conditions the documentation assigns to the contact. Then identify the load type those conditions cover. If the documentation gives distinct values for resistive and inductive loads, use the value for the applicable load rather than transferring a resistive-load rating to an inductive circuit.

AC and DC conditions also must remain distinct. A rating stated for AC does not establish an equivalent DC rating, or the reverse. Use only a value documented for the circuit’s actual AC or DC condition. Likewise, do not assume that matching voltage and current alone resolves the check if the load type or switching duty differs from the documented rating conditions.

If the documentation does not say which load type or electrical condition a value applies to, that value cannot establish a match for a circuit whose conditions are known. Do not fill gaps by treating a number as a general-purpose rating. The correct result in that case is that the comparison is incomplete until the conditions are clarified in authoritative documentation.

## Compare the rating with the documented signaling circuit

The comparison should follow the actual circuit configuration, not a generic idea of how pull stations work. Use the approved documentation for the specific pull station and the connected signaling input to identify the relevant circuit conditions. Establish what voltage and current the contact is expected to encounter and whether the documentation identifies the load as resistive, inductive, or another type. Keep AC/DC and any stated switching-duty distinctions attached to those conditions.

Compare those documented conditions with the contact rating that applies to the same load type and electrical conditions. A comparison is meaningful only when the relevant values and qualifiers are available on both sides. If the circuit documentation describes a condition not covered by the contact rating, or the rating’s conditions are not identified, the match has not been established.

Do not substitute the contact rating for the signaling input’s specifications. Nor does a contact rating specify circuit supervision current, panel input limits, or code requirements. These are separate system or installation questions. A contact may have a documented rating while the suitability of the complete station-and-circuit arrangement remains unresolved.

## Verify the fire alarm pull station micro switch contact rating safely

Begin with legible markings on the component, if they can be read without opening or modifying the equipment. Check those markings against authoritative technical records for the exact switch and equipment. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment; do not infer terminal identity or rating from a generic physical layout.

Compare the component information with the approved documentation for the pull station and its signaling circuit. Confirm that the rating’s voltage, current, AC/DC distinction, and load type correspond to the circuit conditions being evaluated. If markings and records conflict, or if a rating condition is absent or ambiguous, do not resolve the discrepancy by assumption. Treat the rating match as unverified and refer the question to qualified fire-alarm personnel.

Do not open, modify, or electrically test an in-service life-safety system on the basis of a general article. Any work must follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. This verification path is a documentation comparison, not an instruction to alter the pull station or test the operating alarm system.

## What a contact rating cannot tell you

A contact rating alone does not establish device approval, compatibility with a particular signaling input, or code compliance. It does not reveal all system-level requirements, and it cannot show that an installed circuit is correctly configured. Those conclusions require the applicable device and system documentation and, where needed, review by the responsible qualified fire-alarm professional.

Do not generalize the presence of a micro switch or its rating across all fire alarm pull stations. Designs and contact arrangements can differ. If the exact component identity, applicable rating, circuit conditions, or system requirements cannot be verified, leave the conclusion open rather than assuming the switch is suitable. Direct system-specific questions to qualified fire-alarm personnel and the authority having jurisdiction.

The fire alarm pull station micro switch contact rating can be checked against a circuit only when the applicable rating conditions and the documented signaling-circuit conditions are both known and aligned. A matching contact rating is one part of that review, not proof of approval or complete system suitability.

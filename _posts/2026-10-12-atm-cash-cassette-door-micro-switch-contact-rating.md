An **atm cash cassette door micro switch contact rating** is a limit stated for particular electrical conditions, not a universal safe operating point. To assess whether it applies, compare the switch’s documented rating with the cassette-door circuit’s voltage, current, AC or DC supply, and load type. The door’s mechanical function does not establish what electrical load the contacts switch.

A rating can be meaningful only alongside its stated conditions. The available evidence does not identify a particular ATM cassette-door switch or circuit, so it cannot establish a specific rating, contact arrangement, or compatibility. Use the exact switch and equipment documentation, and have circuit conditions checked through an authorized, safe service process.

## What the atm cash cassette door micro switch contact rating tells you

A contact rating describes an electrical limit specified under stated load and test conditions. It is not a promise that the contacts can switch any circuit whose voltage and current appear to fit a number on a label. Rating values are requirements in a specification sheet tied to the conditions given there. The applicable value therefore depends on what the switch is asked to switch and on the conditions under which the rating was established.

Read the rating information as a set of conditions, not as one free-standing figure. Check the stated voltage and current, whether the value applies to AC or DC, and the load category or test condition. If documentation gives separate values for different conditions, each value applies only within its own stated scope. Do not transfer an AC value to a DC circuit, or a value specified for one load type to a different load.

The rating is distinct from the circuit’s actual operating conditions. A marked value does not tell you, by itself, the voltage or current present in an ATM cassette-door sensing circuit. Nor does the fact that a switch is used to sense a door establish its contact rating or electrical function. Those details require documentation for the exact switch and equipment.

When consulting a diagram, identify terminals from its markings and the switch documentation, rather than inferring terminal function from physical position. A diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and documented permissible load should be considered together. A generic layout assumption is not enough to establish which rating or contacts apply.

## Why load type changes contact rating interpretation

Voltage and current are important, but a comparison based only on those two numbers can leave out the load condition attached to a rating. A resistive test load and an inductive load do not necessarily present the same switching conditions. The rating for one cannot automatically be treated as the applicable limit for the other. Use the rating that matches the actual load category and other stated conditions.

An electronic input circuit also may not behave like a simple resistive test load. Its behavior cannot be identified from the phrase “door sensing” alone. The circuit could have characteristics that are not described by a current value in isolation, and the available information does not establish which characteristics apply to a particular cassette-door circuit. Do not assume that an electronic input is equivalent to a resistive load or to an inductive load without circuit-specific evidence.

This distinction matters because the contacts operate as part of an electrical circuit, while the door mechanism supplies the mechanical input. The mechanical role of the switch does not define the electrical load, and the circuit’s response cannot be inferred solely from the door’s movement or monitored status. Keep those questions separate: documentation identifies the switch and its rating conditions; circuit information identifies what the contacts switch.

If the rating information does not specify a load category or other necessary condition, a nearby voltage or current value does not resolve the gap. Treat the missing condition as unknown. Do not conclude that the rating is adequate merely because one measured or documented current appears lower than a printed figure.

## Compare the rating with the cassette-door circuit

First identify the exact switch and equipment covered by the available documentation. Check the switch’s rating information and any connection diagram, then identify the cassette-door circuit conditions from the equipment documentation. Avoid applying specifications from a different switch or assuming that all ATM cassette doors use the same circuit. The door function alone cannot establish a common design.

Next, compare the documented circuit supply voltage and current with the switch rating that applies to the same conditions. Confirm whether the circuit is AC or DC and identify the load type the contacts switch. Keep each comparison within its stated scope: a matching voltage does not settle a difference in AC/DC, load category, or test condition. Similarly, a current figure alone does not establish that the applicable rating has been met.

The goal is not to compare a label with an assumed circuit. It is to match documented information for the exact switch to documented conditions for the exact circuit. If either side is missing a relevant detail, the comparison is incomplete. In particular, do not infer suitability from the fact that the switch operates when the cassette door moves, or from a single rating figure that lacks its associated conditions.

Use the connection diagram and terminal markings to establish the documented connection and circuit function. Do not infer terminal identity from physical layout. A diagram can help identify how the contacts relate to the circuit, but it does not substitute for rating conditions or establish suitability if the load characteristics remain unknown.

## Verify contact conditions safely before judging suitability

Use available service documentation to establish the switch identity, the applicable rating, and the circuit conditions. If measurements are needed, they should be made only through an authorized, safe service process that follows applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. This article does not provide an operational measurement procedure for an ATM cassette-door circuit.

Keep any measurement tied to the point and conditions it actually describes. A measurement at the contacts can contribute to understanding the circuit, but it does not by itself identify every rating condition or prove that the switch is suitable. The comparison still requires the relevant AC/DC distinction, load category, and documented rating conditions. Do not treat a single current reading as a complete verification.

Do not bypass or defeat a monitored door interlock as a way to test the switch or judge its rating. If the service documentation does not establish the load type or switching conditions, obtain circuit-specific technical confirmation through the authorized service process. Without that information, suitability remains unresolved; neither the door’s purpose nor an incomplete rating comparison supports a conclusion.

The **atm cash cassette door micro switch contact rating** can be assessed only by matching its documented conditions to the actual circuit conditions. If those conditions cannot be established for the exact switch and equipment, the sound conclusion is that the available evidence is insufficient, not that the rating is adequate or inadequate.

## Sterilizer door interlock micro switch wiring: the direct answer

There is no safe universal wiring diagram for a sterilizer door interlock based only on the fact that it uses a micro switch. The terminal assignments, connections, and role of the contact in the control circuit must be established from documentation for the particular switch and sterilizer. A diagram for another unit, or an assumed arrangement based on a familiar switch shape, does not establish the wiring for this one.

The switch’s contact arrangement matters because different terminals, when present, represent different contact relationships. The sterilizer circuit matters because it determines how a contact change is used by the host equipment. Neither can be reconstructed reliably from the words “door interlock” alone. In particular, those words do not identify which terminal is connected to which circuit point or what operating response follows a contact change.

Start with the sterilizer’s wiring diagram and the documentation for the installed component. Check that the documentation applies to the equipment and switch being examined. If the switch identity or the applicable wiring information is uncertain, the wiring remains unresolved; do not substitute a generic diagram or infer the connection from the application name.

## How the micro switch fits into a sterilizer door interlock

A micro switch is an electromechanical component: mechanical movement of its actuator can change an electrical contact state. In a door-interlock arrangement, door movement or a connected linkage may move that actuator. If the switch changes state, the resulting electrical effect depends on how the particular contact is connected in the sterilizer’s control circuit.

Keep the parts of this chain distinct. The door is part of the host equipment. An actuator or linkage conveys movement to the switch. The switch’s terminals provide its electrical connections. The sterilizer’s surrounding control circuit determines how a contact change is interpreted or used. Knowing one part of the chain does not establish the details of the others.

For example, observing that a door or linkage moves does not establish that it moves the actuator as intended. Observing actuator movement does not establish the switch’s terminal arrangement or electrical condition. Even a documented contact-state change does not, by itself, show what the sterilizer’s full control circuit will do. The circuit consequence must be read from the equipment-specific documentation rather than inferred from the presence of an interlock switch.

This distinction also limits what can be concluded from an apparent door-related response. A response could depend on the switch, its mechanical input, its connections, or other parts of the host circuit. Without evidence that identifies the relevant design and test conditions, do not attribute a particular alarm, release, shutdown, or other behavior to all sterilizers that use a micro switch.

## Identify terminals and contact states from the documentation

Use the terminal markings and exact component documentation to identify the switch connections. Do not use wire color, a presumed physical layout, or a generic illustration as a substitute for terminal identification. A terminal’s location or a wire’s appearance alone does not establish its electrical function in the documented circuit.

COM, NO, and NC have meaning only when those terminals are documented for the switch arrangement in question. COM is the common terminal in that documented contact arrangement. NO and NC describe contact state relative to a specified reference condition. The reference condition must be known before applying those labels to a state description; they are not an unqualified description of current flow in the installed circuit.

The labels also do not identify the sterilizer’s safe state, normal state, or response to a door position. Those are properties of the equipment’s documented circuit and operating design, not conclusions that follow from NO or NC alone. Do not treat a terminal label as a complete wiring instruction or as proof that the interlock works in a particular way.

Compare the component documentation with the sterilizer wiring diagram to trace the documented connections. If the diagram and component information do not agree, do not choose one interpretation by guesswork. The identity of the switch, the applicable reference condition, and the intended circuit connections must be resolved before a terminal assignment can be stated confidently.

## What documentation and checks can establish

The sterilizer wiring diagram and the component information are the basis for tracing how the documented switch terminals connect to the host circuit. Together, they may establish which terminal arrangement is specified and how the switch is intended to relate to the equipment’s control circuit. They do not justify transferring that arrangement to a different sterilizer or an unidentified switch.

Continuity verification is appropriate to describe only when an applicable safe procedure is documented. That procedure must specify the isolation conditions and the actuator state for the reading. It must also identify the terminals to be checked and the expected behavior for the documented switch and reference condition. Without those details, a continuity result has no established interpretation for this application, so do not supply a generic continuity recipe.

An isolated reading concerns the switch under the tested conditions. It does not establish that the installed wiring is correct, that the switch is being actuated properly in the equipment, or that the complete sterilizer interlock system functions as intended. A result can help assess the particular switch state that was measured, but it cannot stand in for the equipment’s wiring information or a documented system-level verification procedure.

Likewise, a change in mechanical movement or an audible click is not enough to establish terminal identity, electrical continuity, correct installation, or the response of the host circuit. Keep observations separate from conclusions: record what component and state were examined, then interpret the result only against the applicable documentation and procedure.

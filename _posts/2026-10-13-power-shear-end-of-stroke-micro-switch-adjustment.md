For a power shear whose documented design uses an end-of-stroke micro switch, **power shear end of stroke micro switch adjustment** means changing the switch’s position or actuation relationship only as the machine procedure permits. The switch senses movement and provides an input to the control system; its trip point is not automatically the blade’s physical endpoint or proof that the machine stops correctly. Adjustment and verification therefore have to follow the exact shear documentation and its safe service procedure.

There is no universal setting, clearance, adjustment direction, or test sequence for power shears. Before changing anything, identify the documented mechanical path, the adjustment range, the required isolation and guarding procedures, and the acceptance criteria. If the manual does not provide a procedure for the design in front of you, do not infer one from the switch’s appearance or from another machine.

## What power shear end of stroke micro switch adjustment changes

This guidance applies only to a power shear whose documentation identifies a micro switch as part of its end-of-stroke sensing arrangement. It does not apply to every shear, and it does not establish how an undocumented machine is controlled.

The adjustment concerns the relationship between machine motion and the point at which the switch actuator operates. Moving a switch or changing its mounting relationship may change when the switch is actuated. That does not, by itself, move the blade’s physical endpoint, establish the machine’s stopping distance, or show that the control system responds as intended. Those are related parts of the machine’s behavior, but they are not interchangeable measurements.

A useful distinction is between three events: the mechanism reaches the position that moves the actuator; the switch changes state; and the machine’s control system responds and the moving parts stop. The machine-specific design and control logic determine how these events relate. Do not assume that a switch trip is the same as the blade reaching its endpoint, or that the switch alone determines when motion ceases.

The switch’s pretravel runs from its free position to its operating position. Overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These terms describe actuator travel, not blade travel or stopping distance. Any permitted switch travel or mounting adjustment must come from the applicable machine and switch documentation; do not use a travel definition to invent a setting.

## Trace how the shear mechanism actuates the micro switch

Use the machine documentation to trace the mechanical chain from the moving shear mechanism to the switch actuator. Depending on the documented design, that path may include a cam, lever, or linkage. Do not assume that any one of these parts is present. Identify the parts shown for the particular shear and determine how their movement is supposed to reach the actuator.

Then distinguish the mechanical input from the electrical signal. The actuator moves as the machine mechanism reaches a particular position; the contact change supplies an input whose effect depends on the actual circuit and control logic. A generic changeover contact may use COM, NC, and NO markings, but the exact device marking or schematic controls. Those labels alone do not establish how the shear responds, which state is expected at a particular point in its cycle, or whether a contact change commands stopping.

The adjustment should be evaluated against this documented path. If the actuator is not reached as described, possible explanations include a changed mechanical relationship or a problem in the input path; the available information does not identify a cause for a particular machine. If the switch actuates but the machine response does not meet its criteria, do not assume that moving the switch again is the appropriate remedy. The discrepancy could involve the control response or another part of the documented system, and must be assessed through the prescribed service path.

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove that the contacts changed state correctly, that the wiring is correct, or that the shear stopped at the required point. Treat it as an observation, not an acceptance test.

## Before power shear end of stroke micro switch adjustment

Read the exact machine manual and applicable service information before making a change. Confirm that the documentation identifies this switch as an end-of-stroke input and specifies the adjustment method, permitted range, and endpoint or response criteria. If the instructions do not cover the installed arrangement, leave the setting unresolved and use the machine’s documented service channel rather than borrowing values or steps from another shear.

Apply the shear’s documented isolation and guarding procedure before adjustment. The required steps depend on the machine and its instructions; no generic sequence can be supplied here. Do not improvise a live adjustment. If the documentation requires qualified service personnel, or the procedure cannot be carried out within its specified safety boundary, refer the work accordingly.

Before changing the setting, establish the reference points the procedure requires. These may include the documented machine position, actuator position, or mounting reference, but only if the machine instructions specify them. Record the starting condition when the procedure calls for it. Do not substitute a guessed gap, number of turns, visual estimate, or assumed “normal” switch state for the stated reference.

Also check the documentation for the particular switch and circuit where relevant. Use only electrical ratings explicitly stated for the applicable current type, load, and test condition; a rating for one condition does not establish another. This boundary matters if the adjustment procedure calls for electrical checks. It does not provide a rating or compatibility determination for an unidentified switch or shear.

## Make only the documented micro switch adjustment

Follow the machine-specific sequence and remain within its stated adjustment range. The method may concern switch mounting, an actuator relationship, or another documented feature. Without the exact instructions, the direction and amount of movement are unknown. Do not create a clearance, turn count, position, or adjustment order from general switch terminology.

Make only the change the procedure authorizes. Keep the mechanism and switch relationship consistent with the documented reference, and secure the switch or actuator mounting as instructed. Do not bend, reposition, or alter a part unless the procedure specifically directs that action. If the setting cannot be reached within the permitted range, or a part does not match the documented arrangement, stop and follow the machine’s service instructions rather than forcing the adjustment.

After the change, use the documented checks for mounting and actuator movement. A freely moving actuator or an audible click is not enough to establish that the switch operates at the required machine position. Likewise, an observed contact change alone does not prove the machine’s control response or stopping behavior. Keep each observation tied to the state and method specified by the procedure.

If the procedure requires a contact check, use the exact switch documentation and prescribed test method. Contact resistance is a measured switch requirement whose method, connection points, and limit must come from the applicable specification and test procedure. Do not infer a pass or fail threshold, terminal connection, or installed-machine result from an isolated reading or a generic definition.

## Verify the end-of-stroke response after adjustment

Verify the machine using its specific safe test procedure and stated acceptance criteria. The criteria must address the required behavior for that shear, not merely whether the actuator moved. Follow the documented order of checks and preserve the conditions the procedure specifies. Do not substitute an informal cycle, an assumed endpoint, or a test borrowed from another machine.

Separate the evidence into stages. First, the documented mechanism must move the actuator as intended. Second, the switch must provide the state or signal required by the machine documentation. Third, the control system and moving machine parts must meet the specified endpoint and stopping criteria. A click addresses, at most, an observation about mechanism movement. A contact check addresses only the switch test conditions used. Neither alone establishes correct control or stopping behavior.

If any observed result differs from the acceptance criteria, do not keep changing the setting in an attempt to make the machine appear to pass. Stop and use the documented service path, including qualified service where required. A mismatch could arise in the mechanical path, switch, terminal identification, wiring, control logic, or elsewhere in the equipment; the information available here does not determine which. The relevant procedure and further diagnosis must distinguish those possibilities.

The adjustment is verified only to the extent that the specified safe checks meet the machine’s stated criteria. **Power shear end of stroke micro switch adjustment** is not a universal calibration recipe: the documented machine procedure governs the setting, and the machine’s endpoint and stopping response must be verified separately from switch actuation.

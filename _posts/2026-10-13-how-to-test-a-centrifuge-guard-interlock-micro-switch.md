A safe test of a micro switch used in a centrifuge guard interlock begins with the centrifuge-specific service and safety procedure, not with an improvised measurement. How to test a centrifuge guard interlock micro switch depends on whether that centrifuge actually uses a micro switch and on the documented contact arrangement and test conditions. A component-level check may show whether its contacts change state under specified actuator conditions. It cannot, by itself, establish that the guard, installed circuit, interlock logic, or overall machine safety function works.

Do not test an energized or moving centrifuge by improvised methods. The boundaries below describe what an isolated switch check can establish; they do not replace the equipment’s approved service process or authorize work beyond it.

## How to test a centrifuge guard interlock micro switch safely

First confirm from the centrifuge documentation that the guard interlock actually uses a micro switch. A guard interlock is a function of the equipment, and its design cannot be inferred from the presence of a guard. The component discussed here is a micro switch only when the documented design identifies one in that role.

Before access or measurement, follow the specific service procedure for shutdown, isolation, stored energy, access, and prevention of unexpected operation. The procedure must apply to the centrifuge and the work being performed. Do not substitute a generic switch test for instructions about making this particular machine safe.

Work only within the tasks and limits of an approved procedure. If safe isolation, stored-energy controls, access requirements, or the test method are not documented or are not understood, stop and refer the work to qualified service personnel. Do not open or test the assembly on the assumption that an inactive control or an open guard makes the machine safe.

This boundary matters because a switch-level measurement and a centrifuge safety verification answer different questions. The first concerns the isolated component under defined conditions. The second concerns the complete installed safety function and requires the centrifuge-specific procedure. Keep those activities distinct throughout the diagnosis.

## What an isolated micro switch check can establish

A continuity check is meaningful only when the switch is safely isolated from the circuit, its documented terminals are identified, and the actuator states to be checked are known. Use the switch documentation to determine which terminals and reference states apply. Do not assume terminal labels, contact arrangement, or which state should show continuity.

Under those conditions, an isolated check can establish whether the measured contact continuity changes between the specified actuator states, as judged against the documented expected behavior and test method. It does not establish a general rating, suitability for the centrifuge, or an acceptable result under conditions that the procedure does not specify. If the required terminal identification, actuator states, or expected behavior is missing, the reading has no supported pass/fail interpretation; stop rather than guess.

A click may indicate mechanical movement, but it is not an electrical test. Conversely, an isolated continuity result concerns only the switch and the defined measurement conditions. Neither observation alone proves correct installation or operation of the interlock system.

A contact-resistance requirement is also distinct from a basic continuity observation. The applicable specification and test procedure must supply the method, connection points, and limit before a measured contact-resistance value can be interpreted. Likewise, an insulation-resistance result requires specified electrically isolated points and the applicable method and conditions. Do not infer either result from a continuity check.

## How to separate switch symptoms from interlock-system faults

If switch actuation appears absent or inconsistent, consider the external guard linkage or actuator path as well as the switch. A symptom does not identify which part is responsible. Observe only as permitted by the approved procedure; do not force, adjust, or bypass the mechanism to make a test result appear conclusive.

If an isolated check differs from the documented behavior, that narrows concern to the switch or the test setup. It does not, by itself, prove that the switch has failed, identify a repair, or establish a replacement decision. Recheck whether the switch was isolated, the correct terminals were used, and the prescribed actuator states and test method were followed. If uncertainty remains, refer the finding through the authorized service process.

If the isolated switch behaves as documented but the interlock does not, the component check has not located the system fault. Possible causes include terminal identification, wiring, control logic, or another part of the equipment. These possibilities require the centrifuge’s approved diagnostic procedure; the isolated reading does not distinguish among them or show that any one is present.

Keep the evidence tied to the question it answers: an actuation observation concerns movement, an isolated continuity check concerns specified contacts in defined states, and an installed-system test concerns the equipment’s operation under its approved procedure. Do not combine these into a single pass/fail judgment based on one click or one meter reading.

## Why a switch check does not validate centrifuge safety

An isolated continuity result does not test the installed wiring, guard mechanics, control logic, or safety response. It also cannot establish that the interlock will perform its intended function in the complete centrifuge. Component verification and system verification are separate steps with different procedures and boundaries.

Installed functional verification must follow the centrifuge-specific procedure and conditions. Do not defeat the guard or attempt an improvised live test to see whether the machine responds. If the approved procedure requires work beyond the reader’s authorization or competence, that work belongs with qualified service personnel.

Return the machine to service only through the authorized process, after the complete safety function has been verified as required by the centrifuge-specific procedure. The practical limit of how to test a centrifuge guard interlock micro switch is therefore clear: an isolated, documented check can assess specified contact change at defined actuator states, but it cannot certify the interlock or the centrifuge as safe.

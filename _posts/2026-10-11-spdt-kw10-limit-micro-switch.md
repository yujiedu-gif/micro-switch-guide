The phrase “spdt kw10 limit micro switch” identifies a contact configuration, a designation, and a general limit-switch context. It does not, by itself, establish the exact item’s terminal layout, actuator, mounting, ratings, travel limits, or environmental protection. Those details require documentation applicable to the specific switch and equipment.

The key distinction is between a general description and verified specifications. SPDT describes a single-pole, double-throw contact arrangement. The KW10 designation alone does not establish which exact variant is present or what its specifications are. A label or keyword is not a substitute for the applicable connection diagram and specification sheet.

## What can be established from the phrase spdt kw10 limit micro switch?

The phrase combines three kinds of information. “SPDT” describes a contact arrangement: one pole can connect through either of two throws. “KW10” is a designation appearing in the phrase. “Limit micro switch” describes the stated general context. Together, these words do not identify a complete, verified specification for an exact item.

In particular, the phrase does not establish a manufacturer, exact variant, physical dimensions, terminal positions, actuator geometry, mounting method, operating force, or numerical travel limits. It also does not establish a permissible electrical load or environmental protection rating. No such detail should be inferred merely from the contact configuration or designation.

This distinction matters because contact function and physical construction are different questions. SPDT describes how contacts are arranged electrically, not where terminals sit on a housing or how an actuator is shaped. A limit-switch description likewise does not determine a specific application or confirm compatibility with a particular circuit or piece of equipment.

The phrase therefore supports only a bounded interpretation. It signals an SPDT contact arrangement and includes the KW10 designation, but exact identity and operating parameters remain unresolved until documentation for the item is available. Treating the phrase as a complete part specification would go beyond what it establishes.

## Which contact facts are general, and which are unverified?

For a generic changeover contact, the terminals may be labeled common (COM), normally closed (NC), and normally open (NO). “Normally” refers to the unactuated condition. In that condition, the common contact is connected to the normally closed contact, while the normally open contact is not connected to common. When the mechanism changes the contact state, the connection transfers. This describes the general contact function, not a guaranteed physical terminal layout for an item bearing the KW10 designation.

SPDT is also consistent with this general changeover arrangement: one common connection can transfer between two contacts. But the label alone cannot establish which physical terminal is COM, NC, or NO. Exact device markings or the applicable schematic control. A generic drawing, common convention, or apparent terminal position cannot substitute for identification from documentation that applies to the exact switch.

The term “snap action” has a separate, defined meaning. Under MIL-PRF-8805K, 6.5.21, snap action is contact action in which the speed of the moving contacts is relatively independent of the speed of the actuating mechanism. This definition does not identify a particular KW10 variant, establish its dimensions, or supply an electrical or travel rating. Nor does the phrase “limit micro switch” alone establish that the exact item meets any particular specification.

Accordingly, general contact terminology can explain what SPDT commonly denotes, but it cannot resolve device-specific connections. Terminal identity, contact behavior for the exact item, and how the switch is intended to be connected must be checked against applicable markings and documentation rather than inferred from the phrase.

## What does the KW10 designation leave unresolved?

The designation does not, on the evidence available here, identify a specific manufacturer or a complete model specification. It cannot establish the exact variant, actuator form, housing dimensions, mounting arrangement, terminal configuration, or contact diagram. It also does not establish operating force, pretravel, operating travel, overtravel, or other numerical travel limits. Those values must not be guessed from the component category or designation.

Electrical ratings are likewise unresolved. A rating is meaningful only with its stated current type, load, and test condition. An AC rating does not establish a DC rating, and a rating for one load or test condition does not establish suitability under another. The phrase supplies no supported voltage, current, load type, or test condition, so none should be added or assumed. Universal compatibility with an appliance, machine, or circuit cannot be concluded from the designation.

The phrase also does not establish environmental protection or suitability for a particular environment. Those questions require evidence specific to the exact item and are not answered by SPDT, KW10, or “limit micro switch.” Similarly, the general application wording does not prove that a particular switch is appropriate for any specific equipment.

These unresolved fields are not minor details that can be filled in by convention. They distinguish one physical and electrical implementation from another. Until the applicable documentation ties the designation to the exact item, the identity and specifications remain open questions.

## What documentation must be checked before interpreting an item?

Start with documentation that applies to the exact switch and the equipment in which it is considered. Check the exact item marking and model identification against the applicable specification sheet and connection diagram. Confirm that the diagram and ratings correspond to the same variant rather than relying on a similar-looking item or a generic reference.

A neutral verification checklist includes:

- **Identity:** exact model or variant and any markings needed to match the item to its documentation.
- **Contacts and terminals:** the applicable schematic and terminal markings, including which terminal is common, normally open, and normally closed if those labels apply.
- **Circuit role:** the documented circuit function and the connection diagram for the exact switch and equipment.
- **Electrical limits:** the stated rating for the relevant AC or DC current type, load, and test condition. Do not transfer a rating from one condition to another.
- **Mechanical details:** actuator geometry, mounting information, operating force, and travel limits where specified. Treat numerical travel values as specification-sheet requirements, not category-wide values.
- **Environmental information:** any documented protection or suitability details relevant to the exact item, without inferring them from the designation.

For terminal identification and circuit interpretation, use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer wiring from physical layout alone. Any work involving a circuit must also follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements; the general contact explanation here is not a device-specific wiring procedure.

This checklist helps separate what a phrase suggests from what a specification actually establishes. It does not supply missing ratings or authorize a connection. If the exact documentation is unavailable or does not match the item, the relevant details remain unverified.

## When does the available evidence remain insufficient?

Evidence remains insufficient whenever the exact switch cannot be matched to applicable documentation, or when a document leaves the question at issue unanswered. A generic SPDT diagram may explain a changeover contact but cannot identify the terminals on an undocumented item. A designation may help describe the item but cannot, by itself, establish dimensions, actuator geometry, mounting, force, travel, ratings, or environmental protection.

The same limit applies when only part of a rating is known. A stated value for one current type, load, or test condition does not establish a rating for a different condition. Similarly, contact arrangement alone does not establish compatibility with a specific piece of equipment, and a generic travel description does not yield a numerical limit for a particular switch.

The most precise conclusion is therefore narrow: the phrase denotes an SPDT contact configuration, includes the KW10 designation, and places the item in a limit-switch context. It does not resolve exact identity or specifications. **For “spdt kw10 limit micro switch,” use the applicable documentation for the exact item to establish terminals, ratings, mechanical details, and any other device-specific facts; leave them unresolved when that evidence is absent.**

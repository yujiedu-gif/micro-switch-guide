An illuminated micro switch combines a mechanically actuated switch with a light source, but the light does not necessarily report whether the contacts have changed state. The circuit arrangement determines what the illumination means. Before selecting or testing one, evaluate its switching contacts, light supply, mechanical fit, and documented limits separately.

That distinction matters in both selection and troubleshooting. An indicator may be linked to a switch state, or it may be powered independently. In either case, a lit light alone does not establish that the contacts are operating correctly. Verify the switch action and the illumination as separate functions, using the documentation for the exact component and equipment.

## What an illuminated micro switch combines

A micro switch has an actuator, a mechanism that changes contact state, and electrical contacts. An illuminated version combines that switching function with a light source. The actuator receives mechanical movement; the contacts provide the switching function in the circuit; and the light produces illumination. How these parts are electrically connected depends on the particular construction and circuit arrangement.

Do not assume that illumination uses the same terminals or supply as the contacts. Nor should you infer terminal function from physical position or appearance. A connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment are the relevant references. Generic layout assumptions cannot establish which connection serves which function.

The light may be intended to indicate a switch-related state, or it may have a separate power circuit. These are possible design arrangements, not universal properties of illuminated switches. Until the component documentation identifies the arrangement, treat the relationship between light and contacts as unknown. This keeps the indicator from being mistaken for a direct view of contact operation.

## How the contacts and light behave

Actuator movement changes the contacts according to the switch’s configuration. A snap-action mechanism is defined as contact action in which the speed of the moving contacts is relatively independent of the speed of the actuating mechanism. This describes the contact action; it does not specify a particular contact configuration, terminal arrangement, or light circuit.

In a state-indicating arrangement, the light is electrically linked to a switch-related state. Its behavior may therefore provide an indication, but the exact meaning depends on the documented circuit. In an independently powered arrangement, the light can be energized separately from the switching contacts. It can then remain lit or be off without directly confirming the contacts’ state. These possibilities must be distinguished from the actual design rather than assumed from the presence of a light.

Contact-load ratings and light-source supply requirements are separate checks. A permissible load for the switching contacts does not establish what supply the light requires, and a light supply specification does not establish the contacts’ permissible load. Compare each requirement with its intended circuit using the exact component documentation.

For the same reason, a lit indicator is not proof of successful switching. The light could be powered independently, or its circuit could behave differently from the contacts. Conversely, a dark light alone does not establish that the contacts have failed. Check contact operation and illumination independently before drawing a conclusion.

## How to evaluate an illuminated micro switch for fit

Start by confirming the contact configuration and the relevant contact load rating for the intended circuit. Check the documented circuit function and permissible load, not merely a physical resemblance to another switch. If those details are unavailable for the exact component and equipment, treat them as unresolved rather than inferring compatibility.

Next, identify the light-source supply requirements independently. Determine from the documentation how the light is powered and what conditions apply to its circuit. Do not assume it shares a supply with the contacts, or that a contact rating covers the light. The source pack does not establish specific supply values or universal wiring arrangements, so those must remain component-specific.

Then compare the mechanical details with the intended use. Check actuator travel and operating force where documented, along with mounting dimensions and terminal access. A switch that can be actuated in isolation is not necessarily a fit for a particular mounting position or mechanism. The relevant dimensions, travel, and force are not universal, so rely on exact documentation rather than appearance alone.

Consider environmental exposure as a separate selection boundary. Verify any documented protection or environmental limits against the intended conditions. Illumination itself does not establish environmental suitability or protection. If documentation does not state a relevant limit, do not assume one.

Finally, use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. These records anchor the selection checks and prevent a generic physical-layout inference from substituting for verified electrical and mechanical information.

## How to verify switching and illumination separately

Begin with the component and equipment documentation. Identify the terminals, circuit arrangement, applicable limits, and any specified test conditions before testing. If terminal functions or safe test conditions are unclear, do not fill the gaps by inference. Follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements.

For the mechanical check, operate the actuator through its intended movement and observe whether it can be actuated as documented. Compare its travel and operating force with the available specifications, if provided. This observation is about actuator behavior; by itself, it does not confirm electrical continuity or illumination.

For a continuity check, isolate power before testing. Operate the actuator and compare the measured contact behavior with the documented configuration and circuit function. The continuity result concerns the contacts. Do not treat a light that turns on or off as a substitute for this check, and do not assume an undocumented terminal arrangement.

Check illumination as a distinct function, under the specified supply and wiring conditions. Observe whether it behaves as the documented circuit arrangement predicts. If the arrangement is not identified or the supply conditions are not known, the observation cannot establish normal behavior. The available evidence does not provide universal test values or a general wiring procedure.

Record the outcomes separately: actuator operation, contact behavior, and light behavior. This separation makes it possible to recognize, for example, an apparent contact issue without attributing it automatically to the light, or a lighting issue without concluding that the switching contacts have failed.

## Common evaluation mistakes and safety limits

A frequent mistake is treating the contact rating as a rating for the light circuit. They are different requirements and must be checked against their respective circuits. Another is assuming that an illuminated switch always uses one shared supply, or that its light always indicates contact state. Neither follows from the component name.

Do not infer contact state solely from whether the light is on or off. The light may be state-linked or independently powered, and the actual arrangement must be documented. Likewise, do not infer terminal functions from a generic drawing or physical layout when the exact connection information is available or required.

Isolate power before continuity testing, and follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. For wiring, terminal identification, ratings, and limits, use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Keep mechanical, contact, and lighting observations distinct. That approach lets an illuminated micro switch be evaluated without claiming more than its documentation and separate checks establish.

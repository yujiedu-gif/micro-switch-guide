## How to distinguish a door-position switch from a door-lock interlock

The useful distinction is functional. A door-position switch concerns whether a door is in a particular position. A door-lock interlock concerns a door-locking state and an interlocking function. These descriptions identify different concepts; they do not establish how a specific oven implements either function or what an individual switch does.

Similar wording does not make the functions equivalent. A phrase that includes “door switch” could be imprecise, incomplete, or used without distinguishing position sensing from a lock-related interlock. Adding “lock” to a description does not, by itself, verify that the component is an interlock, either. The wording must be supported by applicable documentation that identifies the component and describes its intended function.

This distinction matters when interpreting a part name or record. A general label is not enough to infer the component’s operating states, electrical behavior, terminal arrangement, or role in an appliance. Nor does a shared reference to the door establish that two components can substitute for one another. Similar location or terminology is not evidence of equivalent function or interchangeability.

No particular function should be assigned to the keyword without exact, applicable evidence. The phrase does not establish whether it refers to position sensing, a lock interlock, another door-related function, or a component description that is too broad to resolve the question. Keeping those possibilities open avoids turning a general label into an unsupported identification.

## What evidence can verify the component identity?

Verification requires records that apply to the exact appliance and component, rather than a phrase alone. Relevant evidence may include the appliance’s exact documentation and an authoritative, applicable component record that identifies the part and states its scope or intended function. The records need to correspond to the specific appliance or component under consideration; a similar name, general description, or unlinked reference does not establish that connection.

The evidence should be sufficient to answer the identity question directly. It should distinguish a position-sensing function from a lock-interlock function if that distinction is relevant, and it should show that the record applies to the exact item being described. If the available material does not establish that relationship or scope, the identity remains unresolved. This is a limit on what can responsibly be concluded, not a reason to fill gaps with assumptions based on wording.

Without exact applicable evidence, the manufacturer, appliance model, terminal arrangement, ratings, certification, and compatibility remain unresolved. The same is true of the component’s specific application. None of those details can be inferred from the keyword, from the general distinction between the two functions, or from a record that does not clearly apply to the item in question.

There is also a boundary between identifying a component and working on electrical equipment.

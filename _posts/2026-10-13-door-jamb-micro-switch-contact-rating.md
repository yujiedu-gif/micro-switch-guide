A door jamb micro switch contact rating describes the electrical conditions under which the documented switch contact is rated; it is not a universal capacity for every door-jamb circuit. To interpret the rating, match its voltage, current, AC or DC designation, load type, and stated test or operating conditions to the intended circuit. The door-jamb location alone does not establish the switch’s rating, contact arrangement, or the host system’s response.

## What a door jamb micro switch contact rating tells you

A contact rating is a condition-bound specification for a particular switch. Its meaning depends on the electrical and test conditions attached to the stated value. A rating is therefore not a general promise that the switch can control any circuit with a similar-looking voltage or current, or that it will be suitable wherever a door-jamb mechanism is used.

The distinction matters because the micro switch is only one component in an assembly. A door or jamb may move an actuator, and that movement may change the contacts. The rating describes the switch contact within its documented scope; it does not, by itself, describe every electrical demand or operating behavior of the surrounding equipment.

A door-jamb application does not establish a universal rating or contact arrangement. The physical location cannot tell you which voltage or current applies, whether the circuit is AC or DC, what type of load is connected, or which contact state the installation uses. Those details must come from documentation for the switch and equipment, not from the application name or appearance.

The distinction between a specified rating and a general capacity also limits what can be concluded from an isolated value. A number without its associated conditions may be incomplete for a suitability decision. It cannot safely be treated as an unrestricted maximum or transferred to another electrical situation merely because the switch is small or mechanically similar.

MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets describe electrical rating values as requirements tied to stated load and test conditions. The practical reading is to keep each value attached to the specification and conditions that define it. Without documentation for the particular switch, no exact contact rating can be supplied here.

## Read the conditions attached to the contact rating

Start with the documentation’s stated voltage and current. Confirm that both correspond to the intended circuit, rather than comparing only one value. A voltage entry does not establish an allowable current, and a current entry does not establish an allowable voltage. The applicable values and their relationship must be documented for the switch.

Next, check whether the rating is designated for AC or DC. Do not assume that a value stated for one current type also applies to the other. The distinction must remain attached to the rating: evidence for AC does not establish an equivalent DC rating, and evidence for DC does not establish an equivalent AC rating.

Then identify the load type named in the documentation. A rating tied to one load type should not be carried over to a different load type without supporting evidence. A circuit’s voltage and current alone are not enough to show that its load matches the rating’s stated scope.

Finally, read the stated test or operating conditions. These conditions are part of the specification, not optional background. If a value is established under a particular test condition, do not present it as a general operating guarantee outside that condition. Keep qualifiers close to the value when recording or comparing ratings so that they are not lost in a summary.

A useful interpretation record includes the documented value, voltage, current, AC or DC designation, load type, and relevant test or operating conditions. This is a way to keep the evidence together, not a substitute for the source documentation. If a label or listing leaves any needed item unstated, mark it as unresolved rather than supplying an assumed value.

Likewise, do not transfer a rating between different current types, load types, or conditions just because the equipment appears similar. A condition mismatch means the available rating does not establish suitability for that different case. It does not establish that the switch will fail; it means the evidence provided is insufficient to make the stated rating apply.

## Separate the micro switch rating from the door-jamb circuit

The contact rating belongs to the switch component. The complete door-jamb circuit has its own requirements, and the effect of a contact change depends on the actual wiring and control logic. A switch rating alone does not show what the surrounding system will do when a door moves or a contact changes state.

For that reason, do not infer a normally open or normally closed arrangement from the door-jamb location, the external mechanism, or the switch’s appearance. Nor does the application establish whether a contact change turns something on, turns something off, signals another control, or has a different effect. That behavior depends on the documented circuit and system design, neither of which can be derived from the component rating alone.

Keep two questions separate: what conditions the switch contact is rated for, and whether those conditions cover the intended circuit as installed. The first is answered by applicable switch documentation. The second also requires the actual circuit and equipment requirements. A component value, even when clearly stated, does not by itself prove that the complete installation is suitable.

This boundary also prevents an application label from becoming a substitute for electrical evidence. “Door jamb” identifies a context, not a rating, contact state, or load. Different assemblies may use different circuits or control logic, so a conclusion about one arrangement should not be generalized to door-jamb systems as a class.

The same care applies when comparing a listed rating with an installation description. Match the evidence to the specific switch and the documented circuit conditions. If the circuit’s voltage, current, current type, load type, or operating conditions are unknown, the rating cannot be confirmed as applicable just from the door-jamb function.

## What to verify before relying on a stated rating

First, identify documentation that applies to the specific switch. Confirm that the rating is not merely a generic description and that the document’s scope matches the component under consideration. The rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, as described in MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets. A generic label that omits the relevant conditions is not enough to fill them in.

Next, compare the documented voltage, current, AC or DC designation, load type, and test or operating conditions with the intended circuit. Every applicable condition must be supported. If a required item is missing, conflicting, or outside the documented scope, leave the rating’s application unresolved rather than assuming equivalence.

Also verify the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity from physical layout or assume that a particular contact arrangement follows from the door-jamb application. These checks concern evidence and identification; they do not establish a wiring procedure or tell you how the host system responds.

A mechanical click is not proof that the electrical rating applies. It may indicate movement, but it does not establish the contact arrangement, the connected load, the circuit conditions, or suitability for the installation. Likewise, a stated component rating does not prove the complete assembly meets its requirements.

If the documentation does not establish the relevant conditions, the sound conclusion is limited: the available evidence does not show whether the rating applies. Do not turn that uncertainty into a compatibility claim or a diagnosis of the host system. For electrical decisions beyond what the documented conditions establish, seek qualified review and follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements.

In short, a door jamb micro switch contact rating applies only within its documented voltage, current, AC or DC designation, load type, and test or operating conditions. Verify those conditions against the specific switch and the actual circuit. If the evidence does not cover the intended installation, leave suitability undetermined rather than inferring it from the door-jamb location or switch appearance.

## How is a keyboard different from an individual switch?

In general terminology, a keyboard is an input device. An individual switch is a component that may be used in an input mechanism. The terms describe different levels: one can refer to the overall device, while the other refers to a component. This distinction helps interpret descriptions, but it does not identify what “nimbleback keyboard” means.

The word “keyboard” in a phrase is not proof that the phrase names a complete input device. Likewise, a mention of a switch in text about a keyboard does not prove that the phrase itself names a switch. Read the full sentence and surrounding material, and look for an explicit connection between the identifier and the object being described.

When a source does describe a switch, avoid inferring its behavior or specifications from general terminology. For example, a wiring diagram and terminal identification cannot be established from physical layout alone. The connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment are the relevant basis for interpreting those details. This general boundary does not establish that the phrase refers to any switch.

Technical terms also require care. In the scope of MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.5, the specification distinguishes actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. Those terms have a defined scope in that document. Their presence in a discussion does not identify the phrase or supply specifications for an unidentified item.

Keep the distinction at the level the evidence supports. It is reasonable to explain generally that a keyboard is an input device and a switch may be one of its components. It is not reasonable to turn that general distinction into a claim that this particular phrase names either one.

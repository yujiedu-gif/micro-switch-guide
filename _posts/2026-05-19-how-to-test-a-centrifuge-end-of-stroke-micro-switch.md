A centrifuge end-of-stroke micro switch can be assessed by separating two questions: does the mechanism move the actuator, and does the isolated switch change contact state under defined conditions? **How to test a centrifuge end of stroke micro switch** depends on the centrifuge’s documented isolation procedure, the component’s identified terminals, and its specified actuator states. An isolated continuity check is not a test of the installed control circuit or proof that the centrifuge operates correctly.

Use the centrifuge’s model-specific instructions to establish safe isolation and permitted access before inspecting or measuring anything. Do not treat a stopped machine as proof that it is de-energized. If isolation or access cannot be confirmed, stop and refer the work to qualified personnel. This procedure does not cover testing exposed energized circuitry, bypassing an interlock, or repairing the centrifuge.

## how to test a centrifuge end of stroke micro switch

Begin with the equipment procedure, not with a meter reading. Follow the centrifuge’s documented instructions for isolation and access, including any conditions the procedure specifies. The information here does not establish a particular centrifuge’s isolation steps, access points, or hazards, so do not substitute general assumptions for that documentation.

Do not infer that a machine is de-energized because it has stopped or appears idle. If you cannot confirm safe isolation or permitted access, do not proceed with inspection or testing; refer the work to qualified personnel. Keep energized circuit diagnosis outside this reader-level procedure. It belongs only within a qualified service procedure that explicitly requires it.

The diagnostic boundary is narrow: inspect the accessible mechanical path and, if the equipment procedure permits, check the electrically isolated switch for a documented contact-state change. Neither step establishes the safety or operation of the centrifuge as a whole. Do not bypass the switch or use an isolated result to justify running the equipment.

## Identify the micro switch, actuator, and documented contact states

First confirm from the centrifuge documentation that the end-of-stroke component is a micro switch. A miniature snap-action switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement and can be used for position or limit detection. That general description does not establish that every centrifuge uses this component or identify the arrangement in a particular machine.

Use the applicable documentation to identify the switch terminals and the actuator positions relevant to the test. Establish the expected contact state at each specified reference position before connecting a meter. Do not assume a terminal is COM, NO, or NC, or assume a normal state, unless the documentation identifies it. Labels and contact arrangements are not established here.

Keep the external mechanism distinct from the switch. A linkage or other mechanism may move the actuator, but its specific design and expected movement are not established. The actuator is the part moved by that mechanism; the electrical contact test concerns the switch’s documented terminals and states. Without those references, a meter reading cannot be interpreted reliably as the expected behavior.

## Check whether the end-of-stroke mechanism reaches the actuator

With the equipment safely isolated, inspect the accessible mechanism and actuator path only as the centrifuge procedure permits. Observe whether the mechanism contacts and moves the actuator at the intended point, using the equipment documentation to define that point. Do not assume a particular linkage, travel distance, or actuator direction.

Record what is observable: whether the mechanism reaches the actuator, whether the actuator moves, and whether movement appears obstructed or incomplete. These are mechanical-path observations. If the actuator does not move as expected, that alone does not show that the switch’s contacts are defective. The mechanism may not be delivering movement to the actuator, or the observation may need to be checked against the documented reference position.

Pretravel and overtravel describe different portions of actuator travel in the cited standard: pretravel runs from free position to operating position, while overtravel runs from operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. Those definitions do not supply a travel value or establish how far a particular centrifuge’s mechanism should move. Use the equipment or component documentation for the relevant positions; do not force the actuator or infer a permitted movement range from these terms alone.

## Test the isolated micro switch for a contact-state change

Perform a continuity check only if the equipment procedure permits it and the switch is electrically isolated as required by that procedure. Use the documented terminal pair and compare the reading at the specified unactuated and actuated positions with the documented expected behavior. Record the tested pair and the actuator state for each reading. If the terminal functions, isolation condition, reference positions, or expected states are unknown, the result cannot answer whether the switch changed state as intended.

Interpret the measurement only as an observation of the isolated switch under the tested conditions. A change that matches the documented behavior supports that the measured contact state changed between those positions. It does not establish that the installed wiring, control circuit, or centrifuge operates correctly. A click can indicate movement of a snap-action mechanism, but by itself it does not prove continuity or correct operation.

If the switch cannot be isolated as required, do not treat an in-circuit reading as equivalent to this check. Stop rather than improvise a test on energized or exposed circuitry. The scope here does not provide a procedure for energized diagnosis.

## Interpret observations and decide what remains unverified

If there is no measured state change, first confirm that the documented terminal pair and reference states were used and that the test setup followed the equipment procedure. Then distinguish whether the mechanism moved the actuator as specified. Possible boundaries still include incomplete actuation, an issue at the switch contacts, terminal identification, or test setup. A no-change result alone does not identify which one is responsible.

If the change is inconsistent with the documented behavior, preserve the tested conditions and observations rather than assigning a cause immediately. Recheck the documented terminals and actuator positions within the permitted procedure. An unexpected or inconsistent reading does not, by itself, prove a switch failure; the mechanical path, connections, measurement setup, and equipment circuit remain separate diagnostic questions.

If the isolated switch changes state as documented, the conclusion is limited to that observed behavior under the tested conditions. It does not prove that the mechanism reaches the actuator during operation, that wiring and control logic are correct, or that the centrifuge’s end-of-stroke function works. A click or an isolated continuity result alone cannot establish correct centrifuge operation.

Do not bypass the switch or infer a repair, replacement, or compatibility decision from this check. **How to test a centrifuge end of stroke micro switch** is answered only within the stated boundary: confirm the documented reference states, distinguish mechanical actuation from isolated contact behavior, and leave the installed circuit and overall centrifuge operation unverified unless assessed through an appropriate qualified procedure.

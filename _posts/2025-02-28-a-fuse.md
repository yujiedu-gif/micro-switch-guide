A fuse and a micro switch can both affect whether current flows, but they serve different roles in a circuit. A fuse is associated with circuit protection, while a micro switch is a control or switching component. Understanding that distinction helps explain why one should not be treated as a substitute for the other. ## What does a fuse do?

A fuse has a protective role in a circuit. In plain language, it is intended to interrupt current as part of circuit protection. That role differs from using a component to control a circuit during normal operation. ## How is a fuse different from a micro switch?

The main difference is the intended role. A fuse is associated with circuit protection. A micro switch is a control or switching component: it changes a circuit’s state as part of a control function. Both may affect current flow, but that overlap does not mean they perform the same job.

Within the stated scope of snap-action sensitive switches and switch assemblies, a basic sensitive switch is a self-contained snap-action microgap switch operated directly by a defined force through a defined travel. This definition describes how the switch is operated. It does not establish a specific application, rating, connection arrangement, or suitability for a particular piece of equipment.

A micro switch therefore should not be described as a fuse merely because it can switch a circuit, and a fuse should not be described as a control switch merely because it can interrupt current in its protective role. The distinction is functional, not a claim that either component can never affect the same circuit. A circuit may include both, with each serving a different intended purpose.

In short, a fuse is associated with protection, while a micro switch provides a switching or control function. Their shared ability to affect current flow is not evidence of interchangeability.

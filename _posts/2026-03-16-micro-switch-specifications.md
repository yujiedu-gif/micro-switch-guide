# Reading Micro Switch Specifications: What the Evidence Establishes

Micro switch specifications are useful only when their scope and test conditions are clear. A specification sheet can state requirements for a particular switch configuration, but values should not be treated as universal limits or assumed to apply to another configuration. The accepted evidence establishes distinctions among electrical ratings, operating characteristics, endurance conditions, and enclosure protection. It does not supply a specific switch’s ratings, release-force value, life count, or IP Code digits. Those details remain unresolved unless documented for the exact item and applicable conditions.

## What do micro switch specifications establish about these terms?

A specification sheet records requirements for the configuration and conditions it covers. Under MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, electrical rating values are requirements tied to stated load and test conditions. They are not universal switching capacities. Applicability matters: a value without the relevant configuration, load, or test context cannot establish a general capacity for every switch described as a micro switch.

The evidence also identifies operating-characteristic terminology as a distinct subject from electrical ratings. MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.5 distinguishes actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. This establishes that these terms name separate characteristics; it does not give their numerical values or define a particular switch’s measured performance.

Electrical endurance is likewise conditional. An electrical operation count must be read with its stated load, operating sequence, and test conditions. A bare count does not establish expected service life under other conditions. The same qualification applies to durability results from qualification or conformance tests.

The accepted evidence also establishes the general scope of an IP Code: IEC 60529:1989+A1:1999+A2:2013 classifies degrees of protection provided by enclosures. It does not, by itself, identify a switch’s code or establish what a specific code digit means. Accordingly, the evidence supports careful reading of documented terms, not assumptions about an undocumented switch.

## Which force and travel details are actually defined?

The accepted terminology distinguishes releasing force from actuating force, as well as from free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. This distinction is useful when reading a specification because a value attached to one term should not be substituted for another. In particular, release force is a named operating characteristic, not an electrical rating or an endurance count.

However, the evidence does not provide a release-force definition beyond identifying the term, nor does it state a release-force value, unit, measurement method, direction of movement, tolerance, or test conditions. It therefore cannot establish how much force is required for release on a particular switch or whether any given value is suitable for an application. Those specifics must remain unresolved until supported by the applicable specification sheet or other documentation for the exact configuration.

The same boundary applies to the other force and travel terms. Their names are distinguished in MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.5, but the accepted claim does not give their individual definitions, values, or relationships. Do not infer a travel distance from a force value, or infer a force value from a position term. A specification may document such details, but the evidence supplied here does not establish them.

## What electrical-rating conclusions remain unresolved?

Electrical ratings must be read with the stated voltage, current, AC or DC designation, load type, and applicable test conditions. These fields are related context, not interchangeable limits. A voltage value alone does not establish an allowable current, and a current value alone does not establish an allowable voltage. AC and DC must not be treated as equivalent, and load type must not be discarded when interpreting a rating.

The evidence establishes that ratings under MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets are tied to stated load and test conditions. It does not supply any voltage or current values, identify an AC or DC rating, name a load type, or establish an allowable combination of these parameters for a particular switch. No specific switching capacity or compatibility conclusion can therefore be drawn.

A useful reading practice is to record the rating exactly as documented, including its AC/DC designation, load type, and test conditions. If one of these fields is absent, do not fill it in by assumption or transfer a value from a different configuration. This article does not determine whether any rating is appropriate for a particular appliance or machine.

## What conditions define the mechanical-endurance claim?

The accepted evidence requires a distinction between mechanical and electrical life, but it does not provide a numerical mechanical-life claim or define a universal mechanical endurance value. A durability result from MIL-PRF-8805K qualification and conformance tests applies only with its specified electrical load, operating sequence, cycle count, and environmental or test conditions. Changing or omitting those conditions prevents treating the result as a general guarantee.

Electrical life has its own stated boundary: a published electrical operation count must be read with the load, operating sequence, and test conditions accompanying it. This is not interchangeable with mechanical life. An operation count obtained under electrical loading cannot, without supporting evidence, be recast as a mechanical-life figure; nor does a mechanical endurance result establish electrical endurance under an unspecified load.

The accepted claims do not provide the number of cycles for any switch, the test load, the operating sequence, or the environment used in a particular result. They also do not establish field service life. When a cycle count is reported, preserve the accompanying conditions and identify whether the result is mechanical or electrical. Without those details, the endurance claim remains unresolved.

## What does the IP Code claim establish?

IEC 60529:1989+A1:1999+A2:2013 establishes the scope of the IP Code as a classification of degrees of protection provided by enclosures. The accepted evidence does not provide an IP Code value for a particular switch, state a second-digit value, or define what any second digit signifies. No specific protection against an environmental condition can be inferred from this claim alone.

The distinction between the standard’s general scope and a product’s documented code is important. Knowing that an enclosure-protection classification exists does not identify the classification assigned to an item, establish which enclosure is covered, or show that a reported code applies to a specific switch configuration. Those details require documentation that connects the code to the exact item and scope.

For the question of the second digit, the evidence supplied here is insufficient to state its meaning or interpret a value. Do not infer that digit from the first digit, from a general description of the IP Code, or from an unverified product listing. The code’s application to a particular environment is also outside what these claims establish.

## Which details should remain unresolved until documented?

Before relying on a specification, verify the exact switch configuration and the applicability of the document. Then check whether each claimed value includes its defining context. A neutral review checklist is:

- **Document scope:** the applicable specification sheet and the configuration it covers.
- **Electrical rating:** voltage and current as stated, including AC or DC, load type, and associated test conditions.
- **Operating characteristics:** the documented terms and any available values for actuating force, releasing force, positions, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel.
- **Endurance:** whether the claim is mechanical or electrical, plus its load where applicable, operating sequence, cycle count, and environmental or test conditions.
- **Enclosure classification:** the documented IP Code, the item or enclosure it covers, and the relevant standard scope.

The accepted claims do not settle a particular release-force value, rating combination, mechanical-life count, or second-digit meaning. Until the applicable documentation supplies those details, keep them unresolved rather than treating general terminology or a headline number as a product-specific conclusion. The central value of micro switch specifications is the context they preserve: configuration, defined characteristic, and stated test conditions.

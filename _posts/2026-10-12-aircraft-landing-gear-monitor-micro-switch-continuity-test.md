## What the aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch continuity test can establish

The aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch continuity test checks whether the switch’s designated contacts change electrical state as its actuator moves. It is a component-level contact check, not proof that the landing gear monitor circuit reports the correct status or that the landing gear is in a particular position.

The installed contact arrangement and maintenance requirements must be established from the applicable aircraft information. A generic changeover contact may use common, normally closed, and normally open labels, but the exact device marking or schematic controls. Installations can differ, so do not assign terminal functions from appearance or a generic convention alone.

A useful result is limited to the contact pair tested, the actuator position observed, and the procedure’s acceptance criteria. The test does not establish correct mechanical adjustment, the condition of connected wiring, or operation of the full indication circuit. Keep those conclusions separate when interpreting or documenting the result.

## Prepare the micro switch for an isolated continuity check

Aircraft maintenance instructions and applicable safety procedures take precedence over a general description. Follow them for de-energization, access, terminal handling, and any restrictions on moving the actuator. Perform a continuity check only after the circuit is de-energized and the switch is electrically isolated as specified by the applicable instructions.

Isolation matters because connected aircraft wiring can provide parallel paths. A meter may then indicate continuity through another component or branch rather than through the switch contact being evaluated. The resulting reading may not represent the switch by itself. Disconnect or isolate terminals only as directed; the required isolation method is installation- and procedure-specific.

Before measuring, identify the relevant terminal pair from the installed switch diagram or other applicable documentation. If the device uses a common, normally open, and normally closed arrangement, confirm those labels against its exact marking or schematic. Do not assume that terminal position, appearance, or a generic label convention identifies the correct pair. If terminal identity or the required isolation is unclear, the available information is insufficient for a valid test setup.

Use the meter function and connections specified by the applicable procedure. This description does not establish a universal meter setting, resistance limit, or acceptance threshold. The validity of the observation depends on the prescribed setup, correct terminal identification, isolation, and actuator position.

## Aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch continuity test procedure

With the switch isolated as directed and the correct contact pair identified, observe the contact state at the actuator’s resting position. Record whether the tested pair is open or closed according to the meter indication and the governing procedure. Treat this as a baseline for that actuator position, not as a test of the landing gear indication circuit.

Next, move the actuator only through the permitted motion. Observe whether the designated contact changes state as the actuator passes through its specified operating point. Compare the resting and actuated observations for the relevant contact pair. If the documentation calls for testing another pair, follow its terminal references and procedure rather than extrapolating from the first result.

The specified operating point is important: a contact-state observation is meaningful in relation to the actuator position at which the switch is expected to operate. Do not infer an acceptable mechanical setting or adjustment from a change in continuity alone. The applicable maintenance information defines the required position and criteria; no universal operating point or travel value is established here.

Keep the observation tied to the exact conditions tested: which terminals were measured, whether the switch was isolated, the actuator position, and the observed state. A static open or closed reading at one position does not show that the contact changes state during operation. Likewise, an observed transition at the switch does not verify that connected wiring or the aircraft monitor responds correctly.

## Interpret open, closed, and inconsistent readings

A stable open or stable closed reading describes only the measured contact pair in the tested setup and position. If the state does not change during permitted actuator movement, that observation may point to an issue with the contact, actuator, setup, or terminal identification. It is not a diagnosis by itself. Check the test conditions and compare the result with the applicable instructions and acceptance criteria before drawing conclusions.

Intermittent readings also require cautious interpretation. They may reflect contact behavior, poor test connections, or movement outside the specified actuation range. The observation alone does not identify which explanation applies. Confirm that the meter connections and terminal references are correct, and that the actuator was moved only as permitted and evaluated at the required point.

In-circuit measurements can be misleading if connected wiring creates alternate continuity paths. If isolation has not been performed as specified, do not treat the indication as a definitive reading of the switch contact. Separate component-level contact findings from possible wiring or monitor-circuit findings, and use the applicable maintenance procedure to determine the next required verification.

Do not apply an invented resistance threshold or assume that every installation has the same expected readings. Use only the acceptance criteria governing the specific aircraft and test. A reading that appears stable or changes state is not, on its own, evidence that the switch is correctly adjusted, that the landing gear is in the indicated position, or that the monitor system operates as intended.

## Verify limits, restore connections, and document findings

A continuity change establishes only that the tested contact pair changed electrical state under the observed conditions. It does not confirm correct mechanical position or adjustment, prove the landing gear status, or certify the monitor circuit. Those conclusions require the applicable aircraft procedures and any separate prescribed checks; this contact test does not replace them.

After the measurement, restore connections and complete any prescribed inspections or functional checks under approved procedures. Do not extend the work into an operational or flight test based on this continuity check. Follow the governing instructions for what must be inspected, restored, and verified before the maintenance task is considered complete.

Document the test setup, the terminal pair, the actuator positions or states observed, and any variance from the governing acceptance criteria, as required by the applicable procedure. Keep the record specific to what was actually measured. The aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch continuity test can support a limited contact-state finding, but the approved procedures determine whether that finding meets the maintenance requirement and what further verification is necessary.

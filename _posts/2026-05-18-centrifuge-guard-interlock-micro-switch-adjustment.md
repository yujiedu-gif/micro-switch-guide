Adjustment of a micro switch in a centrifuge guard interlock is a design-specific alignment and verification task, not a universal setting procedure. The phrase centrifuge guard interlock micro switch adjustment describes work that applies only when the particular centrifuge design documents that component and its adjustment. Some designs may use a different arrangement, so the equipment’s service information must establish what is present and how it is intended to function.

The guard, any linkage or target, the actuator, the switch mechanism, and the interlock circuit are related but distinct parts of the system. Guard movement may move an actuator and change the switch mechanism state, but that movement alone does not confirm the electrical state or the centrifuge’s safety response. Use the procedure for the exact design, and leave work affecting hazardous operation to competent personnel under the applicable deenergization and qualification requirements.

## What centrifuge guard interlock micro switch adjustment means

The adjustment task is to set or align a documented micro switch within a specific centrifuge guard-interlock design, using the equipment’s applicable procedure. It is not a general instruction to move a switch until the guard closes, a click is heard, or a test result changes. The appropriate adjustment point, if one is provided, and the intended result must come from the design documentation.

A micro switch is a component, not the whole interlock system. Depending on the centrifuge design, a guard or associated mechanism may operate a micro switch, but some designs may not use one. The switch’s mechanical response and its contacts are only parts of a larger arrangement that includes installation, wiring, and the equipment’s control logic. Do not assume a particular contact arrangement, normal state, or safety response from the presence of a guard or switch.

Before considering adjustment, establish from the relevant service information that the equipment uses this type of switch and identify the specified procedure. If the component, adjustment point, or expected state cannot be confirmed, those details remain unresolved. A generic physical layout or an assumed switch arrangement is not a substitute for exact equipment documentation.

## How guard movement reaches the micro switch actuator

In a documented arrangement, guard movement may be transmitted through a linkage or target to the switch actuator. The actuator is the part moved by the external mechanism. Its motion operates the switch mechanism, but the actual relationship depends on the guard geometry and switch installation in that design. Do not assume the guard contacts the actuator directly or that all centrifuges use the same mechanical chain.

Operating and release are separate events. Under the definitions in MIL-PRF-8805K sections 6.5.14 and 6.5.16, pretravel runs from the free position to the operating position, while overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. Overtravel is the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. These definitions explain terms; they do not provide a setting, travel limit, or acceptance criterion for a particular centrifuge.

The guard’s position, the actuator’s motion, the switch’s internal state, and the interlock circuit’s response should not be treated as interchangeable observations. A guard that appears closed does not by itself confirm that the actuator reached the intended position. Actuator movement does not establish which contacts changed state, and a contact change does not establish what the centrifuge’s control logic does with that input. The equipment-specific design information must define the expected relationship.

## Why there is no universal adjustment setting

Any adjustment depends on the documented switch mounting, actuator, guard geometry, and specified operating conditions. The available claims do not establish a universal clearance, travel, force, contact state, or adjustment amount. Do not supply or infer such values from the general terms pretravel and overtravel, or transfer a setting from a different centrifuge or switch arrangement.

Use the applicable centrifuge service procedure to identify whether adjustment is permitted, where it is made, and what conditions and results the procedure requires. If the documentation does not identify an adjustment point or expected result, treat that information as unknown rather than improvising. The equipment’s design may make the relevant setting dependent on features that cannot be established from a general description of a micro switch.

Keep mechanical alignment separate from electrical ratings and terminal identification. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment rather than inferring terminal identity from physical layout. The applicable load and circuit details must be established for that design; no rating or terminal state can be assumed here. Adjustment guidance does not establish that a switch is compatible with a circuit or that a changed position will produce the intended interlock behavior.

## How adjustment should be verified for the documented design

Verification should follow the centrifuge-specific service procedure, including its isolation requirements and stated checks. Do not improvise an adjustment sequence or testing condition. Where the interlock affects hazardous operation, the work requires the applicable qualification and deenergization requirements and should be handled by competent personnel. If those requirements or the correct procedure are not available, stop rather than treating a general switch explanation as permission to proceed.

Separate a switch check from verification of the installed interlock. An isolated switch-state check concerns the switch under a defined actuator condition; it does not verify installed wiring, the guard mechanism, or the centrifuge’s complete safety function. A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove continuity, correct terminal identification, correct wiring, or the interlock’s response. Likewise, a continuity result alone does not establish correct installed function or safe centrifuge operation.

Follow the design’s documented method to verify the mechanical relationship and the installed circuit function. The expected actuator position, contact state, and system response must come from that procedure, not from assumed conventions. No single observation described here proves that the full centrifuge assembly is safe to operate. If a required check fails, gives an unexpected result, or cannot be interpreted against the design documentation, refer the issue through the equipment’s service process rather than making further unsupported adjustments.

## When not to treat adjustment as the remedy

A symptom associated with a guard interlock may have several possible causes. The mechanical input or linkage may not move as intended; the switch contacts may not behave as expected; terminal identification or wiring may be incorrect; or another part of the centrifuge may be involved. These possibilities are distinct. A symptom alone does not identify which one is responsible, and moving the switch does not establish that the cause has been corrected.

Use the applicable service documentation and its verification process to distinguish the relevant failure path. If the expected state, wiring, or cause is unknown, do not infer it from guard position, actuator movement, a click, or an isolated continuity reading. Do not bypass, defeat, or bridge the guard interlock as a substitute for diagnosis. Stop and use the design-specific service process, with qualified personnel for work involving hazardous operation.

In practice, centrifuge guard interlock micro switch adjustment is appropriate only when the equipment documentation identifies the switch and specifies how alignment is to be set and verified. Mechanical movement, switch state, and system safety are separate questions; evidence for one does not settle the others. When the design-specific procedure or expected behavior is unavailable, leave the adjustment unresolved and seek the documented service path rather than applying a universal setting.

Bottling machine guard interlock micro switch adjustment sets the relationship between a guard, its actuator, and the switch’s operating point. It applies only to a machine design that uses the stated micro switch and provides an adjustment permitted by its service instructions. There is no universal mounting arrangement or adjustment procedure. Some switches or mounts have no intended field adjustment.

The objective is reliable detection of the guard’s required open and closed states, not merely making the switch click. Use the machine-specific service documentation to identify the permitted adjustment, limits, test method, and acceptance checks. If these requirements are unavailable or the design does not authorize adjustment, do not improvise.

## What bottling machine guard interlock micro switch adjustment changes

Adjustment changes where the actuator engages the switch in relation to the guard’s required position. The switch may be repositioned, or an intended actuator adjustment may change engagement, but only if the machine design and service procedure permit that change. The exact arrangement varies. Identify the installed switch, actuator, mounting points, and guard reference position rather than assuming a standard layout.

Keep switch-to-actuator alignment separate from guard or hinge geometry. Moving a switch or its permitted actuator setting changes when engagement occurs relative to the guard. Moving or correcting a sagging guard or hinge changes the guard’s position relative to the switch assembly. These are different sources of a trip-point mismatch. Do not use switch adjustment to conceal a guard or hinge problem, and do not alter guard geometry as a substitute for the specified switch adjustment.

Before changing anything, establish the intended guard position and the machine-specific relationship at which switching should occur. A visible gap or apparent contact alone does not establish correct operation. The service documentation must determine what counts as the required position and which parts, if any, may be adjusted. If there is no authorized adjustment, stop rather than bending, shimming, or modifying components.

## Prepare the machine and identify the adjustment reference

Take the machine out of service and isolate hazardous energy before accessing the guard interlock. Follow the machine-specific procedure for preparing and accessing it. Do not begin adjustment while the machine can operate or while access exposes personnel to hazardous motion.

Consult the service documentation to identify the installed switch, its actuator, the intended guard reference position, and permitted mounting points. Confirm which component is designed to move during adjustment. Do not infer adjustment points from fasteners that appear movable; they may secure fixed parts or maintain a required assembly position.

Before changing position, inspect the area for loose fasteners, guard sag, actuator damage, contamination, and visible switch damage. Check whether the guard reaches its specified position consistently. If it does not, investigate the guard or hinge rather than shifting the switch to compensate. Look for signs of wear or damage that could make engagement unreliable. Correct an identified fault only as directed by the applicable service instructions.

Record or otherwise establish the starting position as the machine procedure requires. This helps distinguish the original alignment from subsequent changes. If the required reference, permitted adjustment, or applicable limits cannot be identified, do not proceed by trial and error.

## Set the micro switch trip position without exceeding its limits

Use only the adjustment the machine design and service procedure authorize. Align the actuator so it engages the switch consistently at the intended guard position. Make the smallest permitted position change needed to correct an established alignment mismatch, then secure the mounting hardware as specified. Do not bend, shim, or modify parts unless the service procedure explicitly specifies that method.

Respect the switch’s specified operating-position, pretravel, overtravel, and force limits where applicable. MIL-PRF-8805K defines **pretravel** as the distance or angle through which the actuator moves from free position to operating position. It defines **overtravel** as the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. These terms describe actuator travel; they do not supply a numeric limit for a particular installed switch. Use the applicable switch data and machine instructions for actual limits, and do not infer values from these definitions.

Do not force the actuator beyond permitted travel or use the switch as a mechanical stop. The guard and its intended mechanical stops must not depend on the switch to limit movement. If the actuator cannot engage at the required guard position without exceeding a specified travel or force limit, stop. That condition is not a reason to continue moving the switch beyond its permitted range.

After adjustment, secure the mounting hardware according to the machine-specific instructions. Recheck that the guard and actuator approach each other as intended and that the adjustment has not introduced a new obstruction or contact condition. Visual alignment is only a preliminary check. It does not demonstrate that the guard-state function switches reliably.

## Verify bottling machine guard interlock micro switch adjustment

Use the documented test procedure to restore power or otherwise conduct functional checks. Keep personnel clear of hazardous motion and follow the machine’s required safety checks. Do not create an improvised live test or assume that a particular indicator, sound, or motion is an acceptable test method. The machine-specific procedure must define how to observe or test the guard-state function.

Check both transitions: the required response as the guard closes and the required response as it opens. Use repeated, controlled opening and closing cycles, observing the specified indicators or applying the specified test method each time. One successful actuation is not proof of reliable operation. An audible click alone is also insufficient because it does not establish that the intended guard-state function responds as designed.

Confirm that each transition occurs at the required guard position and is consistent across the controlled cycles. Apply all additional machine-specific safety checks and acceptance criteria. If either transition is missing, delayed, inconsistent, or otherwise outside the documented requirements, do not treat the adjustment as complete. Stop and determine whether there is a mechanical, switch, wiring, control, or safety-circuit fault; do not keep shifting the switch without diagnosing the cause.

Record the adjustment and verification results as required by the machine’s service process. Return the machine to service only after the prescribed checks pass. If the procedure does not define a reliable way to confirm the required guard states, or the result cannot be confirmed, keep the machine out of service.

## When adjustment is not the fix

A position mismatch may be correctable when the guard and actuator are intact, the design provides an authorized adjustment, and the switch can operate within its specified limits. Continued adjustment is not an appropriate response to a damaged actuator, a worn or damaged switch, loose or unstable mounting, or inconsistent switching. Stop if the required travel limits cannot be met at the intended guard position.

Separate mechanical misalignment from faults elsewhere. Guard sag or hinge movement can change the relationship between the guard and switch. Wiring, control, or safety-circuit faults may also prevent the required response even when the mechanical engagement appears correct. These conditions require diagnosis under the applicable service procedure; do not assume that moving the switch will resolve them.

Never bypass or defeat the interlock to operate the machine or complete a test. If reliable guard-state detection cannot be verified, keep the machine out of service until the fault is corrected and all required checks pass. Successful **bottling machine guard interlock micro switch adjustment** means the authorized setting meets the machine’s limits and both guard-state transitions are confirmed by the documented test, not simply that the actuator appears aligned.

Intermittent recognition of an ATM cash cassette door means the machine reports the door state inconsistently across otherwise similar door cycles. **ATM cash cassette door micro switch intermittent operation** is a symptom to isolate, not proof that the switch itself has failed. The door feature may not move the actuator consistently, the switch contact may behave inconsistently, or a connector, wire, or machine-side input may be involved. An alert alone does not distinguish these possibilities.

## What intermittent operation means at an ATM cash cassette door micro switch

Intermittent operation means that the machine does not recognize the cassette door state consistently across repeated, otherwise similar cycles. For example, the reported state may change on one cycle but not another. The alert or status is an observation about the system; by itself, it does not establish which component or connection caused the inconsistency.

A micro switch is an electromechanical component whose actuator is moved by an external mechanism. In this application, the relevant question is whether the door feature moves and releases that actuator consistently, and whether the switch's electrical behavior changes consistently with that movement. The switch is only one part of the path between door movement and the machine's reported input.

Keep those stages separate. A door that does not reach the actuator reliably presents a different fault path from a switch whose isolated contact behavior is inconsistent. Both differ from a stable switch response accompanied by a discrepant machine-side status. Observations should therefore be tied to a specific door cycle, actuator movement, test state, and approved input-status indication rather than treating every door alert as a switch failure.

## Check whether door movement consistently actuates the micro switch

With the door mechanism accessible under the approved procedure, observe whether its feature reaches and releases the switch actuator consistently. Check the relevant mechanical relationship: whether engagement varies from cycle to cycle, travel appears obstructed, the actuator sticks, or the actuator fails to return when the door movement is reversed. Note what is observed without forcing the door feature or bending the actuator.

Actuator travel provides a useful distinction between movement before switching and movement after switching. Pretravel runs from the actuator's free position to its operating position; overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These definitions do not provide a device-specific travel value or establish how much travel this ATM mechanism requires. Use documented tolerances where available, and do not substitute an assumed measurement or forceful adjustment for them.

Compare movement across repeated, controlled door cycles. If the door feature does not reach the actuator reliably, or the actuator does not release consistently, the mechanical actuation path remains a plausible cause. If movement appears consistent, that observation alone does not prove the switch contacts or downstream input are functioning. Continue with the approved contact check rather than treating visible movement as a complete test.

## Verify switch continuity through repeated actuations

Before interpreting a continuity check, identify the actual contact configuration from the device documentation or markings. In generic changeover terminology, COM means common, NC means normally closed, and NO means normally open. These symbols do not establish which contact arrangement is present on a particular device or which state the machine expects. Verify the exact schematic; do not assume that a particular terminal is connected or that an unqualified open or closed state is correct.

Only authorized personnel should perform a continuity check when the circuit has been isolated as required by the applicable procedure. Observe continuity in the released and actuated states, then repeat the same controlled actuation enough to determine whether the readings are consistent under those defined conditions. Record the actuator state and terminals checked with each observation. Interpret readings against the verified contact configuration, not a universal assumption about which contact should be open or closed.

If readings vary across repeated checks in the same defined state, the observation supports further investigation of switch movement or contact behavior, but it does not identify the precise cause on its own. If readings are consistent, that result applies to the tested switch, terminals, and conditions; it does not prove the installed wiring or machine input is sound. A continuity result should not be used to infer the state of an untested part of the signal path.

## Separate a switch fault from wiring or machine-input faults

After the local check, inspect accessible terminals, connectors, and wiring within the authorized service scope. Look for looseness, visible damage, or a connection that changes as the door mechanism moves. Record the location and conditions of any observed change. Do not treat a visible irregularity as proof that it caused the reported symptom, and do not extend the inspection beyond the access and checks permitted by service documentation.

If the switch's isolated readings are consistent but the machine reports a different or inconsistent status, the evidence shifts attention beyond the switch, including the connector, wiring, or machine-side input path. That pattern does not identify which downstream part is at fault. Conversely, inconsistent isolated readings justify investigation at the switch and actuation stages, but do not rule out an additional wiring or input issue.

Use machine-side input status or service diagnostics only as specified in authorized service documentation. Compare that approved indication with the recorded door movement and local switch observations. Do not probe, reprogram, or otherwise investigate controller functions outside the documented procedure. If approved diagnostics are unavailable, inconclusive, or inconsistent with the local checks, escalate the fault rather than inferring an input failure.

## Decide what the test results support

Use the combined observations to select a fault path, not to claim certainty beyond the evidence. Variable door engagement or inconsistent actuator release points toward further investigation of the mechanical path. Consistent movement paired with inconsistent continuity in the same isolated, defined states supports further investigation of the switch or its actuation. Consistent local switch behavior paired with discrepant approved machine-side status shifts investigation toward terminals, wiring, or the machine input. None of these patterns alone proves the complete assembly good or bad.

Adjustment or switch replacement should be considered only when the observations and applicable service documentation support that work, and should be performed by qualified personnel under authorized procedures. Controller-side investigation also belongs within those procedures and personnel qualifications. If findings conflict, the symptom cannot be reproduced, or the documented checks do not resolve the fault path, refer the machine to authorized service rather than bypassing safeguards or substituting an unsupported repair.

Document the cycle conditions, actuator observations, contact configuration, isolated continuity results, accessible connection findings, and any approved input-status result. This record makes clear what was actually tested and what remains unknown. Return the machine to service only through the applicable authorized procedure; a single continuity check or a temporarily consistent status does not by itself establish that the intermittent symptom is resolved.

A **micro switch** is an electromechanical component that may be used inside a fire alarm pull station. Knowing how to test a fire alarm pull station micro switch means keeping a limited, isolated contact check separate from testing the pull station, its wiring, or the fire alarm system. A continuity reading can describe the contacts in a defined state; it cannot establish that the life-safety system is ready or will respond as intended.

Do not perform a switch-level check on an energized or operational life-safety circuit. Follow the approved procedure for the specific system, and involve qualified personnel when isolation, access, or the required system state is uncertain. The steps below apply only to a safely isolated switch and only where its terminal identities and expected contact behavior are documented.

## What this micro switch test can and cannot establish

A pull station is equipment that may contain a micro switch. The application name alone does not establish that every pull station uses this component, how a particular switch is arranged, or what the switch’s contacts do in each condition. The component-level check described here concerns contact continuity at documented terminals under a defined actuator state. It is not a test of the entire pull station.

A continuity result can show whether the measured contact pair behaves as expected for the specific switch and state, if those expectations are documented. It does not establish that the pull station’s actuator and internal mechanism move correctly, that the switch is correctly installed, or that wiring outside the isolated component is intact. Nor does it establish that a fire alarm signal will be transmitted or that the system is ready for service.

These distinctions matter because similar observations can arise at different points. The contact result concerns the tested pair and measurement conditions. A problem with terminal identification, actuator movement, installation, wiring, or other equipment may require a different check. One isolated reading cannot identify or rule out every such issue.

Treat the check as one narrow observation, not a pass/fail judgment on the life-safety equipment. System inspection and functional testing remain governed by the approved procedure for that specific system. This article does not provide those system-level steps, or instructions for resetting, repairing, or replacing a pull station or switch.

## Before checking a fire alarm pull station micro switch

First determine whether an authorized, safe isolation can be established under the applicable approved procedure. Do not probe an energized circuit or assume that removing or accessing a component makes an installed life-safety circuit safe. If the required state, isolation, or access is unclear, stop and refer the work to qualified personnel. Do not use an isolated contact check in place of the procedure required for the system.

Identify the actual switch and consult its applicable documentation before making a measurement. Confirm the terminal markings and the contact pair to be checked. Do not infer terminal identity from physical position, appearance, or a generic layout. The connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load for the exact switch and equipment are the relevant references; a generic wiring assumption does not establish applicability.

Also establish the reference condition for the contact state you intend to check. Contact-state labels have meaning only in relation to the documented reference condition and the specific switch. Do not assume that a pull station’s application implies a particular normal state, contact configuration, or alarm behavior. If the documentation does not identify the terminals or expected behavior clearly enough to select a pair and state, do not guess.

Before proceeding, make sure the switch itself can be safely isolated from the circuit as required by the applicable procedure. The continuity check described below is for the isolated switch, not for an installed, energized circuit. Where isolation cannot be confirmed, the check is outside this procedure’s boundary.

## How to test a fire alarm pull station micro switch

1. **Confirm the boundary.** Verify that the applicable procedure permits the component check and that the switch is safely isolated. If the circuit is energized, operational, or of uncertain status, do not make the measurement.

2. **Identify the documented contact pair.** Use the markings and documentation for the specific switch to select the terminals. Record which pair you are checking and the documented reference condition. Do not substitute a presumed layout or infer a contact state from the pull station’s purpose.

3. **Set a defined actuator state.** Use only a state that can be identified for the particular switch and that the documentation relates to expected contact behavior. Record how the actuator was positioned for the reading. Do not assume that a visible pull-station position, by itself, defines the switch’s documented reference condition.

4. **Measure continuity across the documented pair.** With the switch isolated, use a continuity meter across the selected terminals. Record the meter’s indication together with the terminal pair and actuator state. Compare the result only with expected behavior documented for that specific switch and reference condition. A meter indication without those details is not a meaningful comparison.

5. **Check another state only when appropriate.** If the applicable documentation specifies expected behavior for another defined actuator state, and the approved procedure permits the check, repeat the measurement in that state. Record the state and result separately. Do not invent an expected transition or treat an undocumented state as a required test condition.

6. **Keep the conclusion narrow.** State whether the observed meter behavior matches the documented expectation for the tested pair and state. A click, movement, or single continuity reading does not by itself prove correct wiring, contact suitability for a load, correct operation of the whole mechanism, or readiness of the alarm system.

Do not extend the measurement to terminals whose identities or expected behavior are unknown. If the applicable documentation does not provide a usable expected result, the reading cannot support a definite pass or fail conclusion. Resolve that uncertainty through the approved procedure and qualified personnel rather than by trying different connections or states.

## Interpreting results without overdiagnosing

If a reading differs from the documented behavior, it indicates a discrepancy under the recorded test conditions, not a confirmed cause. The selected terminals might have been identified incorrectly; the actuator might not have been in the defined state; movement may not have reached the switch as expected; or the measurement setup may need review. A contact problem is also one possibility, but a single reading does not distinguish among these explanations.

A plausible contact change across documented terminals is similarly limited. It supports only the observation that the isolated switch behaved as expected for the measured pair and defined state. It does not verify the pull station’s mechanics or installation, the surrounding wiring, circuit function, alarm signaling, or system readiness. Those questions fall outside an isolated continuity check and require the applicable approved system procedure.

If the terminal identities, reference condition, meter behavior, safe isolation, or result remains uncertain, stop rather than infer a diagnosis. Refer unresolved component or system checks to qualified personnel. Keep the record precise: note the documented terminal pair, actuator state, meter indication, and expected behavior used for comparison. That record describes what was checked without claiming that the whole pull station or life-safety system passed a test it did not undergo.

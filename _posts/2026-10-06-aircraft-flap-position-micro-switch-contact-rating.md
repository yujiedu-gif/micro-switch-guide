An aircraft flap position micro switch contact rating describes electrical switching limits under specified conditions; it does not, by itself, show that a switch is suitable for a particular aircraft circuit. Some flap-position designs use a micro switch, while others may use a different sensing arrangement. Verification therefore starts with the exact switch and circuit documentation, not with an assumption about how flap position is sensed.

The rating must be read with its stated voltage, current, load type, and operating conditions. A circuit that carries only a small indication signal may impose a different switching duty from one in which the contacts directly carry a load. Neither a low normal current nor a headline maximum-current figure settles compatibility on its own.

## What the aircraft flap position micro switch contact rating describes

A contact rating is a specification-sheet limit for switching electrical load under stated conditions. It is not a universal capacity shared by all micro switches, and it is not a general measure of quality. Electrical ratings are tied to the load and test conditions identified in the applicable specification information. A value without those qualifications is incomplete for a suitability comparison.

Read voltage and current together, and retain any AC or DC distinction exactly as stated. Do not treat a value specified for AC as evidence of an equivalent DC capability, or vice versa. Likewise, a current figure does not stand alone: the voltage, type of load, and other stated conditions define the context in which the rating applies.

Load type matters when the documentation distinguishes among resistive, inductive, or lamp loads. Those terms indicate that the rating may be conditional on the kind of load being switched. Use the specific load category and test conditions stated for the switch; do not transfer a value from one category to another just because the numerical current appears adequate.

Operating conditions also belong to the rating. If a specification identifies a particular switching duty or other test condition, that context must remain attached to the stated limit. A rating interpreted without its conditions cannot establish that a circuit falls within the switch’s documented capability.

## Why flap-position circuit conditions affect rating suitability

A micro switch is relevant only if the particular flap-position design actually uses one. The flap-position function alone does not identify the sensor type, contact arrangement, or circuit behavior. This article concerns the electrical rating of a micro switch where one is documented; it does not imply that every aircraft flap system uses this component.

The switch’s electrical role affects the comparison. In one arrangement, contacts may directly carry a load. In another, they may change state in a low-current indication or control circuit. The circuit’s normal signal current is not automatically the whole suitability question: the applicable documentation may also identify contact configuration, switching frequency, inrush, or minimum-load constraints. Consider those factors only where they are specified for the actual switch and circuit.

A switch’s stated capacity and the circuit’s ordinary operating current answer different questions. The rating describes a limit under stated conditions; the circuit data describe what the contacts are expected to switch. A low signal current does not prove that the contact will operate reliably at that level, and a larger maximum-current rating does not prove that the switch is appropriate for the circuit. Any minimum-load limitation or low-level switching condition must be checked rather than inferred.

The contact form and circuit function also need to match the documented arrangement. Physical terminal placement alone is not enough to identify a terminal’s role or establish how the switch participates in the aircraft circuit. Use the documented connection diagram and terminal markings for the exact switch and equipment.

## Check the aircraft flap position micro switch contact rating against circuit data

Use a documentation-first comparison. Identify the exact switch information and the applicable aircraft records that describe the circuit. The goal is to compare documented conditions, not to infer a rating from appearance or from the general purpose of a flap-position system.

Check the following items against those records:

- **Switch identity and rating conditions:** Locate the rating information for the exact switch, including its stated voltage, current, load type, and operating or test conditions. Preserve any AC/DC distinction and any separate ratings by load category.
- **Circuit voltage and current:** Identify the voltage and expected steady current at the contacts from the applicable circuit information. Compare them with the rating conditions rather than comparing a single current value in isolation.
- **Load and switching duty:** Confirm the documented load type and check any available information about inrush, switching frequency, or other duty-related conditions. Do not assume these details from the words “flap position.”
- **Contact and terminal configuration:** Use the exact connection diagram, terminal markings, and stated circuit function to confirm the relevant contact arrangement. Do not identify contacts from a generic physical-layout assumption.
- **Minimum-load limits:** Check whether the switch documentation specifies a minimum load or a limitation relevant to low-current operation. A maximum rating alone does not establish reliable operation at a smaller signal level.

If a required value or condition is missing, the comparison remains unresolved. Do not fill gaps with a rating from another switch, a different load type, or a presumed aircraft arrangement. Installation, inspection, and replacement decisions should be referred to the applicable approved aircraft maintenance information. This verification outline does not provide aircraft-specific wiring, adjustment, or airworthiness instructions.

## What a contact rating cannot establish on its own

A contact rating does not identify the load in an aircraft circuit. That information must come from the applicable circuit documentation. Nor does a rating, by itself, confirm that a particular switch is approved for installation in a particular aircraft or flap-position design. A numerical match is not an approval or compatibility determination.

The rating alone also does not establish contact reliability at very low signal levels or performance over a particular service life. Those conclusions require supporting documentation for the exact switch and operating conditions. Do not treat a headline voltage and current match as proof that a low-current indication circuit is suitable, especially when minimum-load information is absent or unclear.

The useful conclusion is limited: compare the documented switch rating with the documented circuit voltage, current, load type, contact configuration, and switching duty, including any stated minimum-load constraint. If the records do not supply enough information for that comparison, suitability remains unknown and must be resolved through the applicable approved maintenance information. The aircraft flap position micro switch contact rating is one part of that review, not a standalone basis for installation or substitution.

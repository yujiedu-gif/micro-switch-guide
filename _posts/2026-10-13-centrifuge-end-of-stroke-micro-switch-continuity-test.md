A centrifuge end of stroke micro switch continuity test can show whether the isolated switch changes electrical continuity as documented at a defined actuator position. It cannot, by itself, establish that the centrifuge linkage reaches the switch, that the switch is actuated at the stroke endpoint, or that the control circuit responds correctly. Keep those questions separate when interpreting a reading.

Use the equipment’s applicable service procedure to establish safe isolation before access or testing. Identify the exact switch’s terminal arrangement and documented actuator states before comparing measurements. If isolation, terminal identification, or the required procedure is uncertain, stop and refer the work to qualified personnel.

## What a centrifuge end of stroke micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity check addresses contact behavior in the switch under a specific condition: the switch is isolated from the surrounding circuit, and its actuator is in a defined position. The result can be compared with the behavior documented for that exact switch and terminal pairing. Without those references, a reading cannot be called correct or incorrect merely because a meter displays continuity or no continuity.

The test has a narrow boundary. It does not show whether the centrifuge’s end-of-stroke mechanism moves the actuator as intended. It also does not establish that wiring is correct or that the control system recognizes the contact state. A switch may behave as documented when operated by hand while the linkage fails to reach or move it at the endpoint. Conversely, a problem observed in the equipment does not, by itself, prove that the switch’s contacts are faulty.

Treat the isolated result as one observation in fault isolation, not a verdict on the complete centrifuge function. Keep the test condition, actuator position, terminal pairing, and observed reading together. Those details make the result interpretable and help prevent an uncertain terminal assignment or an in-circuit measurement from being mistaken for a switch-only check.

## Before the centrifuge end of stroke micro switch continuity test

Follow the applicable centrifuge service procedure to establish safe isolation and gain access to the switch. Do not improvise a live test or assume that a particular control position makes the equipment safe to work on. Electrical work beyond the supplied safe procedure belongs to qualified personnel using the equipment’s service documentation. If the equipment cannot be safely isolated, stop rather than proceeding to a continuity measurement.

Before testing, identify the exact switch’s terminal arrangement from its marking or documentation. A generic changeover contact may use common, normally closed, and normally open labels, but that description does not identify the terminals on an undocumented device or establish their state for this test. The exact device marking or schematic controls. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment; do not infer a connection from physical layout alone.

Also identify the actuator position or reference states specified by the switch documentation. Record which position will be tested and which terminal pairings are documented for comparison. Do not assume that an unactuated position always corresponds to a particular contact state, or that a centrifuge’s endpoint determines the state without evidence for that arrangement. If the terminal arrangement or relevant actuator reference is unknown, stop and seek qualified help or authoritative service information before interpreting readings.

## Check isolated continuity in defined actuator states

With the switch isolated as required by the service procedure, use a suitable continuity measuring method on the documented terminal pairings. Keep the test to those pairings rather than guessing from where terminals sit or how a generic switch is usually wired. Test at a defined actuator position, then compare the observation with the behavior documented for that exact switch in that position.

For each comparison, record the terminal pair, the actuator position, and the observed continuity result. If documentation specifies more than one reference position, keep the observations distinct; do not combine them into an assumed sequence. The available evidence does not establish a universal open or closed result for any terminal pair, so the expected reading must come from the exact switch documentation rather than a general rule.

An actuator may move or make an audible click without proving that electrical continuity is present or correct. Likewise, a reading obtained while the switch remains connected to the equipment does not isolate the switch’s contact behavior from the surrounding circuit. Use only a properly isolated switch result when drawing a conclusion about the switch itself, and retain the documented state and terminal pairing as part of that conclusion.

## Interpret a mismatch without blaming the switch alone

If an isolated reading differs from the documented behavior, first keep the scope of that observation clear. Terminal misidentification or a measurement setup issue can make a sound switch appear inconsistent with an expectation. If the documented terminal pair, actuator position, and measurement conditions are confirmed and the mismatch remains, internal contact behavior becomes a possible cause. A single observation does not establish which explanation is correct.

If the isolated switch readings match documented behavior but the centrifuge still misses an endpoint, the switch-only result has not verified end-of-stroke actuation. The linkage may not move the actuator as intended, or switch mounting or actuation may not place it in the documented state at the endpoint. Wiring or a fault elsewhere in the control system may also account for the equipment symptom. These are distinct possibilities, not conclusions that can be settled by the isolated continuity result.

Continue only under the applicable service diagnostic procedure. The documented terminal arrangement and circuit function matter when investigating wiring, and the equipment procedure governs further checks of linkage and control response. Do not treat a matching hand-operated reading as proof that the complete equipment function works, or a mismatch as proof that the switch alone is responsible.

## When to stop and seek qualified service

Stop if safe isolation cannot be established, the switch terminals or documented actuator states cannot be identified, or the next check would require access to an energized circuit. Refer those conditions to qualified personnel and the applicable service documentation. Do not substitute a guessed terminal arrangement or a generic switch convention for device-specific information.

Do not bypass the switch or infer that the centrifuge is safe to operate because an isolated continuity result matches documentation. That result concerns only the tested switch, terminal pairing, and actuator position. Whether the linkage actuates the switch at the endpoint and whether the control circuit responds as intended remain separate equipment-level questions. The centrifuge end of stroke micro switch continuity test is useful when those limits are respected: it checks documented contact behavior under defined conditions, but it does not certify the full end-of-stroke function.

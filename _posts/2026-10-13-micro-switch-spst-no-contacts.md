The phrase **micro switch SPST NO contacts** describes a contact arrangement and its state at a reference condition. SPST means single-pole, single-throw; NO means normally open. For the documented arrangement, the NO contact is open in the specified unactuated reference state. The label identifies a contact condition, not by itself the current flow, function, or response of an installed circuit.

To interpret the description accurately, keep three things separate: the switch's contact arrangement, the actuator's mechanical movement, and the behavior of the circuit connected to the switch. The first two can describe the component. The third depends on the actual wiring and circuit design, which the label alone does not establish.

## What micro switch SPST NO contacts describes

SPST expands to **single-pole, single-throw**. In this description, it refers to one switching path with a normally open contact. It is a statement about the contact arrangement, not a complete account of the switch's terminals, ratings, or use in equipment.

“Normally open” needs a reference condition. For the arrangement described here, the NO contact is open when the switch is in its specified unactuated reference state. “Normally” does not mean that the contact must be open in every installation or under every possible condition. It names the contact state used as the reference for describing the switch.

That reference-state definition also keeps the term separate from an assumption about current. An open contact describes the contact state; it does not, on its own, establish whether current flows elsewhere in an installed circuit. Current flow depends on the circuit connected to the switch and its operating conditions. Without those details, the contact label cannot settle the circuit's behavior.

The words SPST and NO should be read together but not treated as interchangeable. SPST describes the switching arrangement as one pole and one throw. NO describes the contact's state at the stated reference condition. Neither term, by itself, provides a wiring diagram or describes what the equipment does when the contact changes state.

These definitions apply to the documented contact arrangement, not automatically to every micro switch. A different device may have a different arrangement or markings. If a particular switch's terminal labeling or schematic is available, use that documentation to identify the actual arrangement. The terms alone should not be used to fill in details that the device documentation does not establish.

## How actuation relates to micro switch SPST NO contacts

Actuation is the movement of the switch's actuator by an external mechanism. It is distinct from the electrical state named by NO. In the described arrangement, the NO contact is open at the specified unactuated reference state; the contact-state description relates that reference to the contact's change associated with actuation. It does not, by itself, specify the full movement, timing, or operating conditions of a particular switch.

The mechanical input and the electrical result are related but are not the same description. A mechanism can move the actuator, while the contact arrangement describes the switching path and state. The label does not identify what moves the actuator, how far it moves, or how a connected system responds. Those details depend on the particular design and circuit and should not be inferred from SPST NO alone.

In particular, a contact changing state does not establish a particular host-device action. A circuit might use a contact change as one input to its logic, but the resulting behavior depends on its wiring and design. The same contact label does not tell a reader that equipment will start, stop, signal, or change modes. No such response follows from the NO label by itself.

It is also important not to turn the reference-state definition into a claim about an installation. “Open when unactuated” describes the contact under the stated reference condition. It does not identify whether an installed mechanism is currently pressing the actuator, whether the circuit is energized, or what other components may affect the circuit. Those are separate facts that must be established from the actual device and circuit documentation.

The useful boundary is therefore narrow: SPST NO identifies a one-pole, one-throw contact arrangement and an open NO contact in the specified unactuated reference state. It helps explain the contact description in relation to actuation, but it is not a complete description of mechanical operation or circuit function.

## What the SPST NO label does not tell you

The label alone does not establish a COM terminal. A generic changeover contact may be labeled common, normally closed, and normally open, but the exact device marking or schematic controls. Do not infer that a particular SPST NO micro switch has a terminal named COM unless its documentation identifies one. The contact description and the terminal names printed on a particular device are related questions, but one does not settle the other.

Nor does SPST NO establish an electrical rating, load suitability, or compatibility with a particular application. Those details must come from the applicable switch specification and the circuit requirements. The contact-state label does not state a voltage, current, load type, or other rating. Supplying any such value or conclusion without device-specific evidence would go beyond what the terms mean.

The same limit applies to safety and installed current flow. An open NO contact in the reference state does not certify that an installation is safe, de-energized, or behaving as intended. Nor does the label prove that current cannot flow through another path in the surrounding circuit. It describes the contact state under a defined condition, not the complete electrical system.

For interpretation, keep the questions distinct: What contact arrangement is documented? What state is specified at the unactuated reference condition? What does the device documentation say about its terminals? And what does the actual circuit do with a change in contact state? The SPST NO description addresses the first two at a basic level; it does not answer the latter questions without additional documentation.

The practical conclusion is limited but useful: **micro switch SPST NO contacts** means a single-pole, single-throw arrangement whose NO contact is open at the documented unactuated reference state. It does not, by itself, identify a COM terminal, define an installed circuit's current flow or function, or establish ratings, safety, or application compatibility. Interpret those matters from the exact switch documentation and the actual circuit rather than from the contact label alone.

# Identifying Micro Switch Wiring in an Aircraft Cockpit Push Button

Aircraft cockpit push button micro switch wiring must be identified from the exact switch documentation and the applicable aircraft wiring diagram, not from a generic pinout. This article covers how to distinguish switch contacts from other circuits in a push-button assembly and how to keep verification within the relevant documentation and maintenance procedures. It does not provide aircraft-specific terminal assignments or instructions to alter aircraft wiring.

A micro switch may serve as a contact device actuated by a button where that design is used. However, not every cockpit push button necessarily contains a micro switch, and an assembly may include separate circuitry. Terminal names, circuit roles, and permitted checks therefore depend on the exact component and aircraft documentation.

## What aircraft cockpit push button micro switch wiring refers to

The wiring at issue is the connection between the micro switch’s contacts and the aircraft circuit identified in the applicable documentation. The switch’s mechanical action changes a contact state when its actuator moves, but that general function does not establish which aircraft function the switch controls or how its terminals are connected.

A cockpit push button can include more than one electrical function. A switch contact may be one circuit, while illumination, if present, may use separate terminals and a distinct circuit. The presence of a button or a visible light does not establish that the lamp and switch share terminals, a return, or an electrical supply. Those details remain specific to the component and aircraft installation.

There is no universal aircraft pinout for this arrangement. A generic drawing or a terminal’s apparent location cannot establish its function in a particular aircraft. The applicable aircraft wiring diagram, the switch’s markings or documentation, and the relevant maintenance instructions define the context for identification. If those sources do not establish the circuit, its identity should remain unresolved rather than being inferred.

## Read the micro switch terminal markings before tracing aircraft cockpit push button micro switch wiring

Some changeover contacts use the labels COM, NO, and NC. These commonly refer to common, normally open, and normally closed terminals. They are general labels, not a guarantee that every device uses the same markings or terminal arrangement. The exact switch marking or schematic controls, so verify the device against its documentation before interpreting a label or connecting it to an aircraft circuit.

For a device documented with these designations, NO and NC describe contact relationships in the specified actuator state. The relevant reference is the switch’s documented released and actuated positions, not an assumed state based on how a button looks or feels. Do not assign a state to a terminal unless the device documentation establishes it.

Physical position is not a reliable substitute for identification. Pins that appear to occupy familiar locations may have different functions on another component. Wire color alone does not establish terminal identity or aircraft circuit function either. Likewise, a continuity result by itself does not tell you which aircraft circuit a contact serves. It can only be interpreted in the context of the exact device, stated test method, actuator state, and applicable documentation.

Use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer a connection from a generic diagram or from apparent terminal layout. Ratings also require care: rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. A rating or contact label alone does not establish that a switch is suitable for an undocumented aircraft circuit.

## Separate push-button contact wiring from illumination wiring

When an assembly is illuminated, distinguish its switch contacts from any illumination terminals before tracing connections. The lamp circuit and the contact circuit may be separate functions within the assembly. Treat them as separate until the component documentation and aircraft wiring diagram establish how each is arranged.

Do not identify a terminal as a lamp connection merely because it is near a lens, or as a switch contact because it resembles a familiar contact terminal. Do not infer lamp voltage, polarity, or a shared return from the appearance of the assembly or its wires. The documentation must establish those details; if it does not, they are unknown.

A useful way to keep the circuits distinct is to match each documented terminal to its stated function, then trace that function in the aircraft wiring diagram. Confirm the switch-contact path separately from any documented illumination path. If the diagram and component documentation do not agree, or do not clearly identify both functions, do not resolve the discrepancy by guessing or by treating the assembly as a single circuit.

## Verify the circuit through aircraft documentation and approved procedures

Begin with the applicable aircraft wiring diagram and maintenance instructions for the installed equipment. Confirm that the documents apply to the aircraft and component under consideration, then compare the diagram’s circuit references with the exact switch’s markings or connection diagram. The purpose is to establish terminal identity and circuit function from authoritative, matching documentation before interpreting any wire or test result.

Next, follow the applicable maintenance procedures for the work and use only checks that those procedures authorize, with the specified test method and isolation conditions. This article does not prescribe a test setup or energized probing. Do not use continuity alone to assign an aircraft function: a result must be evaluated under the documented actuator state and test conditions, and it cannot replace the wiring diagram or switch documentation.

If an authorized check is part of the applicable procedure, compare its result with the expected contact relationship and circuit information in those documents. Keep switch contacts distinct from any illumination circuit throughout that comparison. After any work, use the applicable procedure to determine required restoration, inspection, and documentation steps. No universal reconnection sequence or inspection requirement can be established without the relevant aircraft instructions.

## Limits, safety, and when not to proceed

Do not use a generic switch drawing as an aircraft-specific wiring instruction. Do not infer a pinout from physical position, wire color, or continuity alone, and do not assume that a push button’s illumination shares a circuit with its switch contacts. None of those clues, by itself, establishes the installed circuit’s function or the correct connection.

Do not proceed when the switch markings, component documentation, aircraft diagram, or observed circuit identity conflict or leave a terminal unresolved. Refer the work to appropriately authorized maintenance personnel and use the applicable maintenance procedures. The information here does not authorize work on certified aircraft wiring, provide aircraft-specific pin assignments, or replace required qualification and deenergization controls.

**In short, aircraft cockpit push button micro switch wiring is identified by matching the exact switch documentation to the applicable aircraft wiring diagram and procedures.** If those sources do not establish a terminal’s function or the permitted verification method, do not fill the gap with a generic pinout or an assumption.

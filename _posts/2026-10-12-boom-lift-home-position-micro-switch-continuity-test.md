A boom lift home position micro switch continuity test can show whether an isolated switch contact changes state as its actuator moves. It cannot, by itself, establish that the lift’s position signal, wiring, or control system works correctly. Secure the lift and follow the applicable service instructions before accessing the switch. Do not work under an unsupported boom or defeat an interlock.

## What the boom lift home position micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity or resistance check evaluates the electrical connection across a selected pair of switch terminals in a defined actuator state. Comparing the released and actuated readings can show whether that contact pair changes electrical state when the actuator moves. Use the switch diagram to determine which terminals to test and what behavior is expected. COM means common, NO means normally open, and NC means normally closed in generic changeover terminology, but verify the exact device diagram rather than relying on symbols alone.

This is a test of the isolated switch contact, not a test of the whole lift’s home-position function. The actuator’s movement, the switch’s mounting alignment, external connectors and wiring, and the control circuit are separate parts of the diagnostic path. A control-system display may provide information about the system, but it does not replace the isolated contact test described here. Likewise, a contact that changes state does not establish that the lift receives or interprets the signal correctly.

The test is meaningful only when the switch is disconnected from the circuit and measured in identified actuator states. If the expected contact behavior is not documented, or access cannot be performed safely, do not guess at terminals or test conditions. Follow the applicable service instructions or seek qualified service.

## Prepare the boom lift home position micro switch continuity test safely

Secure the lift and consult its service instructions before accessing the switch. Do not work under an unsupported boom. Do not bypass or defeat an interlock to make the test easier or to produce a desired reading. If the equipment cannot be secured or the correct access procedure is unclear, stop and seek qualified service.

De-energize the relevant circuit and disconnect the switch from the circuit before measuring continuity or resistance. Measuring a connected switch can include paths through other circuit components, so a reading may not describe the switch contact alone. Do not proceed unless the required isolation and disconnection can be completed under the applicable service procedure.

Identify the intended contact pair from the switch diagram. Do not assume that terminal position, labels, or a general NO/NC convention identifies the correct pair on this device. Establish which contact behavior the documentation calls for in the released state and in the mechanically actuated state. Keep those states tied to the diagram’s reference rather than inferring behavior from the lift’s display or from the apparent position of the boom.

This preparation sets the test boundary: an isolated switch, a known terminal pair, and two defined actuator states. If any of those conditions is uncertain, the readings will not support a reliable interpretation.

## Measure continuity in released and actuated positions

Set the meter to continuity or resistance and check the meter leads according to the meter’s instructions. Use the documented contact pair, with the switch disconnected from the circuit. Record the reading with the actuator released, then compare it with the reading when the actuator is carefully moved through its normal mechanical action. Do not force the actuator beyond its intended movement or use an improvised method to hold it.

Compare both readings with the expected behavior for that contact pair, as shown in the switch documentation. The important observation is whether the specified contact changes state between the released and actuated conditions. Do not apply a universal resistance threshold: no such threshold is established here. The applicable meter instructions and switch documentation govern how to interpret the reading.

Keep the actuator state and terminal pair consistent throughout the comparison. A single reading, without a defined state or correct terminal identification, cannot establish the expected change. If the meter result is unclear, repeat only within the documented, safe test procedure; do not infer a passing result from a click, a display indication, or movement alone. A click may accompany mechanism movement, but this test’s evidence is the measured contact behavior in the defined states.

## Interpret readings and separate switch faults from lift-side faults

If the expected state change does not appear, first confirm that the documented terminal pair was used and that the actuator reached the specified actuated state. If terminal identification and movement are correct but the isolated contact still does not show the documented change, the result may indicate a contact problem. It is not, by itself, proof that the switch is the only fault or that the lift has a particular failure.

If the isolated contact changes as documented, attention shifts to other parts of the position-signal path. Inspect actuator movement and mounting alignment separately, then consider connectors and wiring under the applicable service instructions. These are distinct checks: a switch contact can change state while an actuator fails to reach the required position, or while a connection or wire prevents the signal from reaching the control circuit. A successful isolated test therefore does not establish correct operation of the installed lift system.

If the readings conflict with the diagram, the meter procedure, or the observed actuator state, stop rather than guessing. Do not jumper or bypass the switch, and do not treat a control-system indication as a substitute for the isolated measurement. Resolving an uncertain result may require qualified service and equipment-specific instructions.

## Restore and verify operation after testing

After the measurement, restore the switch connections and secure the wiring according to the applicable service instructions. Follow the approved procedure for restoring power and checking operation. Verify normal home-position operation without bypassing or defeating any safety function. Do not treat the isolated contact reading as proof that the whole lift is functioning correctly.

If the switch readings matched the documented state change but home-position operation remains abnormal, the cause may lie in actuator travel, mounting alignment, connectors, wiring, or elsewhere in the control path. If the readings did not match, or terminal identification and safe access remain uncertain, stop and seek qualified service rather than improvising a repair or adjustment.

The boom lift home position micro switch continuity test is limited to comparing an isolated contact in defined released and actuated states. Its result helps distinguish a contact response from other possible faults, but the approved verification procedure is needed to assess normal operation of the equipment.

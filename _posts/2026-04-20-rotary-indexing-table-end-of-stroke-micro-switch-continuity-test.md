A rotary indexing table end of stroke micro switch continuity test checks whether the switch’s documented contacts show the expected continuity in defined actuator states. It does not, by itself, establish that the table’s control circuit or the complete equipment operates correctly. The test concerns the micro switch, not every rotary indexing table; equipment designs and control behavior are not established here.

Compare two states: the switch actuator before contact with the table mechanism, and the specified state when the mechanism reaches its intended actuation point, if the equipment documentation identifies that point. With the switch electrically isolated, measure the documented contact pair in each state and compare the readings with the expected behavior for that particular switch. Do not assume a universal end-of-stroke position, terminal arrangement, or contact transition.

## Direct answer: what the rotary indexing table end of stroke micro switch continuity test can establish

An isolated continuity check can establish whether a documented contact pair has continuity in the specific actuator state being tested. Comparing the before-actuation state with the documented end-of-stroke actuation state can show whether the measured contact behavior matches the expectation for that switch. The test is meaningful only when the switch is electrically isolated, the terminals are correctly identified, and the mechanical states are clearly defined.

A table reaching a stop, changing position, or producing a control response is not itself a continuity measurement. Those observations describe the equipment’s behavior, which can depend on the switch, its actuator or linkage, wiring, control circuitry, and other equipment components. Do not infer that the micro switch has changed contacts merely because the table stops or moves as expected.

Likewise, the test does not establish a universal contact arrangement. The expected result depends on the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. If those details do not identify the relevant terminals and expected contact behavior, a reading cannot be interpreted reliably from physical appearance alone. Resolve the documentation or terminal-identification uncertainty before drawing a conclusion.

## Before testing: isolate the micro switch and identify its documented terminals

A continuity measurement must be made with the switch electrically isolated from the circuit. A switch still connected to equipment wiring may be affected by other electrical paths, so a meter reading may not represent the switch contacts alone. Do not treat an in-circuit reading as an isolated switch result.

Use the connection diagram and terminal markings documented for the exact switch and equipment. Identify the contact pair to test and the expected behavior for each defined actuator state. Do not infer terminal functions from their physical positions, a generic layout, or the fact that the switch is used at an end of stroke. If the documentation does not establish the terminal identity or expected state behavior, the result remains unresolved.

Isolation and electrical work have a safety boundary. The cited United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify applicable requirements. Leave uncertain or hazardous isolation and verification work to a qualified person; this guide does not provide a procedure for working on energized equipment.

Once the switch is electrically isolated and its terminals are identified, keep the measurement focused on the switch contacts. This is not a test of the table’s installed wiring or control logic. Record which documented contact pair is measured and which actuator state is present so the result can be compared with the switch’s stated behavior rather than an assumed arrangement.

## Rotary indexing table end of stroke micro switch continuity test: compare defined actuator states

Define the states by the documented mechanical relationship, not by a presumed table angle or a universal stop position. The first state is before the actuator contacts the table mechanism. The second is the specified state when the mechanism reaches its intended actuation point, where equipment documentation makes that point clear. If the intended position or actuation point is not documented well enough to identify, do not substitute an assumed end-of-stroke location.

With the switch isolated, measure continuity across the documented contact pair in the before-actuation state. Note the meter’s indication and the actuator state. Then place the actuator in the documented end-of-stroke actuation state and measure the same contact pair again. Compare both observations with the expected behavior documented for that switch. Do not assume that either state must show continuity, or that the contact must change in a particular direction.

Keep the mechanical state separate from the electrical result. Reaching a point in the table’s motion is evidence about the mechanism’s position only if the specified state has been identified; it does not prove the actuator moved as intended or that the contacts changed. Conversely, a continuity result describes the isolated contact pair at the tested state, not whether the table reached that state during normal operation.

If the documentation distinguishes operating point from release point, treat them as different events. Do not assume that the point at which the actuator operates is identical to the point at which it releases. Any further interpretation of travel or movement requires applicable documentation; this check does not establish travel values or a universal actuation position. Keep notes tied to the actual documented state and contact pair so another observer can distinguish a measured result from an assumption.

## Interpret the result without confusing switch, linkage, and circuit faults

If the actuator does not reach the documented actuation state, the mechanical input or linkage is a separate issue to examine. That observation does not establish whether the isolated contacts work: the switch may not have been placed in the state required for the comparison. Keep the question of mechanical movement separate from the question of contact continuity.

If the isolated reading differs from the documented expected behavior, first verify that the correct terminals and actuator state were used. If those are confirmed and the mismatch remains, the switch’s contact behavior warrants further verification. One reading alone does not establish the cause. It does not prove whether the problem lies in the switch, an incorrect terminal interpretation, or a mismatch between the tested state and the documented expectation.

If isolated readings match the documented behavior in both states but the indexing table still behaves incorrectly, the continuity check has not identified the cause of the equipment behavior. Wiring or other circuitry may require separate investigation. The table’s control response is not established by the switch-only measurement, and a matching result does not prove installed wiring or the wider assembly is functional.

Keep the distinctions explicit: actuator or linkage concerns relate to whether the switch reaches the intended mechanical state; contact readings relate to isolated switch behavior; terminal identification determines which contacts were measured; and wiring or other equipment circuitry concerns the installed system. These are different diagnostic paths. A result in one path should not be used as proof about the others.

## Limits of a click, continuity reading, and installed-system diagnosis

A click can indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove electrical continuity, contact condition, correct terminal identification, or correct operation of the indexing table. Treat sound as an observation about movement, not as a substitute for the defined-state measurement.

An isolated continuity result applies only to the documented contact pair in the tested actuator state. It does not verify installed wiring, control logic, permissible load, or overall equipment safety. Nor does it establish that the actuator and linkage will reliably reach the tested state during table operation. Those questions require evidence and checks specific to the switch and equipment.

The rotary indexing table end of stroke micro switch continuity test is therefore a limited comparison: isolate the switch, identify its documented terminals, define the before-actuation and intended actuation states, and compare each measured result with documented expectations. If the state, terminal identity, or expected behavior is uncertain, the interpretation remains uncertain. A click, a single reading, or normal-looking table motion does not remove that uncertainty or prove the complete assembly functional.񎟢

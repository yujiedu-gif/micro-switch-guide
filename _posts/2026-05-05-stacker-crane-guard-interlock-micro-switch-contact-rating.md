A **stacker crane guard interlock micro switch contact rating** describes the electrical conditions under which the documented switch contacts are rated. It does not, by itself, establish that a particular switch is suitable for an interlock circuit or that the complete crane installation is safe. A micro switch may be used in a guard interlock in some designs, but the equipment category alone does not establish that it is used in a specific crane.

Interpret the rating by matching the exact switch contact configuration and the circuit’s documented electrical conditions to the rating conditions. If the relevant documentation does not identify a required condition, treat it as unresolved rather than filling the gap by assumption.

## What the stacker crane guard interlock micro switch contact rating describes

A contact rating is a specification for documented switch contacts under stated electrical and test conditions. It is not a universal switching capacity that can be applied to every circuit, load, or operating arrangement. For micro switch electrical ratings, the values in the relevant specification sheet are requirements tied to the stated load and test conditions. Those qualifications are part of the rating, not optional details.

The distinction between a component and a machine matters here. A stacker crane guard interlock may use a micro switch in some designs, but that possibility does not show that every stacker crane uses one, identify the switch fitted to a particular machine, or establish how its contacts function in the interlock circuit. The crane or guard name cannot substitute for the circuit documentation.

A rating also does not describe every aspect of the switch. Its meaning is limited to the documented contacts and conditions. Whether those contacts are the ones used by the interlock, and whether the circuit conditions match the rating, must be established separately from the applicable switch and equipment information.

This is why a number on a label or specification sheet should not be treated as an unconditional answer. Its meaning depends on the conditions attached to it, including the specified load and test conditions. If those conditions are not available, the rating’s applicability to the circuit cannot be determined from the value alone.

## Read the rating with its stated circuit conditions

When reviewing a rating, identify the conditions the documentation actually states. These may include voltage, current, load type, and contact arrangement. Use only values and conditions that are documented for the exact switch and relevant contacts. If a condition is absent or unclear, mark it as unknown rather than supplying a likely value.

Keep electrical distinctions intact. A rating stated for AC does not establish the same rating for DC. A value tied to one load type does not automatically apply to another. Nor does a value for one contact configuration establish the rating of a different configuration. Do not transfer or extrapolate a rating between such conditions unless the applicable documentation explicitly supports that use.

The load and test conditions matter together. A specification-sheet rating is not detached from the conditions under which it is stated. Reading only the voltage or current figure while overlooking the stated load or test conditions can therefore produce an unsupported interpretation. Preserve every qualification that accompanies the value when comparing it with circuit information.

Do not treat missing data as evidence of a match. If the rating documentation does not make a necessary condition clear, the available information has not established that the circuit falls within the rating. The same applies if the circuit description omits information needed for comparison. A partial match is not a documented basis for a compatibility conclusion.

The rating answers a limited question: what electrical conditions are specified for the documented contacts? It does not answer whether the contacts are wired as intended in a particular crane, how the interlock responds to a guard state, or whether the complete machine meets a safety requirement. Those questions require the relevant circuit and equipment documentation, not a broader reading of the component rating.

## Compare the documented rating with the actual interlock circuit

Use a verification sequence that keeps the switch evidence and circuit evidence separate until each is clear:

1. Identify the exact micro switch and contact configuration from the applicable switch documentation. If the exact component, contact arrangement, or contacts used are not documented, record that uncertainty rather than identifying them from appearance or equipment category.
2. Read the rating conditions for those documented contacts. Record the stated voltage, current, load type, and other applicable conditions as given. Do not add an AC/DC equivalence or transfer a value to a different load type.
3. Establish the interlock circuit conditions from the actual electrical documentation for the crane. The machine name, guard description, or physical layout does not establish the circuit load or which contacts are used.
4. Compare the circuit information with the complete set of rating conditions. A conclusion about whether the documented rating covers the circuit requires the relevant details on both sides of the comparison.
5. Mark any unavailable or ambiguous condition as unresolved. If the switch identity, contact configuration, circuit load, or applicable rating condition is not clear, the evidence does not support a compatibility conclusion.

Use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity or contact function from a generic physical layout. When information conflicts, is incomplete, or cannot be tied to the exact switch and circuit, do not resolve the mismatch by guesswork.

This sequence interprets documentation; it does not supply a procedure for working on a crane circuit. Any examination or work involving the equipment must follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. Questions that require resolving an unclear circuit or safety-related condition belong with qualified personnel using the applicable documentation.

## What a contact rating does not establish

A contact rating alone does not establish the interlock’s logic or behavior. It does not show what the equipment does when a guard changes state, prove that a particular contact arrangement is used, or demonstrate guarding performance. Those conclusions depend on the documented circuit and equipment design, which cannot be inferred from the contact rating alone.

The rating also does not prove that the switch is wired correctly, that its contacts are in a particular condition, or that the complete assembly is suitable. A documented electrical rating and an actual installation are different kinds of evidence. Matching stated circuit conditions to stated rating conditions addresses only that comparison; it does not certify the entire interlock or crane as safe.

The decision boundary is therefore straightforward: if the exact switch contacts and the actual circuit conditions are documented and the relevant rating conditions can be compared, the contact rating can be interpreted within that scope. If a required condition, contact configuration, or circuit detail is missing or ambiguous, leave the result unresolved and seek qualified review. Do not broaden the rating or infer machine safety from a label.

In short, the stacker crane guard interlock micro switch contact rating establishes a conditional electrical limit for documented contacts, not a general approval of the interlock or machine. Its application depends on the exact switch documentation and the actual circuit conditions. Where that evidence is incomplete, no compatibility or safety conclusion follows.

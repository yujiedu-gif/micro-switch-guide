A painting robot guard interlock micro switch contact rating describes the electrical conditions under which the specified switch contact rating applies. It does not, by itself, establish that the switch is suitable for every circuit in a robot guard system or that the complete system provides a particular safety response. A micro switch is a component; the guard interlock is an application whose behavior depends on its actual circuit and design.

To interpret a rating, compare the conditions stated for the exact switch with the conditions present in the interlock circuit. Relevant details include supply type, voltage, current, load category, and switching duty. If a required detail is missing, or the documented conditions differ, the rating comparison is unresolved rather than a basis for assuming suitability.

## Painting robot guard interlock micro switch contact rating: direct interpretation

A contact rating is conditional, not a universal switching capacity. Under MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, electrical rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions. Read the value together with those conditions. A value separated from its load category or test conditions cannot establish what the contact can switch in a different circuit.

In a painting robot guard interlock, the micro switch may be one component in a larger arrangement. The contact rating concerns the switch contact under its specified electrical conditions. The guard system’s behavior depends on the actual circuit and design. The switch’s presence alone does not establish what happens when a guard moves, whether the equipment stops, or how the overall safety function is achieved.

This distinction matters because a component-level rating and a system-level safety function answer different questions. The first concerns whether a documented switch rating corresponds to stated electrical conditions. The second concerns how the complete guard circuit is designed and what behavior it provides. Do not treat one as evidence for the other.

The practical starting point is therefore to locate the rating and its conditions for the exact switch, then identify the actual conditions of the circuit in which it is used. If either side of that comparison is incomplete, do not fill the gap with a generic rating, a presumed contact arrangement, or an assumption about the robot’s control logic.

## Match the rating conditions to the interlock circuit

Compare the documented rating with the circuit one condition at a time. First establish whether the circuit uses AC or DC, then identify its voltage and current, its load category, and its switching duty. These details must come from the actual circuit and relevant documentation, not from the name of the application or the physical appearance of the switch. No numerical limit can be supplied without the applicable switch evidence.

Keep the stated load type attached to the rating. A rating for one load category does not establish a rating for another. Likewise, a condition stated for AC does not establish equivalence for DC, or the reverse. If the circuit’s supply type or load differs from the rating conditions, the available rating does not resolve that difference. Do not infer that a value transfers simply because the voltage or current appears similar.

Identify the actual contact arrangement and terminal function from the documentation for the exact switch and equipment. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for that switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity from a generic physical layout. The contact arrangement and circuit function are needed to understand what is being compared, but neither can be presumed from the fact that the switch is associated with a guard.

Then compare the stated switching duty with the circuit’s actual operation. If the relevant duty is not documented, or the switch evidence does not cover the circuit conditions, the comparison remains incomplete. A known voltage alone is not enough to establish a match when current, load category, supply type, or switching duty is unknown.

The result of this check is deliberately limited: it can show whether the documented rating conditions match the identified circuit conditions. It cannot supply missing information. If a necessary value or condition cannot be confirmed, record the rating interpretation as unresolved and seek the relevant documentation rather than substituting a generic figure.

## Keep contact rating separate from guard safety behavior

A contact rating is an electrical boundary for a switch under stated conditions. It does not describe, by itself, the safety performance of the complete painting robot guard interlock. That performance depends on the complete circuit and its design, including how the switch is used and how the rest of the system responds. The contact rating alone does not establish those system-level facts.

Do not infer a normal contact state, shutdown response, or control logic from the presence of a guard or a micro switch. The actual contact arrangement, the circuit connections, and the equipment’s function must be documented. A change in contact state, where documented, has an effect determined by the circuit; the name “guard interlock” does not fill in that information.

A rating comparison also cannot confirm that the complete safety function has been designed or validated. Establishing that function is a separate assessment of the complete system, not an extension of the component rating. Where that assessment requires expertise or validation, it must be handled separately by qualified personnel. This article does not provide wiring, installation, repair, or bypass instructions.

Keeping the boundary clear prevents two unsupported conclusions: that a switch rating proves the guard system is safe, and that a particular system response can be assumed from the switch’s application label. Neither follows from the contact rating alone. The rating can be interpreted only within its documented electrical conditions; system behavior requires evidence about the actual designed circuit.

## What can and cannot be concluded

A meaningful rating comparison requires documented conditions for the exact switch and corresponding details for the actual interlock circuit. At minimum, the comparison must address supply type, voltage, current, load category, and switching duty, as well as the documented contact arrangement and terminal identification needed to relate the switch to the circuit. If any necessary detail is absent, a suitability conclusion is not supported.

If the rating conditions and circuit conditions match, the limited conclusion is that the documented electrical rating conditions correspond to the identified circuit conditions. That match does not, by itself, establish compatibility in every other respect, correct installation, the behavior of the complete guard circuit, safe operation, or compliance. Those are separate questions requiring their own applicable evidence and assessment.

If the conditions differ, or if the rating or circuit details cannot be verified, do not treat the rating as proof that the switch is suitable. Do not replace missing evidence with a presumed design, generic physical-layout inference, or an assumed relationship between a guard and its control logic. The appropriate conclusion is that the available information is insufficient to determine suitability from the rating.

In short, the contact rating establishes an electrical limit only within its stated load and test conditions. Interpret it by matching those conditions to the actual circuit and keeping the component-level result separate from the safety behavior of the whole guard interlock. A rating match is not system validation, and an incomplete comparison cannot establish suitability.

A **dryer lint filter interlock micro switch wiring** diagram should be read from the appliance’s documented circuit, not inferred from the switch’s location or appearance. The key is to distinguish two things: the micro switch’s documented contact change under a specified actuator condition, and the dryer control circuit’s response to that change. The first describes the component; the second depends on the appliance design.

This explanation applies only to dryer designs whose appliance documentation identifies a micro switch as part of a lint-filter interlock. It does not establish that every dryer has such an interlock, or that removing a filter necessarily interrupts power or prevents operation. Use the exact switch and equipment documentation to identify connections and permissible load. Do not probe or alter energized appliance wiring.

## What dryer lint filter interlock micro switch wiring represents

A micro switch is an electromechanical component. In a documented lint-filter interlock arrangement, a filter, filter holder, or related mechanism may actuate it. The precise mechanical arrangement must come from the service information for that dryer; the component’s presence or location alone does not establish how the filter moves it.

The switch changes electrical contact state according to its specified actuator condition. That statement describes the switch, not the dryer’s resulting operation. The control circuit may respond to a contact change in a way that is identifiable only from the appliance’s wiring diagram and service information. Do not infer that the change cuts power, prevents a cycle, or causes a particular indication without documentation establishing that behavior.

Some dryers may use a different interlock arrangement or no lint-filter interlock switch. Therefore, this article does not assign a universal wiring pattern to dryers. First establish from the specific appliance documentation that the component is a micro switch used in this role. If the documents do not identify it, its function and wiring remain unresolved.

The useful interpretation is consequently bounded: identify the documented actuator condition, identify the switch contacts and connections as shown for that particular component, and then trace those connections into the appliance circuit. Keep those stages distinct. A switch-level description cannot, on its own, establish the behavior of the wider dryer.

## Separate the micro switch contacts from the dryer control circuit

Read the wiring diagram as a path from the switch terminals to the connected circuit. Do not infer terminal function from physical position, connector shape, or proximity to the filter. The diagram and terminal markings for the exact switch and appliance are the evidence for interpreting a connection.

Use terms such as COM, NO, and NC only if the specific switch documentation identifies that contact arrangement and its reference condition. Those labels do not provide a universal description of every micro switch. A label’s meaning must be read with the documented state reference; do not assume it tells you what happens when the filter is installed or removed unless the appliance information makes that connection explicit.

Then follow the diagram beyond the switch. A contact state is a component-level fact; what that state means to the dryer depends on the connected control circuit. An open contact, for example, does not by itself prove that the dryer is disabled. The circuit may interpret the state in a particular way, but that response cannot be determined from the switch alone.

This two-layer reading prevents an important misinterpretation: equating a switch’s contact change with a particular appliance outcome. Keep the switch’s documented state and the control circuit’s documented response as separate findings. If the service information does not show how the circuit responds, do not supply a behavior by guesswork.

## What to verify before interpreting the wiring

Start with the appliance’s wiring diagram and service information for the exact dryer. Look for the interlock circuit, the switch terminal designations, and the connections shown between the switch and the rest of the control circuit. These records, rather than a generic layout, establish how the documented circuit is arranged.

Next, check the switch diagram and the actuator condition associated with any stated contact behavior. Confirm whether the appliance documentation describes the filter as present, absent, or in another defined position for that condition. A state reference must be clear before using it to interpret a contact description. If the documents do not establish the relevant condition, leave the expected contact state unresolved.

Treat wire colors and connector positions as identifiers only when the appliance documentation confirms them. Neither color nor physical position alone establishes a terminal’s function or the circuit’s purpose. Do not transfer a connection pattern from another dryer or a different switch: the exact switch and equipment information governs.

Finally, compare the documented switch connections with the circuit they enter. The applicable connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load must be established for the exact switch and equipment. If any of those details are missing or conflict, the available information is not enough to interpret the wiring reliably. Do not fill a documentation gap with assumptions about terminal labels, color conventions, contact state, or dryer behavior.

## Why generic continuity or wire-color advice can mislead

A continuity result has meaning only when the switch is isolated, the actuator is in a defined condition, and the expected contact behavior is documented for that switch. Without those conditions, a reading cannot be compared with a supported expectation. This is not a universal instruction to test a dryer circuit; appliance-specific access and isolation requirements still govern.

A click is also limited evidence. It may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not establish electrical continuity, confirm the correct terminals, or show that the dryer control circuit is correct. Mechanical movement and electrical behavior are separate observations.

Likewise, a reading at the switch alone cannot establish that the appliance wiring, control logic, or other dryer components function as intended. If an observed state differs from the documented expectation, that discrepancy narrows what is known, but it does not identify the cause by itself. A problem could involve the mechanical actuation, the switch contacts, terminal identification, wiring, or another part of the equipment. The available documentation and qualified diagnosis determine what can be distinguished; do not declare the whole assembly good or bad from one observation.

Avoid generic continuity prescriptions, universal wire-color claims, and conclusions based only on whether a switch clicks. Interpret any measurement against the exact documented switch state and circuit context, and keep the result limited to the part and condition actually assessed.

## Safety boundary for dryer interlock wiring

Do not probe or alter energized appliance wiring. Before access or testing, follow the dryer’s applicable, appliance-specific deenergization and qualification requirements. This article does not supply an isolation procedure, authorize access to internal wiring, or establish that a particular test can be performed safely.

If the required service information or a safe isolation method is unavailable, leave electrical diagnosis to a qualified technician. Do not use wire color, connector position, or a guessed terminal function as a substitute for documentation. Do not bypass the interlock as a diagnostic method or operating workaround; the evidence provided here does not establish a safe or appropriate bypass procedure.

The decision boundary is straightforward: use documentation for the exact switch and dryer to establish the contact arrangement, actuator reference condition, circuit connections, and documented circuit response. If those facts cannot be established, the wiring interpretation remains uncertain. The **dryer lint filter interlock micro switch wiring** can be understood only within that documented component-and-circuit context, not from a generic diagram or an isolated click or continuity observation.

Some ATM cash-cassette door designs use a micro switch, but a door or ATM symptom does not establish that the switch has failed. **How to test a atm cash cassette door micro switch** depends on the specific mechanism and its documentation: first observe the mechanical actuation path, then test contacts only if the switch can be safely isolated and its terminals and expected states are documented. A click, an isolated continuity result, and the ATM’s response are different kinds of evidence. None alone confirms the condition of the entire door mechanism or ATM circuit.

## How to test a atm cash cassette door micro switch: establish the test boundary

Confirm that the particular cash-cassette door mechanism contains a micro switch before applying this procedure. Do not assume that every ATM uses one, or that a switch found near a door performs a particular control function. The equipment’s design and service documentation determine what component is present and how it is used.

Treat a door indication, an ATM message, or another observed symptom as a starting point, not a diagnosis. A symptom could relate to mechanical movement, the switch or its actuator, terminal identification, wiring, circuit logic, or another equipment fault. Without design-specific documentation, do not infer what the ATM should report when the door is open or closed, which contact state it expects, or how the circuit responds.

Before accessing or isolating an internal component, follow the applicable service procedure and qualification requirements. Do not perform live-circuit testing or access ATM internals without the required procedure and qualification. If those conditions are not met, stop at observations that the permitted procedure allows and refer the work to qualified personnel. A general description of a continuity check is not permission to open or test an ATM.

The connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment govern identification and use. Physical position or appearance alone is not a reliable basis for inferring a terminal’s function or an electrical rating. This procedure therefore cannot supply assumed terminal labels, normal contact states, or load limits. Resolve those details from the applicable documentation before interpreting a measurement.

## Check whether the cassette-door mechanism moves the micro switch actuator

With the equipment in a safe, permitted state, observe the door and linkage in relation to the actuator. Use the design documentation to determine how the mechanism is intended to engage and release it. Do not force the door or actuator, or treat an undocumented movement as a required operating condition.

Record what can be seen: whether the door or linkage appears to reach the actuator, whether it releases it as expected by the documented design, and whether movement is sticking, obstructed, misaligned, or incomplete. These observations describe the mechanical path. They do not establish whether the switch contacts conduct, whether the terminals have been identified correctly, or whether the ATM circuit is functioning.

A click may indicate that a mechanism moved, but it does not prove electrical continuity, correct wiring, or correct ATM operation. Conversely, no audible click by itself does not establish that the contacts have failed. Keep the mechanical observation separate from any later electrical result: a linkage problem can prevent intended actuation even if an isolated switch test is consistent with its documentation, while an audible movement does not settle the contact condition.

If the actuator does not appear to move as intended, or movement is inconsistent, the observation points to the mechanical path for further assessment. It does not by itself identify whether the cause is the door, linkage, actuator, obstruction, or another part of the mechanism. Use the equipment’s service information and qualified inspection to resolve that branch rather than converting it into a switch-contact diagnosis.

## Test isolated micro switch contacts in defined actuator states

A continuity check concerns the isolated switch, not the installed ATM circuit. Perform one only after the applicable procedure has safely isolated the component and the required qualification is in place. Identify the documented contact pair using the exact switch documentation and terminal markings. Do not assume a terminal arrangement, labels such as COM, NO, or NC, or a normal contact state from a generic diagram or the switch’s physical layout.

Define the actuator state for each reading. The relevant documentation must identify which actuator position or reference condition is being tested and what contact behavior is expected there. Measure the documented terminal pair in each specified state and compare the observation only with that documented expectation. A reading without a known terminal pair and defined actuator state has no reliable interpretation here. This article cannot supply a universal expected reading for an unspecified switch.

Keep the conditions attached to the result: which isolated switch was checked, which documented terminals were measured, and which defined actuator state was used. The result applies to that switch under those test conditions. A reading that matches the documented expectation does not prove that installed wiring, connections, ATM logic, door alignment, or another part of the equipment is correct. A reading that differs does not establish failure until terminal identification, isolation, actuator state, and test setup have been confirmed against the applicable documentation.

Do not substitute a continuity reading for a test of the installed ATM circuit or use it to infer the equipment’s safety or behavior. Circuit function and permissible load depend on the exact switch and equipment documentation. If the documentation does not identify the terminals, reference states, or expected behavior needed for a meaningful check, leave the result unresolved and refer it for qualified assessment rather than guessing.

## Separate a switch result from other cash-cassette door faults

Interpret the observations in separate branches. If door or linkage movement does not reach or release the actuator as the design documentation specifies, the evidence concerns the mechanical path. It does not prove the isolated contacts have failed. Further assessment should follow the documented mechanism and be performed within the applicable service and qualification boundary.

If an isolated continuity result differs from the documented expectation, the switch may be implicated, but first confirm that the correct terminal pair was selected, the switch was isolated as required, and the actuator was held in the specified state. A discrepancy is limited to that test setup; it is not, by itself, a complete diagnosis of the ATM or proof of a particular repair.

If the isolated result matches the documented expectation while the ATM symptom remains, the check has not cleared the installed system. Wiring, terminal identification in the equipment, circuit logic, mechanical engagement under installed conditions, or another ATM fault may still account for the symptom. The effect of a contact change depends on the actual equipment design, so do not infer a door response or interlock behavior without its documentation.

The decision boundary is straightforward: mechanical observations address movement; an isolated continuity check addresses documented contacts in defined states; equipment-level behavior requires the applicable ATM service procedure and qualified assessment. A click or a single meter result cannot bridge those categories or establish that the whole assembly is good or bad. For **how to test a atm cash cassette door micro switch**, use only the design-specific evidence available, keep unresolved results unresolved, and stop when safe access or reliable interpretation requires service qualifications or documentation you do not have.

Intermittent continuity readings do not, by themselves, identify a faulty micro switch. Micro switch intermittent continuity testing is meaningful only when the switch is isolated from the surrounding circuit, its terminals and expected contact behavior are known, and the actuator is held in defined states. A changing reading may reflect changing mechanical input, terminal or probe setup, contact behavior, or a fault elsewhere. The test can describe the isolated switch under the conditions checked; it cannot certify the installed circuit or establish the cause of an equipment symptom on its own.

## What intermittent readings can, and cannot, show

A continuity reading is an observation made with a particular test setup and actuator state. If either changes between checks, readings may differ without showing that the switch itself has an internal contact problem. Uncertain terminal identification or probe contact can also make results difficult to interpret. If the switch is still connected to other circuit elements, the reading may reflect the surrounding circuit rather than the isolated switch.

First separate the question being asked: does the isolated switch show the documented contact behavior at a defined actuator state, or does the equipment behave unexpectedly when assembled? These are different observations. A switch-level test addresses only the first. If isolated readings are consistent but the equipment symptom remains, the test has not ruled out wiring or another circuit element.

An intermittent result therefore calls for controlled repetition, not an immediate conclusion. Record the actuator state and the terminals tested for each reading. A single reading cannot establish reliability, identify a cause, or prove that the installed assembly is good or bad. Nor does an isolated continuity result establish that an installed circuit is safe.

## Prepare an isolated, defined test

Do not interpret a reading taken with the switch energized or connected to the surrounding circuit as an isolated-switch diagnosis. Testing must begin at an appropriate safe isolation boundary. If the circuit cannot be safely isolated or the work would require uncertain installed-circuit procedures, stop and have qualified personnel handle that work.

Before measuring, identify the actual terminals from the switch's documentation. Establish the documented reference condition and the contact behavior expected in each relevant actuator state. Do not assume that all micro switches have the same terminal arrangement or that a terminal label alone tells you what the tested switch should do in every state. If the arrangement or expected behavior is not documented, the reading cannot be confidently classified as correct or incorrect from continuity alone.

Keep the test setup consistent. Use the same identified terminal pair and stable probe placement when comparing readings, and note whether the actuator is unactuated or actuated according to the design's documented reference. The result is meaningful only in relation to those defined conditions. If the actuator does not remain in a clearly identifiable state, resolve that uncertainty before treating changing readings as evidence of contact behavior.

## Micro switch intermittent continuity testing: repeat by actuator state

With the switch isolated and its terminal arrangement understood, check the relevant documented states separately. In each state, hold the actuator at the intended position and use the same terminal pair and measurement method. Compare the reading with the expected contact behavior for that state, rather than interpreting an unexplained beep or a value without context.

Repeat the movement between states and compare the readings at the same defined positions. Ask whether each reading consistently corresponds to its documented state. If the result changes while the actuator is supposed to remain in one state, check whether the actuator is actually held there and whether probe contact and terminal identification remain stable. If those conditions are controlled but the state-specific result still varies, the observation is a switch-level inconsistency under the tested conditions. It does not, by itself, establish why the result varies.

Where the switch design documents distinct operating and release events, keep them separate. The operating event is when the contact changes as the actuator moves; the release event is when the contacts return to the unoperated position as the actuator is released. They should not be treated as one point. Compare the contact behavior at defined states around each event only when the design provides enough information to identify those states. Do not invent a travel, force, or adjustment target.

A repeatable sequence makes the result more useful than a single click, beep, or reading. Record the terminal pair, actuator state, and observed result for each check. If the expected state behavior or test condition is unknown, report that limitation rather than assigning a pass or fail.

## Separate contact readings from mechanical and connection causes

Intermittent readings can have different explanations, so check the conditions that produce them before attributing them to internal contacts. Observe whether the external mechanism reaches and releases the actuator consistently. Variation in that motion can change the actuator state during measurement. This observation does not establish a required force or travel, and it does not prove that the switch is defective.

Next, confirm that the same documented terminals are being tested and that probe contact remains stable. A changed terminal pair or inconsistent probe placement can produce a different observation that should not be mistaken for a change in switch behavior. If the terminal arrangement cannot be verified, the contact-state interpretation remains unresolved.

Then compare the isolated readings at the defined states. If they are consistent with the documented behavior, but the equipment still has a symptom, investigate wiring and other circuit elements separately. The isolated result neither tests those elements nor shows how the complete circuit responds. If readings remain inconsistent after the state, terminal pair, and measurement setup are controlled, describe the result as an inconsistent isolated-switch observation under the test conditions. Avoid claiming a particular internal failure without further evidence.

## Interpret the result and know when to stop

A click can indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove continuity, the correct contact state, correct wiring, or suitability for a connected load. Likewise, one beep or one reading cannot establish repeatability. Conclusions should be limited to the recorded terminal pair, defined actuator state, and test setup.

If repeated isolated checks consistently match the documented contact behavior, that supports a narrow conclusion about the switch under those checked conditions. It does not prove that the switch will behave the same in an installed circuit or that the equipment is functioning correctly. If the checks are inconsistent, report what varied and under which defined states; do not infer the whole assembly is faulty from that result alone.

Stop when safe isolation, terminal identity, expected state behavior, or actuator state cannot be established. Ambiguous or unsafe work on the installed circuit belongs with qualified personnel. The useful outcome of micro switch intermittent continuity testing is a bounded record of state-specific isolated readings, not a broader declaration that the switch, wiring, or equipment is good or bad.

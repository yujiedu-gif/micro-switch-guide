A bottling machine end of stroke micro switch maintenance routine should check both the switch and the mechanical path that moves its actuator. The goal is not merely to observe an electrical change: after service, the change must occur at the machine’s intended stroke endpoint, according to the applicable machine and switch documentation. A continuity check by itself cannot establish correct timing.

Keep the work limited to the end-of-stroke switch and its actuation interface. Construction, terminal arrangement, permitted actuator travel, and adjustment criteria are not universal. Use the installed switch documentation and the machine’s service procedure for those details. If the required procedure or acceptance criteria are unavailable, do not guess; pause and seek the appropriate service guidance.

## What bottling machine end of stroke micro switch maintenance covers

An end-of-stroke micro switch detects a defined motion endpoint through a machine part or linkage that moves the switch actuator. The switch is one component in that arrangement. The host machine’s control logic determines what the resulting electrical change does, so the presence of a switch does not by itself establish a particular shutdown, alarm, or interlock behavior.

This checklist covers the switch, its mounting, its actuator, the mechanical route by which the machine moves it, and accessible electrical connections. It does not cover general bottling-machine maintenance or faults elsewhere in the equipment. If the expected machine response is absent, that observation alone does not identify whether the cause is the switch, the actuator path, wiring, or another part of the control system.

Use documentation for the installed switch and machine to identify the intended endpoint, the permitted actuator movement, connection details, cleaning limits, and any specified tolerance. Designs differ; do not infer these details from the switch’s appearance or from another machine. Record the starting condition and any observed change so that post-service checks can be compared with the approved criteria.

## Isolate the machine and inspect the actuation path

Before access, follow the machine’s applicable isolation procedure and address stored energy as required by that procedure. Do not begin inspection or cleaning until the prescribed conditions for access have been met. If isolation requirements are unclear, stop and obtain qualified service guidance rather than improvising.

Trace the mechanical actuation path from the machine stop or moving feature to the switch plunger or lever. Inspect the mounting for looseness and check whether the moving feature meets the actuator along the intended path. Look for visible misalignment, insufficient clearance, or contact that appears to bind. A missed or inconsistent actuation can have a mechanical cause; it does not prove that the internal switch contacts have failed.

Inspect the actuator and nearby parts for wear, cracking, deformation, corrosion, contamination, or other visible damage. Distinguish a positioning problem from a damaged or worn actuator where observation allows: a loose mount or displaced approach may indicate an alignment issue, while visible deterioration or binding at the actuator calls for closer assessment against the documentation. Do not force the actuator to test its limits. Pretravel is the movement from the free position to the operating position; overtravel is movement from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. Do not exceed the installed design’s permitted travel.

If the path cannot be inspected without moving equipment, defeating a guard, or departing from the approved access procedure, stop. The inspection is limited to conditions that can be checked under the machine’s service instructions.

## Clean the switch and check its connections within stated limits

Clean only by methods allowed by the installed switch documentation and the machine’s service procedure. Keep liquid and debris from entering the switch housing or openings. Do not apply an unapproved solvent, abrasive method, or cleaning process. If contamination cannot be removed within the allowed method, record it and seek the prescribed service decision rather than escalating the cleaning method on your own.

Inspect accessible wiring and terminals for visible damage or looseness, following the applicable procedure. Do not assume a universal terminal layout, wire assignment, or tightening requirement. Confirm terminal identification and any connection requirements from the documentation for the installed switch and machine. If a connection is damaged or its correct arrangement cannot be confirmed, leave the unresolved connection untouched and escalate the issue.

Keep observations specific: note where contamination appears, whether mounting or a connection is loose, and whether visible corrosion or damage is present. A visual inspection cannot establish electrical performance, and cleaning or tightening alone does not prove that the switch will actuate at the required point. Those questions require the approved post-service checks.

## Verify bottling machine end of stroke micro switch operation after maintenance

After inspection or service, use the approved test procedure to confirm that the electrical state changes consistently as the actuator operates. Follow the documented method, reference state, and acceptance criteria; do not substitute an assumed terminal arrangement or an informal click test. A click can indicate movement, but it does not establish electrical switching or correct timing.

Then verify the switching point against the machine’s intended stroke endpoint and the tolerance specified for the installed arrangement. Observe the relationship between the machine’s motion and the actuator’s operation using the approved procedure. A switch that changes electrical state may still operate too early or too late for the intended endpoint. Conversely, a timing mismatch does not by itself prove an internal contact failure: mounting, alignment, actuator condition, or another part of the actuation path may be involved.

Do not treat a continuity check alone as proof that the bottling machine reaches or detects its intended endpoint correctly. Electrical state change and mechanical timing are distinct checks, and the conclusion must be based on both where the approved procedure requires them. If either result is inconsistent or outside the documented acceptance criteria, do not declare the maintenance complete.

After the prescribed verification, restore guards and safety functions and complete the machine’s required operational check. Do not bypass or defeat a safety function to obtain a test result. Record the test method, observed switching behavior, endpoint relationship, and result against the applicable criteria. If the procedure does not specify how to perform or assess a check, pause for service guidance instead of inventing a threshold.

## When inspection is not enough

Stop and escalate if the actuator is damaged, operation is intermittent, the actuation path binds, or the documented operating point cannot be achieved. Also stop if required instructions, terminal identification, or acceptance criteria are unavailable. Do not compensate with an undocumented adjustment or improvised repair.

Use the machine and switch documentation for replacement criteria, configuration, and any adjustment procedure. A replacement decision cannot be made from the switch’s general appearance or an isolated electrical result alone. The installed arrangement and its documented requirements govern.

Record recurring contamination, corrosion, looseness, or mechanical wear for appropriate maintenance follow-up. A recurrence may warrant attention to the surrounding actuation path, but it does not establish a specific cause without further evidence. Close the task only when the approved procedure confirms consistent electrical switching at the intended stroke endpoint and the required post-service checks are complete. If those conditions cannot be confirmed, leave the issue unresolved and refer it for qualified assessment.

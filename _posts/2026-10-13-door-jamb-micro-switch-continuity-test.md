A door jamb micro switch continuity test can show whether a documented contact path changes state when the isolated switch actuator moves between specified reference conditions. It cannot, by itself, show that the door mechanism, external wiring, or control circuit works correctly. First confirm that the component is an electromechanical micro switch; not every door-jamb switch or sensor uses one.

A micro switch responds to mechanical actuator movement by changing an electrical contact state. For a useful continuity check, the switch must be separated from external power and circuitry as required by its documentation, and the meter must be connected to terminals whose functions are documented for that exact switch. The expected reading depends on the contact arrangement and actuator state specified for the component. Do not infer either from terminal position or from how the door appears to move.

## What a door jamb micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity test assesses an electrical path through the isolated switch in a defined condition. Comparing readings across documented actuator states can indicate whether the tested contact path changes state as specified. That is a narrow component-level observation, not a complete diagnosis of the door-jamb system.

Before testing, confirm that the component is a micro switch. A door-jamb component may have another design, and this procedure does not establish that every door system uses a micro switch. If the component is a micro switch, its own documentation must identify which terminals form the contact path and what behavior is expected at each reference state.

The test does not establish whether the switch is correctly installed, whether the door mechanism moves it through the intended range, or whether the surrounding circuit responds as intended. A reading taken while the switch remains connected to external circuitry may include paths beyond the switch, so it cannot be interpreted as a component-only result. Likewise, a single reading without a defined actuator state offers no sound comparison with documented behavior.

Treat the test as evidence about one isolated contact path under stated conditions. Keep that boundary in view when recording results or deciding what they mean. An unexpected result may justify checking the test setup and the switch documentation, but it does not alone identify a cause elsewhere in the equipment.

## Safety and isolation before the door jamb micro switch continuity test

Do not measure continuity on an energized circuit. Before placing meter probes, use the applicable procedure to isolate the relevant power and disconnect the switch from external circuitry as required by the switch and equipment documentation. If safe isolation or disconnection is outside your qualifications or the documented procedure, stop and refer that work to a qualified person.

The purpose of isolation is both safety and interpretability. With external wiring still connected, a meter may encounter an electrical path through other components. In that situation, an indication cannot reliably be attributed to the micro switch alone. Disconnecting terminals without first establishing the required isolation is not a substitute for a safe procedure.

Once the switch has been safely disconnected as required, identify and record which wires or connections were removed before testing. This record helps keep the component test separate from any later wiring work. Do not treat the physical position of a terminal, a wire color, or the door's apparent state as proof of terminal function or circuit role. Use the exact switch and equipment documentation to identify connections and permissible test conditions.

This procedure addresses a component-level continuity check only. It does not provide steps for diagnosing or repairing the complete door-jamb circuit, and an isolated meter result is not a safety assessment of the equipment. Keep work within the applicable isolation and qualification requirements.

## Identify the documented terminals and actuator reference states

Use the switch's own connection diagram and terminal markings to identify the contact pair to test. Do not assume that the switch has COM, NO, or NC terminals. If those names are documented, use them only as defined for that particular contact arrangement and reference condition. Do not infer terminal function from the physical layout alone.

The connection diagram, terminal identification, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment determine what can be tested and how results apply. A general description of a micro switch cannot supply those details. If the documentation does not identify a contact pair or expected state, the correct probe placement or expected indication remains unresolved; do not guess.

Also establish the actuator reference states specified in the documentation. These may distinguish a released condition from an operated condition, but do not assume that an open door or closed door corresponds to either one. The surrounding linkage determines how door movement reaches the actuator, and that relationship must not be inferred from the component test alone.

If the design documents distinct operating and release events, treat them as separate reference conditions rather than interchangeable labels. Move the actuator only within its documented permitted range. A click may indicate mechanical movement, but does not establish which contact path changed or whether electrical continuity is present. Clear terminal and state definitions make the readings comparable; without them, a meter result has no supported expected value.

## Perform the isolated continuity check and compare states

After safe isolation and disconnection as required, set up the meter for a continuity measurement according to its instructions. Place the probes across the documented contact pair, not across terminals selected by appearance or assumption. Keep the switch isolated from external circuitry throughout the measurement so the indication concerns the intended contact path.

Record the actuator condition for each reading. For example, test the documented released reference state and the documented operated reference state only if those are specified for the switch. Move the actuator gently through the permitted motion, and do not force it beyond the documented range. Take and record a reading at each stated reference condition rather than relying on a momentary indication during movement.

Compare the recorded readings with the documented contact behavior for that exact switch. A continuity indication in one state and a different indication in another may support that the tested path changes state, but only if the terminal pair and states match the documentation. No universal reading pattern can be assigned to all door-jamb micro switches: contact arrangements and specified reference states must come from the component's documentation.

If the documentation specifies a method or test conditions, follow them. Insulation resistance, for example, is evaluated between specified electrically isolated points using the applicable test method and conditions; that is not the same as an ordinary continuity check. Do not substitute one test's interpretation for the other.

Keep the result narrow: it describes the measured path, at the tested terminals, in the recorded actuator state, under the isolation and measurement conditions used. It does not establish how the switch behaves when installed or how the door system responds.

## Interpret readings without overdiagnosing the door-jamb circuit

A state-dependent change that matches the documented behavior may support that the tested contact path changes state in the tested conditions. A click alone does not prove continuity, correct terminal identification, or correct wiring. Similarly, a reading that appears plausible in one state does not verify the other state or the full switch assembly.

If there is no change, an unstable indication, or a result that differs from the documentation, first verify that the switch was isolated and disconnected as required, the probes are on the documented contact pair, and the actuator is at the stated reference condition. Confirm that actuator movement stayed within the permitted range and that the meter method matches the applicable instructions. These checks address possible test ambiguity; they do not prove that any one cause is responsible.

If the result remains unexpected, the available evidence does not by itself establish whether the cause is an internal contact problem, a mechanical input or alignment issue, incorrect terminal identification, or another condition. Avoid converting an isolated reading into a diagnosis of external wiring or control logic. Those parts of the door-jamb system are outside this component test.

A continuity result also cannot verify installed wiring, the door mechanism's alignment, control logic, or overall equipment safety. The practical conclusion is limited to the documented contact path and reference state that were actually tested. Further evaluation of the installed system requires its own applicable documentation and qualified procedures.

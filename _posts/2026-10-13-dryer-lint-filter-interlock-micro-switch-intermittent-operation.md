Intermittent behavior in a dryer lint-filter interlock does not, by itself, identify a failed switch. The **dryer lint filter interlock micro switch intermittent operation** question is best approached by tracing the mechanical input, then separating the switch from its connections and the surrounding dryer circuit. Only some dryer designs use a micro switch in this interlock arrangement, so the exact appliance documentation must establish whether it applies.

A micro switch is an electromechanical component, not the complete interlock or dryer control system. Filter seating, a linkage, actuator movement, contact behavior, terminals, wiring, and other circuit elements are distinct parts of the possible signal path. A symptom can originate at more than one point, and observations should narrow the possibilities without being treated as proof of a single cause.

## dryer lint filter interlock micro switch intermittent operation

First confirm from the dryer’s documentation that its lint-filter interlock includes a micro switch and identify the relevant connection diagram and terminal markings. Do not infer the arrangement from the appliance category, the shape of a component, or the fact that the dryer behaves differently when a filter is removed or reseated. Other designs may use a different mechanism or control arrangement.

For a documented design, intermittent operation can have several broad explanations: the filter may not consistently reach its intended seated position; a related linkage may not consistently move the switch actuator; the switch’s contacts may behave intermittently; a terminal or wire connection may be unreliable; or the fault may be elsewhere in the control circuit. These are fault domains, not conclusions. The reported symptom alone does not distinguish among them.

Keep the observation tied to what is actually known. A change in dryer behavior after filter movement shows a relationship in time, but it does not prove that the switch is defective or that the filter itself is the cause. Likewise, a switch click, if one is heard, indicates at most that a mechanism moved. It does not establish continuity, correct wiring, the control system’s response, or the condition of the complete interlock.

The diagnostic goal is therefore limited: establish whether the documented mechanical path appears to move consistently, distinguish that observation from a state-defined check of the isolated switch, and avoid attributing installed-circuit behavior to the switch without evidence. If the appliance documentation does not identify the arrangement or safe test conditions, stop short of internal testing and use qualified service.

## Follow the lint-filter interlock’s mechanical path to the micro switch

Begin with the documented mechanical chain, not with an assumption that the switch itself is at fault. Where the design supports this arrangement, the chain may include the filter reaching its intended seating position, a lever or other linkage responding to that position, and the linkage moving the micro switch actuator. The exact parts and their relationship must be confirmed for the dryer in question; this description does not assert that every dryer has such a chain.

Treat each part as a separate observation point. Filter seating is not the same as linkage movement, and linkage movement is not the same as actuator movement. If access and observation are available without opening energized equipment or defeating the interlock, note whether the documented parts appear to move consistently when the filter is placed as directed by the appliance documentation. A visible difference between repeated, ordinary placements may suggest that the mechanical input is inconsistent, but it does not establish why or prove an internal switch fault.

The relevant question is whether the actuator receives the movement the documented design calls for. A filter that appears seated does not necessarily demonstrate that every intervening part moved as intended. Conversely, observing actuator movement does not prove that the electrical contacts changed state reliably. Keep those conclusions separate.

Do not infer which contact state is normal, whether the interlock should permit operation, or what control response should follow from filter position unless the exact equipment documentation establishes it. A contact arrangement and the dryer’s control logic are design-specific. Without that information, the mechanical observation can describe movement, but not the correct electrical result.

## Separate actuator trouble from micro switch and connection faults

Once the documented mechanical path is understood, compare consistent mechanical input with the intermittent symptom. If filter seating or actuator movement varies, inconsistent input remains a possible explanation. If movement appears repeatable while the symptom persists, that observation may shift attention toward switch behavior, terminals, wiring, or the surrounding circuit. Neither pattern proves a particular fault: visible movement does not verify contact behavior, and repeatable movement does not rule out a problem elsewhere.

Keep four fault paths distinct. First, the mechanical input may not move the actuator consistently. Second, the switch may not produce consistent contact behavior under the relevant actuator conditions. Third, a terminal or wire connection may interrupt the signal even if the switch operates. Fourth, another part of the dryer circuit or control system may account for the symptom. These paths require different evidence; an appliance symptom alone cannot localize the cause.

A click is not an electrical test. It cannot show that the contacts have continuity, that the correct terminals are connected, or that the dryer’s control circuit receives or interprets the signal as intended. Similarly, an apparent change in appliance behavior after filter movement does not demonstrate that the switch is the cause. The switch is one component in a larger system.

For terminal identification and circuit interpretation, use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal function from physical layout or apply a generic wiring assumption. If those details are missing, unclear, or inconsistent with the observed equipment, the installed circuit cannot be responsibly interpreted from appearance alone. Leave that distinction to qualified service rather than trying different connections.

## What an isolated micro switch continuity check can establish

A continuity reading is meaningful only when its conditions are defined. For a switch check, the component must be isolated from the circuit, and the actuator condition being checked must be specified. The expected result also depends on the documented contact arrangement and reference state for that switch. Without a supported diagram and defined states, there is no sound basis for claiming which terminals should show continuity in a particular position.

If the applicable documentation provides those conditions and a safe procedure, an isolated check can report whether continuity is present between the specified terminals in the tested actuator state. Repeating the check across the documented states may provide evidence about the switch’s behavior during that test. The finding applies to the isolated component, the measured terminals, the tested actuator positions, and the measurement conditions; it is not a general verdict on the dryer.

An isolated reading cannot establish that the filter consistently actuates the switch in normal use. It cannot verify the appliance wiring, terminal connections under installed conditions, control logic, or other components in the dryer. A passing result in a limited test therefore does not prove that the intermittent symptom has been resolved or that the complete interlock assembly is sound. A differing or unstable result may justify further assessment, but it does not by itself identify whether the cause is internal contact behavior, test setup, or another condition not evaluated.

Do not improvise a test by probing an installed, energized circuit or by guessing terminal functions. If isolation, actuator states, expected contact behavior, or safe access are not established in the equipment documentation, the evidence is insufficient for a reader-directed continuity procedure. Have a qualified service professional assess the appliance using the applicable documentation and safe procedures.

## When intermittent dryer interlock symptoms need qualified service

Stop before bypassing the interlock or conducting energized internal tests. This diagnostic path does not require defeating the interlock, altering wiring, or operating the dryer with covers or components removed. No observation described here proves that such operation is safe or appropriate.

Qualified service is the appropriate boundary when the exact interlock design is unknown, internal access is needed, the circuit cannot be isolated safely, terminal identification is uncertain, or a documented test procedure is unavailable. Escalate as well when mechanical movement appears consistent but intermittent behavior continues, or when the symptom could come from a connection or another part of the control circuit. Those conditions call for diagnosis beyond what an external mechanical observation or isolated switch reading can establish.

The useful conclusion is narrow: trace the documented path from filter seating through any linkage to the actuator; treat movement, contact behavior, connections, and the wider circuit as separate questions; and interpret a continuity result only for the isolated switch and defined test state. For **dryer lint filter interlock micro switch intermittent operation**, the evidence should guide the next diagnostic boundary, not be stretched into a claim that one component is proven faulty.

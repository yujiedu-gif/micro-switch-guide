## What “dxp topworx” does and does not establish

A phrase entered into a search field is a query, not a technical definition. It may be incomplete, mistyped, ambiguous, or used in more than one context. Without an eligible source that reproduces and defines the exact identifier, the phrase does not establish that it names a micro switch, any other device, a manufacturer, or a commercial product family. It also does not establish that the words belong together as a formal identifier.

Keep that boundary explicit when reviewing material. A page that contains only one part of the phrase, a near match, or a similar-looking label is not evidence that it refers to the same item. Nor does a result’s placement near technical material demonstrate a connection. The match must be exact, and the source must make clear what the identifier denotes.

Even if a source confirms an identity, do not extend that confirmation beyond the source’s stated scope. In the absence of exact-identifier evidence, leave the following unresolved:

- Whether the phrase identifies a particular device or any defined item.
- Any model identity, manufacturer, or product-family relationship.
- Electrical ratings, operating limits, load type, or AC/DC details.
- Dimensions, construction, terminal arrangement, or contact functions.
- Compatibility, replacement status, intended application, or safety suitability.
- Certification, approval, or compliance status.

These are separate facts, not automatic consequences of recognizing a name. A document may verify one field while leaving others unanswered. For example, an exact match that defines an identifier does not, on that basis alone, confirm ratings, terminals, compatibility, or certification. Report only what the evidence actually states, and label the rest unresolved rather than completing the description from expectation.

## Which claims must stay unconfirmed

Each requires evidence that both addresses the particular fact and applies to the exact identifier and scope in question. A source can be relevant to one field without proving another.

This general relationship does not establish that an item associated with “dxp topworx” has a changeover contact, bears those labels, or uses a particular terminal arrangement. Those details remain unconfirmed unless exact-device documentation states them.

Likewise, evidence for a related identifier does not establish facts for this phrase. Similar spelling, shared words, appearance, or an assumed relationship are not substitutes for an explicit link in an eligible source. Even where a link is stated, apply only the claims and variants the source actually covers. Do not convert evidence about one version into a universal statement about all versions.

A careful verification note can separate findings into three categories: **confirmed**, for claims directly supported within the source’s stated scope; **limited**, for claims supported only under a stated version, variant, or condition; and **unresolved**, for fields with no adequate evidence. This prevents a confirmed identifier from being mistaken for confirmation of every technical attribute. It also makes clear when further documentation would be needed without guessing what that documentation might say.

The practical conclusion is narrow: “this topic” should remain an unverified phrase until a suitable noncommercial source reproduces it exactly, defines what it identifies, and states the applicable scope and version or variant limits. Until then, identity and associated technical claims remain unresolved.

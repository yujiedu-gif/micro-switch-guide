## forklift mast guard interlock micro switch NO and NC terminals

COM, NO, and NC are useful labels only when the switch’s documented contact arrangement supports them. COM means common. NO and NC describe the contact relationship to that common terminal at a specified reference condition. That reference condition must come from the switch’s documentation; the labels are not a universal statement about every physical switch or terminal layout.

In a documented arrangement, NO identifies the contact that is open relative to COM at the stated reference condition, and NC identifies the contact that is closed relative to COM at that condition. “Open” and “closed” describe the contact state, not an unconditional promise about current flow in an installed circuit. Wiring, circuit design, and the condition being measured matter. A label alone does not establish that a terminal is connected as expected in a particular forklift.

The reference condition is essential when interpreting a reading. A switch actuator may be in one position at rest and another after an external input moves it. If documentation defines NO and NC relative to the unactuated state, the labels refer to that stated state, not automatically to a guard-up condition, guard-down condition, or equipment-on condition. Do not substitute an assumed mast guard position for the switch’s documented reference condition.

Terminal identification is therefore a documentation question, not a physical-layout guessing exercise. Use the connection diagram and terminal markings for the exact switch, together with the applicable equipment circuit documentation. Confirm that the documented circuit function and permissible load apply to that switch and equipment. A generic terminal arrangement cannot establish the identity or role of an undocumented terminal, and a switch’s labels do not independently define the interlock’s control logic.

## How to verify NO and NC contact states

Verify a contact state only with the switch isolated from the circuit and under an applicable safe, documented procedure. The expected result must come from the exact switch’s terminal documentation and its stated reference condition. Do not treat an installed-circuit reading as equivalent to an isolated switch check: other circuit paths and the equipment’s arrangement are outside what that isolated contact test establishes.

First, use the switch’s markings and connection documentation to identify the terminals and determine the reference actuator state. If the documentation does not establish the terminal arrangement or expected behavior, there is no supported basis for assigning a terminal by appearance or for predicting a continuity result. Use the applicable equipment documentation as well where it is needed to identify the switch or its circuit role.

With the switch isolated, check continuity between the documented terminals in the defined reference actuator state. Record what state the actuator is in and compare the result with the documented expectation for that state. Then, only if the documentation and safe procedure define another actuator state for checking, observe the contact behavior in that state and compare it with the stated expectation. Do not assume that moving the actuator must produce a particular result unless the switch documentation says so.

This check concerns the isolated switch and the defined states tested. A result that agrees with the documentation supports only the conclusion that the measured contact behavior matched the documented expectation under those conditions. It does not prove the installed wiring is correct, that the circuit is safe, that the mechanical input works correctly, or that the forklift interlock as a whole functions as intended.

Likewise, a result that differs from expectation does not by itself identify the cause. The terminal identification, reference state, actuator movement, switch contact, or test setup may need further review. Do not use a click as a substitute for the continuity check, and do not treat a continuity reading as proof that the switch is suitable for the installed load. Follow the applicable deenergization and qualification requirements; when the safe boundary or expected result is unclear, qualified service and the relevant documentation are necessary.

## When terminal readings do not explain interlock behavior

A terminal reading addresses a limited question: what contact behavior was observed between identified terminals, in a defined actuator state, while the switch was isolated? It does not independently diagnose the mast guard interlock. Keep possible causes separate rather than collapsing them into a single switch fault: the mechanical input may not move the actuator as intended; the internal contact behavior may differ from documentation; the terminals may have been misidentified; wiring may differ from the diagram; or the issue may be elsewhere in the equipment.

Use the observation that is relevant to the question. If the actuator state is uncertain, the switch documentation must define the state before the contact reading can be interpreted. If the terminal identity or expected continuity is unclear, the exact switch documentation is needed. If the isolated contact result agrees with the documented expectation but the installed interlock does not behave as expected, that result does not establish a wiring or system diagnosis. The equipment circuit and mechanical arrangement must be evaluated within the applicable service procedure.

Do not infer shutdown, enable, alarm, or other equipment behavior from NO and NC labels alone. Those labels describe contact state at a specified reference condition, not the control logic or the outcome of an installed circuit. The available information does not establish a universal forklift mast guard response or a universal switch arrangement.

The practical boundary is straightforward: identify terminals from the exact switch documentation, interpret NO and NC at its documented reference condition, and compare isolated continuity results only with documented expectations for defined actuator states. If any of those conditions is unknown, the reading cannot settle the question. The **forklift mast guard interlock micro switch NO and NC terminals** identify documented contact relationships; they do not, on their own, explain or verify the complete interlock system.

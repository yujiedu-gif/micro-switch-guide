Creepage distance is one part of an electromechanical micro switch’s insulation information, not a stand-alone verdict on whether the switch is suitable. Micro switch creepage distance selection depends on what conductive parts are being separated, the applicable insulation-coordination requirements, and the conditions represented by the documentation. A distance value without that context cannot establish suitability for a particular design.

Keep creepage distance separate from clearance. Creepage follows an insulating surface; clearance passes through air. Both may matter to insulation assessment, but one is not a substitute for the other. The practical task is to identify what the switch documentation actually says about spacing, then determine how that evidence fits the complete equipment design. If a required detail is absent or unclear, treat it as unresolved rather than filling the gap with an assumed value or universal rule.

## What creepage distance means for a micro switch

Creepage distance is the path along an insulating surface between relevant conductive parts. Clearance is the shortest path through air between those parts. The distinction is about the route being considered: a surface path for creepage, an air path for clearance. A figure for one does not describe the other.

For a micro switch, the relevant conductive parts must be identified from the particular device’s documentation. Do not assume a terminal arrangement or infer which parts a spacing figure covers from the component name alone. A generic changeover contact may use terminals labeled common, normally closed, and normally open, but the exact device marking or schematic controls. Terminal labels identify contact structure; they do not by themselves establish the insulation spacing between terminals or between terminals and other conductive parts.

The insulation question concerns separation through or along insulating regions between the parts under consideration. A drawing, specification, or other device information may describe particular spacing, barriers, or configurations, but its meaning is limited to what it documents. If the evidence does not identify the endpoints, the path type, or the configuration, it cannot safely be treated as a confirmed creepage value.

Pretravel is a separate mechanical parameter, defined as the distance or angle through which the actuator moves from free position to operating position. It does not describe an insulation path. Likewise, insulation resistance is a test interpretation rather than a creepage-distance measurement: it is evaluated between specified electrically isolated points using the applicable test method and conditions. These terms answer different questions and should not be used as substitutes for spacing evidence.

## Micro switch creepage distance selection depends on conditions

A distance value cannot be judged in isolation. The applicable insulation-coordination requirements and design rules determine which conditions matter and how spacing evidence is interpreted. These may include working voltage, pollution degree, material group, and insulation type. The relevant requirements must be identified for the intended design rather than replaced with a universal distance threshold.

The governing standard or design rules also define the scope in which a value or requirement applies. A number in a document may describe a particular configuration, test, or design condition. Without knowing that scope, a reader cannot conclude that the value applies to a different installation or operating environment. Do not transfer a figure between distinct path types, insulation categories, or conditions unless the applicable requirements and documentation support that interpretation.

This is why a component listing that gives a distance, without the associated conditions and endpoints, may be insufficient for selection. It may leave unresolved what parts are separated, whether the value concerns a surface path or an air path, and what insulation assumptions apply. The absence of a universal threshold is not a gap to fill by estimation; it is a reason to consult the governing requirements for the particular design.

Use the requirements applicable to the intended equipment and operating conditions to identify what evidence is needed. Then compare that need with the actual switch documentation. If the required conditions or the meaning of the stated value are not clear, the selection decision remains open. A distance figure alone does not establish that the switch meets the complete design’s insulation needs.

## Check what the micro switch documentation actually covers

Start by confirming which conductive parts the spacing information concerns. Look for documentation that identifies the relevant terminals or other conductive parts and explains the insulating barrier or surface path involved. Verify whether the information is about creepage, clearance, or another insulation characteristic. Do not infer these details from a generic terminal layout or from a distance value presented without its context.

Next, check whether the described configuration matches the one under consideration. The documentation must be read within its stated mounting and operating conditions. If the information applies to a particular configuration, do not assume it also covers another arrangement. Where terminal markings, a schematic, or a spacing description are supplied, use the exact device information to interpret them; generic conventions do not override the specific documentation.

A useful evidence check is therefore specific: identify the parts, identify the path, identify the documented configuration, and identify the conditions and applicable rules used to interpret the information. Keep those items together. A spacing statement whose endpoints or conditions are missing may not answer the design question, even if it appears precise.

If the documentation is missing, ambiguous, or does not match the intended conditions, record the spacing evidence as unresolved. Do not infer a creepage distance from the switch’s overall dimensions, terminal arrangement, or a different device’s information. Nor should a separate insulation-resistance statement be taken as proof of a particular creepage path: insulation resistance is evaluated between specified electrically isolated points using its applicable test method and conditions, which is a different characterization.

This verification path does not require assuming a particular micro switch model or contact arrangement. It asks whether the exact device information addresses the insulation question the applicable design rules require. Where it does not, additional authoritative documentation or qualified review may be needed before the component-level question can be resolved.

## Keep component selection separate from equipment-level insulation review

A micro switch’s documented spacing characteristics are one input to an insulation assessment. They do not, on their own, prove that the installed equipment meets its applicable insulation requirements. The surrounding design has its own conductive parts, insulating structures, mounting arrangement, and operating conditions, all of which must be considered under the requirements that apply to the complete assembly.

Keep the boundary clear: component documentation can support conclusions only about the component features and conditions it actually covers. It cannot establish an equipment-level result when other parts of the assembly or relevant design conditions remain unreviewed. A documented creepage value is therefore evidence to use in the broader assessment, not a substitute for that assessment.

For a sound decision, first identify the applicable insulation-coordination requirements for the complete design. Then determine what component spacing evidence those requirements call for, check whether the particular switch documentation supplies it under matching conditions, and leave any undocumented point unresolved. A qualified review may be needed to evaluate the complete design and interpret requirements where the evidence or application is uncertain.

In short, micro switch creepage distance selection should follow documented surface-path evidence and the applicable conditions, while keeping clearance and equipment-level insulation review distinct. No single spacing value, without its scope and design context, is a universal proof of suitability.

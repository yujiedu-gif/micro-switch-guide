A dryer lint filter interlock micro switch troubleshooting process should begin by confirming that the particular dryer uses this arrangement and that filter placement mechanically moves the switch actuator. A filter-related symptom alone does not establish that the switch contacts have failed: poor seating, a linkage problem, wiring, or another dryer fault may produce a similar result.

Disconnect power before accessible inspection or an isolated test. Do not bypass the interlock. If the switch or its wiring is hazardous or inaccessible, leave the work to a qualified technician. The checks below distinguish what can be observed from what remains unproven; they do not assume a universal dryer design or contact state.

## Start by confirming the dryer uses a lint-filter micro switch

A micro switch is an electromechanical component, not a general name for every dryer interlock. A miniature snap-action switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement and is commonly used for position or limit detection. That general role does not establish that a particular dryer uses a switch at the lint filter, or what the dryer does when the contact changes.

First consult service information for the exact dryer configuration. Confirm whether it identifies a micro switch in the lint-filter mechanism and shows how the filter, linkage, or other moving part reaches the actuator. Some designs may use a different arrangement. Do not infer a contact state, start permission, warning behavior, or shutdown response from the mere presence of a switch.

This applicability check matters because the filter and linkage are upstream of the switch. If the filter does not sit in its intended position, the mechanism may not move the actuator even when the switch itself is functioning. Conversely, visible actuator movement does not by itself show that the electrical contact changes as documented. Keep those questions separate as you proceed.

## Dryer lint filter interlock micro switch troubleshooting: check the mechanical path first

With power disconnected, inspect the lint filter for visible debris and check whether it sits correctly in its intended position. Use the dryer’s documentation for the correct seating arrangement rather than forcing a fit. Look, where safely accessible, for the visible actuator or linkage and whether filter placement appears to move it as the documented mechanism describes.

Do not force, bend, or alter the filter, linkage, lever, or actuator. A lack of movement can arise before the switch: the filter may be misplaced, or a linkage may be obstructed, misaligned, or not reaching the actuator. These are possibilities to distinguish through observation, not diagnoses established by a symptom alone.

If the filter appears seated but the actuator does not move, record that observation and stop short of calling the switch electrically faulty. The relevant next question is whether the documented mechanism should move the actuator under that condition. If the design or access path is unclear, consult qualified service information or a technician rather than probing farther.

If the actuator does move, note whether movement is consistent and whether the mechanism returns as documented. Do not treat movement as proof of correct operation. The switch’s operating position and release position are distinct events; movement through part of the actuator’s travel does not establish that either event occurred at the intended point.

## Interpret a click without treating it as an electrical diagnosis

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism. It does not prove continuity, contact condition, correct terminal identification, wiring integrity, or suitability for the dryer’s circuit. A click can therefore narrow the observation to mechanical movement without resolving whether the installed interlock functions correctly.

When the exact switch documentation provides operating and release information, compare observed actuator movement with those documented events. Do not substitute an assumed travel distance or force. The specification terminology distinguishes free position, operating position, actuating force, releasing force, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. In that terminology, pretravel runs from free position to operating position, while overtravel runs from operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These terms describe distinct points or portions of movement; they do not supply values for an unidentified switch.

If movement is absent, inconsistent, or apparently obstructed, return to the filter and linkage path before attributing the symptom to internal contacts. If movement appears normal but an electrical symptom remains, that observation still does not identify the cause. The contact state, terminal arrangement, wiring, and control circuit require their own documented checks.

## Use continuity checks only with documented terminals and states

Consider an isolated continuity check only when the switch can be safely disconnected and the exact terminal arrangement and expected states are documented. Disconnect power before accessible inspection or isolated testing, and do not test live wiring. If safe isolation or access is uncertain, stop and use a qualified technician.

Use the switch’s connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity from physical layout. Do not assume that terminals are COM, NO, or NC, or assign an expected open or closed reading, unless the exact arrangement and reference state are documented. Define the actuator state for each observation and follow the documented test condition; an unspecified reading has no reliable interpretation here.

A result that differs from the documented expectation may indicate a switch or contact issue, but only after the terminals, actuator state, isolation, and test setup are verified. A result that matches expectations establishes only the isolated observation under that defined condition. It does not prove correct wiring, installed-circuit operation, or dryer function. Never bypass the interlock as a diagnostic shortcut.

## Separate switch findings from wiring and dryer faults

Localize the finding to the evidence. If the filter does not seat or the documented mechanism does not move the actuator, investigate that mechanical path rather than labeling the switch defective. If movement occurs but the isolated contact observation differs from the documented expectation, first verify terminal identification, actuator state, and test setup before treating the switch mechanism or contacts as the likely problem.

If the switch behaves as documented in isolation, the dryer symptom may still involve a connector, wiring, control-circuit behavior, or another fault. An isolated result does not establish that those parts are sound, and the presence of the switch does not reveal the dryer’s control logic. Do not infer that a contact state necessarily permits starting, triggers a warning, or causes a shutdown without model-specific evidence.

Stop when the dryer’s configuration, expected contact states, test conditions, or safe access are undocumented. Consult the exact service information or a qualified technician for further diagnosis. The defensible conclusion is limited: confirm whether the design uses the switch, observe whether the filter mechanism moves its actuator, and interpret any isolated electrical result only against documented terminals and states. No single click or continuity reading proves that the complete interlock or dryer is functioning correctly.

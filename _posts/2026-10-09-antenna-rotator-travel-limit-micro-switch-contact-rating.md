Antenna rotator travel limit micro switch contact rating is not a stand-alone measure of whether a switch will work in a particular circuit. Suitability depends on what the switch actually interrupts, along with the electrical conditions and load type specified for its contacts. A travel limit may act in a motor-current path or switch a control input; the application name alone does not identify which arrangement is present.

The assessment therefore starts with the rotator’s circuit documentation and the exact switch’s applicable electrical data. Compare the documented operating conditions with the rating, rather than assuming that a familiar-looking switch or a general rating applies. If the circuit path or relevant rating conditions cannot be established, suitability remains unresolved.

## What the antenna rotator travel limit micro switch contact rating describes

A contact rating describes the electrical conditions under which the contacts are specified to switch a load. Those conditions can include voltage, current, load type, and switching conditions. A rating is meaningful only within its stated conditions; it does not establish that a switch is suitable for every circuit in which it might physically fit.

For a travel-limit circuit, the relevant comparison is between the rating’s stated conditions and the actual circuit conditions. The switch’s category, appearance, or use as a travel limit does not supply missing electrical information. Numerical travel limits are also device-specific requirements of the applicable specification sheet and must not be inferred from the component category.

Read the electrical data for the exact switch and identify which rating applies to the intended use. A value stated for one current type, load type, or switching condition should not be treated as a general allowance for other conditions. If the documentation does not state the conditions needed for the comparison, do not fill that gap by inference.

This distinction keeps the assessment focused: the question is not simply whether the switch has a contact rating, but whether the documented rating covers the particular circuit and switching duty. The rotator’s wiring information and the switch’s applicable data must both be available to make that comparison.

## Identify what the travel-limit micro switch actually switches

First trace the travel-limit contact in the rotator’s wiring information. Determine whether opening or closing that contact interrupts motor current, or whether it changes a control-circuit signal. Do not infer the arrangement from the fact that the component serves as a rotator travel limit. Different circuit paths can place different electrical demands on the same type of switch.

Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, and documented circuit function for the exact switch and equipment. A terminal’s physical position or a generic layout is not enough to identify its role. The circuit diagram should show how the limit contact relates to the motor or control input; the equipment documentation should establish the intended function of that path.

If the available documentation does not clearly show what the contact switches, treat the circuit path as unknown. Do not assume that the contact carries only a small signal, and do not assume that it interrupts motor current. Either assumption could lead to comparing the switch against the wrong electrical conditions.

Once the path is identified, record the conditions the contact must switch, including the current type and load information documented for the circuit. This classification comes before comparing numbers. A rating that may be relevant to one path cannot be presumed suitable for the other without the applicable specifications.

## Compare voltage, current, and load type with the applicable rating

Compare the circuit conditions with the exact switch’s applicable electrical data. Check whether the circuit is AC or DC, the operating voltage, the switching current, and whether the load is identified as inductive. Then confirm that the rating applies to those conditions and to the switching duty documented for the equipment.

An AC rating is not a DC rating. Do not transfer an AC value to a DC circuit unless the switch documentation explicitly provides that rating. Likewise, do not treat a rating for a resistive load as proof of suitability for a different load type. The comparison must stay within the conditions actually stated in the applicable data.

A useful check is to make a small comparison table with two sides: the circuit conditions established by the rotator documentation, and the conditions covered by the switch data. Compare current type, voltage, switching current, and load type one by one. A missing entry is not evidence of a match. If a value or condition is absent, seek the applicable documentation rather than estimating it.

A rating assessment also needs to account for switching conditions, not just a number printed beside the switch. Confirm that the data applies to the way the contact operates in the circuit. Where the documentation gives no applicable rating for the identified conditions, the available evidence is insufficient to conclude that the contact is suitable.

## Assess motor-load switching and contact stress

If the travel-limit contact interrupts motor current, assess it as a motor-load switching path, not as a low-current control input. Motor current and inductive behavior can create switching conditions different from those associated with a simple resistive load. The contact rating must cover the actual current type, voltage, load type, and switching duty documented for that path.

Do not use a resistive-load value as a substitute for a motor-load rating, or presume that the current drawn during operation alone settles the question. The relevant comparison is the switch’s applicable electrical data against the circuit conditions at switching. Where the equipment or switch documentation specifies permitted switching duty or suppression, follow those stated requirements. Do not invent a suppression arrangement where none is documented.

If the contact switches only a control input, assess that documented control-circuit path instead. Do not conclude that it carries motor current merely because its operation ultimately stops a motor. The circuit diagram must establish whether the contact directly interrupts the motor path or changes a signal elsewhere in the circuit.

This distinction also helps identify the failure path that matters. A contact assigned to motor-current interruption faces the documented motor-load switching conditions; a contact assigned to a control input must be assessed against that input’s conditions. In both cases, the evidence must come from the circuit and switch documentation. If the path, load type, or applicable switching rating remains unclear, a suitability decision cannot be supported.

## Verify the travel limit safely

Before testing, consult the equipment’s connection information and follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. With power isolated, verify the intended limit action and contact continuity using an appropriate procedure. The procedure should be based on the documented circuit and the switch’s terminal identification, not on a generic assumption about terminal layout.

Check that the travel limit changes the contact state as intended at the documented limit condition. Keep the check bounded to the intended function: it is not a reason to bypass the travel limit, defeat a protective function, or work on an energized circuit. If the wiring or terminal function is uncertain, stop and resolve it through the applicable documentation and qualified procedures.

Restore operation only after confirming the wiring and the intended limit behavior under the applicable requirements. A successful continuity check can establish a contact state during that check, but it does not by itself establish that the contact rating is suitable for the circuit’s operating or switching conditions. Rating suitability still depends on comparing the documented circuit path and load with the switch’s applicable electrical data.

The assessment is complete only when the circuit path is identified, the relevant AC or DC conditions and load are known, and the applicable switch data covers those conditions. If any of those elements is missing, leave suitability unresolved rather than treating the travel-limit function or a generic component description as proof. The antenna rotator travel limit micro switch contact rating must be judged against the documented circuit, not the application label alone.

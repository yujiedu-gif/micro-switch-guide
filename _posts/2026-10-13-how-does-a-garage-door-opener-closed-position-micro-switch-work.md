## How does a garage door opener closed position micro switch work?

In a design that uses one, a travel-related mechanical part actuates the micro switch near the closed-position set point. The causal sequence is: the door or an associated travel mechanism moves; a mechanically linked part acts on the switch actuator; the actuator movement operates the switch; and the switch presents a changed contact state to the opener circuit.

A micro switch is an electromechanical component. In a snap-action design, mechanical actuator movement changes an electrical contact state. It is used in this kind of arrangement to detect position or a travel limit. The switch detects its own mechanical input, however, not the door’s position by independent observation. Its relationship to the door depends on the mechanical arrangement that brings the input to the actuator.

The contact change is the component-level output. It is not the same thing as the opener’s interpretation of that output. The circuit may use the signal as part of its control logic, but the switch name alone does not tell whether the contact is open or closed at a particular reference point, what the controller recognizes, or what motor behavior follows. Those details are design-dependent.

This explanation also does not imply that every garage door opener contains a micro switch. Some designs may use a different sensing arrangement. The mechanism described here applies only where a micro switch is actually part of the closed-position sensing path.

## What moves the micro switch at the closed-position set point?

The path matters because the component can respond only to the movement delivered to it. The door’s position, the motion of a travel-related part, and the actuator’s position are related through the design, but they are not interchangeable descriptions. The micro switch changes state when its actuator is mechanically operated, not simply because someone calls the location “closed.”

## What NO and NC mean on a paper shredder bin interlock micro switch NO and NC terminals

Normally open (NO) and normally closed (NC) describe the contact state relative to the documented reference condition for the particular micro switch and contact arrangement. They are not descriptions of a paper shredder’s general state. In particular, “normally” does not mean “with the bin in its usual position” unless the switch documentation explicitly defines that as its reference condition.

For the documented reference condition, an NO contact is identified as open and an NC contact as closed. Those terms describe the contact’s state in that specified condition. They do not, by themselves, establish which terminals are connected in a shredder, how the equipment controller interprets a change, or whether the shredder will run. Keep the reference condition attached to any interpretation: a state label without its stated condition can be misleading.

Identify the contact arrangement and terminal names from the markings and documentation for the exact switch. Do not use a general picture of a micro switch, the apparent order of its terminals, or an assumption about a common physical layout to decide which connection is NO or NC. A terminal’s position is not a substitute for a documented connection diagram or marking.

Also keep switch state distinct from circuit function. Even when a contact is documented as open or closed under a defined condition, that fact alone does not say what the shredder’s control circuit expects. The controller could use the contact change in a way that cannot be determined from NO and NC labels alone. Those labels identify a switch-side property, not the complete interlock logic.

## How a bin can actuate a micro switch in some shredder designs

Some shredder designs may use a bin, a tab, or a linked part to move a micro switch’s actuator. In such an arrangement, movement of the bin-related mechanism may cause actuator movement. This is a conditional description, not a claim that all paper shredders use a micro switch or that every bin acts directly on one.

If the particular switch uses a snap-action mechanism, actuator movement can be associated with a change in contact state. Whether that mechanism and arrangement apply must be established from the switch and equipment documentation. Do not assume a universal sequence, such as a particular bin position always opening or closing a particular contact. The terminal labels do not state how a bin or linkage moves the actuator, or which condition the equipment defines as the switch’s reference state.

There are two separate relationships to consider. First, the switch’s documented contact arrangement relates a terminal pair to the switch’s state under a defined reference condition. Second, the installed equipment’s mechanical arrangement determines whether a bin-related part moves the actuator. The shredder’s wiring and control circuit then determine what effect, if any, a contact change has on equipment operation. Evidence for one relationship does not establish the others.

As a result, bin position alone cannot identify NO or NC. A bin may not directly contact the switch, a linked part may be involved, or the relevant movement may depend on the specific design. Without documentation for that arrangement, the relationship between the bin and actuator remains unknown. Do not convert an observed bin position into a terminal-state conclusion.

## Identify the switch-side contact state without assuming bin position

Start with the exact switch’s terminal markings, contact diagram, and stated reference condition. Use those documents to identify the contact arrangement and the relevant terminals. Do not infer COM, NO, or NC from terminal placement alone. If the arrangement or reference condition is not documented, the labels cannot be reliably assigned from appearance or from the shredder’s bin position.

A continuity check can be considered only for an isolated switch and a defined actuator state, using a suitable measuring method. Compare the observation with the switch’s documented contact state for that condition. Keep the result narrow: it concerns the tested switch contacts under the stated test conditions. It does not identify the shredder’s wiring, show what its controller expects, or prove the interlock works when assembled.

The state used for a check matters. Record whether the actuator is in the documented reference condition or another defined condition, and compare the result only with information that applies to that state. Do not assume that an arbitrary actuator position is the reference condition. If documentation does not specify the relevant state, an observed reading cannot fill that evidence gap by itself.

A click is not a substitute for terminal identification or a continuity observation. It may indicate movement, but it does not establish which contact changed, the contact’s electrical condition, or the equipment’s response. Likewise, one continuity reading does not show that the switch is correctly positioned, that the bin mechanism actuates it as intended, or that installed wiring is correct. Treat the check as one limited observation, not as a verdict on the complete shredder.

## What terminal labels cannot establish about shredder operation

NO and NC labels alone cannot establish which state the shredder controller expects, whether the bin is correctly seated, whether a bin-related mechanism reaches the actuator, or whether the assembled interlock functions. They also cannot establish the shredder’s response to an open or closed contact. Those questions require evidence about the particular equipment’s mechanical arrangement, wiring, and control function; do not infer them from switch-side labels.

Do not use a terminal-state interpretation to justify bypassing an interlock or changing a particular shredder’s wiring. This explanation does not identify a model-specific repair, replacement, or rating. If the required switch documentation or equipment information is unavailable, leave the unresolved arrangement and control behavior unresolved rather than guessing.

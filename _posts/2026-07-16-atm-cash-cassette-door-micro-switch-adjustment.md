# ATM Cash Cassette Door Micro Switch Adjustment

An **atm cash cassette door micro switch adjustment** changes the switch’s position only where the cassette design provides an approved adjustment. The switch detects a defined door state through actuator movement; it does not secure the door. Because cassette designs can use different switch locations and actuation arrangements, there is no universal position or adjustment amount. Use the applicable cassette and switch service documentation to identify the intended state, permitted adjustment, actuator limits, and verification procedure before making any change.

The key distinction is between the cassette door’s physical closed position and the point at which the switch changes electrical state. A door that appears closed does not, by itself, establish that the switch is operating as intended. Adjustment should address a documented mechanical-position issue, not compensate for a failed switch or an unknown design.

## What the ATM cash cassette door micro switch adjustment changes

The switch reports a defined door state when movement of the door engages its actuator. The electrical changeover point is therefore related to actuator movement, not simply to whether the door looks closed. The cassette’s design determines where the switch is mounted, which part of the door contacts the actuator, and what position corresponds to the required state. Do not assume that another cassette uses the same arrangement.

The switch’s operating characteristics include force and travel terms with distinct meanings. For example, releasing force is the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. This distinction helps explain why engagement and release can occur at different actuator positions. It does not provide a cassette-specific setting or travel limit. Those values and the intended switching position must come from the applicable service information.

Adjustment changes the relationship between the mounted switch, the actuator, and the door’s movement. It does not repair a loose door, a damaged actuator, or an electrical fault. First identify the exact assembly and its documented adjustment provisions; otherwise, there is no supported basis for changing the switch position.

## Check service information and make the cassette safe

Before inspection or adjustment, consult the applicable cassette and switch service documentation. Confirm the switch location, approved mounting provision, specified actuator limits, intended door state, and required test method. If those details are unavailable, do not infer them from the appearance of the switch or from a different cassette design.

Take the equipment out of service and follow authorized access, electrical isolation, and cash-handling procedures. Use only the access and work methods authorized for the equipment. Do not bypass the detection circuit, defeat security controls, or work around an interlock. This guidance does not authorize access to cash or a locked cassette.

Keep the work within the documented procedure. If the service information does not describe an adjustment, or if the required isolation or authorized access cannot be established, stop and refer the work to qualified service personnel. The switch’s general operating terminology is not a substitute for equipment-specific instructions.

## Inspect actuator contact, mounting, and door travel

Before moving the switch, inspect the assembly without forcing the actuator or door. Look for a loose or shifted mount, an obstruction, a damaged actuator, and door movement that does not engage the actuator consistently. Check whether contact occurs at the designed surface. An actuator that is bottomed out or driven beyond its specified travel is not a suitable condition to correct by applying more force.

Observe how the door moves through its normal path and note when the actuator is engaged and released. Compare that behavior with the applicable service procedure. Record the observed switching point before making any approved change, so the result can be compared with the baseline. Do not assume that a visually closed door is the required electrical transition point.

Intermittent detection can be consistent with a mechanical alignment or mounting problem, but it does not prove one. If the actuator engages consistently and the mounting appears stable, further positional changes may not address the cause. Separate the mechanical observations from the electrical test result before deciding whether an adjustment is warranted.

## ATM cash cassette door micro switch adjustment steps

Adjust the switch only if the cassette provides an approved mounting adjustment and the applicable service documentation states how to use it. Follow the documented direction and limits. Make small, controlled positional changes, then reassess actuator contact and door movement against the specified conditions. No generic adjustment amount can be supplied for all cassette designs.

Preserve the switch’s specified actuator travel. Do not bend the actuator, force the door against the switch, or shift the mount to make the switch report a state that the design does not specify. If the required position would exceed the stated limits, or if the mount has no documented adjustment provision, stop rather than improvising a change.

After each permitted change, compare engagement and release with the intended door position using the documented test procedure. If the actuator is damaged, the mounting is unstable, or the door does not move consistently, adjustment is not an appropriate substitute for repair. Refer those conditions for qualified service.

## Verify switch operation after adjustment

Use the documented continuity or status-check method and the required isolation precautions. Confirm that the electrical state changes at the intended door position, not merely that the actuator moves. Then check consistency through repeated open-close cycles as directed by the service procedure. A single transition is not enough to establish repeatable operation.

Interpret the result alongside the mechanical inspection. If actuator engagement varies with door movement, or the mount shifts, address that documented mechanical condition before concluding that the switch itself has failed. If mechanical engagement is correct but the state change remains intermittent, investigate the switch, wiring, and connections using the authorized diagnostic procedure rather than continuing to move the mount. Do not infer a wiring or contact fault solely from an intermittent indication.

The switch terminology distinguishes actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. These are separate characteristics, not interchangeable adjustment targets. Use values and test conditions only when the applicable switch and cassette documentation supplies them; do not derive a setting from terminology alone.

## When adjustment is not the right repair

Stop if the actuator is damaged, the mount is cracked or moving, the door has excessive play, an electrical test fails, or the adjustment method is undocumented. Stop as well if the intended state cannot be confirmed or if reaching it would exceed specified actuator limits. In those cases, further positional changes risk masking the underlying condition without establishing reliable operation.

Do not compensate for a faulty switch or door mechanism by defeating the interlock, bending the actuator, or forcing the door against the switch. Adjustment is bounded by the cassette’s approved provisions and the switch’s documented limits. A failed electrical test or unresolved mechanical condition requires the appropriate qualified repair, not an improvised setting.

Return the cassette to service only after the required checks and authorized procedures are complete. The **atm cash cassette door micro switch adjustment** is verified only when the documented test confirms the intended state change consistently through the specified operation; if that result cannot be established, leave the condition for qualified service rather than treating adjustment as complete.

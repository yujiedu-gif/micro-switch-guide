For an elevator landing door open position micro switch operating force is a parameter of the particular switch: it describes the force associated with actuating its actuator under defined conditions. It is not a general force requirement for moving an elevator door, and it does not by itself show whether a switch is suitable for a particular installation. That judgment requires the exact switch documentation and evidence about how the landing door transfers motion to the actuator.

A design may use a micro switch to detect a landing door’s open position, but that arrangement is not universal to all elevators or landing doors. The switch is one component in a larger mechanical and control system. Its force specification should therefore be read as switch-specific information, not as a description of the door system’s total effort or behavior.

## elevator landing door open position micro switch operating force

The operating force specification concerns the force needed to actuate the particular micro switch, as defined and measured under its stated conditions. It does not specify how much force a person or mechanism must apply to move the landing-door panel. Those are different quantities measured at different points in a mechanical system.

A numerical operating-force value is meaningful only with its definition and test conditions. The switch documentation may specify the actuator or measurement method relevant to that value; without those details, the number cannot safely be treated as a universal threshold or compared directly with a force measured elsewhere in the door mechanism.

The application label alone does not identify a switch design, force value, mounting arrangement, or required force. Landing-door position detection may use a micro switch in a particular design, but the presence of a landing door does not establish that every elevator uses the same component or actuation arrangement. The exact switch and its documented conditions must be identified before interpreting a force specification.

## How landing-door motion reaches the micro switch actuator

Door movement can reach a micro switch actuator through different mechanical arrangements. Depending on the installation, the door may make direct contact with the actuator, or motion may pass through a cam, lever, or linkage. The available evidence does not establish one standard layout for landing-door open-position detection, so none should be assumed.

The force at the switch actuator depends on the actual contact geometry and mounting, as well as the mechanism transferring door motion. A force measured at the door panel is not automatically the force applied to the switch actuator. Likewise, the force specified for a switch cannot be read as the force required to move the entire panel. The point and direction of force application matter when interpreting a measurement.

This distinction is important even when door movement and switch actuation occur together. The switch parameter describes actuation at the switch under defined conditions; it does not describe every force along the path from the door to the actuator. Without information about the installed geometry, a door-level measurement cannot be converted into a switch-actuator force by assumption.

Nor does identifying a cam, lever, or direct contact establish what the elevator control system does after the switch changes state. The mechanical relationship explains how motion may reach the actuator, not the resulting control response. That response depends on the particular installation and its documented design.

## Operating force is not release force or door-moving force

Operating force and release force describe distinct switch-related events. Operating force is associated with actuating the switch. Release force is the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. A release-force value therefore does not substitute for an operating-force value.

The specification terminology also distinguishes force terms from position and travel terms, including operating position, free position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. These terms refer to different characteristics; the available definitions do not support treating them as interchangeable with operating force. In particular, release is a separate event from initial operation, so a force value must be tied to the event and definition stated in the documentation.

The force needed to move a door is another distinct quantity. It may be measured or felt at the panel, while switch operating force applies at the switch actuator under specified conditions. A force reading without a defined measurement point, direction, mechanism state, and actuator state may not answer which quantity was measured. It cannot establish switch suitability on its own.

A sound comparison therefore keeps the quantities separate: the documented operating force for the exact switch, any documented release force, and force observations taken elsewhere in the door mechanism. The comparison should not infer a numerical relationship among them when the switch documentation and installation details do not establish one.

## What evidence is needed to judge force suitability

Start with the exact micro switch documentation. Check how it defines operating force, which actuator or configuration the value applies to, and the stated test conditions. If the documentation gives a value but the relevant conditions or definition are unclear, the value is not enough to decide whether the installed arrangement is suitable.

Next, identify the actual landing-door contact geometry and mounting. Determine how door motion reaches the switch actuator in that installation, and make sure any measurement is taken at a clearly identified point and direction. Compare only information that refers to like-for-like conditions. A force measured at the door panel should not be compared directly with a switch-actuator specification as though the two were the same measurement.

Then check the installation requirements applicable to the particular elevator system. The supplied information does not establish a universal force threshold for landing-door open-position detection. If the exact switch specification, relevant installation requirements, or comparable measurements are unavailable, force suitability remains undetermined rather than becoming a value that can be inferred from the application name.

A component force figure alone also does not validate the complete assembly or settle an elevator-system safety decision. Evaluation of the installed elevator system belongs with qualified personnel. This boundary avoids turning an isolated component parameter into a conclusion about the door mechanism, interlock, or control system.

## Keep landing-door position sensing separate from car-door switches

Landing-door open-position detection and car-door position detection are distinct applications. A switch’s force data or mechanical arrangement for one application should not be transferred to another without documentation for the particular design. Search terms such as elevator car open position micro switch operating force or elevator car closed position micro switch operating force do not establish that a landing-door switch has the same specification or role.

The same caution applies to landing-door closed-position sensing: an open-position application does not, by itself, establish a closed-position arrangement or its force requirements. The specific switch documentation and installation evidence must identify the application being evaluated. Do not infer a contact arrangement, normal state, or control response from the fact that a micro switch is present.

A micro switch’s contact change alone does not establish what the host elevator control system does next. Keep the component’s documented force parameter, the door mechanism’s force path, and the system’s response as separate questions. For an elevator landing door open position micro switch operating force, suitability can be judged only when the exact switch conditions and the actual installation requirements are available for qualified, like-for-like evaluation.

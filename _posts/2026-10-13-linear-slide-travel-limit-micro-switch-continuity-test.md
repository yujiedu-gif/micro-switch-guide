A linear slide travel limit micro switch continuity test can show whether the isolated switch contacts change state at defined actuator positions. It cannot, by itself, confirm that the slide is adjusted correctly or that the installed wiring and control system work. The useful comparison is between a documented contact arrangement and the switch’s actual actuator state, not simply between “slide in” and “slide out.”

Before measuring, establish how the slide or its linkage moves the switch actuator and identify the operating and release events for the specific setup. Then isolate the switch, identify its contacts from its markings and documentation, and compare readings at known states. If the expected change is absent, investigate the possible mechanical, identification, and contact paths separately rather than treating one reading as a complete diagnosis.

## What a linear slide travel limit micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity reading on an isolated switch can assess the contact state at a defined actuator state. For a linear slide arrangement, that means recording where the slide is and whether the switch actuator is at rest, has operated, or has released. The slide position is useful context, but it is not itself a guaranteed contact-state reference.

The result can be compared with the contact arrangement and reference condition documented for the exact switch. If the measured state matches that documentation at the tested actuator position, the test supports a limited conclusion about those contacts in that condition. If it does not match, the discrepancy needs investigation; it does not identify the cause on its own.

An isolated continuity check does not establish that the switch is correctly positioned relative to the slide, that a linkage moves it far enough, or that the slide reaches the intended travel positions. Nor does it test installed wiring, permissible load, the surrounding control circuit, or how the host equipment responds. Those are separate parts of the system and require their own documented checks.

Keep the scope narrow: this procedure concerns contact continuity on an isolated micro switch at known actuator states. A click, a slide endpoint, or one reading is not proof that the whole slide system or installed circuit is functional or safe.

## Relate slide position to micro switch actuator state

First observe how the slide, cam, or linkage engages the actuator. Do not infer the actuator state from a slide endpoint alone. The mechanism may contact the actuator before the slide reaches an endpoint, may move it through only part of its available motion, or may not engage it as expected. Which condition applies must be established for the particular assembly.

Identify two distinct events where applicable: operation, when the switch changes state as the actuator moves, and release, when it changes back as the actuator returns. Their positions can differ. Therefore, the slide position at which the switch operates need not be the same as the position at which it releases. Record the observed slide position and actuator state for each event rather than treating travel in opposite directions as identical.

A useful record separates the coordinate or observed position of the slide from the state of the switch actuator. For example, note whether the slide is stationary at a measured or otherwise defined location, whether the linkage is contacting the actuator, and whether the actuator is at rest, operated, or released. Use only states that can actually be identified for the assembly; do not label an endpoint “open,” “closed,” “normal,” or “operated” without supporting documentation.

If the actuator cannot be observed or its state cannot be established, a reading at a slide position alone has limited meaning. Do not use an assumed endpoint state as the expected continuity result. Establish the mechanical relationship from the specific switch and equipment documentation before interpreting a contact measurement.

## Prepare an isolated switch and identify the documented contacts

Do not measure continuity on an energized circuit. The applicable safe isolation procedure must be followed before the test. Under the cited United States workplace rule, exposed live parts generally must be deenergized before work; a qualified person must verify the deenergized condition with test equipment. Work on energized parts is limited to qualified persons. If safe isolation and verification are outside your qualifications or procedure, stop and leave the work to a qualified person.

Once isolation is established, determine which contacts are being tested from the terminal markings and documentation for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity from physical layout, wire color, or a generic diagram. Use the documented connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load rather than assuming that a particular terminal arrangement applies.

Continuity expectations depend on the documented contact arrangement and its reference condition. If the documentation identifies COM, NO, or NC, preserve those designations and the specified state reference. Do not assume those labels are present or that a terminal’s physical position establishes its function. If terminal identity or the reference condition cannot be confirmed, the expected reading is unresolved; do not guess.

The switch must be isolated from the circuit for the reading to represent its contacts alone. A measurement made through connected wiring or other components may include paths outside the switch, so it cannot be interpreted as an isolated switch-contact result. Keep the test limited to the switch and its documented terminals.

## Check continuity at defined slide positions

With the switch isolated and the contacts identified, record the slide position and actuator state before each reading. At actuator rest, measure the documented contact pair and note the result. Move the slide or linkage only as appropriate to reach a defined operated state, confirm that the actuator has actually reached that state, and measure the same documented pair again. Compare the readings with the contact-state documentation for the specific switch and its stated reference condition.

If relevant to the setup, return the slide through the release event and record the position and actuator state at which release occurs. Measure again only when the state is identifiable and the documentation provides an expectation for it. Keeping the operating and release observations separate avoids assuming that the switch changes back at the same slide position where it operated.

A compact test record can include:

- the documented terminal pair and reference condition;
- whether the switch was isolated for the measurement;
- the slide position for each reading;
- the observed actuator state, such as rest, operated, or released;
- the continuity result at each state; and
- whether the result agrees with the switch documentation.

Do not substitute “beginning” and “end” of slide travel for defined actuator states. If the linkage does not move the actuator into a known state, the reading cannot establish what the switch should do at that slide position. Likewise, do not treat an unspecified or undocumented contact pair as though it had a universal expected result.

## Interpret a mismatch without blaming the switch alone

If the measured result does not match the documented expectation, first check whether the test conditions were valid: the switch was isolated, the intended terminals were identified, and the actuator state was known. A terminal-identification error or an unclear reference condition can make a sound measurement appear inconsistent.

Next distinguish mechanical input from contact behavior. The slide may not move far enough to operate the actuator, or the slide-to-switch alignment or linkage may prevent the expected actuator movement. In those cases, the continuity result may reflect an actuator that never reached the intended state rather than an internal contact problem. Do not adjust or repair a particular assembly based on this general test description.

If the actuator state is established and the documented terminals and reference condition are confirmed, a persistent mismatch supports further investigation of the switch contacts. It still does not, by itself, establish the full cause or prove a fault elsewhere. A continuity change also does not verify the installed wiring, permissible load, or the control circuit’s operation.

Keep circuit diagnosis within the applicable safe procedure and qualification boundaries. The cited United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, qualified-person verification of the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits energized work to qualified persons. A switch reading cannot establish that the surrounding equipment is safe or functional. The defensible conclusion is limited to whether the isolated contacts matched their documented behavior at the actuator states actually tested.

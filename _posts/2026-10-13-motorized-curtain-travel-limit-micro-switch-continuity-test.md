A motorized curtain travel limit micro switch continuity test can show whether a specified contact path changes state as the switch actuator moves between defined positions. The result applies only to the isolated switch, the identified terminals, and the states tested. It does not establish that the curtain’s wiring, controller, motor, or complete travel-limit function works correctly.

Some motorized curtains use a micro switch to sense travel, but arrangements vary. The switch is one component in a larger system, and its contact behavior must be interpreted using the actual switch and equipment documentation. A click alone is not proof of continuity or correct curtain operation.

## What a motorized curtain travel limit micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity test can indicate whether a selected contact path is electrically continuous in a defined actuator position and whether that result changes in another defined position. That conclusion is valid only when the switch is isolated from connected circuitry, the terminals are correctly identified, and the expected behavior for those states is known.

The test does not establish that a motorized curtain’s full travel-limit function is sound. It does not test the curtain’s wiring, controller, motor, or the way those parts respond to a contact change. Nor does one result establish that the switch is suitable for its installed circuit or that the curtain will stop at the intended position.

Some curtain designs use a micro switch as a travel sensor; others may use a different arrangement. Do not assume that every motorized curtain contains this switch or that a switch found in one design has the same role in another. The relevant question is narrower: does the identified, isolated switch show the documented contact behavior in the actuator positions being checked?

Keep three levels of evidence separate. The meter reading concerns a contact path under a particular test condition. The switch’s operation concerns whether its actuator and contacts behave as documented. The curtain’s operation depends on the surrounding circuit and equipment as well. A switch-level result cannot by itself settle the latter questions.

## Identify the micro switch and its actuator states before testing

First establish which component is the micro switch and how it relates to the curtain’s travel mechanism. Use reliable equipment documentation or an observable, documented relationship rather than inferring the arrangement from the fact that the equipment is a motorized curtain. Do not assume that a visible switch is a travel limit unless its function is established.

Identify the actuator, the part moved by the mechanism, and the relevant positions to compare. Use defined positions that can be reached without forcing the actuator beyond its designed movement. Record which position is being tested; a reading without a specified actuator state is difficult to interpret or compare.

Next identify the contact path to measure from the switch’s terminal markings or reliable documentation. Do not infer terminal identity from physical layout, wire color, or a generic drawing. If labels such as COM, NO, or NC are present, confirm their meaning and the relevant reference condition for the exact switch. Do not presume which path should be continuous in either actuator position.

Before measuring, establish what the documentation says the contact path should do in each selected state. If terminal identity, reference states, or expected behavior are unknown, the test may produce a reading but not a reliable conclusion about whether the switch is behaving as intended. Do not fill those gaps with assumptions based on another switch or curtain design.

## Motorized curtain travel limit micro switch continuity test: safe, isolated checks

Do not measure continuity on an energized circuit. The switch must be electrically isolated from connected circuitry before its contact path is measured; otherwise, the reading may not describe the switch alone. Follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. If you cannot establish that the circuit is deenergized and the switch is isolated, stop rather than proceeding with the measurement.

With the switch isolated, use a continuity-measuring method appropriate to the test and the identified terminal pair. Record the terminals and actuator position for each reading. Check the documented actuator positions in turn, moving the actuator only within its designed movement. Compare each result with the expected behavior documented for that contact path and state.

Interpret only what was actually tested. A result for one terminal pair does not establish the behavior of another path. A reading in one actuator position does not establish behavior in a different position. An unspecified meter indication, an unidentified terminal pair, or a measurement made while the switch remains connected to other circuitry cannot be treated as a clear switch-level result.

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not establish electrical continuity, correct terminal identification, or correct curtain operation. The electrical measurement and the mechanical observation are different evidence. If the observed movement or reading cannot be compared with documented expected behavior, the result remains unresolved rather than proving the switch good or bad.

## Interpret a continuity result without diagnosing the whole curtain

If an isolated contact path does not show its documented state change, several possibilities remain distinct. The actuator may not be reaching the relevant position; the internal contact path may not be behaving as documented; or the terminals may have been identified incorrectly. A discrepancy is a reason to examine those possibilities, not proof of one particular cause.

If the switch shows the documented behavior in the tested positions, that supports only the limited finding that the specified isolated contact path changed state as expected under those conditions. It does not establish that the installed wiring carries the change correctly, that the controller responds as intended, or that the motor and complete travel-limit function operate properly. The fault may be elsewhere in the equipment.

Avoid turning either result into a whole-system diagnosis. A switch-level continuity result is not a test of the curtain’s wiring or control circuit, and the available test does not establish what those parts should do. Do not infer a repair, compatibility, or installed-system safety from the isolated reading alone. Further conclusions require the relevant equipment documentation and a suitable procedure beyond the scope of this test.

## When to stop and get qualified help

Stop if you cannot confirm deenergization and isolation, identify the terminals and expected states, or access the switch without exposure to energized parts. Also stop if the actuator cannot be moved within its designed range or the documented relationship between the switch and curtain mechanism is unclear. In those conditions, a continuity reading is not a sound basis for a conclusion.

Do not improvise wiring changes, bypass a travel limit, or infer a repair from an isolated contact reading. Use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment rather than a generic physical-layout inference. Work beyond the documented safe test procedure belongs with a qualified person.

The motorized curtain travel limit micro switch continuity test can establish a limited switch-level observation: whether an identified contact path changes as documented across specified actuator states while isolated. It cannot establish that the complete curtain system works correctly. If any part of the test conditions or expected behavior is uncertain, stop and leave further electrical work to a qualified person.

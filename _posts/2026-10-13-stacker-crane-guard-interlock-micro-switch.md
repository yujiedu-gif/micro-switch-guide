In some designs, a stacker crane guard interlock micro switch changes an electrical contact state when mechanical movement associated with a guard moves its actuator. The switch is one component in a larger arrangement: it is not the guard, the complete interlock, or the crane controller. Other guard interlocks may use a different design, so this description does not apply to every stacker crane.

Understanding the role means keeping two events separate. First, guard movement may be transmitted through a mechanical part to the switch actuator, producing a contact-state change. Then, the surrounding control system may respond to that change according to its circuit and design. The component alone does not establish what the crane will do.

## What does a stacker crane guard interlock micro switch do?

In a design that uses one, the micro switch provides a contact-state change in response to mechanical movement associated with a guard. That change can serve as an input to the surrounding equipment, but the switch itself does not determine the control system’s response.

A micro switch is an electromechanical component. Its actuator is the part moved by an external mechanism; movement of that actuator can change the switch’s electrical contacts. A miniature snap-action switch can be used for position or limit detection, but that general role does not establish the arrangement or function of a particular crane interlock.

The distinction matters because “guard interlock” can refer to an overall arrangement, while “micro switch” names only a possible component within it. The guard is the physical barrier or movable part. A linkage or other mechanical element may transfer its movement. The switch detects movement through its actuator and changes contact state. The control system receives or otherwise uses an electrical condition according to the installed design.

These are related parts of one possible chain, not interchangeable names for the same thing. A contact change does not, by itself, prove that a guard is in a particular position, that the crane has stopped, or that a specific safety function is active. Those conclusions depend on the actual mechanical and electrical design.

## How guard movement reaches the micro switch

When a stacker crane design uses a micro switch for a guard interlock, guard movement may reach the switch directly or through a mechanical part such as a lever, cam, or linkage. The moving part acts on the switch actuator. Actuator movement then changes the contact state according to that switch’s design.

The chain is therefore conditional: guard movement, possible mechanical transmission, actuator movement, and contact-state change. The exact parts and their relationship cannot be assumed across installations. A guard may not contact the actuator directly, and the presence of a micro switch does not establish which direction of movement operates it.

Nor does the component name identify the resulting terminal state. The contact arrangement and the reference condition matter. Without evidence for the installed switch, it is not possible to say whether a guard movement opens or closes a particular contact, or to assign a universal NO or NC state. The switch’s actuator motion and contact arrangement are design-specific.

Force-and-travel terminology can describe separate features of a switch mechanism. A specification may distinguish actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. Those terms do not supply values or identify the configuration of a stacker crane installation. They should not be used to infer how far a particular guard moves or what force it applies without applicable documentation.

This mechanical account explains how movement can reach a switch; it does not establish that every guard uses this arrangement or that the switch alone provides the complete interlock function.

## Why a contact change does not specify the crane response

A micro switch’s contact state is not the same thing as the crane control system’s decision. The switch may provide an electrical input, while the equipment’s circuitry and control design determine how that input is interpreted and what, if anything, follows from it.

Without information about the installed circuit and design, a contact change does not establish that crane motion is inhibited, that an alarm is signaled, that the equipment stops, or that restart is prevented or permitted. It is also not possible to infer the guard’s normal state or the switch’s NO/NC arrangement from the general description of a guard interlock.

This boundary avoids treating the switch as though it independently controls the whole machine. Even if guard movement operates the actuator, the resulting contact condition is only one part of the equipment’s signal path. The actual response may depend on control logic and other design features not described by the switch’s role alone.

Accordingly, the general explanation supports a limited conclusion: in some designs, guard-related mechanical movement can operate a micro switch and change its contacts. It does not establish the operational or safety behavior of a particular stacker crane. Those claims require evidence about that equipment’s actual arrangement.

## What determines the behavior of an installed interlock

Understanding a particular installation requires information about the actual switch contact arrangement, the mechanical linkage between the guard and actuator, the wiring, and the control-system design. Each part answers a different question: what moves the actuator, what contact state changes, how the circuit carries that condition, and how the equipment interprets it.

Equipment documentation is the relevant source for installation-specific interpretation. If the documents do not clarify the arrangement or response, qualified service personnel can assess the specific equipment. The general description here does not provide instructions for wiring, bypassing, repairing, or testing an installed crane interlock.

Keep the conclusion proportional to the available evidence: a micro switch can be one component that converts guard-associated movement into a contact-state change. The installed circuit and equipment documentation determine what that change means for the crane. The phrase stacker crane guard interlock micro switch identifies that possible component relationship, not a universal design or guaranteed system response.

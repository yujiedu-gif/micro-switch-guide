A postal scale platform micro switch contact rating describes the electrical switching conditions the contacts are rated to handle, not the platform’s weight capacity or the force needed to move the actuator. To check whether the rating fits the scale, identify the circuit voltage, current, AC or DC supply, and load type, then compare those conditions with the exact switch’s markings and documentation. The platform application by itself does not establish the electrical load.

## What postal scale platform micro switch contact rating means

A contact rating is an electrical switching limit stated for particular operating conditions. A voltage and current value should therefore be read together with the conditions attached to it, including whether it applies to AC or DC and whether the load is resistive or inductive. A marked current value is not a universal allowance for every circuit or load.

The switch’s location under or near a postal scale platform does not reveal what its contacts switch. The platform may actuate a micro switch, but the surrounding scale circuit determines the electrical role and load. Platform size, capacity, or movement cannot establish the voltage, current, or load category at the contacts.

Start with the switch markings and the scale’s applicable documentation. Identify the contact rating that corresponds to the relevant supply type and load category, rather than selecting a number based on appearance or assuming all ratings apply in the same conditions. If the markings do not state the necessary conditions, treat those details as unresolved until the exact switch documentation or circuit information clarifies them.

A contact rating concerns electrical switching. It does not specify how much platform force the actuator requires, how far it must move, or how long the mechanism will operate mechanically. Those are separate questions and need separate evidence.

## Read the rating against the scale circuit load

The comparison requires the actual electrical conditions at the switch contacts. Determine the circuit voltage and the current the micro switch switches, then check those values against a rating stated for the same AC or DC supply and the applicable load category. Matching only one number, such as current, does not establish that the circuit is within the rating.

Load category matters because a resistive-load rating is not automatically applicable to an inductive load. An inductive or other load condition may impose different contact stress, so the rating must be matched to the load named in the switch documentation. Do not convert a resistive rating into an inductive allowance, or assume that a value stated for one category covers another.

The scale platform’s physical characteristics do not substitute for circuit information. A larger platform or greater capacity does not, by itself, establish a greater switched current. Likewise, the force that moves the platform or actuator does not identify the electrical load. These mechanical observations may matter to whether the switch is actuated correctly, but they cannot verify its contact rating.

If the scale documentation and switch markings do not make the switched load clear, the rating comparison is incomplete. Do not infer the load from the switch’s position or from the scale’s general function. The relevant point is the circuit function assigned to the contacts in the particular scale assembly.

## AC, DC, and switching duty boundaries

AC and DC ratings can differ. Use the rating that applies to the circuit’s actual current type; do not treat an AC value as a DC value or substitute one for the other without documentation that establishes the applicable rating. Voltage and current must also be considered in the relevant load category, not as isolated numbers detached from their stated conditions.

Switching duty is another boundary. A platform that changes the switch state only occasionally may involve less frequent switching than one that repeatedly interrupts a load. But infrequent actuation does not remove the need to meet the applicable electrical rating. The contacts still need a rating that covers the circuit’s voltage, current type, and load condition.

Do not infer switching duty solely from how often someone places an item on the scale. The circuit may determine what changes when the switch operates, and the available evidence here does not establish how any particular postal scale is designed. Confirm the scale’s circuit function and switching conditions from its documentation or through qualified inspection rather than assuming that platform movement means the contacts carry a particular load.

These boundaries prevent a common but incomplete check: comparing one marked current figure with a presumed circuit value while overlooking AC versus DC, load category, or repeated switching. A valid comparison accounts for the conditions attached to the rating and the actual load switched by the contacts.

## Electrical contact rating versus mechanical suitability

Electrical rating and mechanical capability describe different aspects of a micro switch. The contact rating addresses electrical switching under stated conditions. Operating force, pretravel, overtravel, and mechanical endurance concern movement and operation of the mechanism. A contact rating does not establish any of those mechanical specifications.

The scale’s platform arrangement must engage the switch as intended, but a correct electrical rating alone does not prove that engagement, travel, or operation is suitable. Conversely, a switch that appears to move when the platform moves is not thereby shown to have an appropriate contact rating. Mechanical operation and electrical suitability need to be assessed separately against the relevant scale and switch documentation.

Do not use platform capacity, actuator force, or apparent travel as a proxy for voltage or current limits. Nor should a stated electrical rating be treated as evidence that the switch fits the platform mechanism. If mechanical dimensions, travel, or endurance matter to the assessment, consult documentation for the exact switch and the scale’s arrangement; do not infer values that are not provided.

This distinction also keeps replacement decisions bounded. A switch that seems to actuate in the same place is not proven electrically appropriate by that observation. Likewise, a rating that matches the circuit does not prove that the mechanism is correctly engaged. The available information must address both sides if both are relevant.

## How to verify a postal scale platform micro switch contact rating before replacement

Use the exact switch markings and applicable switch documentation to identify the contact rating and its conditions. Use the scale documentation to determine the circuit function and the electrical load switched by the contacts. Then compare the circuit voltage, current, AC or DC type, and load category with the rating that applies to those conditions. Consider switching duty as well; occasional platform operation does not waive the electrical limits.

Do not compare only one marked value, and do not assume a rating applies across load types or current types. Also keep mechanical specifications separate: force, travel, engagement, and mechanical life cannot be established from a contact rating. A replacement assessment needs evidence for the electrical conditions and, where relevant, the mechanical arrangement.

If the markings or documentation do not identify the load or the applicable rating conditions, the circuit comparison remains unresolved. Do not use a trial substitution to fill that information gap. Have the assembly checked by a qualified technician when circuit details are unknown, following applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. The wiring diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment should guide identification, not a generic assumption based on terminal layout.

The practical decision is conditional: the contact rating is suitable only when the actual scale circuit conditions fall within the rating documented for that switch. The platform’s presence alone cannot establish that match, and a single matching number cannot establish it either.

How to test a cartoning machine end of stroke micro switch depends first on confirming that the machine actually uses an electromechanical micro switch for this function. The procedure below separates mechanical actuation from isolated contact testing and does not diagnose the machine’s full control system.

A miniature snap-action switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement and is commonly used for position or limit detection. That general function does not establish how a particular cartoning machine is built, which contact arrangement it uses, or what the machine should do at end of stroke. Use the machine’s documentation to establish those facts before testing.

## First confirm the cartoning machine uses an end-of-stroke micro switch

A cartoning machine may use different sensing arrangements. Apply this procedure only when the machine’s documentation or inspection identifies an electromechanical micro switch as its end-of-stroke input. Do not treat every sensor or switch used in cartoning equipment as a micro switch.

The micro switch is a component that responds to mechanical actuator movement. The machine’s wiring and control logic determine how a change in the switch’s contact state is interpreted and what action follows. A stop, signal, or other machine response therefore does not, by itself, establish that the switch is functioning correctly. Likewise, a machine symptom does not prove that the switch is the cause.

Before proceeding, establish which component is being tested and what the machine documentation identifies as its end-of-stroke input. If the arrangement or component identity is unclear, do not apply a micro-switch continuity procedure by assumption. Consult the applicable machine documentation or qualified personnel.

## Make the machine safe before testing the micro switch

Do not test an energized or moving machine. Before inspecting the actuator or performing a contact check, follow the machine’s documented isolation and energy-control procedure. Work beyond the safe procedure documented for the machine belongs to qualified personnel.

Do not disconnect, probe, or interpret an unidentified circuit. A connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment govern how its terminals and electrical role are understood. Physical position or appearance is not a reliable substitute for that documentation.

This procedure is limited to an isolated component check after the relevant circuit has been safely isolated. It does not provide instructions for live-machine testing, troubleshooting the control system, or determining whether the machine is safe to operate. If the documented isolation procedure is unavailable or cannot be followed, stop and refer the work to qualified personnel.

## Check whether the end-of-stroke mechanism actuates and releases the micro switch

With the machine safely isolated, inspect the mechanical input path. Where the design uses a cam, lever, or linkage, observe whether it reaches the switch actuator at the intended end-of-stroke position and then permits the actuator to release. The exact arrangement must be established from the machine’s documentation; do not assume every machine uses the same mechanism.

Look for an obstruction, visible damage, misalignment, or binding that could prevent the mechanism from reaching or releasing the actuator. These observations identify possible input-path issues, not a confirmed switch fault. Do not force a mechanism or infer an acceptable travel distance or operating force from appearance. Use documented machine and switch limits where they are available.

A click may accompany actuator movement, but it does not establish that the contacts changed state, that the terminals are correctly connected, or that the machine’s end-of-stroke function is correct. Keep the mechanical observation separate from the electrical check that follows.

## Identify the micro switch terminals and reference states

Before taking a continuity reading, use the applicable wiring diagram or switch documentation to identify the terminals and the reference condition for the documented contact descriptions. Use COM, NO, or NC only if that contact arrangement is documented for the switch being tested. Do not infer a normal state from the machine’s end-of-stroke role or from terminal placement.

The actuator state matters when interpreting a contact reading. Establish which documented state corresponds to the actuator released and which corresponds to the specified actuated condition, if both are given. Compare readings only with expected contact behavior documented for those states. Do not transfer an expectation from another switch or from a generic layout.

If the terminal identity, contact arrangement, reference state, or expected readings are uncertain, do not interpret a meter result. Stop and consult the machine or switch documentation, or qualified personnel. A reading without a known contact pair and defined actuator state cannot establish whether the switch behaved as expected.

## How to test a cartoning machine end of stroke micro switch with an isolated continuity check

Only after safely isolating the switch from the relevant circuit, use a continuity meter according to its instructions and check across the documented contact pair. Keep the switch isolated for this component-level check; a reading taken through an unidentified or connected circuit may not represent the switch alone.

Record the actuator state for each reading. If the documentation specifies contact behavior in both released and actuated states, take and compare the readings in those defined states. The relevant result is whether the observed change matches the documented contact arrangement and expected states. Do not substitute an assumed NO or NC sequence for missing documentation.

Treat the result narrowly: it describes the isolated contact behavior under the tested conditions. A click does not prove continuity, and a single continuity reading does not prove that the contact changes correctly between states. Neither observation establishes correct wiring, permissible load, or correct machine operation.

## Interpret results without confusing switch faults with machine faults

If the documented contact change is absent under the specified isolated states, report that switch-level result and the states tested. The result does not, by itself, establish a definitive cause or explain the machine’s symptom. Keep any observed obstruction, binding, or failure to reach or release the actuator as a separate mechanical input-path observation.

If the isolated switch responds as documented but the machine symptom remains, the switch check has not verified the installed wiring or the machine’s control response. Possible causes may lie in the linkage, wiring, terminal connection, control logic, or other equipment. These are separate areas of diagnosis, not conclusions established by the isolated continuity test.

Do not extend this procedure to installed-circuit diagnosis or live testing. Those tasks require the machine-specific procedure and qualified personnel. If isolation, terminal identity, reference state, or expected readings cannot be established, stop and consult qualified personnel or the applicable documentation. In short, how to test a cartoning machine end of stroke micro switch means verifying the documented mechanical input and isolated contact response separately, while leaving unresolved machine-level behavior to its own qualified diagnosis.

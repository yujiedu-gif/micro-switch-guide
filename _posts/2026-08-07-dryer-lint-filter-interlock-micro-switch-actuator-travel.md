Actuator travel is the movement of a micro switch’s actuator between defined positions. For a **dryer lint filter interlock micro switch actuator travel** question, that movement must be distinguished from how far the lint filter moves and from what the dryer’s controls do in response. The linkage and resulting control behavior depend on the particular appliance design; a general travel figure cannot establish how a given dryer operates.

## dryer lint filter interlock micro switch actuator travel

A micro switch is an electromechanical component. In a lint-filter interlock arrangement, some part of the filter, its holder, or a connected mechanism may move the switch actuator, but the exact arrangement is design-specific. The switch itself is not the entire interlock system: its mechanical input and electrical state are only parts of the relationship between the filter and the dryer’s control logic.

Actuator travel refers to motion at the switch actuator. Filter displacement is motion of the filter, and linkage movement is motion of any intervening parts. Those distances are not automatically equal. Their relationship depends on the installed geometry and design, so actuator travel should not be estimated from the filter’s apparent movement alone.

The term also needs a reference. A stated travel value may describe movement between particular switch positions or a permitted range of actuator motion. Without documentation identifying the switch, measurement reference, and relevant position, a number cannot be interpreted reliably. The appliance’s response likewise cannot be inferred solely from the presence of a switch: the effect of a contact change depends on the actual circuit and control design.

## How switch travel relates to operation and release

A specification may distinguish free position, operating position, and extreme permitted actuator position. Under the terminology in MIL-PRF-8805K sections 6.5.14 and 6.5.16, **pretravel** runs from the free position to the operating position. The operating position is where the specified actuation event occurs; it is not a measurement of the filter’s full travel.

**Overtravel** runs from the operating position to the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. It describes additional actuator movement within the specified limit, not a universal allowance for mounting, adjustment, or forcing a mechanism farther. The applicable switch documentation must establish the relevant position and permitted motion. A value for one design cannot be transferred to an unidentified switch or appliance arrangement.

Operation and release are distinct events. The operating point is associated with the switch’s change during actuation. The release point is associated with its return as the actuator moves back. Where the design uses this terminology, movement differential describes the separation between those events. It should not be confused with pretravel, overtravel, or filter displacement. The specification distinguishes actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel; those terms describe different characteristics rather than interchangeable measures of one travel distance.

These distinctions matter when interpreting an observation. A mechanism may move before the switch operates, and it may have permitted motion after operation. Its release event occurs separately on return. Without the specified reference conditions and design information, observed motion cannot be assigned a precise operating point, release point, or permissible overtravel.

## Why there is no universal lint-filter interlock travel value

No universal travel value follows from the phrase “lint-filter interlock.” The value, if documented, belongs to a particular switch design and its measurement reference. The filter, mounting location, actuator geometry, and any intervening linkage may affect how movement reaches the switch, but their relationship requires evidence for that appliance arrangement. A generic dimension would conflate component movement with system movement.

A useful interpretation therefore starts with applicable documentation for both the installed switch and the dryer arrangement. The documentation needs to identify what is being measured and the reference positions or conditions. For example, a value described as pretravel is not the same as one describing overtravel or the separation between operating and release points. If the reference is missing, the number does not by itself establish when the interlock changes state or whether a particular component fits.

Compatibility cannot be concluded from a travel value alone. A matching-looking actuator movement does not establish that a switch is suitable for the dryer’s mechanical arrangement or control circuit. Likewise, the existence of a lint-filter interlock in one design does not show that all dryers use a micro switch, or that different dryers implement the same control response. Keep the claim limited to the documented appliance and switch.

## What observations can and cannot verify

An audible click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove electrical continuity, correct terminal identification, correct wiring, or proper interlock operation. A quiet mechanism also does not, on its own, establish a particular electrical fault. Treat sound as one observation, not a complete test result.

An isolated continuity check concerns the switch rather than the dryer’s complete interlock system. Its interpretation requires a documented terminal arrangement, a defined actuator state, and a safe isolation procedure. A reading in one state cannot establish how the installed circuit behaves, whether the dryer’s control logic responds as intended, or whether the appliance is safe to operate. Do not infer an installed circuit’s safety from an isolated reading.

Keep possible causes distinct. A problem may involve mechanical input or linkage movement, an internal contact issue, terminal identification, wiring, or another part of the dryer. One observation does not distinguish all of these possibilities. Any check must be interpreted within the applicable documentation and its stated conditions; this explanation does not provide a procedure for opening, modifying, or operating a particular appliance.

## What to check before interpreting actuator travel

Find the documentation that applies to the installed switch and the specific dryer arrangement. Check which position or event a travel figure describes, what reference condition is used, and whether the information concerns pretravel, operating position, release position, movement differential, or permitted overtravel. If those details are absent, leave the travel value and compatibility unresolved rather than substituting a generic estimate.

For electrical work, the cited United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements. Leave energized-appliance testing and work beyond a documented safe procedure to qualified personnel.

The practical conclusion is bounded: **dryer lint filter interlock micro switch actuator travel** means movement of the switch actuator under a defined design and measurement reference. It does not automatically equal filter movement, identify the dryer’s control response, or supply a universal value. Use the applicable switch and appliance documentation to interpret the movement; a click or isolated reading alone cannot verify the complete interlock or dryer function.

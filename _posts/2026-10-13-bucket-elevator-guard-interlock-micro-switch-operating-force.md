It does not, by itself, specify how much force a person must apply to move the guard or how the complete interlock behaves. Those depend on the installed arrangement and its documented conditions.

## bucket elevator guard interlock micro switch operating force

Operating force, also called actuating force in the cited definition, is the force or torque applied to a micro switch’s actuating mechanism to operate the contacts. This defines a switch-actuator parameter. It is not a force specification for a bucket elevator guard, a guard handle, or an entire interlock assembly.

The cited definition is in MIL-PRF-8805K, section 6.5.1. The available evidence supplies the definition but does not give a numeric force, a test method, a measurement location beyond the actuating mechanism, or a particular switch’s limits. A numeric value therefore cannot be interpreted here as a requirement for a guard or as a comparison between installed designs. Any value used for a specific switch must be read with the conditions and definitions in its applicable documentation.

That boundary matters because a switch may not be moved directly by the guard. If the guard’s motion reaches the switch through another component, the operating force remains a property of the switch input as defined, while the force applied at the guard belongs to the larger mechanical arrangement. The two values are not automatically the same.

Other force-related terms do not fill in missing information. For example, the cited definition of release force describes the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. Overtravel describes the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. These are distinct concepts; neither establishes the force required to move a guard or the design limits of a particular installation.

## How force travels from the guard to the micro switch actuator

The relevant mechanical path begins with guard movement and ends, if the arrangement is designed that way, at the micro switch actuator. A guard may act through a cam, lever, or other linkage before it moves the actuator. These are possible arrangements, not a description of every bucket elevator guard interlock. At each point in that path, the movement and force are associated with the part being considered. The force applied to the guard is not automatically the force delivered to the switch actuator. Between them, the mechanism’s geometry and condition affect how motion is transferred. Alignment and adjustment can also affect where and how the actuator is reached. Without design-specific evidence, those effects cannot be quantified or assumed to have a particular direction or magnitude.

The switch’s operating-force definition answers a limited question: what force or torque is applied to its actuating mechanism to operate its contacts. It does not describe the full guard motion, establish how much actuator travel occurs, or state whether the linkage reliably carries the guard through the intended motion. Nor does the definition identify which component contacts the actuator or whether the switch is mounted in a particular orientation.

This distinction prevents a common interpretation error. A value documented for a switch actuator should not be treated as the force an operator will feel at a guard, and a perceived change in guard force should not by itself be treated as evidence of a change in switch operating force. ## Why switch operating force is not the guard’s operating force

The switch operating force applies at the switch’s actuating mechanism. The force applied at the guard applies at a different point in the mechanical path. If an intervening mechanism exists, the forces at those points cannot be equated without evidence about the arrangement and its behavior.

It would therefore be unsupported to convert a switch’s operating-force value into a guard-opening force, set a guard-force target from it, or declare that a particular guard should feel a certain way.

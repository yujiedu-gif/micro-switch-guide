# Understanding the term electrical pull string

The phrase **electrical pull string** can describe different things depending on the task being discussed. In raceway-conductor work, it means a line used to draw conductors through a raceway. In another context, a similar phrase may refer to a cord that operates a switch. The words alone do not establish which meaning is intended, so readers should use the surrounding description and equipment context to identify the task.

This distinction matters because a conductor-drawing line and a pull-cord switch have different functions. One is associated with moving conductors through a raceway; the other is part of a device operated by pulling its cord. The term itself does not specify a line’s material, capacity, installation method, or suitability for any particular work.

## What does electrical pull string mean?

When the discussion concerns conductors being routed through a raceway, an **electrical pull string** is a line used to draw those conductors through the raceway. The name describes the line’s role in the task: it is used in the process of bringing conductors through the raceway. It does not, by itself, identify the line’s construction or establish how it should be used for a particular installation.

The phrase is not self-explanatory in every setting. Someone discussing a raceway and conductors is likely using it to mean a conductor-drawing line. Someone describing an accessible cord attached to a switching device may instead mean a pull-cord switch or its operating cord. Without that context, treating either meaning as certain risks misunderstanding the work being described.

A useful way to read the phrase is to ask what is being moved or operated. If the stated task is to draw conductors through a raceway, the line is being discussed as a drawing aid. If the stated task is to change a switch’s state by pulling a cord, the conversation concerns a switching device. This distinction is about function, not a claim that every installation uses a particular arrangement.

The term also does not answer practical design or compliance questions. It does not establish how many or what kinds of conductors can be drawn, what limits apply, which materials are suitable, or whether a method meets requirements for a specific location. Those details depend on the actual work and applicable documentation and requirements. Do not infer them from the label alone.

## Electrical pull string versus a pull-cord switch

The central difference is function. A pull string used for raceway-conductor work is associated with drawing conductors through a raceway. A pull-cord switch is a switching device operated by pulling a cord. The first description concerns moving conductors; the second concerns operating a switch. Similar wording does not make the two items interchangeable.

Context can help identify which meaning is intended. References to conductors, a raceway, or a conductor-pulling task point toward a drawing line. References to a switch, its operation, or a cord that changes the device’s state point toward a pull-cord switch. These are clues for interpreting a description, not proof of a particular device, wiring arrangement, or installation method.

Nearby components may provide additional context, but they should not be treated as conclusive on their own. A raceway mentioned alongside conductors supports the conductor-drawing interpretation when that is also the stated task. A switch described as being operated by a cord supports the switching interpretation. If the description is incomplete, ask for clarification instead of assuming that a visible cord or line serves one specific purpose.

This distinction also sets a boundary on what can be answered from terminology alone. Identifying a cord as part of a switch does not establish how that switch is wired. Identifying a line as a conductor-drawing aid does not establish a pulling procedure or allowable load. The phrase answers a basic vocabulary question only when the task context is clear; it is not a substitute for equipment information or installation instructions.

## What to verify before planning a conductor pull

First, confirm that the task is actually to draw conductors through a raceway. If the description instead concerns operating a switch, it is a different task. When the language is unclear, ask whether the line is intended to move conductors or to actuate a switching device before discussing an installation method.

For a conductor-pulling task, consult the applicable installation documentation and requirements for the specific work. The appropriate information depends on the actual raceway, conductors, equipment, location, and other conditions that apply. The phrase **electrical pull string** does not supply those details and should not be used to fill gaps in the documentation.

Do not infer conductor capacity, pull limits, material suitability, or code compliance from the term alone. No universal method or numeric limit follows from the wording. A general description of a drawing line cannot establish whether a proposed line or procedure is appropriate for a particular raceway and conductor combination.

Electrical work must be handled within applicable requirements and instructions. In the United States workplace-rule context, the cited rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements for the work; this general statement does not determine the rules for a particular building, circuit, or installation.

## When the phrase needs clarification

If the phrase appears without a clear task, ask a direct question: is the line meant to draw conductors through a raceway, or is a cord meant to operate a switch? That question focuses on function and can resolve the terminology without assuming an installation arrangement.

When more information is needed, request the equipment context or installation description. For example, ask what the line is connected to or what action it is meant to perform. A description that identifies conductors and a raceway supports one interpretation; a description that identifies a switching device and a pull-operated cord supports the other. Neither clue alone establishes specifications or wiring details.

Keep any follow-up focused on the task. If the work concerns a conductor pull, identify the applicable documentation and requirements before planning a method. If the work concerns a switch, use the instructions and requirements that apply to that device and installation. Application-specific electrical work must follow applicable instructions and requirements, and ambiguous terminology should be clarified before it is used to guide the work.

In short, **electrical pull string** means a line used to draw conductors through a raceway when that is the task under discussion. A pull-cord switch instead uses a cord to operate a switching device. Context, nearby components, and the described task help distinguish them, but the term alone does not establish technical specifications or an installation method.

Intermittent home-position indication can come from inconsistent mechanical actuation, a micro switch or its contacts, wiring and terminals, or another part of the lift’s control path. The phrase “scissor lift home position micro switch intermittent operation” describes a symptom, not a confirmed component fault. First verify that the particular lift uses a micro switch for this function and establish its documented role. The arrangement and control response are design-specific; a home-position signal does not, by itself, establish how the lift behaves or whether it is safe to operate.

## scissor lift home position micro switch intermittent operation

Treat the indication as a system-level symptom until the actual design and signal path are confirmed. A scissor lift may use a miniature snap-action switch to change an electrical contact state in response to actuator movement, including for position or limit detection. That general use does not establish that every lift uses a micro switch for home position, or that a particular switch controls a particular lift response.

Start with the lift’s applicable documentation. Confirm whether a micro switch is present, what mechanism acts on it, and what role its signal has in the control circuit. Do not identify a component by location or appearance alone, and do not assume a contact arrangement, normal state, terminal function, or control response from a generic layout.

Once the documented arrangement is clear, keep four possible fault areas distinct: the mechanism may not engage the actuator consistently; the switch may not change contacts consistently; a terminal or wire may interrupt the signal; or the problem may be elsewhere in the control equipment. These are possibilities to distinguish, not conclusions from the intermittent indication alone.

## How the home-position mechanism can interrupt the signal

The documented signal path may run from lift mechanism or linkage, to the switch actuator, through a contact-state change, along wiring, and into a control input. Verify each part of that path against the specific lift documentation. The relationship between the home-position signal and the lift’s controls is not universal.

If the mechanism sometimes reaches or moves the actuator differently, the switch may receive inconsistent mechanical input. That can produce an intermittent indication even if a switch-only check does not reveal a contact problem. Conversely, apparently repeatable actuator movement does not prove that the internal contacts, wiring, or control input work correctly.

Observe only what can be checked under the applicable safe procedure. If the documented mechanism appears not to reach or engage the actuator consistently, that observation supports investigating the mechanical input path. It does not establish why engagement varies or prove the switch is good. Do not bypass guards or enter hazardous areas to watch or move the mechanism.

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not confirm electrical continuity, correct wiring, or the signal received by the control circuit. Nor does the presence of a contact change establish the lift’s response. That response must be determined from design-specific information, not inferred from the component’s general purpose.

## Separate inconsistent actuation from a micro switch fault

If an approved procedure permits an isolated switch check, follow that procedure and keep the switch isolated as it requires. Identify the actuator states to be tested and the expected readings from the applicable documentation. Without a documented contact arrangement and reference states, do not assume which terminals should show continuity in a given condition.

A useful distinction is whether the documented mechanism moves the actuator consistently and whether the isolated switch produces the documented result in each defined state. If actuation is inconsistent, investigate the mechanical input path within the permitted procedure. If actuation is repeatable but isolated results do not match the documented behavior, a switch or contact issue becomes a possibility. Neither finding alone establishes the condition of the installed lift circuit.

A single continuity reading is limited evidence: it applies to the tested switch, isolation condition, actuator state, and method. It does not show that the switch is installed or wired correctly, that a signal reaches the control input, or that the complete lift system functions as intended. Repeated checks are meaningful only when the procedure defines the states and expected behavior. Do not substitute an audible click for a defined electrical check.

If the applicable documentation requires a specified contact-resistance method or limit, use that specification and test procedure. Contact resistance is a measured switch requirement whose method, connection points, and limit must come from the applicable documentation; an improvised measurement does not establish compliance.

## Check wiring and control-path possibilities without assuming the switch is at fault

After distinguishing mechanical engagement from an isolated switch result, consider the installed signal path. A problem at a terminal, in a harness or connection, or elsewhere in the control circuit may resemble an intermittent switch. An isolated switch reading cannot locate those faults because it does not test the full installed path.

Use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity or wiring from physical position, a generic diagram, or an assumed NO/NC state. Any electrical check must follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. If a safe, documented procedure does not identify what to check or how to interpret it, do not improvise a live test or bypass.

Keep evidence attached to the part of the circuit it concerns. A mismatch during an isolated switch check points to a question about that switch under the tested conditions. A switch that meets its documented isolated check still leaves wiring, terminals, and control equipment unresolved. Likewise, an intermittent indication alone does not identify which part of the signal path is responsible.

Do not infer a shutdown, enabling, or other control response from the words “home position.” The response depends on the actual lift design and circuit. A home-position signal by itself also cannot establish the lift’s operating condition or safety.

## When diagnosis must stop

Stop when the applicable safe procedure ends, the circuit or terminal identification is uncertain, or diagnosis would require hazardous access, undocumented tests, bypasses, or adjustments. Refer those conditions, and any unresolved intermittent fault, to qualified service personnel. Do not use an isolated reading or a click as a basis for declaring the lift safe to operate.

The diagnostic conclusion should match the evidence: inconsistent documented engagement suggests a mechanical-input question; an isolated state-defined result that conflicts with documentation suggests a switch question; and neither resolves the installed wiring or control path. For scissor lift home position micro switch intermittent operation, identifying the actual design and separating these fault areas is more defensible than replacing or blaming a component based on the symptom alone.

An **air compressor travel limit micro switch continuity test** checks whether the switch’s contacts change state as its actuator moves from released to operated and back. It does not, by itself, show that the compressor’s complete control circuit works or that the switch is positioned correctly in the equipment. Keep the contact reading, actuator motion, and wiring as separate parts of the diagnosis. Do not infer terminal functions from a switch’s appearance, assume a universal travel distance, or use a meter’s continuity function on an energized circuit.

## What the air compressor travel limit micro switch continuity test checks

A travel-limit micro switch responds to movement at its actuator by changing contact state. The test observes that electrical transition under controlled conditions. Record the released state, move the actuator through its actual available travel, watch for a change, then release it and observe whether the reading returns consistently. The required operating and release behavior depends on the applicable device and equipment information; the component category alone does not establish numerical travel limits.

This test addresses the switch’s contacts, not the full compressor control system. A changing reading does not prove that the control circuit is wired correctly, that the switch is actuated at the intended point during operation, or that the compressor responds as intended. Conversely, no observed change does not identify a single cause. The selected terminals, actuator movement, switch condition, and disconnected wiring all affect what the meter shows.

Keep travel-limit testing distinct from pressure-control testing. This procedure follows actuator movement and contact response. It does not assess compressor pressure, a pressure-control device, a motor starter, or other equipment functions. Do not treat a successful contact transition as evidence about those separate components.

The test also separates two questions that can be confused: does the contact state change when the switch actuator is moved, and does the equipment mechanism move the actuator sufficiently in its installed arrangement? A bench-style manual movement can show the first behavior, but not establish the second. Compare actuator motion with the applicable specification information rather than assuming an adjustment distance.

## Make the travel-limit micro switch safe to test

Before testing, de-energize the compressor equipment and establish that it cannot start unexpectedly. Do not use continuity or resistance mode on a live circuit. For electrical work in the United States workplace context, the cited rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and work on energized parts to be limited to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements. If the required isolation or verification is outside your qualifications or the equipment instructions, stop and have the work handled by qualified personnel.

Use the equipment wiring information and the applicable switch schematic to identify the switch and its terminals. Do not guess from terminal layout, molded markings alone, or wire color. In generic changeover terminology, COM means common, while NO and NC mean normally open and normally closed; verify the exact device schematic before applying those labels. Those names do not establish which contacts should conduct in the particular released or operated state without the device’s documented arrangement.

Disconnect at least one switch lead before measuring. A connection left in the compressor control circuit can create parallel paths through other components, causing a meter reading that does not represent the isolated switch contacts. Keep track of the lead and its original connection so it can be restored correctly. Set the meter to continuity or resistance only after confirming the circuit is de-energized and the measurement path has been isolated as required.

If a lead cannot be identified or safely disconnected, do not substitute an in-circuit reading for an isolated contact test. That reading may reflect another circuit path, and the available evidence would not establish the switch’s own contact behavior.

## Perform the air compressor travel limit micro switch continuity test

With the switch isolated for measurement, place the meter probes on the terminals identified by the applicable wiring information. First leave the actuator released and record the meter response. Do not assume that a particular pair must show continuity merely because it is labeled COM, NO, or NC; the exact contact arrangement and state reference must come from the device information.

Next, move the actuator through its actual travel while observing the meter. Note whether the contact reading changes, and where in the movement the observed transition occurs. Do not force the actuator beyond its permitted movement. Then release it and observe whether the meter returns to the previously recorded state. Repeat the movement only as allowed by the applicable equipment and switch information, watching for consistent operation rather than relying on a single momentary indication.

Compare the observed actuation with the applicable specification sheet and equipment instructions. Pretravel runs from the free position to the operating position; overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These definitions describe different portions of actuator movement, not a universal adjustment procedure. Numerical travel limits are device-specific requirements in the applicable specification sheet, so do not infer a required distance from the fact that the component is a micro switch.

Keep a simple record of the released reading, the reading during actuation, the return reading, and whether the actuator completed the permitted movement. These observations help distinguish an absent electrical transition from insufficient mechanical movement. They do not, on their own, confirm correct installed alignment, wiring, or compressor operation.

## Interpret contact readings alongside actuator travel

If the reading does not change, several possibilities remain: the selected terminals may not be the intended contact pair, the switch contacts may not be changing state, the actuator may not have moved far enough to reach its operating point, or a wiring or measurement setup issue may remain. Recheck terminal identification against the schematic and confirm that the switch is isolated. Then consider actuator movement separately. A no-change reading alone does not prove that the switch has failed.

If the reading changes inconsistently or does not return reliably after release, record that behavior rather than treating one successful transition as conclusive. Unstable contact behavior is one possible explanation; a loose connection or inconsistent actuator movement is another. Inspect connections and mechanical movement as separate diagnostic paths, with power isolated and within applicable equipment instructions. The meter result does not by itself identify which path is responsible.

A clean, repeatable change and return show that the tested contacts responded to the actuator under the test conditions. They do not prove that the installed mechanism moves the actuator far enough at the intended travel limit, that terminal connections are correct in the compressor circuit, or that the rest of the control system works. Check the actual mechanical relationship against the applicable specifications instead of prescribing a universal adjustment distance.

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it is not a substitute for the meter observation. Sound alone does not establish contact continuity, correct terminal selection, or correct actuation in the installed equipment. Likewise, a meter transition during manual movement does not establish that the compressor’s control sequence is correct.

## Verify the repair without a live continuity test

If the observations point to alignment or wiring, address those issues only with power isolated and within the applicable equipment instructions. Do not adjust the compressor’s pressure control or other excluded equipment settings as part of this contact test. Where the cause remains uncertain, preserve the distinction between a switch-contact result and an unresolved mechanical or wiring condition rather than assuming one reading settles the diagnosis.

Before returning equipment to service, restore disconnected conductors to their documented connections and replace protective covers or guards. Do not perform a continuity test while the circuit is energized. After reassembly, any functional check must be appropriate to the equipment instructions and the person’s qualifications; the isolated contact test is not a substitute for that check.

The **air compressor travel limit micro switch continuity test** can establish whether the isolated contacts change state as the actuator moves through the tested range and whether they return on release. It cannot establish the complete control circuit’s operation or prove correct installed positioning. Base conclusions on the contact observations, the independent check of actuator travel, and the applicable device and equipment information.

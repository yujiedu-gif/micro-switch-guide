An **air compressor end of stroke micro switch contact rating** describes an electrical limit under specified operating conditions. It does not, by itself, show that the switch can handle every compressor circuit or that its actuator is mechanically suited to the machine’s end-of-stroke movement. To assess the rating, compare the actual circuit conditions with the exact switch’s documentation, keeping voltage, current, AC or DC, and load category together. Then assess mechanical actuation separately. A single number on a switch is not enough to establish either kind of suitability.

## What the air compressor end of stroke micro switch contact rating means

A contact rating is an electrical limit tied to the conditions stated for that rating. It is not a universal switching capacity that applies to every circuit. Specification-sheet requirements are associated with their stated load and test conditions; the rating must be read within that context (claim_ratings_conditions).

Read the relevant details together: voltage, current, whether the circuit is AC or DC, and the load category. A voltage or current value alone does not establish that the switch is suitable. A value specified for one supply type or load condition should not be treated as applying to another unless the exact switch documentation says so. Likewise, do not infer a permissible load from the switch’s size, appearance, or physical arrangement.

For an air compressor end-of-stroke application, the switch is an electromechanical component whose contacts may be used within a circuit associated with the compressor. The rating concerns the electrical conditions those contacts are specified to handle. It does not describe the force or motion that the compressor mechanism applies to the actuator. Those are separate questions, and evidence about one cannot substitute for evidence about the other.

A useful first step is therefore to identify the exact switch and locate its applicable documentation, including any connection diagram, terminal markings, and rating information. Do not assume that a generic diagram or a familiar-looking terminal layout identifies the contact arrangement of the installed device. The exact device marking or schematic controls, and its documented permissible load must be used rather than a generic physical-layout inference (claim_wiring_diagram_and_rating_boundary; claim_terminal_contact_structure).

## Why compressor load type changes rating interpretation

The load category matters because a rating is tied to specified conditions, not merely to a current value. A resistive-load rating does not automatically establish suitability for a motor or other inductive load. The switch’s stated rating category and instructions must cover the actual load before the rating can be used as evidence of suitability.

A compressor circuit may involve a motor, but the presence of a compressor does not by itself establish what the micro switch contacts directly switch. The contacts might be associated with another part of the control circuit, or the arrangement may differ. Do not infer the switch’s exact circuit function from the equipment name or from the fact that the switch detects a machine position. Establish the actual circuit function from the applicable documentation and circuit information.

If the contacts do switch a motor or another inductive load, do not substitute a resistive rating as if it were equivalent. A rating may be stated for particular load categories and test conditions; unless the actual category and conditions are covered by the switch’s documentation, the available information does not establish whether that use is permitted. Avoid assuming that a single voltage or current number resolves this question.

This is a boundary on interpretation, not a claim that every motor circuit has the same switching behavior or that every compressor uses the same control arrangement. The relevant comparison is between the actual circuit and the conditions documented for the exact switch. If the load type is unknown, or the documentation does not make the applicable category clear, the rating cannot be confidently matched from a number alone.

## Check the circuit against the stated contact conditions

Use a documentation-led comparison rather than an estimate based on a single marking:

1. **Identify the exact switch.** Use its device markings and documentation. Confirm the connection diagram, terminal identification, circuit function, and permissible load for that switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal purpose from physical position alone.
2. **Establish the actual circuit conditions.** Determine the voltage and current at the contacts, whether the circuit is AC or DC, and the load type the contacts switch. Do not assume that the switch directly controls the compressor motor; verify the documented circuit function.
3. **Read the complete applicable rating.** Find the rating information for the exact switch and identify the stated voltage, current, supply type, load category, and test or operating conditions relevant to the circuit. Keep each condition attached to its value.
4. **Compare like with like.** Check the actual circuit against the documented rating under the applicable conditions. Do not transfer a rating across AC and DC, across load categories, or from one set of conditions to another unless the documentation supports that interpretation.
5. **Resolve gaps before drawing a conclusion.** If the load category, actual circuit conditions, or applicable rating is unclear, the evidence is insufficient to establish suitability. Consult the switch documentation and a qualified electrical professional rather than treating an ambiguous marking as approval.

This process determines whether the available rating information can be matched to the known circuit. It does not establish that the whole compressor control system is correctly wired, operates as intended, or is free of other problems. Follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements when verifying electrical information or equipment (claim_wiring_diagram_and_rating_boundary). Do not treat an isolated component rating as a substitute for the actual equipment documentation.

## Keep electrical rating separate from end-of-stroke mechanics

The contact rating addresses electrical loading under specified conditions. It does not state the actuator’s required force, travel, operating point, or allowable movement after operation. Those mechanical details are not established by an electrical rating, and the available evidence does not provide values for them.

For an end-of-stroke arrangement, assess the mechanical interface independently: the equipment mechanism must actuate the switch in the manner specified for the exact device. Do not infer from an adequate-looking electrical rating that the actuator can tolerate the mechanism’s movement, or that the mechanism reaches the required operating state. Conversely, a mechanical arrangement that moves the actuator does not establish that the contacts are rated for the circuit load.

The decision boundary is straightforward: the rating supports an electrical comparison only when the actual circuit conditions and the switch’s applicable stated conditions are known and aligned. Mechanical suitability requires separate information about the specific switch and how it is actuated. If either side is undocumented or uncertain, do not declare the switch suitable based on the other. For an **air compressor end of stroke micro switch contact rating**, the defensible conclusion comes from matching the complete electrical conditions to the exact switch documentation while leaving mechanical requirements as a separate check.

A **cylindrical grinder end of stroke micro switch continuity test** checks whether an isolated switch’s documented contacts change state between defined actuator positions. It does not establish that the grinder’s stroke control, wiring, or complete machine works correctly. Some cylindrical grinders use a mechanically actuated micro switch at stroke end, but the switch’s presence and arrangement must be verified on the actual machine rather than assumed.

The useful comparison is between the switch’s released and operated actuator states, measured across the documented contact pair under the same isolated setup. Interpret those readings only against the contact arrangement and reference condition documented for that switch. A click, an end-of-stroke event, or a changed meter indication is not, by itself, proof of correct machine operation.

## What a cylindrical grinder end of stroke micro switch continuity test can establish

The test can show whether a specific isolated contact pair has continuity in each of two defined actuator states. It is a component-level observation, not a test of the grinder’s entire stroke-control function. The machine’s end-of-stroke event does not automatically establish that the switch actuator reached its operated position: the mechanism may not move the actuator, or its movement may be incomplete. Observe the actuator itself rather than inferring its position from the grinder’s motion or from a reported symptom.

Some cylindrical grinder designs use a micro switch mechanically actuated near the end of a stroke; others may use a different arrangement. Confirm that the actual machine has a micro switch and identify how its mechanism acts on the switch before treating the switch as the source of a problem. A lever, cam, or linkage may be part of the external actuation path, but its presence and function must be established on the machine being examined.

A continuity comparison answers a limited question about the contacts and test setup. It cannot establish that the switch is correctly positioned, that the control circuit is wired as intended, or that the grinder will respond correctly. Keep those questions separate when interpreting any result.

## Prepare the micro switch for a continuity test

Do not make continuity measurements on an energized circuit. De-energization and safe isolation must follow the applicable machine-specific procedure. If isolation is uncertain, stop rather than attempting a measurement. Disconnect the switch from the circuit as appropriate under that procedure so the reading concerns the switch contacts rather than an installed circuit path. Work that requires energized measurements, or that falls outside the applicable safe procedure, belongs with qualified personnel.

Identify the switch terminals from the connection diagram and terminal markings documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer COM, NO, or NC from a terminal’s physical position or appearance. Those labels and contact functions are meaningful only when they are documented for the switch and interpreted at the stated reference condition. If the relevant terminal information or contact arrangement is unavailable, the expected reading is unknown; do not substitute a generic terminal layout.

Before testing, note whether the grinder mechanism actually moves the actuator through the intended stroke. This observation helps distinguish an actuation-path issue from a contact reading, but it does not verify the switch electrically. Avoid adjusting the stroke, stops, or linkage as part of this continuity check. The task here is to observe the existing actuator states and test the isolated contacts, not to alter the machine’s setup.

Use a consistent test arrangement for both readings: the same isolated switch, the same documented contact pair, and the same measurement method. A change in setup between states can make a comparison misleading. If the switch cannot be isolated or its terminals cannot be identified from documentation, the measurement does not provide a reliable basis for interpreting contact behavior.

## Cylindrical grinder end of stroke micro switch continuity test: compare defined actuator states

First identify the released actuator state from the switch’s documented reference condition. With the switch isolated and the meter connected across the documented contact pair, record the continuity indication while the actuator is released. Then move the actuator to its defined operated state, if that movement can be made safely and consistently, and record the indication across the same pair. Keep the test setup unchanged between readings.

Compare the two observations with the contact behavior documented for that contact arrangement and reference condition. Do not assume a particular pair must be open or closed merely because it is labeled, appears in a familiar location, or is associated with the grinder’s end of stroke. If the documentation does not establish the expected states, the readings alone cannot tell you what the intended behavior should be.

The grinder reaching the end of a stroke is not a substitute for defining the switch state. Verify whether the external mechanism has actually moved the actuator to the operated position. If the actuator remains released, a reading taken at the machine’s stroke end is still a released-state reading for the switch. Conversely, observing actuator movement does not by itself establish electrical continuity.

A click may indicate mechanical movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove that the contacts have continuity, that the terminals were identified correctly, or that the circuit is suitable for its load. Likewise, a changed meter indication is an observation under the test setup, not a complete diagnosis. Record which contact pair and actuator state produced each reading, then interpret the comparison only within the documented conditions.

## Separate switch readings from end-of-stroke faults

If the actuator does not move, or does not appear to reach its intended operated position, consider the external mechanical actuation path as a separate possible source of the symptom. A lever, cam, linkage, or other machine feature may be involved, but the actual arrangement must be verified. An absent or incomplete actuator movement does not establish that the switch’s internal contacts have failed.

If the isolated readings differ from the documented behavior, the switch or the test setup may need further evaluation. Check that the correct documented contact pair was measured and that the released and operated states were defined correctly. A single unexpected result does not identify its cause conclusively, and a click cannot settle the question. Do not infer a specific failure or repair from the reading alone.

If the isolated readings appear consistent with documented contact behavior but the grinder still malfunctions, treat that as a separate machine-side problem. Terminal identification, wiring, control logic, or another cause elsewhere in the equipment may be involved. The component test does not show which, if any, of these is responsible. Diagnosing or repairing the complete grinder control circuit is outside the scope of this test.

Electrical ratings also require their own documentation. Use only a rating explicitly stated for the applicable current type, load, and test condition; a rating for one condition does not establish another. A continuity result is not evidence that the switch is suitable for the grinder’s circuit or load.

## When the continuity result is not enough

An isolated continuity test cannot establish installed-circuit safety, the grinder’s control behavior, or the switch’s suitability for an electrical load. Even readings that match documented contact behavior establish only what was observed across the tested pair in the defined states and isolated setup. They do not validate the external actuation path or the rest of the machine.

Stop and use qualified personnel if safe isolation is uncertain, energized measurements are needed, wiring or terminal identification is undocumented, or a machine-side fault remains unresolved. Do not treat a component reading as permission to operate the grinder or as proof that the complete assembly is good or bad. The **cylindrical grinder end of stroke micro switch continuity test** is a limited comparison of isolated contacts at defined actuator states; its conclusions should remain within that boundary.

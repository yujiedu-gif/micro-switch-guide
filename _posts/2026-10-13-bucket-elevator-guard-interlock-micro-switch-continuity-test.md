A **bucket elevator guard interlock micro switch continuity test** can show whether a documented contact path is continuous in the isolated switch at a specified actuator position. It cannot, by itself, establish how the guard interlock behaves when installed or whether the elevator is safe. The guard and its linkage may move the switch actuator, but the electrical consequence depends on the actual circuit and equipment design.

Interpret a reading only after the switch’s terminal identities, contact arrangement, reference condition, actuator state, and safe isolation have been established from applicable documentation. Do not infer a contact state from whether the guard appears open or closed, from a switch’s physical layout, or from a generic wiring diagram. If the documented procedure or conditions are unavailable, leave the test to qualified personnel.

## What a bucket elevator guard interlock micro switch continuity test can establish

The test is limited to continuity through the isolated micro switch’s documented contacts while the actuator is held in a defined position. In that narrow scope, it can indicate whether the measured contact path is continuous under the stated test conditions. The meaning of that observation depends on which terminals are being measured, how the contact arrangement is documented, and which actuator state is being checked.

A guard or linkage may mechanically move a micro switch actuator. That relationship alone does not establish what the switch contacts do in a particular guard position or what the elevator’s control system does with a contact change. The installed circuit, control logic, and equipment design determine those effects. This test should therefore be treated as a component-level observation, not as a test of the guard interlock as a complete system.

A continuity result does not establish that the switch is correctly wired, that the intended control response occurs, or that the guard and elevator provide a particular protective function. Nor does it show that the switch is suitable for the installed electrical load. Ratings must be checked for the applicable current type, load, and test condition; a rating for one condition does not establish another. Use the specification that applies to the exact switch and equipment.

## Identify the micro switch contact arrangement and actuator state

Before interpreting any measurement, establish the terminal identities and contact arrangement from the exact switch’s connection diagram and markings. Also identify the reference condition used by the documentation. Do not assign terminals by position, appearance, or a generic diagram. Terminal identification and the switch’s electrical state are separate questions: a reading has no clear meaning if the measured terminals or reference state are uncertain.

Define the actuator position being tested. Record or describe it in terms that identify the actual switch state, rather than assuming that a guard-open or guard-closed position corresponds to a particular contact state. The guard’s movement, linkage, and switch actuation may depend on the equipment design. The available information does not establish a universal relationship between guard position and contact continuity.

Compare a measurement only with the documented contact arrangement and the specified actuator position. If documentation defines expected contact behavior for that state, use those stated conditions. If it does not, the result can be reported as an observation, but it cannot be labeled correct or incorrect on the basis of an assumed guard or actuator relationship. Do not turn an unknown contact arrangement into an expected reading.

## Check continuity only under a safe, documented isolation procedure

Use a continuity measurement only when the switch has been isolated under the applicable, documented procedure and the required qualifications are met. This article does not specify an isolation sequence, identify equipment-specific isolation points, or provide permission to work on an elevator. Follow the applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. If safe isolation cannot be established, do not proceed; leave the test to qualified personnel.

With isolation established, test only the identified switch contacts and the defined actuator state, using a measurement method appropriate to the documented procedure. Keep the tested state and terminal pair attached to the result. A reading without that context is not a useful basis for deciding what the switch is doing. Do not treat an observation made on the installed circuit as equivalent to a measurement of the isolated switch, because other circuit elements may affect the observation.

The result should be recorded narrowly: which documented terminals were measured, which actuator position was used, whether the switch was isolated under the applicable procedure, and what the measurement showed. Do not extend that record into a claim about guard behavior, circuit operation, or equipment safety. If the procedure, terminal identities, or required conditions are unclear, the evidence is insufficient for a meaningful interpretation.

## Interpret readings without diagnosing the whole interlock

Compare the observation with the documented contact arrangement for the actuator state actually tested. A reading that differs from the documented expectation is a discrepancy to investigate, not proof of a single cause. It may relate to the switch contacts, mechanical input, terminal identification, measurement setup, wiring, or another part of the circuit. A reading that agrees with the documented expectation establishes only the measured contact result under those test conditions.

Keep possible causes separate. A mechanical input problem concerns whether the actuator reaches the intended state. A contact finding concerns the measured path inside the isolated switch. A terminal-identification issue concerns whether the correct terminals were selected. A wiring or host-equipment issue concerns parts beyond the isolated component. The continuity check alone does not distinguish all of these possibilities or diagnose the entire interlock.

In particular, a switch-only result does not prove that the installed wiring is correct, that the control logic responds as intended, that the guard performs a particular function, or that the elevator is safe. Those conclusions require evidence about the actual equipment and circuit that this test does not provide. Report the switch result with its conditions, preserve uncertainty about unresolved causes, and stop at the component-level finding unless an applicable equipment-specific procedure supports further work.

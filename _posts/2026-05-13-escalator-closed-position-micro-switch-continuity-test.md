An **escalator closed position micro switch continuity test** checks whether an isolated switch contact matches its documented state at a defined actuator position. The phrase “closed position” describes a position in the escalator mechanism; it does not, by itself, establish which contacts should have continuity. The expected reading must come from the documentation for the specific switch and equipment.

Keep this check to the component level. It cannot establish that an installed escalator circuit is correctly wired or that the escalator operates safely. Electrical testing must follow an approved procedure, including applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. If the state, switch, terminal pair, or isolation boundary is unclear, stop and refer the work to qualified escalator personnel.

## escalator closed position micro switch continuity test

First define what “closed position” means for the particular escalator mechanism, then identify the corresponding actuator position for the switch. Use the applicable equipment documentation to establish that relationship. A miniature snap-action switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement and is commonly used for position or limit detection, but that general function does not define the state of this particular switch.

Do not infer that the escalator’s named closed position means COM–NC continuity, or that it corresponds to any particular escalator condition. A contact state must be referenced to the documented switch terminals and specified mechanical state. The position name alone is not a substitute for that information.

This procedure applies only where the escalator uses the specified micro switch arrangement and its state and terminals can be identified from applicable documentation. Some equipment may use a different arrangement; the presence of a position-detection function does not prove that every escalator uses this switch or contact configuration. If the documented actuator position cannot be identified, the test has no established reference point and should not proceed on an assumed state.

## Safety and isolation before checking continuity

Continuity testing belongs within an approved escalator procedure performed by qualified personnel. Follow applicable deenergization and qualification requirements before carrying out electrical work. Do not test a live circuit, improvise access to the switch, or treat a component-level reading as evidence that the escalator is safe to operate.

The approved procedure must define the isolation boundary and permit the switch to be tested as an isolated component. The switch should be disconnected as required by that procedure; otherwise, a reading may reflect other circuit paths rather than the switch contact alone. This article does not supply escalator access, isolation, or disconnection steps. Those details depend on the applicable equipment documentation and qualified procedure.

Stop if the switch identity, actuator state, terminal pair, isolation boundary, or approved access method is uncertain. Do not resolve an uncertainty by guessing from the switch’s physical layout, the escalator position label, or a continuity reading taken from an installed circuit. For terminal identification and load or circuit applicability, use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment.

## How to test the isolated micro switch in the defined state

Use the documentation for the specific switch and equipment to identify the terminal pair to be checked and the contact state specified for the defined actuator position. Do not assume terminal names, terminal locations, or an expected open or closed reading. A physical layout or position label alone does not establish the required pair or reference state.

With the switch safely isolated and disconnected as required by the approved procedure, place its actuator in the documented position corresponding to the escalator’s defined closed position. Use a continuity meter according to its instructions and check only the documented terminal pair. Compare the result with the specified contact state for that pair at that actuator position.

The test is meaningful only with those conditions stated: the switch is isolated, the actuator is in the documented state, and the measurement is across the documented terminals. If any of these conditions cannot be confirmed, the reading cannot be interpreted against a reliable reference. Do not substitute a test of the installed circuit for this isolated component check.

## What the continuity result can, and cannot, show

A reading that matches the documented state supports only the isolated contact check for the tested terminal pair and actuator position. It does not establish wiring integrity, control logic, other equipment operation, or overall escalator condition. Nor does it show that the actuator will reliably reach that position in the installed mechanism.

A result that differs from the specified state is a reason to investigate, not a complete diagnosis. Possible issues include a problem with the switch contact, actuator movement or linkage, the definition of the tested state, or terminal identification. A single isolated reading does not distinguish among those possibilities. The relevant documentation and qualified assessment are needed to determine what the result means.

An installed-circuit symptom can also arise from wiring or other equipment, rather than from the isolated switch contact. Because the component check does not test those parts of the system, a matching switch reading cannot rule them out. Keep the conclusion narrow: report whether the tested contact matched its documented state under the defined conditions, and leave broader escalator diagnosis unresolved unless it is established through the applicable process.

## When to stop and refer the escalator for diagnosis

Do not bypass or adjust the switch based on a continuity result. Stop if the state cannot be defined, the terminal pair cannot be verified, the approved isolation or access method is unclear, or the reading does not match the documented state and the cause remains uncertain. Refer unresolved questions about the switch, actuator or linkage, wiring, or wider equipment to qualified escalator personnel using the applicable documentation.

Keep testing another position separate. An **escalator open position micro switch continuity test** concerns a different defined state, and its expected contact behavior may differ. Do not transfer the closed-position reference or expected reading to that separate check.

The **escalator closed position micro switch continuity test** is therefore a limited comparison: isolate the identified switch under an approved procedure, check the documented terminal pair with the actuator in the documented closed-position state, and compare the reading with the specified contact state. The result describes that component check only; it does not by itself diagnose or establish the condition of the escalator.

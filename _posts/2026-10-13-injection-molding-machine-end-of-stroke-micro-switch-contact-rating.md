An **injection molding machine end of stroke micro switch contact rating** is a documented electrical limit for the switch contacts under stated conditions. It does not, by itself, describe the machine’s control behavior or establish that the switch is suitable for a particular circuit. The rating phrase alone gives no numerical value and does not identify the load being switched.

To interpret the rating, keep the switch’s specified voltage, current type, load, and test conditions together. Then distinguish those documented contact limits from the actual electrical conditions and function of the machine circuit. End-of-stroke describes an application context, not a guaranteed contact state, terminal arrangement, or stop response.

## What injection molding machine end of stroke micro switch contact rating means

A contact rating is a specification tied to defined electrical conditions. Under MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, electrical rating values are requirements associated with the stated load and test conditions. They are not universal switching capacities that apply regardless of how a switch is wired or used.

For an end-of-stroke micro switch, the rating can therefore establish only what its applicable documentation states for the covered condition. The words “end of stroke” do not establish a voltage, current, load type, or numerical rating. Nor do they show whether the switch contacts directly interrupt a machine load or provide an input to a control circuit.

This distinction matters because an application label describes where a switch may be used, while an electrical rating describes a documented contact capability under specified conditions. One cannot be substituted for the other. Without the exact rating information and its conditions, the application phrase does not support a conclusion about electrical capacity or suitability.

The rating also does not establish the machine’s response when the switch actuates. A switch may provide an input to a control circuit, but the actual function depends on the machine’s documented wiring and design. The phrase “end of stroke” alone does not prove that actuation stops a machine, identifies which contacts are used, or reveals their state at any reference condition.

A useful interpretation is therefore limited: identify the rating and the conditions attached to it, then compare those conditions with the documented circuit. If either side of that comparison is missing, the rating phrase alone cannot settle whether the arrangement is appropriate.

## Read the rating with its conditions

Read each stated value together with its voltage, AC or DC designation, load type, and applicable test condition. A value detached from those qualifiers is incomplete for interpretation. Use only the rating explicitly stated for the applicable current type, load, and test condition. An AC rating does not establish a DC rating, and a rating for one load or condition does not establish another.

Do not transfer a rating between conditions merely because the switch is described as a micro switch or because it is installed at an end-of-stroke point. The relevant specification sheet must support the particular condition being evaluated. If the marking is missing, illegible, or separated from the specification that defines its scope, treat the rating as unresolved rather than filling in a value by inference.

The same boundary applies to statements that provide only one part of the electrical description. A voltage value without the applicable current type, load, and test conditions may not establish the full rating needed for comparison. Likewise, a current value should not be treated as a general capacity independent of its documented voltage and load conditions.

This is not a request to infer missing data from the host machine, a familiar switch shape, or a typical control arrangement. The supported interpretation comes from the applicable specification sheet and the conditions it states. If the documentation does not connect a value to the relevant condition, the available evidence does not establish that value for the circuit under review.

Keep the qualifiers close to the value when recording or comparing it. For example, do not restate a documented value later as though it were an unconditional rating. If a specification covers one current type and load, preserve those limits in any summary; do not broaden the statement to another type or load.

## Separate the switch contacts from the machine control circuit

An end-of-stroke micro switch may act as an input to a machine control circuit where that arrangement is documented. In that case, the contact rating and the electrical conditions at the switch contacts still need to be interpreted from the applicable switch documentation. The machine’s larger load or control function cannot be assumed to be switched directly by those contacts.

Conversely, the application name does not prove that the switch only signals a control circuit. The actual connection must be established from the documented circuit information. Avoid deciding either way from the words “end of stroke,” from a presumed machine sequence, or from the physical position of the switch alone.

The contact rating does not identify the terminals used, the circuit function, or the contact state. Those details require the applicable connection diagram and terminal markings. A generic assumption about physical layout is not a substitute for documented terminal identification. Without the circuit information, the switch’s role in the machine remains unresolved.

The same limit applies to machine behavior. The presence of an end-of-stroke switch does not establish what the control system does when its input changes. It does not, by itself, establish a stop response, a particular sequence, or a safety function. Those conclusions require documentation for the specific machine and circuit; they cannot be derived from the component’s rating or application label.

Keeping these questions separate prevents a rating from being mistaken for a complete circuit assessment. The switch documentation addresses the conditions under which its contacts are rated. The machine documentation identifies how the switch is connected and what role that connection has. Both kinds of information may be needed, and neither should be replaced by an assumption about the other.

## Verify what the available documentation establishes

Start with legible switch markings and the applicable specification sheet. Identify the rating information actually documented, including the voltage, AC or DC designation, load type, and test condition. Preserve these conditions when interpreting the value. If the marking cannot be read or its scope cannot be matched to a specification, the rating remains unresolved.

Then consult the documented connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load for the exact switch and equipment. Use these sources rather than a generic inference from terminal position or from the machine’s end-of-stroke label. The circuit information is needed to understand whether the contacts provide an input or switch a load, and what electrical conditions apply at those contacts.

Compare only information that is actually documented. If the rating conditions are known but the relevant circuit conditions are not, suitability cannot be determined from the rating alone. If the circuit is documented but the switch’s applicable rating is missing, illegible, or does not cover the stated condition, the comparison remains incomplete. An application description cannot fill either gap.

This verification boundary is also a practical limit on conclusions: a match between a stated rating and a documented circuit condition supports only that comparison under the stated scope. It does not establish the machine’s complete behavior or prove facts that the documents do not show. Keep machine electrical work within the applicable deenergization and qualification requirements; do not infer a safe procedure from the rating information.

In short, the **injection molding machine end of stroke micro switch contact rating** establishes a documented contact limit only for its stated electrical conditions. Interpreting it requires the applicable switch specification and enough circuit documentation to identify the relevant connection and load conditions. If those details are absent, the evidence does not establish suitability.

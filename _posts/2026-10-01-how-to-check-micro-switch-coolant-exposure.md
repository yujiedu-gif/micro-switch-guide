Possible coolant exposure does not by itself establish that a micro switch has failed, and a normal continuity reading does not establish that the switch or its equipment is safe. How to check micro switch coolant exposure depends on first isolating the equipment, then separating visible exposure evidence from a bounded check of the isolated switch. If the equipment cannot be safely isolated, or access is uncertain, stop and use qualified service rather than probing the assembly.

A micro switch is an electromechanical component within a larger circuit. Coolant-related findings may involve the switch, its terminals, nearby wiring or connectors, an enclosure, or other equipment. These are distinct parts of the assessment: evidence on one does not prove the condition of the others. The result should be treated as limited to what was observed or tested under defined conditions.

## How to check micro switch coolant exposure safely

Do not evaluate or probe an energized assembly. Before inspection or testing, isolate the equipment under its applicable procedure. The cited United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements. If you cannot establish that isolation has been completed, or cannot safely access the switch, stop and refer the assessment to qualified service.

Keep the boundary between an installed circuit and an isolated component clear. A reading taken on a switch while it remains connected to equipment may be affected by other paths in that circuit, so it is not the same as a check of the switch alone. The bounded continuity check described below is for a switch isolated from the circuit. It does not tell you whether the installed wiring is correct, whether the equipment circuit behaves as intended, or whether the assembly is safe to operate.

Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment, rather than inferring terminal identity from physical layout. If those details are unavailable, do not guess which terminals to test or what result to expect. Likewise, do not treat a generic switch arrangement as proof of the contact arrangement in the unit being assessed. Where isolation, documentation, or the expected behavior is unclear, qualified service is the appropriate boundary.

## Inspect the switch and surrounding area for exposure evidence

With the equipment isolated and accessible, distinguish what can be observed from what those observations establish. Note visible liquid or residue around the switch, housing, terminals, wiring, and connectors. Also note visible corrosion or housing damage. These observations can document evidence of contact with coolant or other external change, but they do not prove that coolant reached internal contacts or that the contacts have failed.

Record findings by location rather than describing the whole assembly as simply “wet” or “damaged.” Residue on a nearby connector is evidence about that connector area; it does not establish the internal condition of the switch. Visible evidence on the switch housing does not, by itself, establish how far any liquid traveled. Similarly, an affected wire or terminal may be a separate issue from the switch’s internal contact condition. The component and the surrounding electrical parts require separate consideration.

The reverse inference is also unwarranted: no visible residue does not establish that coolant did not enter the switch. The extent of exposure may be unknown, and the available observations cannot resolve internal condition unless a supported check does so. Do not use appearance alone to declare the switch electrically sound or failed. Nor does an apparently clean switch establish that its wiring, connectors, enclosure, or equipment circuit is unaffected.

Keep the inspection within the safe access available under the equipment procedure. If examining the switch would require uncertain disassembly, if exposure extent cannot be determined, or if the observed condition leaves the equipment’s status unclear, do not fill those gaps with assumptions. Record the observation and leave the unresolved assessment to qualified service using applicable equipment documentation.

## Check continuity only under defined conditions

A continuity result is meaningful only when the switch is isolated from the circuit, the tested actuator state is defined, and the expected contact behavior is documented for that switch. Use a suitable method supported by the applicable procedure. Do not probe an energized assembly or use an assumed terminal layout. If a suitable method, terminal identification, or expected result is not available, the continuity check cannot be interpreted reliably from a generic diagram or physical appearance.

Identify COM, NO, and NC only when the exact switch’s contact arrangement is documented. Those labels refer to a particular documented arrangement and reference condition; they should not be assigned by guesswork. For each reading, state the actuator condition being tested and compare the result with the documented behavior for that condition. A reading without a defined actuator state or a supported expected result is not enough to determine whether the switch behaves correctly.

Treat an unexpected or unstable reading as a finding to investigate, not as proof of one unique cause. It may indicate a switch-related issue, but the limited check does not identify its cause or rule out other problems. A click may indicate mechanical movement, but it does not verify continuity, contact condition, correct wiring, or suitability for a load. A normal reading, in turn, applies only to the tested switch state and test conditions; it cannot establish the condition of every actuator position or the entire assembly.

Do not extend a continuity result into a claim about operation under load or coolant suitability. The documented permissible load and exact equipment connection information are relevant to interpreting the application, but a continuity check alone does not establish either. If the test conditions, contact arrangement, or expected behavior are unknown, report that limitation instead of calling the switch good or bad.

## Decide what the check can and cannot establish

The assessment has separate evidence layers: external observations, an isolated switch check under defined conditions, and the condition and behavior of the installed equipment. Visible coolant evidence can justify recording an exposure concern, but it does not prove internal contact failure. A normal isolated reading addresses only the tested switch states and test conditions. Neither finding establishes that the surrounding wiring, connectors, enclosure, or equipment circuit is functioning correctly or is safe.

A result also cannot establish whether the switch is suitable for coolant exposure. That question depends on information about the exact switch and its documented conditions, which may not be available. Do not infer suitability from a normal continuity reading, a clean exterior, or an assumed construction. If the exposure extent, switch construction, contact behavior, or applicable documentation remains unknown, the assessment remains incomplete.

The practical decision boundary is therefore limited: preserve the observations, report the exact states and conditions of any isolated readings, and do not treat either as approval to operate the equipment. If safe isolation cannot be verified, if the expected behavior is undocumented, or if the wider circuit remains uncertain, leave the decision to qualified service and applicable documentation. How to check micro switch coolant exposure is not a single pass-or-fail test; it is a cautious assessment whose conclusions must not exceed the evidence gathered.

Choosing a switch for a specific mechanism starts with the job it must perform, not its appearance. To understand how to choose a micro switch, define the switching task, identify the electrical load, and compare the required actuator movement and installation conditions with documentation for the exact candidate. A familiar shape or a rating from a different device is not evidence of suitability.

The available information does not identify a particular switch or establish dimensions, terminal functions, compatibility, environmental performance, or application suitability. Treat those details as unresolved until confirmed in applicable documentation. The following process helps organize what to verify without assuming that a generic description applies to a specific part.

## How to choose a micro switch for an application

First describe what the mechanism must cause the contacts to do and when that change must occur. Record the machine or equipment function, the point in the operating cycle when switching is needed, and the movement available at the switch actuator. This defines the task before physical size or resemblance becomes part of the comparison.

Next identify the actual circuit and load. Gather the voltage, current, AC or DC type, load type, and relevant operating or test conditions. Compare these facts with the applicable switch specification sheet and equipment documentation. A general label such as “micro switch” does not establish electrical capacity or suitability for a particular circuit.

Then check how the switch is installed and actuated. The candidate must have documented dimensions and mounting details that fit the equipment, and its actuator must be reachable by the mechanism through the required movement. Do not infer fit, function, or compatibility from photographs or a similar-looking housing. If required facts are missing, the choice remains unverified rather than confirmed by appearance.

This process is a screening method, not a substitute for exact-part evidence. Keep the candidate’s identity and application unresolved until the relevant documentation confirms its ratings, terminal function, mechanical characteristics, and installation requirements. The next steps separate those checks so that one supported fact is not mistaken for proof of overall suitability.

## Define the switching task and electrical load

Write down the circuit function the switch must perform, but do not assume terminal labels or contact arrangements based on a generic diagram. The connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load must be documented for the exact switch and equipment. This article does not establish how to wire common, normally open, or normally closed terminals.

Record the load’s voltage and current, whether the circuit is AC or DC, the load type, and the conditions under which switching occurs. Rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. A value stated for one load or test condition does not establish a rating for a different one.

Use only a rating explicitly stated for the applicable current type, load, and test condition. In particular, an AC rating does not establish a DC rating, or vice versa. Check the applicable specification sheet rather than transferring a value across current types or conditions. If the documentation does not cover the actual circuit, the available evidence is insufficient to confirm the candidate.

Keep the electrical check separate from the mechanical check. A switch that appears to fit, or whose actuator can be reached, is not thereby shown to handle the load. Likewise, a documented electrical rating alone does not establish that the switch’s dimensions, terminal function, or operating characteristics suit the mechanism. Confirm each requirement against documentation for the exact switch and equipment.

## Match actuator movement to the mechanism

Describe the motion the mechanism can deliver at the actuator, including its direction and available distance or angle where known. Compare that movement with documented operating characteristics for the exact candidate. Do not assume that two switches with similar shapes need the same force or travel, or that the mechanism will reliably reach the required position.

MIL-PRF-8805K section 6.5 distinguishes actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. In that terminology, operating force, also called actuating force, is the force or torque applied to the actuating mechanism to operate the contacts. Pretravel is the distance or angle through which the actuator moves from free position to operating position. Overtravel is the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved.

These terms describe different aspects of force, position, and movement; they are not interchangeable. Use the terminology and values given in the applicable documentation, and do not infer them from actuator appearance. The definitions do not supply numerical requirements for a particular switch or mechanism. If the mechanism’s available movement or the candidate’s relevant characteristics are unknown, the match cannot be confirmed.

## Check installation and environmental conditions

Confirm mounting dimensions, actuator access, and other installation details in documentation for the exact switch and equipment. A similar outline or apparent mounting arrangement is not proof that the candidate fits. The supplied information does not specify dimensions, mounting details, or an exact model, so those fields must remain open until verified.

Consider the environment in which the switch will be installed and the protection the application requires. IEC 60529 classifies degrees of protection provided by enclosures under that standard. That scope does not, by itself, establish that an unspecified switch is protected to a particular degree or is suitable for a particular environment. Confirm any claimed protection level and its applicability in documentation for the exact product and installation.

Do not treat an enclosure-protection classification as evidence of electrical ratings, terminal function, mechanical fit, or overall application compatibility. Those are separate checks. If environmental exposure matters and the candidate’s applicable documentation is unavailable or incomplete, the needed protection is unresolved; appearance alone cannot fill the gap.

## Verify the candidate before treating it as compatible

For an exact candidate, compare its documented terminal markings and connection diagram with the equipment’s required circuit function. Use the documented permissible load and applicable electrical rating, including the stated AC or DC type, load, and test conditions. Do not transfer a rating from another switch or from a different condition.

Also compare dimensions, mounting information, actuator characteristics, and relevant environmental documentation with the actual installation. Compatibility requires evidence for the requirements that matter to the application; matching one feature does not establish the others. A photograph, similar housing, or shared generic description cannot establish exact identity, ratings, terminals, or fit.

The available source information provides definitions and interpretation boundaries, but no specifications for a particular candidate. It therefore cannot confirm a model’s dimensions, terminals, ratings, compatibility, manufacturer, or application. Keep each unverified field unresolved and seek documentation that identifies the exact switch and equipment. Do not treat a candidate as compatible unless the applicable records support the required checks.

## What information to collect before choosing a micro switch

Collect a concise application record before evaluating candidates: the switching task and circuit function; load voltage and current; AC or DC type; load type; and relevant operating and test conditions. Record the mechanism’s available actuator movement and the force or travel characteristics that its design requires, using the terminology in the applicable documentation.

Also gather the equipment’s mounting constraints, required dimensions, actuator access, environmental exposure, and any enclosure-protection requirement. For each candidate, request or locate exact-part documentation for terminal identification, connection diagram, permissible load, electrical ratings, mechanical characteristics, dimensions, and applicable environmental information. Confirm that the documents apply to the candidate and equipment under consideration.

If any needed value, condition, or identity is missing, mark it as unknown rather than filling it in from appearance or a rating stated for another condition. The choice is supported only to the extent that the application requirements and exact-candidate documentation can be compared. Where that comparison cannot be made, the available information is insufficient to establish suitability.

This concludes the explanation of how to choose a micro switch.

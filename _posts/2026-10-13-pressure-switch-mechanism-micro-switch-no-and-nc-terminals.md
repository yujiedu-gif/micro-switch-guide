## How the pressure switch mechanism micro switch NO and NC terminals relate

In some pressure-responsive designs, a change in pressure moves an element within the assembly. A mechanical connection may transmit that movement to the actuator of a micro switch. When the actuator reaches the switch’s operating position, the switch’s contact arrangement changes state according to its design. This describes a possible mechanism, not a universal construction: not every pressure switch uses a micro switch, and the available information does not establish the internal construction of any particular model.

The micro switch is a component within the possible mechanism, not another name for the complete pressure switch. The pressure-responsive assembly supplies or controls the movement; the micro switch responds to mechanical actuation; and the connected circuit determines how a contact change is used. These are separate parts of the causal chain. A description of one does not, by itself, establish the behavior of the others.

That distinction matters when interpreting a pressure switch. Seeing a contact label does not reveal how pressure reaches the actuator. Knowing that a pressure-responsive mechanism moves does not reveal which contacts are present. And knowing that contacts change state does not establish whether the host equipment starts, stops, signals, or takes another action. Each step depends on the documented design and circuit.

The mechanism can therefore be summarized conditionally: pressure-responsive movement may be mechanically transmitted to a micro switch actuator, and the switch contacts may change state when actuated. The exact parts, movement, contact arrangement, and system effect cannot be filled in from the general term “pressure switch.”

## What NO and NC mean at a stated reference condition

NO means normally open, and NC means normally closed. These labels describe contact states at a specified reference condition for the documented contact arrangement. The reference condition must be known before the labels can be interpreted for a particular switch. Without it, the words “normally open” and “normally closed” do not identify the relevant pressure state or establish what the contacts are doing at a given moment.

The labels describe contacts, not pressure thresholds. They do not say that a contact changes at a particular pressure, identify a setpoint, or state whether pressure is increasing or decreasing when operation occurs. Nor do they establish the behavior of the pressure-responsive mechanism. A pressure value and a contact-state label answer different questions: one concerns the assembly’s pressure-related behavior, while the other describes the contacts at a defined reference condition.

These labels also do not determine the connected equipment’s response. A contact-state change may be used by a circuit, but the effect depends on the circuit and the design. NO or NC alone does not show whether the host system will run, stop, signal, or respond in another way. It is therefore inaccurate to translate a contact label directly into a system action without the relevant circuit information.

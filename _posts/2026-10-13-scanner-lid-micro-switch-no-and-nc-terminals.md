A scanner lid micro switch NO and NC terminals describe contact states at a specified reference condition, not a guaranteed response from the scanner when its lid moves. To identify the contacts, use the switch’s documented markings or connection diagram and, when appropriate, check continuity with the switch isolated from the equipment. Do not infer a terminal’s function from its physical position or assume that every scanner lid uses the same arrangement.

The key distinction is between the switch’s contacts and the scanner’s lid-detection behavior. A lid or linkage may move the switch actuator, but the equipment response depends on the actual circuit and design. A contact reading can help identify or check the isolated switch; it does not by itself establish how the scanner is wired or whether the complete assembly functions correctly.

## Direct answer: what scanner lid micro switch NO and NC terminals mean

NO and NC describe contact states at the reference condition specified for the particular switch. In that condition, a normally open contact is open, and a normally closed contact is closed. These labels are not unconditional promises about whether current flows in an installed scanner circuit. Current in the equipment depends on the wiring and circuit, as well as the contact state.

The reference condition matters. Read the switch documentation for the condition used to define its contact labels, including the stated actuator position or state where provided. Do not assume that a label describes the contact state while the scanner lid is open, while it is closed, or at an arbitrary point in actuator travel. The lid’s position and the switch’s specified reference condition are related only through the actual mechanical arrangement.

The labels also do not reveal what the scanner will do. A contact change might be part of a lid-detection circuit, but the switch labels alone do not establish whether the scanner enables operation, stops, displays a message, or takes another action. Those outcomes depend on the equipment’s circuit and control design. A micro switch may be actuated by the lid or by a linkage, but the presence of a switch does not establish a particular scanner behavior.

For identification, begin with the exact switch’s terminal markings and connection diagram. Use those documented details rather than guessing from a familiar-looking package or terminal layout. If the documentation does not establish a terminal function or reference condition, treat it as unknown. A physical arrangement that resembles another switch is not enough to assign NO, NC, or a common terminal.

## How NO, NC, and COM relate to the micro switch contacts

NO and NC are contact designations tied to the specified reference condition. They describe whether the relevant contact path is open or closed in that condition. They do not, on their own, describe the voltage, current, or signal present in the scanner, nor do they explain what the control circuit does with a contact change.

COM means common only when the particular switch’s contact arrangement and documentation identify a common terminal. Some designs or arrangements may not document a COM terminal in the way a reader expects. Do not assign COM based only on terminal position, color, or the assumption that every micro switch has the same layout. Follow the terminal labels and connection diagram for the exact switch and equipment.

Keep contact state separate from installed-circuit behavior. An open contact means the documented contact path is open in the stated test condition; it does not prove that the scanner’s circuit is deenergized or that no other path affects a measurement. A closed contact reading likewise does not establish that the equipment receives or interprets the expected signal. Wiring and control logic determine how the contact is used.

When documentation includes a connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load, use the information applicable to the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer a permissible load from NO or NC labels, or treat a generic diagram as proof of the scanner’s actual wiring. If the applicable details are unavailable or unclear, the terminal function and system behavior remain unresolved rather than safely inferable from appearance.

## Identify scanner lid micro switch NO and NC terminals with an isolated check

A continuity check can help verify contact behavior, but it must be performed on the switch isolated from the equipment. Identify the actuator states to be checked and the reference condition used in the switch documentation. Then compare the readings for the documented contact arrangement in those states. Without a defined arrangement and test state, a reading cannot reliably assign a terminal function.

For a documented NO contact, check whether the contact path is open at the specified reference condition; for a documented NC contact, check whether it is closed there. If the documentation defines another actuator state for comparison, check that state separately and compare it with the stated behavior. This is a check of the isolated switch’s contact path, not a test of the scanner’s control circuit. Do not transfer an expected result from one actuator state to another without support from the switch documentation.

The actuator may be moved by the scanner lid or its linkage, but first distinguish the state being tested from the lid position. The actual mechanism determines whether a particular lid position moves the actuator, and the design determines when contact operation occurs. If those relationships are undocumented, do not assume that “lid open” or “lid closed” corresponds to a particular NO or NC state.

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove continuity, identify a terminal, or confirm correct wiring. If continuity readings do not match the documented arrangement, the observation alone does not identify the cause. Possible issues include actuator input, the contact, terminal identification, wiring, or another part of the equipment. Keep these possibilities distinct instead of treating one isolated result as a complete diagnosis.

An isolated reading also cannot establish that the installed wiring is safe or that the scanner assembly is functional. The test concerns the switch under the defined isolated conditions. Work beyond those conditions, including assessment of the equipment’s wiring or circuit, requires the applicable deenergization and qualification requirements. Do not use the isolated contact result as evidence about an energized or otherwise untested assembly.

## Why scanner lid behavior may not match the terminal labels alone

The lid or a linkage can provide mechanical input to the actuator, while the switch contacts provide an electrical change. The scanner’s response to that change depends on its actual circuit and control design. The NO and NC labels describe contact states at the specified reference condition; they do not assign a scanner action to either state. In particular, the labels alone cannot establish which state the scanner interprets as a closed lid, an open lid, or a fault.

Scanner designs may use different switch arrangements and circuit logic. Do not generalize one scanner’s terminal layout or lid response to every scanner, or assume that the physical order of terminals identifies their functions. If documentation does not resolve how the switch is connected or how the equipment interprets the contact, that relationship remains unknown. The appropriate boundary is to identify only what the exact switch documentation and isolated test establish.

If the observed lid behavior seems inconsistent with a continuity result, separate the questions. Does the lid or linkage move the actuator as intended by the documented design? Do the isolated contacts behave as specified? Are the terminals identified from the correct markings or diagram? Is the equipment wiring consistent with its documentation? Does the scanner’s control circuit respond as designed? Each concerns a different part of the mechanical or electrical path, and one answer does not automatically settle the others.

Use the connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. A mismatch between a test and expected contact behavior warrants checking that the reference condition, actuator state, and terminal identification are correct before drawing a conclusion. If the evidence still does not resolve the cause, do not infer a replacement, repair, or system-level diagnosis from the labels alone. For scanner lid micro switch NO and NC terminals, the reliable boundary is the documented contact state and the isolated test result, not an assumed lid response.

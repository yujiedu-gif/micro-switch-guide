For an automatic door that uses a micro switch to detect or control its open position, automatic door open position micro switch operating force means a force parameter of the switch, not a universal force requirement for the door. Its meaning depends on the switch’s applicable specification, reference conditions, and direction of actuation. The force applied at the switch actuator may differ from force measured at the door or its linkage because the mechanical arrangement affects how force is transmitted. Reaching the switch’s operating point is only one part of interpreting the arrangement: it does not by itself establish suitable post-operation travel, release behavior, or the door’s response.

## What automatic door open position micro switch operating force describes

Operating force should be interpreted from the documentation for the particular switch and under the reference conditions stated there. The supplied terminology distinguishes actuating force from releasing force and also distinguishes free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. These terms describe separate aspects of switch travel and operation; they should not be treated as interchangeable names for one force measurement.

The force relevant to the switch is applied to its actuator in the documented direction. A measurement made at the door, a cam, or a linkage is at a different point in the mechanical path. It is not automatically the switch’s operating force. Relating those measurements requires the actual mechanism and its geometry, as well as clear identification of where and how each force was measured.

No generic numeric force requirement follows from the phrase “open position.” A value would need to come from applicable switch and mechanism specifications, with their conditions attached. Without that evidence, the parameter can be explained, but a required or suitable force cannot be assigned to an automatic door as a class.

The same boundary applies to any specification value that appears alongside force data. A figure only has meaning within its stated scope and test conditions. Do not transfer a value from a different switch, mechanism, or measurement location, or treat a switch specification as a door-system requirement without supporting documentation.

## How door motion transfers force to a micro switch at the open position

In an arrangement that uses a micro switch for open-position detection or control, door motion may reach the switch through a direct actuator contact or through a cam, lever, or linkage. The relevant path is from the moving door, through the components that transmit motion and force, to the switch actuator. The switch’s operating force concerns that final actuation at the switch, not an unqualified force applied somewhere on the door.

The shape and arrangement of the mechanism affect the relationship between force at the door or linkage and force at the switch. A lever or cam can alter how movement and force are transmitted. Therefore, a force measured at one point cannot be converted into force at another point from the switch’s operating-force term alone. The actual geometry and the documented measurement conditions are needed to interpret the relationship.

This explanation applies only when the door arrangement actually uses a micro switch at its open position. It does not establish that all automatic doors use this component, or that every switch is actuated by the same type of mechanism. The door’s motion, the actuator path, and the switch’s actuation direction must be identified for the particular arrangement before the switch specification can be related to it.

Also keep the component’s role separate from the door’s control logic. A switch may change contact state when actuated, but what that change does depends on the documented contact arrangement and the circuit. The open-position label alone does not establish whether the door stops, signals a state, or responds in another way. Those outcomes require evidence about the actual circuit and system design.

## Operating point, overtravel, and release are separate checks

The operating point is the position at which the switch operates under its specified conditions. Before interpreting a force measurement, identify that reference and the direction in which the mechanism acts on the actuator. A statement that the door reaches its open position does not, by itself, show exactly how the switch actuator moves or whether it reaches the documented operating point.

Overtravel is a separate travel boundary. It is the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. If the mechanism continues moving after switch operation, assess that post-operation movement against the switch’s stated allowance, where the applicable documentation provides one. A force reading alone does not establish how much travel occurs after operation or whether that travel is within the switch’s limits.

Release is also distinct from operation. The release force, or releasing force, is the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. It is not the same event as reaching the operating point. To assess release behavior, the relevant force reduction and return condition must be considered under the switch’s applicable specification.

Consequently, observed actuation at the open position is not a complete fit assessment. It does not alone establish adequate overtravel, reliable release, or appropriate door control. Those conclusions depend on the specific switch, the actual mechanism, and, for system response, the documented contact arrangement and circuit. Do not substitute an assumed margin or an unverified force value for those checks.

## What evidence can establish whether the force relationship is suitable

Start with the applicable documentation for the particular switch. Identify its stated operating-force reference, reference conditions, and actuation direction. Then identify the point at which the door or linkage force is measured and the mechanical path from that point to the switch actuator. The measurement location and conditions must remain explicit when comparing the door mechanism with the switch parameter.

The mechanism’s actual geometry is necessary to interpret how force and movement relate across that path. Documentation or measurements that do not identify the relevant cam, lever, linkage, contact point, or measurement location may not establish the force reaching the switch actuator. Likewise, a specification for another switch or arrangement cannot fill that gap unless its applicability is documented.

If the question concerns what the open-position signal makes the door do, operating force is not enough evidence. Confirm the documented contact arrangement and the circuit that uses it. Do not infer a stop command, status indication, or other system response solely from the presence of a micro switch at the open position.

A published electrical operation count, if relevant to reviewing switch documentation, must be read with its stated load, operating sequence, and test conditions. It is not a substitute for evidence about the force path or the open-position mechanism.

If the applicable switch specifications, mechanism geometry, or measurement conditions are unavailable, suitability cannot be determined from operating force alone. The defensible conclusion is limited: the switch has a force parameter defined by its documentation, and the force at its actuator must be distinguished from force measured elsewhere in the door mechanism. No numeric requirement or judgment about the door’s safety, compliance, adjustment, or control behavior can be established from that parameter by itself.

For an automatic door that uses a micro switch at the open position, automatic door open position micro switch operating force is therefore a switch-specific measure interpreted through the documented actuator direction and the actual mechanical force path. A useful assessment keeps switch force, door or linkage force, overtravel, release, and circuit response separate. Without the applicable specifications and mechanism evidence, the force relationship and its suitability remain unresolved.

A centrifuge travel limit micro switch location is specific to the equipment’s design; it cannot be determined from the word “centrifuge” or from the travel endpoint alone. Not every centrifuge necessarily uses a micro switch for this function. To identify one, follow the documented mechanical path from the moving part through any linkage to the switch actuator, then confirm the switch body and mounting point against documentation for the exact design. This identifies a component and its position, not the centrifuge’s electrical response when the limit is reached.

## What a centrifuge travel limit micro switch location depends on

A travel limit is a function within an equipment design, not a universal mounting position. The switch, if the design uses one, must be considered in relation to the mechanism that moves and the circuit that receives the contact change. Those relationships may differ between designs. The travel endpoint does not show where a switch is mounted, or whether a micro switch is used at all.

Keep three locations distinct when reading a drawing or examining documentation: the moving part that approaches a limit, the point where that motion is transmitted to the switch, and the switch body’s mounting point. The actuator is the part moved by the external mechanism. A linkage or cam, if documented, may transmit motion between the moving part and the actuator. The switch body can therefore be mounted away from the point where the centrifuge part reaches its travel limit.

The category “micro switch” does not establish a particular travel dimension. Numerical travel limits are device-specific requirements of the applicable specification sheet; they must not be inferred from the component category. A drawing or specification for the relevant design is needed to establish any such value. Do not use a generic assumed dimension to decide whether a component is the limit switch.

## Trace the actuator path to identify the centrifuge travel limit micro switch location

Begin with documentation that shows the relevant moving part and the travel-limit mechanism. Trace the relationship in order: moving part, documented linkage or cam if present, actuator, and switch body. The switch location is the mounting point of the body, while the actuator is the part that receives mechanical movement. Neither should be confused with the endpoint reached by the centrifuge mechanism.

If documentation identifies a direct contact between a moving part and the actuator, follow that specified relationship. If it shows an intervening linkage, use the depicted path rather than assuming the switch sits beside the moving part. If the drawing does not show how motion reaches the actuator, the location or connection remains unconfirmed; do not fill that gap by guessing from the housing shape or apparent endpoint.

Overtravel has a specific meaning in the supplied technical definition: it is the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. This definition helps distinguish actuator movement after operation from the location where the switch body is mounted. It does not provide a centrifuge-specific travel value or identify a mounting point. Any numerical travel limits remain dependent on the applicable device specification sheet.

This tracing method is a way to interpret the documented design, not a direction to move equipment parts. If the actuation path cannot be established from applicable documentation, the switch’s location should be treated as unresolved rather than inferred from an observation alone.

## Confirm the component and its role from documentation

Cross-check the mechanical drawing or service diagram against the parts list and wiring diagram for the exact equipment design. Use the documentation to confirm that the depicted component is the micro switch, identify its mounting location, and establish which moving part or linkage acts on it. A diagram may locate the component without explaining the electrical result of its operation, so keep mechanical identification separate from circuit interpretation.

Treat terminal labels, symbols, and contact states only as the applicable documentation defines them. Do not infer a normally open or normally closed state, a particular control response, or what happens when the travel limit is reached unless the design-specific documentation states it. A micro switch’s contact change alone does not establish whether the centrifuge alarms, stops, brakes, or responds in some other way. That result depends on the documented contact arrangement and the host-equipment control circuit.

If documents disagree, are incomplete, or do not match the equipment design, they do not provide a confirmed location or role. A visible component that resembles a micro switch is not sufficient identification by itself. The component’s relationship to the moving part and the diagram’s designation should agree before treating it as the documented travel-limit switch.

## Safety and limits when locating a micro switch

Use the applicable safe procedure for the specific equipment before any inspection that would require opening or moving centrifuge equipment. Do not open, move, or probe the equipment or its wiring without that procedure. If the procedure or relevant documentation is unavailable, do not treat visual access or an assumed safe state as permission to proceed; the location remains a documentation question until it can be confirmed appropriately.

A visible switch, apparent actuator movement, or audible click does not by itself prove electrical function, correct wiring, or the centrifuge’s response. Those observations also do not establish that the component is the travel-limit switch unless the mechanical relationship and applicable documentation support that identification. This guide does not establish a test procedure or authorize adjustment, repair, bypass, or replacement.

In short, identify a centrifuge travel limit micro switch location by tracing the documented actuation path and confirming the switch body’s mounting point against the applicable equipment documents. Where that path, component identity, or circuit role is not documented, leave it unresolved rather than generalizing from another centrifuge or inferring behavior from the switch alone.

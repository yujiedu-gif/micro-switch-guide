An aircraft cockpit push button micro switch replacement is not a generic consumer switch swap. It concerns the small switching component within a push button mechanism, and only applies when aircraft documentation identifies a micro switch in that mechanism. The complete push button assembly is a different scope.

The replacement requirement must come from the aircraft-specific approved maintenance instructions and applicable parts data. Appearance alone does not establish that a switch is suitable. The actuator, mounting, terminals, contact arrangement, and electrical ratings all need to correspond to the documented installation. A bench check can assess component operation, but it cannot establish aircraft suitability or authorize return to service.

## What aircraft cockpit push button micro switch replacement means

A micro switch is the switching component that responds to actuator movement. In this context, it is part of a cockpit push button mechanism, not necessarily the entire button assembly. The distinction matters: replacing a component does not, by itself, mean replacing the assembly or establish that assembly-level work is required.

This discussion applies only where aircraft documentation identifies a micro switch in the push button mechanism. It does not establish which switch is installed in a particular aircraft, how the assembly is constructed, or what replacement part is approved. Those details must come from the applicable aircraft-specific documentation.

Treat the work as an aircraft maintenance task. Approved maintenance instructions and applicable aviation requirements govern identification, replacement, inspection, and any return-to-service decision. A general description of switch characteristics cannot substitute for those controls. If the documentation does not establish the required part and configuration, compatibility remains unresolved; do not fill that gap by choosing a switch that merely looks similar.

## Identify the installed switch before choosing a replacement

Start with applicable maintenance and parts data, along with relevant wiring diagrams. Use these records to establish the specified part identification and interface requirements. Physical inspection can help compare the installed component with its documented arrangement, but inspection alone should not override the records or establish approval.

Record the features the installation data requires, including mounting dimensions and arrangement, actuator form and geometry, actuator travel, terminal or connector layout, and contact arrangement. Keep the documented requirement distinct from what can be observed on the installed switch. If a feature is unclear, consult the applicable maintenance information rather than inferring it from a broad switch category.

Actuator travel has defined reference points in the cited standard: pretravel runs from the free position to the operating position, while overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These terms describe different portions of actuator movement. They are not interchangeable measures, and neither supplies an aircraft-specific required travel value. Use the installation documentation and applicable switch specification to determine what travel is required.

Do not treat button appearance, a similar package, or a matching number of visible terminals as proof of compatibility. Those observations do not establish mounting constraints, internal contact arrangement, electrical suitability, or approval for the aircraft installation. Selection should remain tied to the documented configuration.

## Aircraft cockpit push button micro switch replacement compatibility checks

Compare each replacement parameter against the documented installation rather than relying on one matching feature:

- **Mounting:** Check the required mounting dimensions and arrangement, including the constraints specified for the installation. A switch that appears to fit is not thereby shown to meet the documented mounting requirements.
- **Actuator interface and travel:** Compare actuator form and geometry, engagement with the push button mechanism, and required operating travel. Distinguish pretravel from overtravel, and use the documented values and reference points rather than assuming a generic travel requirement.
- **Terminals and contacts:** Compare terminal or connector layout and contact arrangement with the applicable documentation and circuit requirements. A terminal layout does not, on its own, establish the internal contact configuration.
- **Electrical ratings:** Match the specified ratings to the stated circuit, load, voltage, current, and test conditions. Under MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. A rating without its conditions is not sufficient evidence of suitability.
- **Environment and endurance:** Check environmental and endurance requirements where the installation data specifies them. Do not assume a requirement, or that a candidate meets it, when the applicable documentation does not establish the relevant criteria.

These checks define a comparison, not permission to substitute. Reject a candidate whose match is uncertain or unsupported by applicable approved data. A generic description or bench result cannot resolve missing aircraft-specific requirements.

## Bench checks and what they cannot establish

Only check a component when it is safely isolated and the applicable maintenance instructions permit the work. Use suitable test equipment to assess the specified continuity states and whether actuation is consistent. Compare the observed operation with the switch documentation and the required circuit logic; do not assume a particular contact state or test value without documentation.

Continuity testing has a limited purpose. It can indicate whether a component exhibits the specified continuity behavior under the test conditions, but it does not establish that the switch has the correct mounting, actuator interface, terminal layout, contact arrangement, or electrical ratings for the aircraft. Nor does it prove installation correctness, aircraft-level operation, or approval for return to service.

A component that does not behave as specified should not be treated as verified. Conversely, a component that passes an isolated check is not automatically acceptable for installation. The result must be interpreted against the applicable component specification and aircraft maintenance data, and within the work permitted by qualified personnel.

## Installation records, inspection, and return to service

Follow the applicable approved maintenance instructions and aviation requirements, and use qualified personnel for the work. These controls govern the installation and the required inspection and operational checks. This article does not supply aircraft-specific procedures, establish a release criterion, or replace approved maintenance data.

Record the installed part identification and required maintenance actions as applicable. Complete the prescribed inspection and operational checks for the aircraft and installation. The required checks and acceptance criteria must come from the applicable approved information, not from a general continuity test or an assumed universal procedure.

Return to service is an aircraft-level decision governed by the applicable maintenance and aviation requirements. A bench pass alone does not justify release, and identifying a plausible replacement does not establish that it was installed correctly or approved. Keep component verification, installation inspection, and return-to-service decisions distinct, and leave unresolved any requirement not established by the governing documentation.

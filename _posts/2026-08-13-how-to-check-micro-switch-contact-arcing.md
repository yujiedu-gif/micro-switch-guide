A click or a visible mark alone cannot confirm that a micro switch has arced. How to check micro switch contact arcing is best treated as a limited evidence question: observations may be consistent with contact damage, but they do not by themselves identify the cause, establish correct operation, or show that the installed circuit is safe. The micro switch is the component whose contacts and actuator are being assessed; terminals, external wiring, the load, and the surrounding circuit are separate parts of the diagnosis.

A miniature snap-action switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement and is commonly used for position or limit detection. That mechanical action does not reveal whether an electrical arc occurred. A useful check therefore separates what can be observed at the switch from what would require examining the connected circuit, and it keeps any measurement tied to a known switch state and test condition.

## What checking micro switch contact arcing can establish

Contact arcing is an electrical event at contacts, while a click is an audible sign of mechanical movement. The two may occur in the same operation, but hearing a snap does not establish that the contacts arced, made reliable electrical contact, or are undamaged. Conversely, a switch that does not click as expected may have an actuator or mechanical issue; that observation alone does not prove contact arcing.

Visible discoloration, pitting, or other apparent contact marks may be evidence worth noting, but appearance by itself does not establish when or why the marks formed, whether they resulted from arcing, or whether the switch still functions correctly. A visual observation is more meaningful when its location is clear: a mark at an internal contact is not the same observation as a mark at a terminal or on external wiring. Do not treat these locations as interchangeable evidence.

Intermittent operation is also a symptom rather than a diagnosis. It could relate to actuator movement, the switch's contacts, a terminal connection, wiring, or conditions elsewhere in the circuit. Without a defined test condition and evidence that separates these possibilities, it is not sound to assign the symptom to internal contact arcing.

The narrow conclusion available from a switch-level check is that an observation is, or is not, consistent with a particular concern under the checked conditions. It cannot certify the switch, prove an arc occurred, or establish that the component is suitable for the circuit. If the actual question requires observing electrical behavior while energized, that goes beyond a basic isolated check and requires an appropriately qualified assessment.

## How to check micro switch contact arcing safely

Begin with a boundary, not a measurement: do not improvise energized probing to look for an arc. If access, isolation, or the condition of the equipment is uncertain, stop and have the assessment handled by qualified personnel. De-energize the equipment and isolate the switch from the circuit when contemplating a continuity check. The check is intended to assess the isolated switch, not to infer that the surrounding equipment is safe.

Before interpreting a reading, identify the switch being checked and the documented terminals, if that information is available. Record the actuator condition at the time of the observation, such as its specified resting or operated condition, rather than reporting a reading without saying how the switch was positioned. Do not guess terminal functions or assume a normal contact state from the switch's appearance. If its arrangement or reference condition is not documented, that part of the interpretation remains unresolved.

Inspect only areas that can be accessed safely without opening sealed or hazardous equipment. Note whether apparent damage is at a contact, a switch terminal, or an external connection, and keep the observation factual. If the contacts cannot be seen without unsafe disassembly, do not open the switch to look for evidence. Inaccessible internal damage cannot be ruled in or out by an external look.

If a continuity check is appropriate, take it only on the isolated switch and associate the result with the recorded actuator condition and identified terminals. A reading describes continuity under those specific test conditions. It does not show that an arc occurred, explain the cause of an intermittent symptom, or prove the switch will behave the same way in its installed circuit. A click and a continuity reading answer different, limited questions; neither should be treated as a complete diagnosis.

Keep the observations separate in the record: actuator response, visible condition and location, identified terminals, actuator state during measurement, and continuity result. If a result is unexpected but the terminal arrangement or reference state is uncertain, do not turn that uncertainty into a fault finding. The available evidence may simply be insufficient to interpret the check.

## Distinguish contact evidence from wiring and load causes

The location of a mark helps define what was observed, not necessarily what caused it. Apparent damage on an internal contact raises a different concern from damage at a terminal or on external wiring. A terminal or wiring observation should not automatically be described as internal contact arcing. Likewise, a visible internal mark does not, on its own, rule out an external connection or circuit condition as part of the broader problem.

Mechanical and electrical evidence should also stay distinct. If the actuator does not move as expected, that is a mechanical observation; it does not establish an electrical arc. If a continuity result changes with the documented actuator condition, report the states and readings rather than inferring an undocumented contact arrangement. When terminal identity, reference condition, or expected state is unknown, the result cannot support a confident conclusion about correct operation.

Circuit and load conditions may affect contact behavior, but this evidence pack provides no threshold or universal capacity for interpreting them. Under MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, electrical rating values are specification-sheet requirements tied to stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. That boundary matters here: a continuity check on an isolated switch does not reproduce those load and test conditions, and it cannot establish load suitability. Do not infer that a switch is appropriate, or inappropriate, for an unspecified load from a click, visual mark, or isolated reading.

To distinguish possible causes, compare only observations that can be made safely and interpreted under known conditions. A repeatable actuator observation may help describe mechanical behavior; an isolated continuity check may describe contact state for identified terminals in a defined actuator condition. Neither alone separates every internal, wiring, and circuit possibility. If the suspected behavior occurs only while energized or under a particular load, the basic checks described here cannot confirm its cause.

## When the check is inconclusive or needs qualified help

Treat the check as inconclusive when access to suspected damage is unsafe, terminal functions or the switch's reference state are unknown, or the symptom cannot be reproduced or interpreted under the defined isolated conditions. Inconclusive does not mean that arcing did or did not occur. It means the available observations do not establish that conclusion.

Qualified help is needed when investigation would require energized testing, when isolation is uncertain, or when the equipment is hazardous or internal damage is inaccessible. Do not file, bend, clean, or reuse contacts on the basis of these observations. This diagnostic boundary does not provide a repair method or a basis for deciding that damaged contacts remain usable.

A micro switch can change an electrical contact state in response to mechanical actuator movement, but the effect of that change in an installed system depends on the actual circuit and design. An isolated continuity result cannot establish installed-circuit safety, confirm a definitive cause, or establish suitability for the load. The most defensible answer to how to check micro switch contact arcing is therefore limited: gather safe, state-specific observations, keep contact evidence separate from terminal and circuit evidence, and defer conclusions requiring energized or inaccessible inspection to qualified personnel.

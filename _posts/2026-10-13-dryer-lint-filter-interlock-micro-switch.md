## What a dryer lint filter interlock micro switch does

Only some dryer designs use a micro switch as part of a lint-filter interlock. In a design that does, the switch can serve as a position-detecting input: mechanical movement associated with the filter’s position moves the switch actuator, which can change an electrical contact state. This is the switch’s role in the relationship; it does not describe a universal feature of dryers.

The switch is not the lint filter. The filter collects lint, while the switch is an electromechanical component that responds to actuator movement. Nor is the switch the dryer’s complete control system. Its contact change is an input that the appliance’s wiring and control design may use. Without evidence about that specific design, the component’s role should not be expanded into a claim about what the appliance will do.

This distinction matters because the same general component function does not establish the same appliance behavior. A micro switch may change a contact state in response to mechanical movement, but that fact alone does not establish that the dryer checks for a filter, prevents operation, signals a warning, or reacts in any other particular way. Those outcomes require information about the individual dryer’s circuit and controls.

The most precise general description is therefore conditional: if a dryer design uses a micro switch in its lint-filter interlock, the switch can detect a mechanically represented filter position by changing a contact state. The switch’s presence, placement, and purpose in a particular appliance must be established from documentation for that design rather than assumed from the appliance category.

## How a micro switch changes state

Actuator movement operates the contact mechanism. With snap action, the moving contacts act at a speed relatively independent of the actuator’s speed. The switching event therefore cannot be described merely by saying that the contacts follow the actuator continuously at the same rate.

The operating point is the position associated with operation, while the releasing point is the position associated with the contacts returning as force is reduced. The return occurs when the force or torque on the actuator has been reduced enough to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position. The distance or angle from the operating position to the releasing position is movement differential. Because this differential separates the two positions, the actuator’s return path does not use the same position for operation and release.

These definitions do not supply numerical operating or reset positions, travel, or force. Those values should not be guessed or generalized across components. The available claims define the releasing force and movement differential, but do not provide a particular switch’s operating point or reset point. Treat those values as unknown unless the component’s own documentation states them.

For an end-of-stroke check, test the micro switch separately from the vibratory feeder’s wiring and control circuit. **How to test a vibratory feeder end of stroke micro switch** depends on knowing which terminals are documented and comparing continuity in defined actuator states. A live feeder signal or an audible click is not a substitute for that isolated test.

Stop and de-energize the feeder, then follow the applicable energy-isolation procedure before accessing, disconnecting, or testing the switch. This procedure addresses the electromechanical micro switch and its actuator, not every feeder design or control system. If the required electrical work goes beyond the stated procedure, leave it to qualified personnel.

## Safety and scope before testing a vibratory feeder end of stroke micro switch

Stop the feeder and follow the applicable de-energization and isolation requirements before accessing the switch. Do not probe an energized circuit as part of this isolated-switch test. If you cannot establish the required isolation or are unsure how to disconnect the switch safely, do not proceed with the test; refer the work to qualified personnel.

The test is limited to a micro switch used to detect an end-of-stroke condition. It does not establish how every vibratory feeder detects stroke, what its control logic does, or whether its installed circuit operates correctly. The linkage, switch, wiring, and control circuit are separate parts of the diagnostic picture.

An isolated continuity result describes only the tested switch terminals in the defined actuator condition. It does not prove the feeder assembly is safe, the installed circuit is correct, or the equipment will operate as intended. Keep the component test separate from any later circuit-level diagnosis.

## How to test a vibratory feeder end of stroke micro switch: identify the switch and its states

First identify the component that is intended to detect the feeder’s end-of-stroke condition. Where documentation describes the relationship, observe which feeder mechanism moves the switch actuator and how the actuator is meant to change position. Do not assume that a particular linkage or detection arrangement is used on every vibratory feeder.

Before measuring, find reliable documentation or markings for the exact switch. Use them to identify the terminal pair to test and the specified contact behavior for each relevant actuator state. Do not infer COM, NO, or NC from the terminal layout, the switch’s appearance, or its role in the feeder. The connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment govern; a generic physical-layout inference is not enough.

Define the actuator conditions you will compare. At minimum, distinguish the actuator’s unoperated condition from its mechanically operated condition, and record which condition applies to each reading. If the feeder mechanism cannot move the actuator through the expected motion, that is a separate observation about actuation, not proof of an internal contact failure.

Actuator travel terms can help describe motion where the relevant documentation uses them: pretravel runs from the free position to the operating position, while overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These terms describe actuator travel; they do not identify terminals or establish what continuity result the feeder requires. Use the exact switch documentation to determine whether the stated positions and contact behavior apply to the component being tested.

## Check continuity with the switch isolated and the actuator states defined

With the feeder stopped and isolated under the applicable procedure, disconnect the switch from the circuit only if that work is within your qualifications and the procedure. Testing an isolated switch avoids treating a reading through the feeder’s wiring or control circuit as the switch’s own contact behavior. If safe isolation or disconnection is uncertain, stop and refer the work to qualified personnel.

Set a continuity meter to test the documented terminal pair. Record the result with the switch isolated and the actuator unoperated. Then place the actuator in the mechanically operated state, where that movement can be made safely and the relevant position is documented, and record the result again. Keep the state, terminal pair, and reading together; a continuity value without those details is difficult to interpret.

Compare the two observations with the contact behavior documented for the exact switch. The evidence here does not establish a universal open-or-closed result for either state, so do not assume one. If documentation does not identify the terminal pair or expected behavior, the readings alone cannot resolve whether the switch is functioning as intended.

A click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove continuity, correct terminal identification, correct wiring, or suitability for the feeder’s load. If readings are missing, inconsistent, or different from the documented behavior, treat the result as unresolved rather than assigning a cause from the sound or from one measurement alone.

## Separate a switch result from feeder linkage, wiring, or control-circuit faults

Interpret the isolated reading alongside the actuator movement. If the feeder mechanism does not reach or move the actuator as expected, the actuation path is a distinct possible problem. That observation does not by itself establish that the internal contacts have failed. Conversely, actuator movement alone does not establish that the contacts change state as documented; that is what the isolated continuity comparison checks.

If the isolated readings match the documented behavior, the result supports only the tested switch terminals and actuator states. It does not confirm that the correct terminals were used in the installed circuit, that the wiring is correct, or that the control circuit responds as intended. A terminal-identification error, wiring issue, or fault elsewhere in the control circuit remains a separate possibility.

Do not use a live feeder signal to bypass these distinctions. This procedure does not cover testing an energized feeder or diagnosing the control circuit while powered. If the isolated switch test is inconclusive, or if the question shifts to circuit-level behavior, leave that diagnosis to qualified personnel. Do not infer that an entire feeder is working or faulty from one switch reading.

## What the test can and cannot confirm

A properly documented isolated continuity check can compare the tested terminal pair in the specified unoperated and mechanically operated actuator states with the contact behavior stated for that switch. Keep the conclusion limited to those conditions. If the terminal identity, actuator state, or expected behavior is uncertain, the test does not provide a reliable pass-or-fail conclusion.

The check cannot establish that the feeder’s linkage reaches the actuator during operation, that the switch is connected to the correct circuit terminals, or that the wiring and control system function correctly. It also cannot establish load suitability, safe operation, or the condition of the full feeder assembly. An isolated reading is not a certification of the installed equipment.

For **how to test a vibratory feeder end of stroke micro switch**, the decision boundary is the documented, isolated switch behavior: compare defined actuator states and terminal readings, then keep mechanical actuation, wiring, and control-circuit questions separate. Use the exact documentation for interpretation, and escalate unresolved electrical or apparatus-level issues to qualified personnel.

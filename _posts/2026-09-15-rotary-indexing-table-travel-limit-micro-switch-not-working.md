A rotary indexing table travel limit micro switch not working can indicate several different problems: the index mechanism may not reach the actuator, the switch may fail to change contact state, or the signal may be lost after it leaves the switch. These possibilities should be checked separately. First confirm from the machine documentation or wiring labels that the suspected micro switch actually provides the table’s travel-limit signal. Appearance alone does not identify its function.

Make the machine safe before inspecting the switch or its wiring. Isolate energy and follow the site’s lockout and stored-energy controls before opening guards or touching components. Do not defeat guards or bypass the limit circuit to keep the table operating. The checks below distinguish the mechanical sensing point from the switch contacts and the downstream signal path; they do not establish a universal adjustment or repair.

## rotary indexing table travel limit micro switch not working: establish the safety boundary

Begin by locating the limit switch through the machine documentation, wiring labels, or approved maintenance information. Confirm that it is the component assigned to the travel-limit signal. A switch mounted near the table may serve another purpose, and its appearance is not enough to establish its role. If documentation does not identify the component or circuit, do not infer the function from its position.

Before opening a guard or touching the switch, actuator, mounting, or wiring, stop and isolate the machine’s energy sources. Follow site procedures for lockout and stored energy, and use only an approved method to establish a safe condition. If the required isolation or safe access is unclear, stop and seek qualified assistance rather than proceeding by trial and error.

Keep the limit circuit intact during diagnosis. Do not jumper, bridge, or bypass the switch to make the indexing table continue operating. A bypass can conceal the very fault being investigated and may defeat a limit function. This article does not prescribe operation with a suspected limit fault, nor does it establish that a particular table uses a particular contact arrangement or control response.

Once the component and safe boundary are established, diagnose in sequence: determine whether the mechanism reaches and operates the actuator, test whether the isolated switch contacts change state, and then trace the signal toward the control input. A result at one point does not prove the condition of the next point. Keep the machine’s documented design and approved procedures in view throughout.

## Check whether the index mechanism actuates the micro switch

With safe access established, examine the physical relationship between the table’s index position and the switch actuator. The relevant question is whether the intended cam, target, or other mechanism reaches and operates the actuator at the correct point in the sequence. Do not enter a hazardous area to observe the mechanism, and do not assume every rotary indexing table uses the same geometry or type of actuator.

Look for evidence that the target misses the actuator, the mounting has loosened, or a bracket has shifted. Also check for an obstruction, visible wear or damage, and signs that the mechanism travels too far or places excessive preload on the actuator. These are observations to investigate, not proof by themselves that a specific part has failed. Compare what is visible with the machine’s intended mechanical sequence and documentation.

If the target does not reach the actuator, or reaches it in a way that does not operate it, the fault may be in the mechanical actuation path rather than the electrical contacts. If the actuator is visibly operated at the intended index position, that observation narrows the question but does not show whether the contacts changed state. Verify that electrical behavior separately.

Do not compensate for a suspected alignment problem by applying a guessed travel, position, or preload value. Travel limits are specific to the applicable design and specification sheet; no universal value follows from the component category. If alignment or adjustment requirements are undocumented, use qualified service procedures rather than improvising a setting.

## Verify the micro switch changes electrical state

Test the switch independently of the control system, with power isolated. Safely separate conductors as needed so that the measurement concerns the switch rather than an uncertain parallel path. Identify the terminals from their actual markings or machine documentation. Do not assume that a terminal is normally open or normally closed based on appearance, position, or a generic switch convention.

Use a meter to compare the relevant contact state before and during actuator movement, following an approved procedure. The useful observation is whether the documented contact configuration changes as the actuator is operated. Record which terminals were tested and the actuator state for each reading. If the contact changes as specified, the isolated check supports the conclusion that the switch changed state under those test conditions. It does not prove that the installed wiring or control input works.

If the actuator is correctly operated but the expected contact change is absent, first confirm that the terminal identification, contact configuration, isolation, and test setup are correct. Then consider a sticking or damaged actuator, contamination, or an internal contact problem. A failed reading alone does not distinguish these causes. If the test setup or expected state cannot be established from markings or documentation, stop rather than assigning a fault from an ambiguous measurement.

Keep the test confined to the isolated switch and the defined actuator states. Do not treat an isolated reading as evidence that the installed circuit is safe or that the whole machine is functioning correctly. The contact check answers a narrow question: did the relevant switch contacts change state during this test?

## Trace the limit signal from switch terminals to the control input

If the switch changes state in the isolated test, continue beyond the component. Inspect the connectors and cable route for loose terminals, breaks, pinching, or visible damage. A contact change at the switch does not rule out an interruption or connection problem between the switch and the control input. Keep any inspection within the established safe boundary and the site’s procedures.

To check the control input, use only approved energized diagnostic procedures. Do not restore power or expose yourself to moving or energized parts as an improvised test method. Under an approved procedure, observe whether the corresponding input responds when the switch operates. The relevant input must be identified from the machine’s documentation; do not infer it from a nearby indicator or from the switch’s physical location.

Interpret observations by where the signal is present or absent. If the switch does not change state in a correctly performed isolated test, investigate the mechanical operation, terminal identification, and switch condition. If it changes state but the expected signal is not observed at the control input, inspect the wiring and connections along the path. If the signal is present at the input but the table does not respond as expected, the issue may be downstream in the input or control logic. That observation does not, by itself, identify a specific logic or programming fault.

Do not collapse these outcomes into a single diagnosis. A failed component, wiring interruption, and control-input or logic issue are different possibilities, and each requires evidence at its own point in the signal path. If the approved procedure does not make clear how to observe the input or how to interpret its response, stop and refer the diagnosis to qualified service personnel.

## Decide what the test results mean and when to stop

Use the sequence of observations to classify the fault without claiming more than the checks establish:

- If the intended target does not reach or operate the actuator at the index position, the evidence points toward the mechanical actuation path. Inspect the mounting, alignment, obstruction, wear, or damage against the documented design.
- If the actuator is correctly operated but the documented contacts do not change state in an isolated test, confirm the test setup and terminal identification, then investigate the switch for sticking, damage, contamination, or internal contact failure.
- If the contacts change state but the signal does not reach the expected control input, investigate connectors and wiring for a loose connection, break, pinching, or damage.
- If the signal is observed at the input but the expected control response is absent, the remaining issue may be in the input or downstream control logic. Further diagnosis belongs within approved machine procedures.

These results narrow the fault location; they do not prove that the entire table, safety system, or control sequence is good or bad. Use the machine’s documented design and qualified service procedures for any adjustment or repair. Do not apply universal travel or preload values, and do not bypass the limit circuit as a substitute for diagnosis.

Stop if safe access, circuit function, terminal identification, or control response is uncertain. The rotary indexing table travel limit micro switch not working is not a single diagnosis: separate mechanical actuation, isolated contact behavior, wiring continuity through the signal path, and control-input response. Continue only when the next check can be performed safely and its expected result is documented.

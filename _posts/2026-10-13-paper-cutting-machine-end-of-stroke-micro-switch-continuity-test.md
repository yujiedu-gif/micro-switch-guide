A paper cutting machine end of stroke micro switch continuity test can check whether specified contacts change continuity in defined actuator states, but only when the switch is isolated from its circuit. It does not test the complete machine, establish that its end-of-stroke control is safe, or explain how the machine responds. The machine’s end-of-stroke position also does not, by itself, identify which contacts should be continuous.

Use the switch’s own terminal markings and documentation to determine which terminals to measure and what behavior is expected. Compare readings only under documented released and operated conditions. If safe machine isolation, access, or disconnection is uncertain, stop and refer the work to qualified personnel rather than measuring an energized circuit.

## What a paper cutting machine end of stroke micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity test is a component-level check. With the micro switch disconnected from its circuit, it can show whether the documented terminal pair has continuity in a particular, defined actuator state. Comparing readings across documented states can show whether the contact behavior agrees with the switch’s specified behavior under those test conditions.

That finding has a narrow boundary. It does not establish that the switch is correctly positioned in the paper cutting machine, that the actuator or linkage moves through the required range, or that the machine reaches and detects its intended end of stroke. Nor does it establish that the conductors, connections, or control equipment work correctly.

Keep the switch test separate from machine-level diagnosis. A component may show the documented contact change when operated directly, while a machine problem remains elsewhere. Conversely, an unexpected reading does not by itself prove that the switch has failed: terminal selection, test setup, and whether the actuator is actually in the defined state all matter.

The isolated test also cannot establish that the machine is safe or functioning correctly. Do not treat a favorable meter result as evidence about the complete control circuit or the machine’s safety. Those questions require appropriate machine-level procedures, not an inference from isolated contacts.

## Identify the micro switch terminals and documented reference states

Before connecting a meter, identify the terminals from the switch’s markings and documentation. Measure only a terminal pair whose purpose and expected behavior are documented. COM, NO, and NC are relevant only if that contact arrangement is identified for the particular switch. Do not infer an arrangement from the machine application or from the switch’s location.

Likewise, define the actuator state by the switch documentation, not by the machine’s end-of-stroke position. The machine position may involve a lever, cam, or linkage, and the relationship between that mechanism and the switch’s contacts is not established merely because the switch is used at end of stroke. Do not assume that the machine’s end position means a particular NO or NC state.

Plan the comparison before measuring: identify the documented terminal pair, the documented released state, the documented operated state, and the expected continuity behavior in each. If the documentation does not identify the terminals or expected states, the result cannot be interpreted reliably from the application alone. Do not guess a terminal pair or expected reading.

## Isolate the switch before measuring continuity

A continuity reading intended to assess the switch must be taken with the switch isolated from its circuit. Before accessing conductors or disconnecting the switch, use a safe, verified energy-isolation procedure appropriate to the machine. Do not test an energized machine or measure through connected machine wiring as though that were an isolated switch test.

If you cannot establish that the machine is safely isolated, are unsure how to access or disconnect the conductors, or cannot verify that the switch is separated from the circuit, stop. Refer isolation and circuit work to qualified personnel. Do not improvise a procedure or proceed on the assumption that the machine is de-energized.

Once the switch is safely disconnected, keep track of the identified terminals so the measurement corresponds to the documented pair. Follow the meter’s instructions for its continuity function. This procedure addresses only the isolated component; it is not a method for checking the machine’s full control circuit or confirming machine safety.

## Compare continuity with the actuator released and operated

With the switch disconnected from the circuit, set up the meter according to its instructions and connect it to the documented terminal pair. Record the reading with the actuator in the documented released state. Then move the actuator to the documented operated state, only within its permitted travel, and record the reading again. Compare each result with the behavior specified for those states and that terminal pair.

Do not force the actuator beyond its permitted travel. A click may indicate movement, but it does not prove that the measured contacts have continuity or that their behavior matches the documentation. The meter reading, terminal pair, and actuator state together provide the relevant comparison.

If the documentation does not define the states or expected contact behavior, the two readings cannot be judged against an assumed NO/NC pattern. Stop short of declaring the switch good or bad on that basis. Preserve the uncertainty and seek the appropriate documentation or qualified help rather than using the paper cutting machine’s end position as a substitute reference.

## Interpret the result without overdiagnosing the machine

If a reading differs from the documented behavior, first check that the switch was isolated, the correct terminal pair was measured, the meter was used as instructed, and the actuator was in the defined state. A mismatch after those checks can indicate a switch issue, but the continuity result alone does not prove its cause.

If readings agree with documented behavior, that supports only the observed contact behavior in the tested states. It does not show that the machine’s actuator or linkage moves the switch correctly at end of stroke, that wiring and connections are intact, or that the machine’s control equipment responds as intended. Those are separate possible causes and require appropriate procedures beyond this isolated component test.

Do not infer that the paper cutting machine is safe, correctly timed, or functioning properly from the switch readings. If the remaining question concerns machine isolation, wiring, linkage, or control behavior, leave that diagnosis to qualified personnel using procedures appropriate to the machine. The continuity test is useful only for its defined purpose: comparing an isolated switch’s documented contacts in documented actuator states.

## A practical checklist for evaluating identifier evidence

Use this checklist to assess a claim without turning a phrase into an assumed product identity:

1. **Check the source.** Is it noncommercial and authoritative for the specific kind of claim? A source’s authority for general terminology does not necessarily make it authoritative for the identity or rating of an exact item.
2. **Match the identifier.** Does the source state an exact identifier, including any variant information it provides? Do not substitute a similar name, appearance, or broad category for an exact match.
3. **Identify what the source actually proves.** Does it establish identity, a technical specification, a terminal marking, or compatibility? Keep those questions separate, and do not extend evidence beyond the claim it supports.
4. **Record scope and conditions.** Note applicable units, load type, AC or DC distinction, test conditions, and other stated boundaries. For durability results, include the electrical load, operating sequence, cycle count, and environmental or test conditions.
5. **Preserve terminology.** Follow the source’s definitions and distinguish the terms it treats separately. Do not convert general descriptions into values for an unidentified item.
6. **Leave gaps open.** If a source does not establish manufacturer, ratings, dimensions, terminals, compatibility, or application for the exact identifier, mark that detail unconfirmed.

A dryer lint filter interlock micro switch continuity test can assess the switch’s isolated contact behavior in defined actuator states. It cannot, by itself, show that the dryer’s complete interlock circuit or control logic works. The filter, its mechanical linkage, the switch, the dryer wiring, and the downstream response are separate parts of the question, so verify them separately rather than treating one meter reading as a full-system diagnosis.

## What a dryer lint filter interlock micro switch continuity test can establish

A continuity measurement is meaningful only when the switch is isolated from the dryer circuit and the actuator state for that measurement is known. Under those conditions, the result concerns the tested switch contacts. It does not establish how the switch behaves in the installed circuit, whether the dryer wiring is correct, or whether the dryer responds as intended.

A micro switch is an electromechanical component. In a snap-action design, contact action has a speed relatively independent of the speed of the actuating mechanism. That mechanism description does not identify the terminals or expected continuity state for a particular switch. Those details depend on the documented arrangement and reference condition for the exact switch.

The filter’s position is not automatically the same thing as the switch’s actuator position. The filter may engage a linkage, and the linkage may move the actuator; the details are design-specific. Likewise, a change at the switch contacts does not establish that a downstream control circuit receives or responds to that change. Keep each observation tied to the point where it was made.

## Make the dryer and switch safe to test

Before making a continuity measurement, follow the applicable dryer service instructions to disconnect power and isolate the switch from the circuit. A meter reading from a switch still connected to the dryer may reflect paths elsewhere in the circuit, so it cannot be treated as an isolated-switch result. Do not test energized equipment.

The safe procedure and access requirements are specific to the dryer and the work being done. If the applicable service instructions do not provide a procedure you can follow, or if reaching and isolating the switch requires work beyond your qualifications, stop and refer the task to a qualified technician. Do not use a continuity reading as proof that equipment is safe to touch or work on.

Once isolation has been completed according to the applicable procedure, keep the switch disconnected during the measurement. This boundary matters: the test described here is not a method for checking the complete installed interlock circuit. It addresses only the switch contacts under the defined test conditions.

## Identify the documented terminals and actuator states

Use the connection diagram and terminal markings for the particular switch and equipment to identify which terminals to measure. Do not infer terminal function from physical position, appearance, or a generic layout. Do not presume that the switch has COM, NO, or NC terminals unless its documentation identifies them. The expected contact behavior also depends on the documented reference condition, so record which actuator state that condition describes.

Next, determine how the filter or its linkage moves the switch actuator on this dryer design. Observe the relationship without forcing the mechanism. Filter seating, linkage movement, and actuator movement are distinct observations: a filter can appear seated without establishing that the actuator has moved, and visible actuator movement does not prove the contacts changed electrically.

For each measurement, note the actuator state being tested and how that state was reached. Use the switch documentation to identify the relevant reference condition and expected behavior. If you cannot identify the terminals, state, or applicable documentation, the reading cannot be interpreted reliably; do not substitute a guessed terminal map or state description.

## Measure continuity in each defined state

With the switch isolated, use a continuity meter according to the meter’s and switch’s documentation. Measure only the documented terminal pair for the state under test. If the design has more than one relevant actuator state, keep the readings separate and label each with its corresponding state. Compare each result with the expected behavior documented for that switch and reference condition. No generic reading can be supplied for every dryer interlock switch.

A click, if one is heard, can indicate that a mechanism moved. It does not prove continuity, identify the contacts, establish correct wiring, or show that the switch is suitable for the dryer circuit. Similarly, actuator movement alone is not an electrical test. Interpret the meter result only for the isolated switch, documented terminals, and recorded actuator state used in that measurement.

If a result does not match the documented expectation, first confirm that the switch was isolated, the correct terminals were selected, the meter was used as directed, and the actuator was in the intended state. A result should not be compared with an expectation drawn from a different terminal arrangement or reference condition.

## Separate an unexpected reading from a dryer-level fault

An unexpected measurement does not identify a cause on its own. It may reflect incorrect terminal identification, an incomplete or unsuitable test setup, failure to reach the intended actuator state, a problem with the switch contacts, or another issue in the equipment. These possibilities are separate, and one isolated reading does not distinguish among all of them.

Check the mechanical path as well as the electrical test conditions. Confirm, without forcing anything, whether the filter engages its linkage and whether that linkage moves the actuator as the design documentation describes. Then check that the isolated measurement used the documented terminals and actuator state. These checks can clarify where the observation differs from the expected setup, but they do not prove that the complete dryer interlock is functioning.

If the isolated switch result matches the documented expectation, that establishes only the tested contact behavior under those conditions. It does not establish correct installation, wiring, control logic, or dryer operation. If the result remains unexpected, or the relevant documentation is unavailable, use the applicable service procedure and involve a qualified technician rather than extending the isolated test into an unsupported diagnosis.

## Decide what the test does not answer

An isolated continuity test answers a narrow question: whether the documented contacts show the expected continuity behavior in the defined actuator state under the specified test conditions. It does not verify filter seating, prove linkage operation throughout its movement, diagnose the dryer’s wiring or control circuit, or establish that the dryer will operate correctly.

Keep the decision boundary clear. Use the particular switch and equipment documentation to interpret the tested state and terminals. Leave installed-circuit diagnosis, repair, and any bypass decision to the applicable service procedure and qualified personnel. A single isolated reading is evidence about the tested switch contacts, not a verdict on the whole dryer.

In short, the dryer lint filter interlock micro switch continuity test is useful only when the switch is safely isolated, its terminals are identified from documentation, and each reading is tied to a defined actuator state. Interpret the result within those limits, and do not treat it as proof that the complete interlock or dryer works.

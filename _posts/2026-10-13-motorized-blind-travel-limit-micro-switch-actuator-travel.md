## Actuator travel is not the same as blind travel

Actuator travel concerns movement of the switch actuator, rather than the blind's overall movement. The operating point is the position at which the switch operates. Under the stated travel definitions, pretravel runs from the free position to the operating position; overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. Overtravel is therefore the distance or angle between the operating position and the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved.

The release point is a separate event from the operating point. The actuator can reach an operating point as the mechanism moves in one direction, and the switch can release at a different point as movement reverses. These are switch events, not descriptions of the blind's upper or lower physical endpoint. No numerical travel, angle, or relationship to a blind position is established here.

This distinction matters when describing a limit. The switch's operating position says where the switch operates in terms of actuator position. It does not, by itself, specify where the blind is in its range. The mechanism connecting blind movement to actuator movement would determine that relationship in a particular design, and the available evidence does not define that mechanism or its geometry.

Likewise, movement beyond the operating point cannot be translated into additional blind travel from these definitions. Overtravel describes the permitted movement of the actuator beyond the operating position, not a specified distance the blind continues to move. The terms describe different parts of the causal chain and should not be used as though they shared one measurement scale.

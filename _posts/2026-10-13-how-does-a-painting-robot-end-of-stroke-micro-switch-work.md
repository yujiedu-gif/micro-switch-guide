## What the end-of-stroke signal does, and does not, mean

An end-of-stroke signal represents a position-detection event associated with a particular mechanism and threshold. The micro switch changes contact state when actuated; the controller receives or otherwise uses that change only as provided by the actual circuit and design. Controller logic determines what the input means and whether it contributes to stopping, sequencing, or another response.

This boundary prevents several unsupported conclusions. The presence of a micro switch does not establish which contact state is considered normal, whether a particular contact is used, or what logic follows a state change. It does not prove that the robot stops immediately, that all motion ceases, or that the input has a safety function. Those outcomes depend on system details not specified by the component-level description.

Nor does the switch’s operation prove that a programmed sequence has completed. It indicates its own actuated state in response to mechanical movement. A controller may interpret that state as one step in a larger process, but the switch does not independently confirm the process or the controller’s response.

In short, separate three questions: Did the mechanism move the actuator? Did the switch change contact state? What did the controller do with that input?

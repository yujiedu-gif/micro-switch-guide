An **aircraft cockpit push button micro switch continuity test** checks whether the switch’s specified contacts change state as the push button is released and pressed. It is a component-level check, not a test of energized aircraft wiring or the complete cockpit system. Perform it only when the switch is identified in the push button design and applicable approved maintenance instructions authorize the work. Qualified personnel should follow those instructions and their safety controls. The switch documentation, rather than a generic terminal assumption or meter reading, determines which contacts to check and what result is expected.

## What this aircraft cockpit push button micro switch continuity test can verify

A continuity check can establish whether a specified pair of switch contacts presents an open or closed indication in the button’s released and pressed states. For this purpose, the test object is the micro switch itself. A reading does not establish that the aircraft wiring, connector, push button assembly, or complete cockpit system works correctly.

The switch’s documentation or wiring diagram must identify the contact pair to test. A generic changeover contact may be labeled common, normally closed, and normally open, but those labels do not establish the exact terminal arrangement or contact configuration of a particular device. The device marking or schematic controls. Use it to identify the required pair and expected states before measuring.

The test also has a defined boundary: check only the switch contact behavior under the approved test conditions. Do not extend a component-level result into a conclusion about aircraft operation or airworthiness. If the documentation does not identify the switch or specify the appropriate test, stop and consult the applicable maintenance process rather than guessing.

## Safety and isolation before an aircraft cockpit push button micro switch continuity test

Use current, applicable aircraft maintenance instructions and follow the safety controls they require. This article does not authorize access to, disconnection of, removal of, or testing on a particular aircraft. Only qualified personnel working under approved procedures should determine whether and how the switch may be isolated for a component check.

Do not use a continuity meter on energized aircraft wiring. Isolate the component only when authorized by the applicable instructions, and do not infer permission to disconnect wiring or remove the switch from the fact that a continuity check is being considered. If the instructions do not define the required isolation or test conditions, do not proceed on the basis of a generic procedure.

Preserve the identification of terminals and wiring as directed by the approved procedure. Do not assume that terminal position, a generic COM/NO/NC label, or the physical form of the push button identifies the contacts. If the switch, wiring, or required test points cannot be positively identified from the applicable documentation, pause and seek approved guidance.

## Identify the micro switch contacts and expected states

Before connecting a meter, consult the switch documentation or wiring diagram to confirm the specified contact pair. Determine whether the pair is expected to be normally open or normally closed, and record the documented expected condition with the button released and pressed. “Normally” describes a defined switch state in the relevant documentation; it is not a substitute for confirming the actual state expectations for this test.

Do not infer the terminal layout from a generic changeover-contact arrangement. A micro switch may use a contact arrangement that differs from an assumption based on a common terminal convention. The exact device marking or schematic controls, so use the documented contact pair and state references.

Also confirm the applicable test conditions and any specified acceptance criteria. Do not create a universal resistance threshold or treat any particular meter display as a specification. Contact resistance is a measured switch requirement whose method, connection points, and limit must come from the applicable specification and test procedure. A basic open/closed indication alone does not supply those details.

## How to perform the isolated continuity check

Proceed only after approved instructions authorize the component’s isolation and define the relevant test conditions. With the switch safely isolated as directed, set up the continuity meter according to its instructions. Connect the meter leads to the specified contact pair, not to terminals selected by guesswork.

1. With the push button released, check the specified pair and note whether the meter indicates an open or closed condition.
2. Actuate the push button as intended, without forcing or modifying the mechanism, and check the same pair again.
3. Compare both indications with the documented expectations for the released and pressed states.

Repeat a check only when the approved procedure permits it. Observe whether the indicated state change is stable, but do not treat repeated button operation as permission to adjust, dismantle, or otherwise alter the switch. If test conditions are unclear, or the meter indication cannot be interpreted using its instructions, stop rather than expanding the test beyond its authorization.

## Interpret continuity readings and recognize test limits

Compare the open and closed indications with the documented contact arrangement, state references, applicable test conditions, and meter instructions. A reading that matches the expected open or closed state supports only the contact-state observation made under those conditions. It does not establish that aircraft wiring or a connector is sound, nor does it verify the operation of the complete aircraft system.

Unexpected state changes, unstable indications, or results outside a stated specification are findings that require approved follow-up. Do not diagnose the cause from a continuity result alone. The issue could require checks beyond the switch, but a component-level continuity check cannot determine whether the fault lies in wiring, a connector, the surrounding assembly, or the aircraft system.

A simple continuity check also should not be represented as a contact-resistance test unless the applicable specification and test procedure define the method, connection points, and limit and those conditions are followed. If a resistance requirement applies, use the specified procedure and criteria rather than a generic meter threshold. A matching continuity indication is not a substitute for any other required inspection or test.

## If the aircraft cockpit push button micro switch continuity test does not match specification

If the result differs from the documented expectation, first confirm that the correct contact pair was tested, the released or pressed state was identified correctly, and the authorized test conditions were followed. Check that the meter indication has been interpreted according to the instrument instructions. Do not make assumptions about terminal layout or revise the expected state to fit the reading.

Do not bridge contacts, adjust the switch, or continue into aircraft circuit diagnosis unless that work is specifically authorized by applicable maintenance instructions and performed by qualified personnel. A discrepant switch result does not, by itself, identify a wiring or aircraft-system fault. Keep the finding within the scope of the test actually performed.

Document the observed states and relevant test conditions as required by the applicable maintenance process. If the result remains unresolved, or the necessary documentation and criteria are unavailable, stop and refer the finding through that process. The aircraft cockpit push button micro switch continuity test is useful only when its object, contact pair, expected states, and limits are defined; it cannot replace approved maintenance procedures or establish whole-system operation.

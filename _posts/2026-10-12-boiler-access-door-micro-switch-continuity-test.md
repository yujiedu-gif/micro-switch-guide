A boiler access door micro switch continuity test can show whether a selected pair of switch contacts changes electrical state as the actuator moves. It cannot, by itself, establish that the boiler’s complete interlock circuit works or that the boiler is safe to operate. The useful result is a comparison made under controlled conditions: the circuit is de-energized, the contact pair is identified, and readings are taken with the actuator released and operated.

Keep the switch-level check separate from a measurement through connected boiler wiring. Other connected paths can affect a meter reading, so an in-circuit result may not represent the switch contacts alone. If safe isolation, terminal identification, or correct restoration of wiring is uncertain, stop and have the work handled by a qualified service professional.

## What this boiler access door micro switch continuity test can establish

This test is limited to the electrical state of a selected contact pair in a micro switch and whether that state changes when its actuator moves. It does not establish that the door mechanism engages the actuator correctly in every operating condition, that other interlocks work, or that the boiler is safe to run.

A continuity indication must be interpreted against the identified terminals and their expected states. In a documented changeover arrangement, COM means common, NO means normally open, and NC means normally closed. Those labels describe contact terminology, not a universal reading for every switch or every door position. Confirm the exact device arrangement and intended pair from its markings or circuit information before measuring.

The door being open or closed does not, on its own, identify the contact type or prove what the meter should show. The relevant comparison is between the selected contacts when the switch actuator is released and when it is operated. A reading that does not match the expected state change is a finding to investigate, not a complete boiler diagnosis.

## Make the boiler access door micro switch safe to test

Before using a meter’s continuity or resistance mode, isolate the relevant electrical supply and verify that the circuit is de-energized using an appropriate method. Do not use continuity mode on an energized circuit. Follow applicable safety procedures, and do not proceed if safe isolation cannot be confirmed.

The test concerns a switch connected to boiler equipment, so the boundary matters: a continuity reading is not a substitute for establishing a de-energized condition. If you cannot identify the relevant supply or verify absence of voltage, stop and seek qualified service rather than relying on the meter’s continuity function.

Only after de-energization has been confirmed should you prepare to measure the contacts. Keep the door mechanism in the condition needed to observe the actuator’s released and operated states. Do not bypass or defeat the switch to obtain a reading or to make the boiler operate.

## Identify the contact and expected state before measuring

Locate the switch terminals and look for COM, NO, or NC markings. Use the switch’s schematic or other circuit information to confirm the intended contact pair and which state is expected with the actuator released and operated. Do not infer the contact arrangement from the boiler door position alone.

For a changeover contact arrangement, COM is the common terminal, while NO and NC identify the normally open and normally closed terminals. The words “normally open” and “normally closed” should not be turned into an assumption about the boiler’s door state. The specific reference condition and intended circuit behavior must come from the device information or wiring documentation.

If markings are absent, unclear, or inconsistent with the available circuit information, do not guess which terminals to test. An incorrect pair can produce a reading that is real but irrelevant to the intended check. Resolve terminal identification before proceeding, or refer the work to someone qualified to do so.

## Isolate a lead when boiler wiring could affect the reading

A meter connected across switch contacts while the boiler wiring remains connected may detect a path through other components or wiring. That parallel path can make an in-circuit measurement appear continuous even when it does not describe the switch contact by itself. Therefore, a reading through connected wiring should not automatically be treated as an isolated contact result.

When needed to obtain an isolated measurement, disconnect at least one contact lead from the switch, noting its original position so it can be restored correctly. The purpose is to separate the selected contact pair from connected paths that could affect the measurement. Whether a lead must be disconnected depends on the circuit and the reading being sought; do not assume every in-circuit result is invalid, or that every circuit has a parallel path.

Do not disturb wiring unless you are competent to restore it correctly. If the lead cannot be identified, safely disconnected, or returned to its original position, stop and arrange qualified service. Keep the circuit de-energized throughout this preparation and measurement.

## Measure the micro switch with the actuator released and operated

After confirming de-energization and identifying the intended contact pair, set the meter to continuity or resistance mode. If a lead was disconnected to isolate the contacts, measure the switch-side contact pair rather than relying on a path through the boiler wiring.

First place the actuator in its released state, using the door mechanism’s normal movement rather than forcing or modifying the switch. Measure the identified pair and record the result. Then operate the actuator as the door mechanism normally would and measure the same pair again. Record that reading as well. The comparison matters more than a single reading because it shows whether the selected contact pair changes state across the two actuator conditions.

Repeat the released-and-operated comparison to see whether the readings are consistent. Keep the terminal pair, meter mode, and actuator conditions the same between observations. Do not treat a click or physical movement alone as proof of electrical continuity; the meter readings are the observations relevant to this contact check.

If the mechanism does not move the actuator clearly between released and operated states, the electrical comparison may not establish the intended behavior. Do not force the mechanism or infer a fault from an unclear state. Further inspection may be needed to distinguish a problem with actuation from a contact or connection issue.

## Interpret open, closed, and unchanged readings

An open indication means the meter is not detecting continuity across the selected pair under the measurement conditions; a closed or continuity indication means it is detecting a conductive path across that pair. Compare those observations with the expected states for the identified contact type and the actuator’s released and operated positions. Do not assume that every switch should be closed in one particular door position.

If the readings change as expected, the result supports the limited conclusion that the tested contact pair changed electrical state during this check. It does not prove that the switch will behave correctly in every condition, that the wiring has been restored correctly, or that the rest of the boiler circuit functions as intended.

If the readings do not change when the documented contact behavior calls for a change, possible areas for further inspection include the switch, actuator movement, door mechanism alignment, or a connection. An unchanged reading does not identify which one is responsible. Intermittent readings likewise call for further diagnosis rather than proving a particular failure. Confirm the terminal pair, actuator state, isolation of the measurement, and wiring condition before drawing a switch-specific conclusion.

## What a continuity result does not prove

A successful contact check does not verify every boiler interlock, prove the complete circuit is correct, or certify safe boiler operation. It also does not establish that a door-position signal produces a particular response elsewhere in the equipment. Those conclusions require information and checks beyond an isolated continuity reading.

Do not bypass the access-door micro switch or treat a passing reading as authorization to operate the boiler with an access door unsecured. If the result is unexpected, the door mechanism does not actuate the switch clearly, wiring has been disturbed, or a safe test cannot be completed, leave the equipment in a safe condition and seek qualified service.

The useful endpoint is a bounded finding: whether the identified contact pair changed state under the tested released and operated conditions. Any unresolved discrepancy belongs to further diagnosis of the switch, its actuation, the connections, or the wider boiler circuit. A continuity test alone does not decide among those causes or establish that the boiler is safe.

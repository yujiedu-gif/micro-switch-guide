A **textile loom end of stroke micro switch contact rating** is a conditional specification, not a universal statement of how much electrical load any switch can control. It applies only under the load and test conditions stated in the relevant specification. The loom’s end-of-stroke function is a separate matter: a mechanical limit may move a switch actuator, but what happens electrically depends on the particular switch and the installed control circuit.

To interpret the rating, compare its documented conditions with the actual circuit: supply type, voltage, current, and load characteristics where those details are available. A match on one item alone does not establish suitability. If either the rating basis or the loom’s circuit conditions are unknown, the conclusion remains unresolved.

## What textile loom end of stroke micro switch contact rating tells you

A contact rating states electrical requirements or limits for a switch under specified conditions. For micro switch electrical ratings addressed by MIL-PRF-8805K 6.1.1 and applicable specification sheets, the rating values are tied to the stated load and test conditions. They are not universal switching capacities. A value without its associated conditions is therefore incomplete evidence for judging a particular circuit.

This distinction matters in a loom because “end of stroke” describes a mechanical event: a moving part reaches a travel limit and may actuate a switch. The contact rating describes electrical conditions for the switch contacts. Neither statement, by itself, establishes the other. A mechanical actuation point does not identify the circuit’s supply or load, and a contact rating does not prove that the complete loom control arrangement will respond in a particular way.

The rating should be read with its specification sheet or other applicable switch documentation, including the stated load and test conditions. Do not detach a number from those qualifications or treat it as applicable to any voltage, current, or load. If the documentation does not give enough information to identify the relevant conditions, the rating cannot support a definitive suitability conclusion for the loom circuit.

The same boundary applies to the end-of-stroke arrangement itself. Loom designs vary. The available information does not establish that every loom uses a micro switch, or that a particular loom uses one in a particular part of its control system. The rating question applies only after the component and its electrical role have been established from the relevant documentation.

## Match the documented rating to the loom circuit

Compare the documented rating and the actual circuit condition by condition. Start with the supply type, such as AC or DC, as stated in the switch documentation and loom circuit information. Then compare voltage and current. Finally, compare the load characteristics when they are documented. The comparison is useful only if the values refer to the same relevant circuit and operating context.

Do not assume that a rating stated for AC also applies to DC, or that a rating for one load type applies to another. A transfer between conditions is supported only when the applicable documentation says it is. If the rating sheet identifies a particular load and test condition, preserve that scope when interpreting the value. If the loom documentation does not identify its actual load or electrical conditions, those details remain unknown rather than being supplied by the fact that the switch is used at a stroke limit.

The circuit information should describe the electrical load associated with the contacts, not merely the physical motion that actuates them. An end-of-stroke linkage, lever, or other mechanism may explain when the switch is operated, but it does not establish the load connected to the contacts. Likewise, the fact that a loom reaches a travel limit does not identify its supply type, voltage, current, or load characteristics.

If either side of the comparison is incomplete, record what is missing. For example, a documented switch rating without the loom’s circuit conditions cannot establish a match. Conversely, circuit values without the switch’s rating conditions cannot show that the component is covered by its specification. A partial match is not evidence that the remaining conditions are equivalent.

Keep the conclusion narrow: documented conditions can be compared, but an undocumented condition cannot be presumed to match. If supply type, voltage, current, or relevant load characteristics are absent or unclear, suitability for those conditions remains unresolved. Do not fill the gap by assuming the loom uses a familiar circuit arrangement or by treating the end-of-stroke purpose as a substitute for electrical evidence.

## Separate stroke actuation from circuit response

At the mechanical level, an end-of-stroke mechanism may move an actuator when a loom component reaches a travel limit. That describes the input to the switch. It does not establish which contacts change state, how the contacts are connected, or what the control system does in response. Those points depend on the actual switch and circuit.

A generic changeover contact may use the labels common, normally closed, and normally open. Those labels should not be assumed for a particular loom switch. Use the exact switch marking or schematic to identify its terminals and contact arrangement. Even when the terminal arrangement is documented, the downstream effect still depends on the installed circuit and its control logic.

For that reason, do not infer that reaching the limit necessarily stops the loom, raises an alarm, or produces another specific result. The switch may be part of an arrangement whose effect is not established by the rating alone. Nor does a contact rating identify the mechanical point at which the actuator moves. It addresses electrical conditions, while the stroke limit concerns mechanical actuation.

Keeping those roles separate prevents a common reasoning error: treating a known mechanical purpose as proof of a known electrical behavior. The documentation must connect the particular switch, its contact arrangement, and the actual circuit before a specific response can be stated. If that information is not available, describe only the possible mechanical actuation and leave the circuit response open.

## What to verify before drawing a suitability conclusion

Use the switch documentation and the loom’s circuit information together. Establish the rating’s stated load and test conditions, then identify the actual supply type, voltage, current, and load characteristics for the circuit in question. Check that each comparison is supported by documentation. If the contact arrangement or circuit response matters to the interpretation, use the exact device marking or schematic and the loom’s circuit information rather than assuming a generic arrangement.

A component rating is evidence about the switch under its stated conditions. It is not, on its own, a determination that the complete installed circuit is suitable or that the loom will behave as intended. The circuit’s actual connections and control logic are separate parts of that conclusion. A rating cannot establish them when they are not documented.

Keep inspection or electrical testing within an appropriate safe procedure. Do not infer the safety or condition of the complete installed system from a component rating alone. This interpretation does not establish a method for adjusting or repairing the loom mechanism, identify a replacement switch, or provide wiring instructions; those questions require information beyond the rating comparison.

A defensible conclusion is limited to what the records establish. If the switch’s rating conditions and the loom’s corresponding circuit conditions are documented and can be compared, state the result within those conditions. If information is missing, name the missing item and leave suitability unresolved. The **textile loom end of stroke micro switch contact rating** can inform that comparison, but it cannot by itself establish the switch’s mechanical actuation point or the response of the complete loom circuit.

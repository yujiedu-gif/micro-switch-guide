## Which specifications require exact-model evidence?

Electrical ratings must be tied to the exact documented item and its stated conditions. Rating values are specification-sheet requirements associated with a stated load and test conditions, not universal switching capacities. A value without its relevant load type or conditions may be insufficient for judging whether it applies. Do not transfer a rating from a similar-looking or similarly named switch.

Mechanical details also require exact-variant evidence. Dimensions, actuator form, mounting features, and terminal layout should come from documentation that identifies the applicable configuration. A generic drawing or an image may not distinguish variants. Do not infer dimensions or terminal positions from the phrase, from token order, or from physical resemblance alone.

A generic changeover contact may use the labels common, normally closed, and normally open. Those labels do not verify the contacts or layout of an unidentified device: the exact switch’s markings or schematic control. When interpreting documentation, use the documented connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load for the exact switch and equipment rather than inferring connections from a generic physical arrangement. This is an evidence boundary, not wiring guidance.

Environmental protection and certification claims likewise need documentation for the relevant configuration and test scope. A statement about one variant, assembly, or test does not necessarily cover another. Durability results are also condition-specific: they apply only with the specified electrical load, operating sequence, cycle count, and environmental or test conditions. A result without those conditions should not be treated as a general life guarantee.

Terminology in a technical specification may distinguish actuating force, releasing force, free position, operating position, movement differential, pretravel, and overtravel. These are separate characteristics, not interchangeable descriptions. Use the definitions and measurement context in the applicable documentation rather than assuming what a term means for an unverified item. In each case, the identifier and the scope of the evidence must match before a specification can be applied.

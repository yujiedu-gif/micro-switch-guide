A visual inspection can identify apparent seal damage on a micro switch, but appearance alone cannot establish sealing performance or rule out moisture ingress. Micro switch seal damage testing therefore begins by separating what can be observed from what requires a documented test. A seal may be difficult or impossible to inspect on some designs, so do not assume that every switch has an accessible seal or a common construction.

Keep the inspection focused on the switch itself. The surrounding appliance or enclosure may have separate sealing features, and observations about those features do not establish the condition of the switch seal. Record visible findings and any test conditions, then use the applicable design documentation to determine what those findings mean.

## What micro switch seal damage testing can establish

An inspection can establish that an accessible area appears displaced, torn, gapped, deformed, or contaminated at the time it is examined. It cannot, by itself, establish that moisture has entered the switch, that the seal still performs as intended, or that the switch is suitable for a particular environment. Those conclusions require evidence and criteria applicable to the specific design.

Keep three kinds of information distinct:

- **Observation:** what is visible at an accessible seal area, and where it appears.
- **Test result:** what a documented procedure found under its stated conditions.
- **Conclusion:** what the applicable design documentation says those observations or results establish.

The subject here is the micro switch seal, not the complete appliance or enclosure. A host enclosure could have its own sealing arrangement, but its condition does not prove the switch's condition. Likewise, a switch-level inspection does not establish the sealing performance of the whole equipment assembly.

If the seal is not accessible without disturbing the switch, do not treat that lack of access as evidence that it is intact or damaged. The construction and inspection access remain unknown unless the specific design documentation establishes them.

## Inspect the micro switch seal without assuming its construction

First follow the equipment's documented procedure for making the inspection safe. Examine only seal areas that are accessible without prying apart sealed parts or disturbing the switch. Designs differ, and an inspection should not assume a particular seal shape, material, location, or exposed surface.

If an area is visible, look for apparent displacement, tears, gaps, deformation, or contamination. Describe what you see rather than diagnosing an internal condition from appearance. For example, note the location and nature of an apparent gap, but do not conclude from that observation alone that moisture has entered or that the switch has lost all sealing ability.

Record where the observation was made and the conditions at the time. Note whether the relevant area was accessible and whether any disturbance occurred during inspection. These details help keep later interpretation tied to what was actually observed. They do not substitute for a design-specific test or acceptance criteria.

Do not pry, probe, or dismantle a sealed area to improve visibility unless the applicable procedure specifically establishes that method. The available evidence does not establish universal access points or a general procedure for exposing a seal. If inspection would require disturbing sealed parts, leave the condition unresolved and consult the relevant design documentation or qualified service.

An apparent defect is a reason for further assessment, not proof of the switch's internal condition. Conversely, a seal that looks undamaged does not prove that it has prevented ingress or that it meets a particular environmental requirement.

## Distinguish seal damage, ingress, and electrical symptoms

These are separate findings. **Suspected seal damage** means an accessible area appears defective or its condition is uncertain. **Confirmed moisture ingress** requires evidence that moisture reached a protected area, or a result from a suitable documented test interpreted against its criteria. **Electrical malfunction** means an electrical behavior does not match the expected behavior under a defined check. One finding does not automatically establish either of the others.

A switch symptom can have several possible causes. It may involve actuator movement, internal contacts, terminal identification, wiring, or another part of the equipment. A symptom alone does not identify seal damage as the cause. A click can indicate movement, but it does not verify contact condition or sealing. An isolated continuity result concerns electrical contact behavior under the tested state; it does not show whether moisture has entered or whether the seal performs as intended.

Do not infer ingress merely from an electrical symptom, and do not infer normal electrical operation from an intact-looking seal. Keep the observation, the electrical behavior, and any evidence of moisture separate in the inspection record. If a documented test identifies moisture in a protected area, report that result within the test's scope rather than generalizing it to every part of the switch or the host equipment.

This separation matters because the possible failure paths are different. A visible seal defect may warrant assessment even if no electrical symptom is present. An electrical symptom may have a cause unrelated to the seal. Evidence of ingress needs its own basis and cannot be supplied by a click or a basic continuity check.

## Use only a documented ingress test for the specific micro switch

Before interpreting an ingress test, identify the procedure and acceptance criteria that apply to the specific switch design. A meaningful test must be tied to the applicable method, conditions, and criteria. Without those details, the result cannot establish whether the switch passes or fails its sealing requirement.

Do not invent a pressure, duration, exposure method, rating, or threshold. Do not transfer a procedure or rating from a different switch design, and do not treat generic water exposure as a valid test. The available evidence does not establish a universal ingress test for micro switches.

A standard's insulation-resistance requirement is a distinct electrical test, not a seal or water-ingress test. Under the applicable method and conditions, insulation resistance is evaluated between specified electrically isolated points. That interpretation does not establish sealing performance or moisture resistance unless separate, applicable documentation makes that connection.

If no applicable ingress procedure and acceptance criteria can be established, report the sealing condition as unverified. Do not convert an informal exposure or an undocumented test result into a claim that the switch is sealed, damaged, or suitable for an environment. The uncertainty is about the evidence available for that design, not a general conclusion about all switches.

## When electrical checks are relevant, and what they cannot prove

An electrical check may help investigate a reported switch symptom, but it answers a different question from a seal inspection. A continuity check concerns the isolated switch in a defined actuator state. Its interpretation depends on knowing the documented contact arrangement, the reference state, and the expected behavior for that design. Without those details, a reading should not be assigned a pass or fail meaning.

Keep the switch isolated according to the applicable documented procedure before making such a check. If the contact arrangement or actuator reference state is unknown, do not guess which contacts should show continuity. Use the relevant documentation or qualified service to establish the test setup and expected result.

Even when a reading matches the documented expectation, it does not verify the seal, rule out moisture ingress, or establish that the installed circuit is safe. A continuity result is limited to the tested switch, contacts, state, and conditions. It does not diagnose wiring or other equipment, and it cannot substitute for an applicable ingress test.

Work beyond the documented isolation procedure, or any assessment of installed-circuit safety, belongs with qualified personnel. Do not use an isolated reading as proof of the safety of the equipment in which the switch is installed.

## Decide what to do after micro switch seal damage testing

Document the visible observations, including the location and accessibility of the area examined. For any test, record the method, conditions, and result, and identify the acceptance criteria used. If the applicable method or criteria are not established, say that the sealing condition remains unverified rather than implying a pass or failure.

If damage or ingress is suspected, do not rely on an undamaged appearance, a click, or a continuity result as proof of environmental protection. Those observations address different questions and cannot establish sealing performance on their own. A suspected defect or documented ingress finding should be assessed using the applicable design information or qualified service.

The decision boundary is evidence-based: visible inspection can describe accessible appearance; a documented, design-specific ingress test can be interpreted only within its stated scope; and an isolated electrical check can address only its defined electrical behavior. Use the applicable documentation or qualified service to determine the next safe step. Do not infer repairability, environmental suitability, or a particular rating from inspection alone.

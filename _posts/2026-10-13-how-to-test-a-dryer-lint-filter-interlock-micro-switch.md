A safe check of a dryer lint-filter interlock micro switch starts with the dryer’s model-specific service information, not a generic wiring diagram or assumed terminal layout. **How to test a dryer lint filter interlock micro switch** depends on whether that dryer actually uses this design and on the documented access, isolation, terminals, actuator states, and expected readings. A continuity observation can describe a disconnected, isolated switch under specified conditions. It cannot, by itself, confirm that the dryer’s full interlock circuit works.

Use the service procedure for the exact dryer to determine whether the switch is present and how it can be accessed and isolated safely. Do not test a live circuit or bypass an interlock. If the procedure, terminal identification, or expected switch behavior is unclear, stop short of assigning a pass or fail and leave the work to qualified service personnel.

## Direct answer: how to test a dryer lint filter interlock micro switch

First confirm from the dryer’s model-specific service information that its lint-filter interlock uses a micro switch. Some dryer designs may use this arrangement; it should not be assumed for every dryer. The same documentation must establish the safe access and isolation procedure, identify the switch terminals, and specify the actuator states and expected contact behavior for a test.

Only measure continuity after the switch has been disconnected and isolated according to the applicable service procedure. Test the documented terminal pair in the documented actuator state, then compare the observation with the expected behavior in the service information. If the needed details are not available, there is no supported basis for choosing terminals, selecting a test state, or interpreting a reading as a pass or failure.

This check has a narrow scope: it concerns the isolated switch under stated conditions. It does not verify the dryer’s wiring, control logic, mechanical linkage, or complete interlock function. Do not infer that the appliance is safe to operate, or that a fault has been fully diagnosed, from a switch-only reading.

## Trace how the lint filter can actuate the micro switch

In a design that uses this interlock, placing or moving the lint filter may act through a mechanical linkage that moves the micro switch actuator. The exact arrangement and the movement expected at the actuator are design-specific. Consult the dryer’s service information rather than assuming that the filter directly contacts the switch or that all dryers use the same linkage.

If access and observation are included in the documented safe procedure, distinguish the mechanical input from the electrical response. The relevant observation is whether the documented mechanism moves the actuator as expected. Visible movement is not proof that the switch contacts change state. A click is also not proof of continuity, correct wiring, or correct operation of the installed interlock.

Keep possible mechanical input problems separate from possible contact problems. A filter or linkage that does not move the actuator as documented may point to an issue in the mechanical path, but that observation alone does not establish the cause. If the actuator moves yet an isolated continuity check differs from the documented expected behavior, that is a different observation. It still does not, by itself, prove which part is responsible: the switch, its actuation, or the test setup may need further evaluation.

Do not force the actuator or improvise an access method to produce a test state. If the mechanism is hidden, obstructed, or does not behave as the service information describes, stop and seek qualified service rather than treating movement by hand as a valid substitute for the specified condition.

## Identify the switch terminals and documented test states

Before measuring, use the dryer service information or the switch documentation for the exact assembly to identify the terminals and the actuator reference states. A physical position or terminal arrangement that looks familiar is not enough to establish its electrical function. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment rather than inferring a connection from appearance.

Use COM, NO, or NC only if the actual contact arrangement and terminal labels are documented. Those terms describe contacts in relation to a specified reference condition; they are not a substitute for identifying the correct terminals and actuator state for this test. Do not infer a terminal’s role from its location, shape, or proximity to another terminal.

The expected reading must also be tied to the documented state. An isolated reading has meaning only when the tested terminal pair, actuator condition, and expected behavior are known. If service documentation does not identify those details, do not guess a terminal pair, assume what an open or closed reading should be, or label the switch good or bad. The missing model-specific information is a limit of the available test, not a reason to borrow assumptions from another dryer design.

## Check continuity on the isolated switch

Follow the exact dryer service procedure for safe access, deenergization, isolation, and disconnection before measuring. This article does not provide a general disassembly sequence or a live-circuit test. If the procedure is unclear, the switch cannot be isolated as directed, or the work is outside your qualifications, stop and have qualified service personnel perform the assessment.

With the switch disconnected and isolated, use the documented measuring method on the specified terminal pair and place the actuator in the specified reference state. Compare the observation with the expected behavior for that state. Check additional actuator states only when the service information specifies them, and record which state and terminal pair each observation concerns. Do not transfer an expected result from another model or from an undocumented switch layout.

A continuity observation concerns the isolated switch and the conditions under which it was taken. It does not establish that the switch can carry the installed circuit’s load or that the whole dryer interlock functions correctly. A click or apparent actuator movement cannot replace the electrical check, and a continuity result cannot replace a documented assessment of the mechanical path or installed circuit.

If terminal identification, isolation, measurement conditions, or expected behavior cannot be established, the result is not a meaningful pass/fail test. Do not improvise the missing steps or reconnect the component based on an assumed terminal arrangement. Leave uncertain access or electrical work to qualified personnel following the exact service information.

## Interpret the result without diagnosing the whole dryer

An isolated reading that differs from the documented expectation is a reason for further assessment, not a complete diagnosis. The cause could involve the switch, actuator movement, or test setup; the reading alone does not distinguish among them. Recheck only within the documented procedure. If the observation remains uncertain or inconsistent, refer the work to qualified service rather than making an unsupported repair decision.

A switch that behaves as documented in isolation does not prove that the lint-filter linkage moves it correctly when installed. Nor does it verify the dryer wiring, control logic, or the complete interlock function. A problem observed in the dryer can arise elsewhere in the circuit or equipment, so a switch-only result should not be treated as a diagnosis of the whole appliance.

Do not bypass the interlock to test operation or to keep the dryer running. The available evidence does not establish how a particular dryer responds to a bypass or what other components may be involved. If determining installed circuit behavior requires tests beyond the documented isolated check, defer that work to qualified service personnel using model-specific procedures.

The decision boundary is simple: an isolated continuity result can be compared with documented expectations for a specified switch state, but it cannot certify the installed system. Use **how to test a dryer lint filter interlock micro switch** as a model-specific, isolated-check question, not as a universal dryer diagnostic procedure. If the switch design, safe isolation, terminal functions, or expected behavior is unresolved, stop rather than guess.

## From machine travel to a switch signal

The causal path begins with motion of a machine part. Near the defined travel limit, a linkage or another mechanism transfers that motion to the micro switch actuator. The actuator then moves into the switch’s operating range, and the switch changes its contact state. The resulting electrical change is the switch signal available to the host circuit.

The actuator’s movement is not necessarily identical to the movement of the machine part. The linkage connects them, and its geometry and arrangement are specific to the machine. The available evidence does not establish a particular lever, cam, plunger, mounting position, or linkage for food slicing machines as a group. Operating position and return behavior also need to be kept distinct. A switch has an operating event as its actuator moves, and a release event as the actuator returns. The points and movements involved depend on the switch and its mechanical input. In the terminology used for switch travel, pretravel runs from the free position to the operating position, while overtravel runs from the operating position to the extreme permitted actuator position. These terms describe actuator movement; they do not specify how far a slicer mechanism travels or where its end point lies.

This distinction helps keep the sequence precise. Depending on the specific circuit design, a state change might be interpreted as an input to some control action, but without evidence about that circuit it is not possible to say what action follows. In particular, one cannot conclude that the machine stops, changes sequence, or signals an operator merely because a switch is used at a travel limit.

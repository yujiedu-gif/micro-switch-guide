An aircraft flap position micro switch continuity test checks whether the switch’s contacts change state as the switch actuator moves. It is a component-level check, not a test of the complete flap system or a basis for deciding that an aircraft is airworthy. Use the applicable aircraft maintenance instructions for access, electrical isolation, prevention of unintended flap movement, test setup, and acceptance criteria. Do not substitute assumed terminal behavior or generic meter limits for aircraft-specific information.

## Safety and scope before an aircraft flap position micro switch continuity test

Before access or measurement, consult the applicable aircraft maintenance instructions. Follow their directions for reaching the switch, isolating electrical power, and preventing unintended flap movement. The available information here does not specify an isolation procedure, so the aircraft procedure governs; do not treat a general continuity-check description as authorization to work on a particular aircraft.

Keep the test limited to the switch and its directly observable actuation. A continuity result can indicate whether identified contacts conduct in the tested conditions. It does not establish that the flap system works correctly, that the switch changes state at the required aircraft position, or that the aircraft meets airworthiness requirements. Those matters require the applicable aircraft maintenance instructions and acceptance criteria.

Plan to compare the switch’s released and actuated contact states, and to relate actuation to the prescribed flap position and switch actuation point. Do not force the mechanism beyond its allowed travel. If access, isolation, movement prevention, or permitted actuator travel is unclear, follow the aircraft-specific instructions rather than improvising a procedure.

## Identify the switch terminals and expected contact states

Before connecting a meter, identify the terminals from the markings on the actual switch and the applicable wiring information. Record the installed circuit configuration and the expected contact state for each measurement. Do not assume that terminal names alone establish how the switch is wired in the aircraft or what the aircraft procedure expects at a particular flap position.

In general changeover terminology, COM means common terminal, NC means normally closed terminal, and NO means normally open terminal. These meanings provide terminology only. Verify the exact device schematic and applicable circuit details before testing; do not infer the installed configuration from a generic convention. The available evidence does not establish the terminal arrangement of any specific aircraft switch.

If the applicable information identifies COM, NO, and NC contacts, record which pair is to be checked and the expected continuity in both the unactuated and actuated conditions. Use the state references specified for the actual installation. Where the information does not identify a pair, expected state, or test condition, treat that detail as unresolved and obtain the applicable maintenance information before interpreting a reading.

## Perform the continuity check in both switch positions

With the circuit isolated as directed by the aircraft maintenance instructions, use a continuity-capable meter across the identified contacts. Keep the test at the switch contacts as prescribed. Do not assume that an in-circuit reading represents the switch alone; use the isolation and measurement setup specified for the aircraft.

Record the result with the switch actuator released, including the contact pair tested and the state observed. Then actuate the switch at the prescribed point and record the result for the same contact pair. If the procedure calls for checking more than one identified pair, document each separately. The useful comparison is whether the measured states match the verified expectations in both positions, not whether a generic pattern seems plausible.

Relate the actuation to the specified flap position and switch actuation point. Do not replace those criteria with an assumed position or force the mechanism past its allowed travel. If the aircraft instructions do not state the required position, actuation point, or permitted movement, a continuity change alone cannot supply that missing criterion.

## Interpret readings and separate contact faults from actuation problems

A missing, unstable, or unexpected transition is a reason to check the test setup and evidence before drawing a conclusion. First confirm the terminal identification, selected contact pair, electrical isolation, and measurement conditions against the applicable information. A wrong terminal reference or test setup can make a result appear inconsistent without establishing that the switch itself has failed.

If the result varies with actuator travel, distinguish contact behavior from whether the switch is being actuated as specified. Under the aircraft procedures, check the permitted relationship between switch actuation and flap position, and examine relevant linkage contact, alignment, and travel. Also consider external wiring conditions within the scope of the prescribed checks. This helps separate a contact transition problem from a missed or inconsistent transition associated with actuation or wiring; a continuity reading alone does not identify which cause applies.

Do not adjust or rig flap position switches as part of this generic test description. Those tasks are outside its scope and must follow the aircraft-specific maintenance instructions. Likewise, a switch-level result does not diagnose the complete flap control or indication system. Record what was measured and which conditions were verified, and leave unresolved causes open for the required maintenance process.

## Document results and follow aircraft-specific acceptance criteria

Document the identified contacts, installed circuit reference, test conditions, switch state, observed continuity, and the prescribed flap position or actuation point used for the check. Compare those observations with the applicable aircraft maintenance criteria. No universal resistance value, travel limit, or acceptance threshold is established here, so do not invent one or treat an audible meter indication as an aircraft-wide acceptance decision.

If readings are outside the applicable criteria, or the transition remains missing or inconsistent after confirming the setup, follow the required maintenance process and escalate the result as directed. Use any required post-maintenance checks specified by the aircraft instructions. Do not infer that the aircraft is serviceable, certify it, or make a return-to-service decision from continuity alone.

An aircraft flap position micro switch continuity test can document contact behavior under defined conditions. The meaningful conclusion is limited to whether the identified contacts changed state as expected at the prescribed actuation point; aircraft-level acceptance remains governed by the applicable maintenance instructions and criteria.

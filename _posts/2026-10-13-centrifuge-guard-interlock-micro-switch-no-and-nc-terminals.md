## centrifuge guard interlock micro switch NO and NC terminals: what the labels mean

NO and NC are contact-state descriptions, not names for guard positions. In a documented contact arrangement, normally open (NO) identifies the contact that is open at the specified reference condition, while normally closed (NC) identifies the contact that is closed at that condition. The reference condition matters: it must be clear what actuator state or other documented condition the switch information uses. Without that information, the labels alone do not tell you the contact state at a particular moment.

These terms concern the micro switch’s contacts. They do not mean “guard open” and “guard closed.” A guard might be mechanically related to a switch actuator, but the relationship between guard position and actuator position depends on the actual mechanism. The available information here does not establish a centrifuge’s linkage, its switch’s reference condition, or the terminal arrangement for any particular unit. Therefore, neither guard position can be assigned to NO or NC on that basis alone.

Before interpreting a terminal marking, identify the connection diagram and terminal markings for the exact switch and equipment.

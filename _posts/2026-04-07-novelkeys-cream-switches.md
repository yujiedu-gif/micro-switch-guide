## Which claims require separate evidence?

Identity is the first separate claim. Evidence should establish what item the exact phrase denotes, rather than merely showing that the words occur together. Even a confirmed identity would not, on its own, establish who manufactured an item, how it is built, or what it does. Each additional assertion needs support that applies to the identified item.

For a generic changeover contact, terminals may be labeled common, normally closed, and normally open; however, the exact device marking or schematic controls. That general relationship does not establish that an unidentified item has a changeover contact, and it cannot be used to infer terminals for the phrase under review.

Operating characteristics and ratings also need distinct, applicable evidence. A value or behavior should not be reported unless documentation links it to the exact identified device and states the conditions that give the result meaning. Test results are not universal properties detached from their methods. For example, a durability result under MIL-PRF-8805K qualification and conformance tests applies only with its specified electrical load, operating sequence, cycle count, and environmental or test conditions. This is a condition-specific boundary, not evidence that the phrase identifies a device covered by those tests.

Dimensions, compatibility, and application are further independent claim types. A dimension needs a record tied to the exact item and relevant configuration. Compatibility requires evidence addressing the particular items or systems involved; similar naming or apparent resemblance does not prove it. An application claim requires evidence that establishes the intended or supported use. None of these conclusions follows merely from a name, a color description, or a general category.

The practical rule is to avoid bundling claims. A source that establishes identity may leave construction unresolved. A construction description may say nothing about operating limits. A test result may apply only under its documented conditions and does not automatically establish compatibility or suitability for another use. State only the supported point, retain its scope and conditions, and leave every unsupported point open.

## First establish whether the centrifuge travel limit uses a micro switch

Before testing, determine whether the travel-limit component is a micro switch and what it does in the specific equipment. Check the centrifuge documentation and the physical arrangement. A travel limit may involve a mechanical actuator and a switch, but the centrifuge category alone does not establish that design. Do not assume the component type, its normal state, or how a contact change affects machine operation.

Follow the equipment-specific isolation and access instructions before reaching the component. Verify all applicable requirements. Work outside the documented safe procedure belongs with qualified personnel.

This boundary matters because the check described here concerns the switch, not the centrifuge's complete electrical or mechanical system. ## How to test a centrifuge travel limit micro switch in isolation

Identify the terminals using the documentation for the actual switch and equipment. Do not assume that terminals are COM, NO, or NC, or assign those labels based on appearance. If the documentation does identify COM, NO, or NC, interpret those designations only in the specified reference condition. The contact arrangement and reference state must be known before a continuity reading can be judged against an expectation.

Continuity checks require the switch to be isolated from the centrifuge circuit and the actuator state to be defined. Use a continuity method appropriate to the documented contact arrangement. Compare the observed continuity across the documented terminals at the defined actuator states, then compare those results with the behavior specified for that switch. The procedure requires an isolated component; it is not an instruction to test energized wiring or to infer a safe condition for the machine.

Record which documented terminals were checked, the actuator state for each observation, and whether the expected contact behavior appeared to change consistently. If the terminal identity, reference states, or expected behavior is unavailable, a reading alone cannot establish whether the switch passed. Do not convert an unexplained reading into a general conclusion about centrifuge travel limits. The evidence supports a switch-level comparison only when the specific switch and the expected behavior have been established.

## Separate actuator travel from contact behavior

Observe mechanical movement and electrical behavior as distinct parts of the check. The actuator is moved by the external mechanism. Note whether the mechanism reaches and releases it as intended under the equipment's documented procedure, but do not assume a particular operating point or travel value when none is specified. A visible movement shows movement; it does not by itself show that the contacts changed state.

An audible click may indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but a click alone does not prove continuity, contact condition, correct wiring, or suitability for the centrifuge load. Likewise, an observed actuator movement does not establish the expected electrical response. The isolated continuity comparison provides separate evidence about contact state at the defined actuator states. Keep those observations distinct rather than treating one as proof of the other.

Describe what is observed without prematurely diagnosing the cause. For example, incomplete actuator movement, failure of the mechanism to reach or release the actuator as intended, and absence of the expected state change are different findings. Their significance depends on the actual equipment and switch documentation. Do not assign an operating point, release point, travel amount, or contact arrangement that the documentation does not provide. These checks do not authorize adjustment or repair.

## Interpret the result without diagnosing the whole centrifuge

If an isolated, correctly identified switch does not show the documented expected state change, that result supports a switch-level concern. It does not identify why the expected behavior is absent, and it does not by itself establish a particular defect. Keep the conclusion limited to the tested switch, terminals, and actuator states.

If the isolated switch changes state as documented, that result does not prove that the installed terminal connections, wiring, control logic, mechanical linkage, or centrifuge assembly function correctly. A problem elsewhere in the equipment remains a separate diagnostic path. Do not use an isolated reading to declare the centrifuge operational, and do not infer the machine's response from a contact change without the equipment-specific circuit information.

When the component identity, expected behavior, test result, or safe procedure is uncertain, stop at that boundary and follow the equipment procedure or seek qualified service. Do not improvise live testing, bypass the travel limit, or attempt a repair based on this isolated check. **How to test a centrifuge travel limit micro switch** is therefore a component-level question: confirm the design, use documented terminals and actuator states on an isolated switch, and keep the result separate from machine-level diagnosis.

An **atm cash cassette door micro switch replacement** applies only to an ATM cassette-door assembly that uses a micro switch to detect door position. The switch responds to mechanical movement of an actuator and changes an electrical contact state; it does not, by itself, explain how the ATM responds to that state. The equipment’s circuit and service documentation define that behavior.

Use the authorized service procedure for the specific assembly. Before disconnecting anything, confirm authorization and follow the equipment-specific isolation and access requirements. Preserve the original wiring and mechanical arrangement, then inspect the door’s trigger path before deciding that the switch itself needs replacement. After installation, verify electrical state changes and repeatable recognition of both open and fully closed door states through the authorized service check. A door that appears to shut is not sufficient evidence of correct detection.

## When a cassette door uses a micro switch

This procedure is limited to cassette-door designs that use a micro switch for door-state detection. Do not assume every ATM or cash cassette uses one. In an assembly that does, a door feature may move the switch actuator directly or act through a linkage. The resulting contact change is a signal within the equipment’s circuit; its meaning and effect depend on the documented design.

Before removing a component, trace the mechanical path from the door feature to the actuator. Confirm whether contact is direct or through a linkage, and note how the mechanism is positioned when the door opens and closes. This distinction matters because a switch can be in working condition while a door tab, linkage, or mounting position fails to move its actuator as intended. Conversely, a correctly moving trigger does not establish that the switch’s electrical contacts or wiring are sound.

Keep the diagnosis focused on this door-state path. Other ATM interlocks and equipment functions are outside the scope of this procedure. Do not infer an alarm, shutdown, or access-control response from the presence of a micro switch. Follow the assembly’s service documentation to identify the state the equipment expects and the approved method for checking it.

## Safety and identification before atm cash cassette door micro switch replacement

Confirm that the work is authorized and follow the equipment-specific isolation and access procedure before handling the switch or wiring. The applicable deenergization and qualification requirements depend on the equipment and work; do not substitute a generic procedure. Do not defeat, bypass, or spoof an interlock to carry out replacement or testing. If the required authorized procedure or qualifications are unavailable, stop rather than improvising access or isolation steps.

Before disconnecting the switch, photograph or otherwise document the wiring, terminal markings, switch orientation, mounting position, and linkage position. Record the door state and actuator relationship as found. These observations provide a reference when fitting the specified component and help distinguish an existing alignment issue from a change introduced during work.

Identify terminals from markings on the exact switch or from the equipment documentation. Use the connection diagram, terminal marking, circuit function, and permissible load documented for the exact switch and equipment. Do not infer terminal identity from physical layout or from an assumed convention. The correct connection and allowable load are assembly-specific; if the documentation does not resolve either, do not guess.

## Inspect the door trigger and switch failure path

With the assembly handled under its authorized procedure, inspect the door feature and any linkage that moves the actuator. Observe whether the intended feature reaches the actuator along the expected path during opening and closure. Look for loose mounting, wear, deformation, contamination, damaged wiring, or an actuator that appears stuck. These observations identify possible causes; they do not alone prove which component has failed.

Separate a mechanical trigger problem from an electrical switch problem before replacing parts. If the door feature does not reach the actuator, or the linkage is loose or deformed, the door mechanism may be preventing operation. If the trigger path appears intact, the switch or its wiring may still be at fault. A visible click, if one occurs, indicates movement but does not prove an electrical transition or correct terminal connection.

Use the authorized service check to compare the switch’s electrical state with the specified door and actuator states. Interpret the result only under the prescribed conditions and method. If the actuator moves but the required electrical change is absent, the switch or connection warrants further checking. If the electrical state changes but the equipment does not recognize the door state, the issue may lie elsewhere in the wiring or equipment. Do not treat either observation as a complete diagnosis without the assembly’s procedure.

## Choose and fit the specified micro switch

Select a replacement only by matching the requirements documented for the assembly: contact configuration, electrical ratings, actuator form, operating force, and travel. No substitute specification can be inferred from the switch’s appearance or from a generic description of a micro switch. If any required characteristic is not available in the equipment documentation or approved service information, leave compatibility unresolved and do not assume that a physically similar part is suitable.

Transfer wiring according to the recorded terminal markings and documentation for the exact switch and equipment. Do not rely on a wire’s position alone or assume that terminals share a standard layout. Preserve the documented circuit function and permissible load. If markings, records, and the connection diagram do not agree, stop and resolve the discrepancy through the authorized service process before reconnecting.

Mount the specified component in its intended functional position, using the documented orientation and trigger relationship as the reference. Confirm that wiring has clearance and that it does not obstruct the linkage or door movement. Move the door through its normal travel under the authorized procedure and observe whether the feature or linkage actuates the switch as intended. Do not alter the intended actuation point to compensate for an unresolved alignment problem. Avoid forcing the actuator beyond the movement allowed by the specified design; the switch documentation defines its operating characteristics and travel limits.

## Verify door-state detection after replacement

Use the authorized service check to confirm that the switch’s electrical state changes as the door moves. Identify the expected open and fully closed states from the equipment documentation rather than assuming a particular contact arrangement or state convention. Check the switch state and the equipment’s door-state recognition through the prescribed method. A visual check that the door closes, or a sound from the mechanism, does not establish that either electrical transition or system recognition is correct.

Repeat the approved check through opening and full closure to confirm that the assembly consistently recognizes both states. The trigger should reach the actuator through the intended direct or linked path, and the state indication should be repeatable rather than intermittent. Do not infer successful operation from a single transition if the service procedure calls for repeated checks.

If detection is inconsistent, stop before returning the assembly to service. Recheck the door feature and linkage alignment, mounting position, terminal identification, wiring, and whether the installed switch matches the specified contact configuration, ratings, actuator geometry, operating force, and travel. If the cause remains unresolved, follow the authorized service process rather than bypassing an interlock or assuming that replacement alone has corrected the problem. Complete the **atm cash cassette door micro switch replacement** only when the required service verification confirms repeatable door-state detection.

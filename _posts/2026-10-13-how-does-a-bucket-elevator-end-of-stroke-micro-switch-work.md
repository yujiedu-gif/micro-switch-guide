In a documented arrangement, movement of an elevator part or a linked mechanism is transmitted through physical contact or a linkage to the micro switch actuator. As the actuator moves, it operates the switch mechanism, which changes contact state when it reaches its operating event. This is the basic mechanical-to-electrical sequence behind the bucket elevator end of stroke micro switch function.

The phrase “end of stroke” describes the role assigned to the switch in a particular installation. The endpoint is where that installed mechanism actuates the switch. It is useful to keep three elements distinct:

- **Elevator movement:** a part of the elevator or a linked mechanism moves.
- **Switch actuation:** physical contact or linkage moves the switch actuator and operates its mechanism.
- **Control response:** the connected circuit may use the resulting contact change to produce an action defined by the equipment design.

Only the first two describe the component-level actuation path. The third cannot be determined from switch operation by itself. A micro switch reports a mechanical event through a contact transition; it does not independently establish the elevator’s control behavior.

## What the switch action does, and does not, tell you

Switch actuation establishes a component event: the switch mechanism was operated and its contact state changed. It does not, by itself, establish that the elevator stopped, that an alarm occurred, that direction changed, or that a safety function was performed. Any such result depends on the actual circuit and equipment design.

The boundary is important because a micro switch is only one part of a possible actuation and control chain. The elevator’s moving part or linkage supplies mechanical input; the switch converts actuator movement into a contact transition; and the surrounding circuit determines how that transition is used. Inferring the final response from the switch alone would collapse these separate roles and exceed what the component event proves.

For a specific installation, the documented connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load for the exact switch and equipment are the appropriate boundaries for interpreting its electrical role. A generic switch layout does not establish those details. This explanation does not identify a particular model, rating, contact arrangement, wiring, or control response.

The conclusion is narrow: where a bucket elevator uses a documented end-of-stroke micro switch arrangement, elevator movement is transmitted by contact or linkage to the actuator, and the switch changes contact state when its mechanism reaches its operating event. That describes how does a bucket elevator end of stroke micro switch work at the component level. It does not establish a universal elevator arrangement or prove what the complete elevator will do afterward.

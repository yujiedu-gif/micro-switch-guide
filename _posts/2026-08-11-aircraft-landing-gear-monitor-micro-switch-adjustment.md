# Aircraft Landing-Gear Monitor Micro Switch Adjustment

Aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch adjustment sets the relationship between a switch actuator and a gear-linked target so the monitoring circuit changes state at the specified point. The correct position depends on the particular installation. Use its applicable approved maintenance data for adjustment limits, required test conditions, and acceptance criteria; there is no universal setting to apply across aircraft.

A switch trip alone does not establish that the landing-gear indication is correct. Verification must cover both the switch’s mechanical actuation and the resulting indication through the monitor circuit. If the installation, limits, or required test method cannot be established, do not proceed by estimating a position or clearance.

## What aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch adjustment changes

The adjustment concerns the mechanical relationship among the gear-linked target or linkage, the switch body, and its actuator. Depending on the installation, the switch may respond to a position directly or through a linkage. Identify those parts and the circuit being monitored before disturbing the installation. Do not assume that switches in different locations or aircraft use the same arrangement.

Two travel terms help describe the actuator’s movement. **Pretravel** is the distance or angle the actuator moves from its free position to its operating position. **Overtravel** is the distance or angle from the operating position to the extreme position to which the actuator may be moved. These definitions describe travel; they do not supply an installation’s permitted values or adjustment point. Obtain those from the applicable maintenance instructions.

The purpose is to have the switch change state at the specified gear condition while maintaining the required mechanical relationship and permitted actuator travel. The switch can actuate without the complete monitor circuit producing the prescribed gear-state indication. Treat those as separate checks: first establish that the mechanism actuates the switch as required, then verify the indication through the circuit.

## Safety and approved limits before adjustment

Before any change, identify the approved instructions that apply to the aircraft and installation. Follow their required procedures for making the aircraft and landing-gear mechanism safe, gaining access, isolating systems if required, and moving or supporting the mechanism. Do not infer a safe method from the switch’s appearance or from a procedure for another installation.

Locate the specified actuation point, clearances, permitted travel, adjustment sequence, securing requirements, and electrical test or acceptance criteria in the governing instructions. Do not substitute an estimated dimension, a generic switch position, or an assumed torque value. If required limits or test conditions are unavailable, stop rather than adjust by trial and error.

Proceed only when the mechanism can be made safe and the approved procedure permits the planned inspection and movement. If a safe condition cannot be established, or the installation cannot be identified well enough to find its applicable instructions, do not disturb the switch or linkage. Have the discrepancy referred for appropriate maintenance review.

## Inspect the micro switch, target, and linkage

Inspect before changing the switch position. Check that the switch mounting is secure and the body and actuator show no evident damage. Inspect the target and linkage for looseness, wear, interference, misalignment, or restricted movement, as permitted by the approved procedure. Look for signs that the actuator is being contacted in an unintended way or that the linkage is binding before it reaches the required condition.

Also inspect the wiring and connector for evident damage or poor integrity within the scope of the authorized inspection. Do not assume that a missed or incorrect indication proves the switch position is wrong. A mechanical defect, a wiring or connector issue, or a problem elsewhere in the monitored circuit may be responsible. Adjustment cannot correct a damaged component or an electrical fault.

Move or cycle the mechanism only as the applicable instructions allow. Observe how the target contacts the actuator and whether the linkage moves freely through the relevant range. If there is binding, excess looseness, interference, damage, or uncertainty about the observed movement, resolve the cause under the approved procedure before attempting to set the switch.

## Adjust the micro switch within installation limits

Use only the installation-specific sequence for releasing, positioning, and securing the switch or its mounting feature. Position it so the gear-linked target actuates the switch at the specified condition and the actuator remains within the travel limits defined by the applicable instructions. The travel definitions describe what to assess, but do not authorize a particular amount of pretravel or overtravel.

Do not force the actuator, use the switch as a mechanical stop, or compensate for linkage wear by moving the switch beyond its specified range. Avoid making an adjustment before the target, linkage, and mounting have been inspected. If the specified actuation point cannot be achieved while maintaining the required clearance and travel, stop and investigate the mounting, linkage, target, or component condition instead of continuing to reposition the switch.

Secure the switch using the specified method and requirements. Then recheck its relationship to the target and confirm that the adjustment has not introduced interference or restricted movement. Where the instructions do not establish the adjustment limits or the required securing method, leave the setting unresolved and obtain the applicable approved guidance.

## Verify aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch adjustment

Verification has two parts. First, use the approved procedure to position or cycle the mechanism through the required conditions. Confirm that the target actuates the switch at the specified gear condition, that the actuator movement stays within its permitted range, and that the switch changes state as established by the prescribed test. If the instructions call for a continuity check or another electrical test, perform that test as specified; do not substitute an unapproved method.

Second, confirm the actual monitor-circuit indication for each required gear state. A switch-state change is not, by itself, proof that the complete circuit reports the correct condition. Check the required indications and transitions against the applicable acceptance criteria. Do not infer that an indicator is correct solely because the linkage moved or the switch actuated.

Confirm that the result is repeatable under the approved test procedure, the mounting remains secure, and movement is unobstructed. Check for unintended indications during transitions where the governing procedure requires it. If either the mechanical actuation or the monitor indication fails its specified check, the adjustment has not been verified. Return to approved fault-isolation steps rather than treating one successful layer as sufficient.

## When adjustment does not resolve the indication

If the indication remains incorrect, recheck the target and linkage, mounting, actuator contact, wiring, connectors, and monitor response using the applicable approved fault-isolation procedure. The failure may not be caused by switch position. Do not shift the switch outside its specified range to conceal wear, damage, binding, or an electrical problem.

Stop if travel, clearance, actuation, or the circuit response cannot be verified, or if the adjustment cannot be brought within the installation limits. Record the adjustment, checks performed, results, and unresolved discrepancies as required by the applicable maintenance process. Refer unresolved mechanical or electrical issues to qualified maintenance personnel. Aircraft landing gear monitor micro switch adjustment is complete only when the prescribed mechanical and indication checks meet the installation’s approved criteria.

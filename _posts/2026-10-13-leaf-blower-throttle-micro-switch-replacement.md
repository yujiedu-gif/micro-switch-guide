A leaf blower that won't respond when you squeeze the trigger, or won't shut off, usually sends its owner looking for a new switch. The trouble is that "the switch at the throttle" means different parts on different machines. **A gas blower's throttle can be all linkage, with the stop switch as a separate part, while a battery blower's trigger can press a real micro switch that tells the motor controller to run.** The steps below cover how to tell which kind you have, the safety steps for each, how to test a micro switch and how to fit the new one.

## Quick answer

On a battery leaf blower, the throttle trigger can press a micro switch, a small snap-action switch that signals the motor controller; Ryobi's repair sheet for its RY40407VNM 40-volt blower shows one in the wiring diagram. On a gas blower, Echo's PB-2520 manual lists the throttle trigger and the stop switch as separate controls, so check which part has failed before buying one.

## Which blowers actually have a micro switch at the throttle

A micro switch, also called a snap-action switch, is a small switch that flips its contacts with a click once its button or lever is pushed a set distance. Whether your blower has one at the trigger depends on what powers it.

| | Gas blower (example: Echo PB-2520) | Battery blower (examples: Ryobi RY40407VNM, Sealey CP108VBBO) |
|---|---|---|
| What the trigger does | Works the throttle; Echo's PB-2520 manual lists a throttle connecting rod | Switches the motor on; on Ryobi's RY404017 it also sets speed |
| Electrical switch near the trigger | Stop switch, a separate control | Switch assembly, which can include a micro switch |
| What the parts list calls it | Stop switch | Micro switch (Ryobi wiring diagram, Sealey parts list) |

Echo's operator's manual for the PB-2520 shows how a gas blower is laid out. The manual lists the throttle position lever, the stop switch and the throttle trigger as separate parts of the handle. Echo's maintenance schedule for the PB-2520 calls for greasing the throttle connecting rod. The electrical control on the handle is the stop switch.

Battery blowers are built differently. Ryobi's repair sheet for the RY40407VNM 40-volt blower lists a trigger and a variable speed switch as parts. The same sheet's wiring diagram labels a motor, a switch, a micro switch, a motor controller and the battery contact plate. Sealey's parts list for its CP108VBBO 10.8-volt cordless leaf blower names a micro switch and a switch button as separate parts.

To find out which your blower has, look up the repair sheet or parts diagram for your exact model number. If the parts list names a micro switch, that's the part this guide is about. If the only electrical part near the throttle is a stop or on/off switch, see our trigger switch explainer for how those differ.

## Safety before you open the handle

The two kinds of blower carry different hazards, and each maker's manual sets out its own precautions.

**Gas blowers.** Echo's PB-2520 manual says to always stop the engine, disconnect the spark plug, and wait for all moving parts to stop before clearing debris or servicing the unit. Echo also says to let the unit cool first. The same manual warns never to do maintenance with the engine running. Disconnecting the spark plug wire keeps the engine from firing if the recoil starter (the pull cord) gets pulled while you work. Work away from sparks and flames: Echo's manual also warns that fuel vapors are extremely flammable.

**Battery blowers.** Ryobi's manual for its 40-volt backpack blower says to always remove the battery pack before doing maintenance or reaching any moving parts. Ryobi's manual for its RY40406 blower adds that the switch should be in the locked or off position before you insert the battery pack, and that a blower the switch can't control is dangerous and must be repaired. Don't run a jumper wire across the trigger switch to test the motor with the battery in.

Ryobi also puts a limit on home repair: its RY40406 manual says blower service must be done only by qualified repair personnel and calls for identical replacement parts.

## Confirm the switch is the problem

Before ordering a part, match the symptom to the switch.

- **Battery blower does nothing when you squeeze the trigger.** Check the battery charge and the battery contacts first, since Ryobi's wiring diagram for the RY40407VNM runs the switch circuit from the battery contact plate. If the battery is good and nothing happens, the trigger switch, the micro switch, the wiring or the motor controller can be at fault, and the continuity test below separates the switch from the rest.
- **Battery blower won't turn off.** Remove the battery at once. A switch whose contacts stay closed, or a trigger that sticks down, can both cause it.
- **Gas engine won't stop.** Echo's PB-2520 manual says that if the engine doesn't stop when the stop switch is moved to STOP, close the choke to stall it and have your dealer repair the stop switch before using the unit again.
- **Gas engine won't start.** Echo's troubleshooting chart lists the ignition set at STOP, an electrical problem and the interlock switch as causes of no spark, along with fuel and spark plug causes. A trigger that doesn't change engine speed points to the throttle linkage or cable, not to a micro switch.

## How to test a leaf blower micro switch

With the battery out, open the handle and photograph how the wires connect before you unplug anything. Most micro switches have three terminals: common (COM), normally closed (NC) and normally open (NO). Electronic Design's explainer on snap-action switches describes the at-rest state: with the plunger fully released, the common contact rests against the NC contact, so COM to NC conducts. "Normally" means with nothing pressing the switch.

Disconnect the switch's wires, set a multimeter to continuity, and probe the terminals the blower uses.

| Switch button | COM to NC | COM to NO |
|---|---|---|
| Released | Continuity | Open |
| Pressed past the click | Open | Continuity |

A switch that reads the same in both positions, or that flickers as you press slowly, has failed or is dirty inside. One switch maker's technical notes on its micro switches put closed-contact resistance generally under 20 mΩ, so a good contact reads as a near-zero short on a hobby meter. If your photo shows only two terminals wired, the blower uses just that pair, so judge the switch on that pair. Our step-by-step guide on how to test a leaf blower throttle micro switch covers probe placement in more detail.

If the switch tests good, press the trigger by hand and watch whether it actually pushes the switch's button far enough to click. A worn or cracked trigger, or a switch that has shifted in its mount, can leave a working switch unpressed.

## Fitting the replacement so it works

Ryobi's RY40406 manual calls for identical replacement parts when servicing a blower, so order by the part number on your model's repair sheet where one is listed. If you're matching a generic micro switch instead, check these against the old one:

- **Size and mounting holes.** Micro switches come in size classes with different hole spacing; our guide to micro switch mounting holes lists datasheet figures by size.
- **Actuator.** A pin plunger, a lever or a roller lever each sit at a different height and need the trigger to press them a different way.
- **Terminals.** Solder lugs, quick-connect tabs and PCB pins aren't interchangeable without rewiring.
- **Which pair the blower uses.** Wire COM and NO, or COM and NC, exactly as your photo shows. Our guide to NO and NC terminals explains the difference.

After fitting it, check the trigger travel before you close the housing. A switch maker's FAQ on how far to push a basic switch says the operating part shouldn't stop near the point where the switch clicks, because the switch can chatter. The same FAQ sets the pushing distance at 60% to 90% of the switch's rated overtravel, the extra distance the button can safely move after it clicks. In practice, a full squeeze of the trigger should click the switch clearly and still not push its button flat against the body.

Route the wires so the trigger can't pinch them, close the housing, and test continuity once more through the handle if you can reach the connector.

Insert the battery only with the switch in the locked or off position, as Ryobi's manual requires, and check that the blower starts with the trigger and stops when you let go.

## FAQ

### My blower won't start. What should I do?

On a gas blower, Echo's troubleshooting chart for the PB-2520 starts with fuel at the carburetor and cylinder, then the spark: the ignition left at STOP, an electrical problem or the interlock switch can each stop the spark. On a battery blower, check the battery and its contacts, then test the trigger switch and any micro switch with a multimeter and the battery removed.

### Why won't my leaf blower run at full throttle?

On a gas blower, Echo's chart for an engine that doesn't accelerate properly lists the air filter, fuel filter, fuel vent, spark plug, carburetor adjustment, cooling system and spark arrestor screen. None of those is a switch. On a battery blower, Ryobi's RY404017 manual says its variable speed switch trigger gives higher speed with more trigger pressure, so a trigger that won't squeeze fully can cap the speed. A micro switch only switches on and off.

## What to check before ordering a part

Find the repair sheet or parts diagram for your exact model before buying anything, because it tells you whether the throttle has a micro switch at all and gives the part number to order. If your blower's diagram names only a stop switch, there's no throttle micro switch to replace, and the manual's troubleshooting chart is the next place to look.

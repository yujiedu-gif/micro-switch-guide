For an installation that uses a micro switch to detect an automated guided vehicle guard’s position, adjustment should correct documented actuator engagement, not alter or bypass the interlock logic. The automated guided vehicle guard interlock micro switch adjustment must follow the equipment and switch instructions for that installation. Those instructions define applicable operating points and travel limits; there is no universal adjustment dimension for every switch. Prepare the vehicle as prescribed, inspect the guard and mounting before changing position, then validate both physical engagement and the approved interlock response. A controller indication alone does not establish that the guard and actuator are correctly aligned.

## What the guard interlock micro switch adjustment is meant to correct

In an installation that uses a micro switch for guard detection, guard movement acts on the switch actuator. The switch’s operation provides an input associated with guard position, while the equipment’s interlock circuit or control logic determines how that input is used. The switch is a component of the installation, not the complete guard-protection function.

Adjustment is meant to correct the physical relationship between the guard, its moving parts, and the switch actuator. If that relationship is wrong, the actuator may not engage at the intended point or may not operate consistently as the guard moves. A change in switch position cannot correct every possible fault. It also does not change the intended safety logic or authorize operation with the guard interlock defeated.

Apply this procedure only when the installed equipment uses the specified guard interlock micro switch and the applicable instructions permit adjustment. Confirm the switch and adjustment method from the equipment and switch documentation. If the component or permitted adjustment cannot be identified, do not assume a familiar-looking switch has the same operating point or travel limits. Numerical travel limits are device-specific requirements to check in the applicable specification sheet, not values to infer from the term “micro switch.”

## Prepare safely before automated guided vehicle guard interlock micro switch adjustment

Before access, follow the vehicle and site procedures to prevent vehicle motion and isolate energy as prescribed. Use the documented access and test procedure for the installation. These steps are specific to the vehicle and site; do not substitute an improvised isolation method or assume that a stationary vehicle is in a safe condition for adjustment.

Keep the guard interlock functional throughout the work. Do not bypass or defeat it to reach the switch, reposition the actuator, or obtain a system indication. If the prescribed procedure does not allow the required access or test under the current conditions, stop and have the work handled through the appropriate qualified process.

Identify the applicable equipment instructions and switch specification before touching the mounting. They must establish the permitted adjustment method and the operating-point or travel limits used for verification. Do not proceed on assumed terminal assignments, signal states, or test steps. The available information does not establish those details for every installation.

## Inspect guard movement, mounting, and switch actuator engagement

First examine the physical path that transfers guard movement to the switch. Move or inspect the guard only as allowed by the documented procedure. Check whether it travels as intended, and look at the hinges or guides, fasteners, switch mounting, and actuator for visible looseness, binding, damage, or wear. These observations help distinguish a position problem from a guard or mounting fault that repositioning cannot correct.

Next observe where the guard or its mechanism contacts the actuator during the intended travel. Determine from the applicable instructions whether engagement occurs at the documented operating point and whether it is consistent as the guard moves through its expected positions. Do not infer correct operation merely because the actuator is touched or a controller changes its indication. Contact at the wrong point, inconsistent engagement, or movement that forces the actuator may signal an alignment or mechanical condition requiring further attention.

If a hinge, guide, fastener, actuator, or mounting is damaged or unstable, stop before adjustment. A switch position change should not be used to compensate for a guard that binds or a mounting that moves. Record the observed condition and refer it for correction under the equipment’s service procedure.

## Adjust alignment within the documented operating limits

Only adjust when the switch instructions identify an allowable mounting method and the physical inspection has not found an unresolved defect. Use the specified method and the operating point or travel limits documented for the installed switch. The applicable specification sheet governs numerical travel limits; the component category alone does not supply them.

Make controlled positional changes, then observe the guard-to-actuator relationship again. The aim is consistent engagement at the documented point while the guard follows its intended movement. Do not force the actuator, assume a common adjustment direction, or introduce a generic clearance or travel dimension. Those details depend on the installed design and are not established universally.

After each change, check that the mounting remains secure and that the guard still moves as intended. If the documented limits cannot be met without excessive repositioning, force, or interference, stop. Do not continue adjusting in an attempt to make an unsuitable, damaged, or unstable arrangement appear to work. Follow the equipment instructions or refer the unresolved condition to qualified personnel.

## Verify guard-open and guard-closed response after adjustment

After adjustment, use the approved functional test for the specific installation. Check the physical sequence: the guard should move through its intended positions, and its mechanism should engage the switch actuator as prescribed. Compare the observed engagement with the documented operating point and travel limits. This check addresses mechanical alignment; by itself, it does not confirm the electrical signal or the complete interlock response.

Then perform the permitted electrical or system-level confirmation described by the equipment procedure. Verify the required response for both guard-open and guard-closed states. The expected indication and test method are installation-specific, so do not infer them from a generic contact arrangement or from another vehicle. A controller indication is useful only as one part of the approved check: it does not prove that physical engagement is correct.

Treat the physical and electrical observations as separate checks. If the actuator engages as expected but the system response is absent or inconsistent, a circuit, switch, or other system fault may remain; the observation alone does not identify which one. If the system indicates a permitted state while the guard is open, or the response changes inconsistently, stop and escalate rather than accepting the adjustment. Do not infer that one successful indication proves the whole guard-protection function is correct.

## When adjustment is not enough and what to record

Adjustment is not a remedy for a damaged switch or actuator, unstable mounting, binding guard, or failure to meet documented operating limits. Do not compensate for such conditions by excessive repositioning or by defeating the interlock. Stop when the permitted adjustment cannot produce the documented physical engagement and approved functional response, or when the fault remains unclear.

After a successful check, restore protective covers and normal safeguards as required by the equipment procedure. Record the adjustment made, the applicable instructions or limits used, and the outcome of the approved functional test, including the guard-open and guard-closed responses. If any check fails, the result is inconsistent, or the installation cannot meet its documented limits, leave the issue unresolved and refer it to qualified personnel under the applicable service process. The automated guided vehicle guard interlock micro switch adjustment is complete only when the permitted mechanical checks and functional verification have been completed and documented; adjustment alone is not proof of a correct interlock.

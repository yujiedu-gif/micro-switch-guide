# Testing an Adjustable Desk Travel-Limit Micro Switch

An **adjustable desk travel limit micro switch continuity test** checks whether the switch contacts change electrical state when its actuator moves between released and operated positions. It can help distinguish a contact-state problem from a travel-limit mechanism that does not reach or operate the switch. The test alone does not establish that the controller, motor, or complete desk travel-limit system works.

Before interpreting a meter reading, identify the switch’s contact arrangement and terminal markings. A beep is not a universal pass result: what matters is whether the relevant contact pair behaves as expected in each state. Keep the desk isolated from power during continuity measurements, and follow the applicable service guidance.

## What the adjustable desk travel limit micro switch continuity test can establish

The test compares electrical continuity at the switch terminals with the actuator released and with it deliberately operated. A change between those readings supports the conclusion that the tested contacts change state. It does not, by itself, show that the switch is correctly positioned, that the desk mechanism engages it at the intended travel limit, or that the controller responds properly.

Identify the contact pair before judging the result. For a generic changeover switch, **COM** means common, **NO** means normally open, and **NC** means normally closed. “Normally” refers to the unoperated state. The expected readings therefore depend on which pair is being measured and on the switch’s actual schematic; do not assume a particular beep pattern or terminal layout without checking the device markings or circuit documentation.

This is a contact-state check, not a complete desk diagnosis. A state change can coexist with a mechanical engagement problem, incorrect installation, or a separate wiring or controller issue. Conversely, a reading that does not change is not conclusive evidence of a failed switch until the correct terminals, actuator movement, and measurement conditions have been checked.

## Make the adjustable desk safe and identify the switch contacts

Disconnect the desk from power before using continuity mode. Do not test an energized circuit in continuity mode. Follow the desk’s applicable service instructions and any relevant workplace requirements. The cited United States workplace rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work, requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment, and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. This test does not call for working on energized parts.

Locate the micro switch associated with the travel limit using the desk’s service information. Identify COM, NO, and NC from markings on the switch or the circuit diagram. Generic terminal symbols describe common changeover terminology, but the exact device schematic must be verified. Do not infer a terminal arrangement from the component category alone.

Set the meter to continuity or resistance mode and check its leads according to the meter’s instructions. Make a note of the initial reading or indication so you can compare it with the reading after actuation. If the switch remains connected to the desk wiring, other circuit paths can affect what the meter shows. An in-circuit result may therefore be ambiguous; do not treat it as a direct measurement of the switch contacts without considering the connected wiring.

## Measure continuity with the micro switch released and actuated

With power disconnected, place the probes across the identified contact pair. A common comparison uses COM–NO or COM–NC, but choose the pair from the switch markings or circuit diagram rather than assuming which contacts are present or relevant. Keep the probes on the same pair for both measurements.

First, record the reading with the switch actuator released. Then operate the actuator deliberately and gently, and record the reading again. Do not force the actuator or damage the mechanism. The purpose is to compare the two states, not to measure how far the actuator travels or to establish a desk adjustment value.

Interpret the pair according to its identified contact type. For a normally open pair, compare the released and actuated readings for the expected open-to-closed change. For a normally closed pair, compare for the expected closed-to-open change. A continuity indication may be a beep, a resistance value, or another meter display; its form depends on the meter. The important observation is whether the reading changes consistently between the released and actuated states as expected for that pair.

If the result is unclear while the switch is connected to the desk wiring, stop and consult the applicable service instructions. Isolating a lead may help distinguish the switch contacts from other circuit paths, but do so only when safe and appropriate under those instructions. Do not infer a definitive switch fault from an ambiguous in-circuit reading.

## Interpret the adjustable desk travel limit micro switch continuity test results

A consistent change between the released and actuated readings supports contact operation for the pair tested. It does not confirm that the switch is mounted or adjusted correctly, that the desk reaches the switch at its intended limit, or that the controller and motor operate properly. Keep those questions separate from the electrical state comparison.

If the reading does not change, check the diagnostic basics before concluding that the switch has failed: confirm that the probes are across the intended terminal pair, that the actuator actually moves between released and operated states, and that connected wiring is not affecting the measurement. A wrong terminal pair, incomplete actuator movement, or an in-circuit measurement path can produce a result that does not establish the switch’s contact condition. If a lead is to be isolated for a clearer check, follow service instructions and proceed only when safe and appropriate.

An unstable reading can be consistent with intermittent contact, loose connections, or unreliable probe contact. It is not enough on its own to identify which cause applies. Check the probe contact and inspect accessible terminals and wiring for looseness or damage, without assuming that the switch itself is the source. Use the documented contact arrangement and compare readings across both switch states before drawing a conclusion.

The test also cannot supply a universal travel or adjustment threshold. Numerical travel limits are requirements of the applicable specification sheet and must not be inferred from the component category. If the service procedure requires a particular mechanical position or acceptance limit, use that procedure rather than deriving a value from a continuity reading.

## Check travel-limit engagement and wiring before concluding the switch failed

With power isolated, observe whether the desk mechanism reaches and physically operates the travel-limit switch at the intended point. A switch that changes state when operated by hand may not be engaged by the desk mechanism. Conversely, a mechanism that does not reach or operate the actuator can explain why the desk’s limit function is not being triggered even when the contacts change state during a meter check.

Inspect accessible terminals and wiring for looseness or damage, while keeping the check within the applicable service instructions. Do not alter the adjustment or assume a universal setting. If the mechanism does not engage the switch, the contact test alone cannot identify the correct adjustment or establish a repair procedure.

After testing, reconnect or reassemble only as directed by the desk’s approved service procedure. Confirm operation only through that approved process, keeping hands and objects clear of moving parts. A meter result can support a conclusion about whether the tested contacts changed state; it does not certify the full travel-limit system.

In short, the adjustable desk travel limit micro switch continuity test is a comparison of the identified contacts in released and actuated states, performed with the desk isolated from power. Interpret the change according to the verified terminal pair, then check whether the mechanism actually engages the switch and whether accessible wiring is intact. If readings remain ambiguous, use the applicable service instructions rather than treating the meter result as a complete diagnosis.

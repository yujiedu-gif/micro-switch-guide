## Distinguish switch mounting location from the gear position being monitored

A micro switch is a physical component with an actuator and electrical contacts. In a monitoring installation, movement of a landing-gear component may operate the actuator through a mechanical feature. The switch contacts then form part of an electrical circuit whose state is used in the monitoring or indication system. The exact arrangement is installation-specific and cannot be inferred from the name of the switch or the displayed indication alone.

This gives three separate points to trace. First is the switch’s mounting position on the aircraft. Second is the mechanical contact or engagement point where motion acts on its actuator. Third is the gear position or condition that the monitoring system represents. A switch’s mounting point and the gear position reported by the system are connected through the installation, but they do not necessarily describe the same physical point.

The mechanical path and electrical path answer different questions. Installation information helps show where the switch is mounted and what moving feature operates it. Circuit documentation helps show how the contacts relate to the monitoring function. Following both paths avoids treating a contact state as if it directly disclosed the component’s physical location.

An indicator can show a monitored state, but it does not establish where the switch is mounted. A schematic symbol can show a switch’s place in a circuit, but it does not necessarily establish the physical mounting point or actuator engagement location. Use aircraft-specific documentation to connect the indication, circuit, switch, and mechanical movement rather than inferring a location from any one of them.

## Verify the identified switch without assuming its operating point

Identifying a switch’s mounting position does not establish exactly when its contacts change state. The physical location, the point where the actuator is engaged, and the switch’s operating characteristics are separate matters. Do not infer an adjustment value or operating point from the switch’s apparent position, an indicator state, or a generic drawing.

The terminology itself distinguishes several characteristics. In section 6.5.17, releasing force is the force or torque to which force on the actuator must be reduced to permit the contacts to return to the unoperated position after operation. These terms describe operating characteristics; they do not provide an aircraft-specific mounting location or authorize adjustment of an installed switch.

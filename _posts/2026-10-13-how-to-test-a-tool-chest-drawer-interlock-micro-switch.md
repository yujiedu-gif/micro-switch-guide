A switch-level continuity check can show whether the isolated contacts change state as the actuator moves, but it cannot establish that a tool chest drawer interlock or the chest as a whole works correctly. How to test a tool chest drawer interlock micro switch depends first on confirming that the particular interlock actually uses an electromechanical micro switch and identifying its documented contact arrangement. Drawer behavior, wiring, and the host circuit remain separate questions.

The procedure below is limited to identifying the switch’s mechanical input and checking its contacts in defined actuator states. Do not infer which drawer position should enable, disable, or signal anything. If the switch, contact terminals, or safe isolation conditions cannot be identified, the result is inconclusive; do not substitute assumptions for documentation.

## how to test a tool chest drawer interlock micro switch

Begin by checking whether the actual interlock contains a micro switch. A drawer interlock may use some other arrangement, and this guidance does not establish how every tool chest interlock is built. Follow the physical mechanism only when the component and its role can be identified.

An isolated continuity check can assess the contact state of the identified switch with its actuator released and at a defined actuated position. It does not test the chest’s complete interlock function. The installed result also depends on the documented wiring, circuit function, permissible load, mechanical alignment, and other parts of the chest. The connection diagram, terminal markings, circuit function, and permissible load for the exact switch and equipment govern; do not infer terminal identity or behavior from physical layout alone.

Do not assume that an open or closed contact reading corresponds to a particular drawer state or intended system response. That relationship requires the specific switch documentation and equipment circuit information. A click or one meter reading cannot establish that the installed interlock works safely.

## Identify the micro switch and its mechanical actuator

Locate the switch by tracing the actual drawer, latch, or linkage mechanism, if present. The external mechanism may move the switch actuator as a drawer or latch changes position, but the available information does not establish a universal arrangement, travel, or intended state. Do not assume that the drawer itself contacts the actuator directly.

Observe the mechanism without forcing it or adjusting it. Note whether the drawer, latch, or linkage appears to reach and release the actuator. No apparent movement, incomplete movement, or uncertain release is an observation about the mechanical input, not proof of an internal contact fault. Likewise, actuator movement alone does not prove the contacts work.

Identify the exact switch and consult its connection diagram and terminal markings alongside the equipment information. Confirm which terminals the documented contact arrangement uses. Do not assign COM, NO, or NC based on where a terminal sits or how it looks. If the switch identity, markings, or diagram are unavailable or unclear, terminal selection and expected contact state remain unresolved. Stop rather than guessing.

## Prepare for an isolated continuity check

A continuity measurement must be made with the switch electrically isolated. Do not improvise a live-circuit test. If isolation, access, or the switch’s separation from other circuit paths is uncertain, stop and refer the work to a qualified person.

In the United States workplace context, the cited rule generally requires exposed live parts to be deenergized before work. It also requires a qualified person to verify the deenergized condition with test equipment and limits work on energized parts to qualified persons. Verify all applicable requirements for the circumstances; this article does not provide a procedure for working on energized equipment.

Before measuring, establish the documented contact terminals and the contact-state description for the particular switch. Use the meter’s continuity or resistance function as appropriate to its instructions. A reading through an installed circuit may involve wiring or other components, so it is not an isolated switch observation. If isolation cannot be confirmed, the measurement cannot be interpreted as a switch-only check.

## Check the tool chest drawer interlock micro switch at defined actuator states

With the switch isolated and the documented terminals identified, record the meter reading with the actuator released. Describe the state precisely: the actuator is released, and the switch is electrically isolated. Do not label this state as the drawer’s “normal” condition unless the equipment documentation defines that relationship.

Next, move the actuator only through its permitted range. Record the reading at a defined actuated state, again keeping the switch isolated. Do not force the actuator or infer a permitted range from a click or from the appearance of the mechanism. If the mechanism does not reach a clearly defined state without force, leave the result unresolved.

Compare the two observations only with a supported contact-state description for that specific switch. The evidence here does not establish a universal expected resistance, continuity indication, terminal pair, or transition for every micro switch. If documentation does not specify the expected state, a change or lack of change is an observation, not a conclusive pass or failure.

Keep the test record narrow: identify the documented terminals, whether the actuator was released or at a defined actuated position, and the meter observation. This helps distinguish a repeatable switch-level result from an assumption about drawer behavior or the whole interlock.

## Interpret a click, unchanged reading, or changing reading

A click can indicate movement of a snap mechanism, but it does not prove electrical continuity, correct terminal selection, wiring integrity, or suitability for the equipment’s load. The switch may move while the measured contacts do not behave as expected, or the sound may not identify which contact state was tested.

If the reading does not change between the defined states, several explanations remain possible. The mechanical input may not move the actuator far enough; the selected terminals may not be the documented pair; the meter setup or isolation may be unsuitable; or the contacts may not change as the documentation describes. The observation alone does not distinguish among these possibilities.

If the reading changes, that is limited evidence about the isolated contacts between the tested states and terminals. It does not verify the installed wiring, confirm the interlock’s logic, establish the expected drawer response, or prove safe operation. Interpret the result only against the specific switch documentation and the defined test conditions.

## Decide what the test leaves unresolved

Keep mechanical input, switch contacts, terminal identification, wiring, and faults elsewhere in the chest as distinct possibilities. A linkage that does not actuate the switch is not the same finding as contacts that fail to change in an isolated test. An uncertain terminal assignment is not evidence that the switch is defective, and a switch-level change does not rule out a wiring or host-circuit problem.

An IP code, if one is documented for an enclosure, states defined enclosure-protection characteristics. It does not by itself establish electrical rating, chemical compatibility, corrosion resistance, vibration endurance, condensation performance, or complete application suitability. Evaluate application requirements separately; an enclosure code cannot settle the interlock’s electrical or mechanical behavior.

Do not infer that the chest is safe or that its drawers will behave as intended from a click or an isolated continuity result. If the documentation, isolation, mechanical actuation, or result remains uncertain, leave the system-level diagnosis unresolved and seek qualified assessment where needed. How to test a tool chest drawer interlock micro switch has a firm boundary: a defined isolated contact check can inform switch-level assessment, but it cannot certify the installed interlock or chest.
